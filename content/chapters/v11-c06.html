Control the whole payment, not just the application

Capability. Trace a critical payment from instruction to authorized settlement, quantify how queues, shared dependencies and control coverage change outcomes, and design evidence that can close an incident or restrict a model's use. Distinguish financial capacity, operational completion, legal authority and empirical validation. The prerequisite is liquidity, funding and asset-liability management; the accounting distinctions of Chapter 2 and the model-governance reasoning of Volume 10 Chapter 12 also apply.

Cedar's settlement account has enough cash. Its staffing model fits every training observation. The recovery dashboard ends with fourteen acknowledgments and the expected total cash debit. Yet four obligations missed their deadlines, one intended payment is absent, and one instruction was paid twice. A control review must explain those mechanisms; favorable totals cannot substitute for the promised service.

Cedar, its instructions, capacities, probabilities and policy limits are original synthetic teaching material. CU denotes a currency unit. A cutoff is the end of one processing window; all instructions released in that window arrive before its service begins. The examples assume one entity, one currency, unit-sized processing jobs and fully available cash. No current incident, empirical control-effect estimate, national legal rule or real approval is asserted.

1. Begin with the obligation and its failure mechanism

Operational risk concerns adverse consequences of failed processes, people, systems or external events. Legal uncertainty concerns what rights, duties and remedies actually apply. Model risk concerns adverse decisions based on an unsuitable or incorrectly used representation. These mechanisms interact: an accurate cash forecast can still be unusable if payment authority is missing; a valid mandate does not make a broken gateway process a transfer.

Define a critical operation by the service delivered to its users. For Cedar, it is settling each authorized instruction to its intended destination by its agreed cutoff, with a reconstructible record. Application uptime is only an intermediate condition. A working login screen does not establish that an instruction reached the settlement system or that the beneficiary received the right amount.

The final March 2021 BCBS operational-resilience principles provide international method context for identifying critical operations, mapping dependencies and testing response and recovery. They do not supply Cedar's capacities, tolerances or a national-law compliance conclusion. Our calculations below specify the actual assumptions needed for each result.

2. Conserve work and respect the deadline

For instruction i, let ri be its release window and di its inclusive deadline, with ri≤di. Each instruction consumes one service slot. Let at be arrivals, μt usable slots, qt unfinished work after window t and q0=0. Under a work-conserving policy that retains overdue work, service is st=min(μt,qt−1+at), so

qt=qt−1+at−st;   cumulative arrivals = cumulative completions + current backlog.

Use earliest deadline first (EDF) among released unfinished instructions; break ties by release and then ID. Completing late work reduces backlog but does not undo lateness. A disappeared item is not a completion. Amounts and counts also differ: one large payment need not consume more processing slots than a small one, while its consequences may be larger. Our equal-amount example isolates the identity and timing problem.

Feasibility result for this unit-job setting. If any schedule meets every deadline with the stated releases and slots, EDF does too. To see this, take a feasible schedule and compare its first different service choice with EDF. EDF selects an available instruction a with a deadline no later than the other available choice b. The feasible schedule must place a at some later position no later than a's deadline. Exchange a and b: a is already released, and b can occupy the later position because it was available earlier and its deadline is at least a's. If the original position was idle, move a forward instead. Repeating the exchange makes the feasible schedule agree with EDF. Multiple slots in a window can be ordered consecutively in this argument.

The result requires equal one-slot work, availability at the start of a window, and no extra routing, precedence or exclusivity constraint. It does not say that EDF maximizes the number of timely jobs for every infeasible instance. Nor is a cumulative-capacity total alone a sufficient test: one instruction released and due in window 2 cannot use the sole slot in window 1 when capacities are (1,0). The sum is adequate but the usable date is wrong.

3. Reconcile identities, not just totals

The queue is a proposed execution plan. Evidence of actual settlement is a different object. For each authorized ID, retain its intent, approval, release, external settlement reference and final status. An aggregate debit total is useful for cash reconciliation, but it cannot prove that every intended obligation was discharged exactly once. An acknowledgment may report receipt by an intermediate system rather than final settlement; its semantics must be verified.

Worked example 2 — Fourteen debits, thirteen obligations discharged

The observed log agrees with the first three rows above. At cutoff 4, however, it reports B05,C01,C02,B02,D01,D02: B02 was already paid at cutoff 3 and C03 is absent. There are still fourteen acknowledgments and 140 CU of cash debits. The ID reconciliation finds one duplicate and one missing instruction. Ten legitimate payments are timely, three legitimate payments are late, and one remains unpaid. The set of obligations missed by their deadlines still has four members.

For the bookkeeping illustration, Cedar opens with cash 200, payment liabilities 140 and equity 60, with no other accounts. A first correctly authorized settlement discharges its 10-CU liability; each duplicate, unknown or premature debit is assumed irreversible and borne by Cedar without recovery. These are stipulated case conventions, not a general legal conclusion about payment finality or loss allocation.

Matching the 140-CU aggregate debit to the expected 140-CU instruction total would miss the unpaid liability and the 10-CU loss. Conversely, a final accounting identity can balance while concealing the wrong classification. An independent closure test must reconstruct both the identity-level discharge and its cash/equity entries. A later correction needs its own dated transaction and evidence; rewriting the original log would destroy that evidence.

4. Two controls need not provide two independent protections

Map dependencies as well as application names. A primary and backup route may share an identity service, network, signing service, data source, operator pool or third-party control plane. Moving to a different application on the same failed dependency may provide no usable capacity. Likewise, separate approver names do not establish independent judgment if both rely on the same compromised instruction or credential boundary.

Use a deliberately stipulated probability experiment to see the consequence. Conditional on an unauthorized attempt, let c be the probability of a common event that defeats both controls. Outside that event, the first and second controls miss with probabilities q1 and q2, independently conditional on that branch. The second control is applied to fraction f of attempts, selected independently of these failure states. When it is absent, only the first protects the payment. Then

m1=c+(1−c)q1,   m2=c+(1−c)q2,
j=P(both miss when both applied)=c+(1−c)q1q2,
e=P(escape | unauthorized attempt)=(1−f)m1+fj
=c+(1−c)q1(1−f+fq2).

The first two equalities condition on whether the common event occurs. The last conditions on coverage. Expanding the product of the marginal miss probabilities gives

j−m1m2=c(1−c)(1−q1)(1−q2)≥0.

Thus multiplying the unconditional marginals generally understates joint failure in this construction. The identity is not an empirical estimate of any institution's dependence. If the second control is bypassed precisely for unusually risky cases, a single f and the stated conditional miss rates no longer justify the mixture; estimate or stress the relevant conditional populations separately.

Worked example 3 — Coverage cannot remove a common failure floor

Set c=.05, q1=.10, q2=.20 and f=.80. Marginal misses are .145 and .24; their product is .0348. The correct joint miss with both controls applied is .069, and incomplete coverage raises escape probability to .0842.

In a separate 1,000-request experiment, an unauthorized attempt occurs with probability .02 per request and each escaped attempt causes a fixed irrecoverable loss of 25 CU. Expected escaped attempts are 1000(.02)(.0842)=1.684; expected loss is 42.1 CU. These are conditional scenario calculations, not estimated frequencies from the fourteen-payment incident. Independence across requests is not needed for this expectation when the stated marginal law applies to each request; a tail distribution or probability of at least one loss would need further dependence information.

Full second-control coverage reduces expected loss to 1000(.02)(.069)(25)=34.5. It cannot meet Cedar's fictional 20-CU expected-loss bound while the stated common event persists. At full coverage the bound requires 500[.02+.98c]≤20, or c≤1/49. If the common cause were actually removed, full coverage would give expected loss 10. Claiming that change requires a changed architecture and evidence, not merely typing zero into the probability field. Expected loss also does not replace deadline, tail-severity or authorization tests.

5. A perfect fit can be the wrong deployment

Separate the question a model was fitted to from the decision it now supports. Cedar fits completed processing jobs y to available staff x using the through-origin model y≈θx. Minimizing Σ(θxi−yi)² gives θ̂=Σxiyi/Σxi² when Σxi²>0. This follows by expanding the quadratic or setting its derivative to zero; its positive quadratic coefficient makes the minimizer unique. It is a fitting result, not proof that throughput has a stable causal relationship with staffing.

Worked example 4 — Zero calibration error, four wrong deadline predictions

The original training pairs are (staff, completed jobs)=(1,2),(2,4),(4,8), measured over comparable windows with no binding downstream constraint. Hence Σx²=21, Σxy=42, θ̂=2 jobs per staff-window and every training residual is zero. Deployment has staffing (1,1,4,3), so a staff-only rule forecasts slots (2,2,8,6) and fourteen timely payments.

The independently supplied gateway limits are (2,2,4,6). Under the case's deterministic capacity assumption, effective slots are min(floor(θ̂xt),gatewayt), giving (2,2,4,6). The operational plan therefore has only ten timely payments, as in Worked example 1. The model's fitted staffing relationship has not changed; the deployed decision rule omitted a binding part of the process. The error occurs even at a staffing level present in the training data.

Repairing the deployment rule to include the bottleneck makes the forecast honest but supplies no new capacity. Removing the deadline failure additionally requires the authorized resource change to (2,2,8,2), or some other proved feasible plan. A new rehearsal can then test identity-safe execution. The historical missed deadlines and duplicate debit remain facts of the original case, even if the repaired rehearsal succeeds.

Validation should challenge data definitions, units, timestamps, missing constraints, calibration, implementation and the use being proposed. Freeze the candidate artifact and inputs, compare meaningful benchmarks, test changed loads and dependencies, and examine outcomes in periods not used to choose the model. Assess the financial or service consequence of an error, not only its average fit statistic. A vendor certificate or a familiar version label does not prove that the operating artifact and configuration match the reviewed one.

For dated US context, 17 April 2026 SR 26-2 supersedes SR 11-7 and SR 21-8. Its interagency attachment discusses tailored model use, challenge, validation and monitoring; it is guidance, not prescriptive enforceable standards. Its definition excludes some simple arithmetic and deterministic tools. Cedar's queue calculator is not automatically a model under that definition. Such scope distinctions do not remove operational controls, and our fictional acceptance thresholds are not agency requirements.

6. Treat unresolved rights as a decision constraint

A contract interpretation, disputed authority or jurisdictional restriction cannot be settled by a successful simulation. Before relying on a fallback route, identify the legal entity and client mandate, the provider agreement and permitted service, the effective dates, the governing jurisdiction, any needed consent, and who can give the relevant decision. Ask qualified legal and compliance owners for documented conclusions within their scope; a numerical risk analyst should not silently invent a favorable legal rule.

In Cedar's hypothetical file, the current gateway reservation supports only (2,2,4,6). The proposed (2,2,8,2) reservation requires provider confirmation and a documented authority review before cutoff 3. Until that evidence exists, the extra early slots are a counterfactual, not an approved resource. A legal review arriving at cutoff 4 may support a future arrangement; it cannot make the earlier payment timely.

7. Defend integrity through protection, detection, response and recovery

Cyber controls here are defensive controls around authorized systems. Protection includes least-privilege access, separation of release authority, managed credentials and changes, and protected copies of instruction and settlement data. Detection should look for mismatched intent, anomalous duplicate IDs, unexpected privilege changes, missing confirmations and a growing deadline backlog. Sensitive customer data and credentials should be protected in both operational and test records.

During a suspected compromise, follow the authorized incident process: establish an incident lead, contain affected release paths, preserve evidence, determine which instructions have actually settled, and communicate through the approved channels. A suspected bad record should not be “fixed” by destroying its history. Do not blindly replay a pending queue while external settlement status is uncertain.

Recovery is restoration of a correct, usable service, not merely restarting a server. A restored database can omit a payment that settled after its backup point. Before re-enabling release, reconcile against a trustworthy event history and external confirmations, test permissions and duplicate protection, and account for all unresolved IDs and cash. An isolated restore exercise should test the actual dependencies and representative volume. An unreadable backup, inaccessible key or shared failed identity service can defeat an apparently separate recovery path.

A recovery time objective is a chosen target for restoring a capability; a recovery point objective concerns the point to which data can be restored. Neither alone measures client impact. Cedar could restart in window 4 and clear its queue, while still missing the window-3 deadline. The final BCBS 2021 text connects ICT protection, detection, response, recovery and testing; it does not establish that a particular backup or supplier has worked.

8. Close findings with a reproducible outcome

At the end of window 2, Cedar has five outstanding instructions and expects three more due in window 3, against four usable slots. That is an actionable warning before the deadline. A review that waits for the final backlog to reach zero loses both the prevention opportunity and the true impact measure.

Separate containment, restoration and remediation. Operations owns the instruction inventory, queue and release procedure; treasury reconciles the actual account debits; technology owns the deployment and recovery mechanism; legal/compliance owners resolve the scoped authority question; independent risk challenge tests the argument and evidence. Senior decision authority decides the permitted residual exposure and resources. Names and dates are useful only when the responsibilities and decisions are real.

A defensible closure record states the observed failure, affected population, timing and harm; the causal explanation and competing hypotheses; a specific change with an owner; a frozen retest; and an independent acceptance criterion. For Cedar, repeat the adverse-capacity case, verify the corrected model still reports four misses without extra resources, then test the authorized changed reservation and confirm all fourteen exact IDs settle once by their deadlines. Test restart and retry behavior against external confirmations. Reconcile cash, remaining liabilities and any loss. Preserve the original incident beside the new test; do not replace one with the other.

The risk-probability example additionally needs evidence about actual control coverage and common dependencies before its assumed loss reduction can support a real decision. A successful small rehearsal does not estimate a rare-event frequency or establish that an attacker cannot adapt. Closure may properly mean restricted use with a documented unresolved limit, escalation trigger and review date, rather than a declaration that all operational risk is gone.

9. Reproduce the original control file

The portable package contains inputs, the standard-library Python model, saved output, a four-file manifest and a domain and execution guide. The quiet run(data) API preserves its input; financial fractions are exact strings. From the downloaded directory, run python3 v11-c06-analysis.py or use --input and --output. Validation also operates under -O; output cannot overwrite input or implementation through direct, symbolic or hard-link aliases.

The lab deliberately reports a staff-only forecast, a constrained plan and a separately observed log. It fits the original staffing slope, schedules unit jobs by EDF, reconciles every observed ID, and evaluates the conditional control experiment. Explicit closure gates keep fit, resource feasibility, executed identities, timing, loss assumptions and declared evidence separate. Passing all gates yields only eligible_for_independent_closure_review. Metadata booleans and version labels do not authenticate real documents or deployed artifacts.

Independent checks should reconstruct cash from matched versus unmatched identities, prove the service deficit by a deadline cut, compare all-on-time feasibility with an independent allocation method, and enumerate the small stated failure branches. A saved output comparison is useful for reproduction but does not independently establish those claims. Changes to the historical log are sensitivity exercises or labeled new rehearsals, not corrections to what actually happened.
