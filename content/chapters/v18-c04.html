Hiring, evaluation and talent development

Capability. Build an inspectable process for selecting, evaluating and developing experts, while separating evidence of performance from reputation, opportunity and uncertain outcomes. Prerequisite: organizations, incentives and culture; the cases also use randomization and bounded missing outcomes and covariance reasoning, with the required calculations derived here. Every applicant, employer, response and decision below is fictional. No actual hiring, promotion or workforce improvement is claimed.

1. Define the role before evaluating the person

A role is a set of consequential decisions and responsibilities, not a list of impressive credentials. An academic research-and-teaching role may require a valid identification argument, reproducible work, clear explanation and responsible supervision. A financial risk role may require correct exposure calculations, respect for limits, reliable escalation and the ability to explain uncertainty. A prestigious affiliation or a profitable quarter can prompt a question; neither substitutes for evidence of those capabilities.

Selection compares applicants for a specified role. Evaluation judges documented work against explicit expectations. Development provides practice, feedback and resources to close a demonstrated gap. Promotion assigns a broader role with different responsibilities. Succession ensures that critical responsibilities can be covered when an incumbent is unavailable. A strong analyst is not automatically a ready supervisor, and naming a successor does not demonstrate that the successor can perform the work.

Write a capability-evidence map: important task, required reasoning, observable work, scoring criterion and remaining uncertainty. Decide which errors are critical before seeing candidates. A composite score can organize evidence, but a high average should not silently compensate for a failure essential to the role. State how missing evidence, conflicting references, assistance and revised work will be handled. An unobserved capability is not an observed failure; it can still prevent a decision that requires evidence of that capability.

The U.S. Office of Personnel Management's structured-interview overview describes predetermined job-related questions and common rating standards. We use that narrow design principle. Its assessment-strategy guidance distinguishes consistency from evidence that a measure relates to the job, and discusses resources, standardized records and applicant communication. We do not adopt its historical validity-coefficient table, numerical thresholds, legal section or linked studies as evidence for these cases. A professional guide does not establish a universal hiring law or validate a newly invented assessment.

2. Structure the evidence, then challenge the judgment

A proposed process has five stages: define the work and constraints; collect comparable work samples and structured answers; have reviewers record independent judgments against anchored criteria; calibrate disagreements by returning to actual evidence; issue a reasoned decision with limits and a correction route. Give candidates clear instructions, permitted resources and a realistic account of the role. Arrange appropriate access or adjustments through the actual institution's process while preserving the capability being assessed. Equal-looking conditions do not necessarily provide equivalent opportunities to demonstrate it.

For calibration, reviewers score the same sample before discussion, identify the exact sentence or calculation behind each point, and revise a rating only for a recorded reason. Averaging two unsupported impressions does not create evidence. Preserve initial and calibrated ratings; apply any changed rule to every affected candidate. Do not move weights after learning which candidate benefits. If the role itself changes, document that change and reconsider the whole process under the new role rather than presenting the old ranking as unchanged.

Bias controls are procedures to test and reduce irrelevant influence, not a claim of bias-free judgment. Separate work evidence from prestige signals where feasible; disclose conflicts; use more than one informed reviewer; record support and opportunities; inspect the stages at which applicants disappear; permit correction of factual errors. Removing names does not remove all identity cues or all prior differences in opportunity. Neither a small score gap nor a difference in selection rates establishes its cause. Do not infer protected characteristics, health, beliefs or other sensitive traits from names or indirect signals.

Evaluate later work in context: task difficulty, resources, available information, support, decisions and outcomes. A favorable outcome can follow a bad decision, and an adverse state can follow a reasonable one. Inspect reasoning and conduct as well as results. Development should name the gap, practice, support, next evidence, reviewer and decision date. Promotion needs evidence at the proposed scope, including judgment and responsibility for others; succession needs actual coverage, access and tested handover, not only a rank order.

3. Complete academic selection and development case

Case A — a research-and-teaching fellow who must handle missing outcomes

Role and selection contract. A fictional institute seeks a fellow to audit applied research, teach an introductory methods session and explain limitations to collaborators. The work sample tests calculation, identification and communication. It does not test an entire research agenda or establish publication quality. All three applicants receive the same thirty-minute written task and ten-minute explanation opportunity, the same equations and a calculator, with substantive help recorded. Two reviewers score independently before discussing evidence. The institute proposes an identification minimum3/4, then ranks eligible applicants by the twelve-point total. These are authored criteria, not externally validated cutoffs. Actual appointment additionally requires the institute's applicable authorization and role-relevant portfolio/reference review; the short task cannot certify all professional conduct.

Complete common task. “A randomized assignment A∈{0,1} is independent of both arms' potential outcomes and retention indicators. Consistency and no interference hold. Outcomes lie in[0,10]. Treat all supplied quantities as exact population moments, ignoring sampling error for this exercise. In arm1 the retained share is3/4 and its observed outcome mean is8; in arm0 the retained share is1/2 and observed mean6. Missing outcomes are unobserved. Derive the average assignment effect for the full population in terms of missing-outcome means, give its sharp bounds, and explain whether the observed difference2 identifies that effect. Provide one useful next evidence step.” Inputs(retention1,observed mean1,retention0,observed mean0,lower,upper) are (3/4,8,1/2,6,0,10). Random assignment is maintained; outcome retention is not assumed random.

Full key and proof. Let u=E[Y(1)|R(1)=0] and v=E[Y(0)|R(0)=0], each in[0,10]. By total expectation the population means are6+u/4 and3+v/2; their difference is τ=3+u/4−v/2. The retained-sample difference8−6=2 targets neither missing mean. Because the coefficient on u is positive and that on v negative, the smallest possible τ usesu0,v10 and equals−2; the largest usesu10,v0 and equals11/2. The result(observed gap,lower effect,upper effect) is (2,-2,11/2). Every intermediate value is feasible by convexly mixing the missing-outcome distributions. For each endpoint, retain the stated fractions at their stated observed means and give all missing units the endpoint outcome. Couple the two arms' potential-outcome/retention marginals in any joint distribution and randomize assignment independently. Both constructions satisfy the supplied observations and assumptions. Thus the bounds are sharp, not just convenient inequalities.

Random assignment identifies the arm-specific full-population distributions only if their outcomes are observed or otherwise identified; it does not make retention ignorable. A missing-outcome restriction is extra information. For example, assuming u=v=m yields τ=3−m/4 and the narrower interval (1/2,3). That assumption is not implied by assignment, and it still does not identify the effect as2. If additional evidence suppliedu4,v8, the full effect would be0 despite the unchanged retained difference2. No confidence interval or actual program conclusion is available from these invented population moments.

Anchored twelve-point rubric. Quantification Q, four one-point items: retained gap2; arm1 mean6+u/4; arm0 mean3+v/2; difference3+u/4−v/2. Identification I, four one-point items: exact bounds; feasible endpoint witness; distinction between random assignment and retention; restriction labeled as extra rather than fact. Communication C, four one-point items: limitation stated early; missing fractions explained; a conditional interpretation rather than an unsupported recommendation; a concrete next evidence step. The teaching explanation is judged by whether it makes this reasoning recoverable, not by accent, confidence or resemblance to a reviewer.

Complete applicant evidence. Applicant A writes: “The retained gap is2. Full means are6+u/4 and3+v/2, so their difference is3+u/4−v/2. Applicant B writes: “The effect is not point identified from these observations. The retained gap is2, but full means are6+u/4 and3+v/2 because one quarter and one half are missing. Randomization does not identify outcomes lost after assignment. Equal missing means would be an additional assumption, giving0.5to3, not an observed result. These dates are proposed milestones, not completed events.

4. Complete financial selection and development case

Case B — hedge optimization that respects a cash floor

Role and common task. A fictional treasury seeks a risk analyst who can calculate exposures, honor a binding limit and escalate a model change. Candidates receive the same four-state work sample, calculator and forty minutes, followed by a structured question: “What condition would make you stop implementation and who should decide?” The task tests a defined calculation and decision, not an actual trading license or a complete record of integrity. All four states have probability1/4. An unhedged end-period gain R takes values(−6,−2,2,6); one hedge unit pays H=(−2,−2,2,2), and the residual gain is R−hH. The permitted hedge is continuous0≤h≤3. Inputs are ((-6,-2,2,6),(-2,-2,2,2)).

“Minimize residual variance, subject to the hedge bound and a cash floor. Opening cash is10, required cash floor5, and each hedge unit requires3 cash units of margin posted before the outcome: (10,5,3,3), ordered opening cash,floor,margin per unit,hedge cap. Margin is held and assumed refundable; it is unavailable cash at this decision date, not a resource expense to subtract again from terminal gain. Ignore financing returns, additional margin calls, default and all other flows only for this toy model. Calculate the unconstrained and feasible optimum, remaining cash and residual state outcomes. Explain risk and operational limitations.” The floor is an internal stipulated constraint, not a regulatory ratio.

Full mathematical key. Both R and H have mean0. E[R²]=(36+4+4+36)/4=20, E[H²]=4 and E[RH]=(12+4+4+12)/4=8. Hence V(h)=E[(R−hH)²]=20−16h+4h²=4(h−2)²+4. The unique unconstrained minimizer is h2 with residual variance4, not zero. The cash condition10−3h≥5 gives h≤5/3. On[0,5/3], the squared distance from2 decreases strictly with h, so the unique feasible optimum is5/3. It leaves cash5 and state gains(−8/3,4/3,−4/3,8/3), whose mean is0 and variance40/9. The checked moment tuple is (0,0,20,4,8); the solution(hunconstrained,Vunconstrained,hfeasible,Vfeasible,cash) is (2,4,5/3,40/9,5), and residuals are (-8/3,4/3,-4/3,8/3). At h2 cash would be4, violating the floor by1.

General proof and boundaries. For arbitrary finite state gains with positive hedge variance σ²H, Var(R−hH)=Var(R)−2hCov(R,H)+h²σ²H. Completing the square centers it at h0=Cov(R,H)/σ²H. For opening cash C≥F and margin m>0, the feasible interval is[0,L] with L=min{hedge cap,(C−F)/m}; project h0 onto this interval to get the unique optimum. Negative covariance can make the lower boundary optimal, and a low cash limit can force the upper boundary. If C<F, even h=0 is not cash feasible: report no feasible hedge under this cash-only menu, not a fictitious feasible zero. If hedge variance is zero, covariance is also zero and variance is constant in h; every feasible h minimizes variance. Nonuniqueness occurs only when the feasible interval has positive length; a singleton feasible set still has its unique feasible choice, and an empty set has no optimum. A separate criterion is needed to choose among multiple feasible minimizers. A constant hedge can alter mean payoff without altering variance, so a variance-only objective is itself a limited choice.

Twelve-point rubric and critical rule. Quantification Q gives one point each for means, variance/covariance moments, unconstrained h and its residual variance. Feasibility F gives one each for the cash equation, feasible bound, constrained variance/cash and a recommendation inside the constraint. Judgment J gives one each for recognizing residual risk, distinguishing margin from expense, stating consequential model limits and naming a stop/escalation owner. Recommending a knowingly infeasible hedge fails the essential implementation gate regardless of total. This is an authored selection rule, not a universal professional standard.

Succession coverage records which tasks the deputy can perform, current access, escalation contacts and unresolved gaps. 5. Audit the process as well as its selected people

Record applicant counts at each stage, the relevant denominator, missing or withdrawn assessments, timing, resources and criteria. A source-channel comparison can locate a process question without establishing its cause. Suppose a fictional open-notice channel has20 applicants,8 assessed and2 advanced; a referral channel has10 applicants,8 assessed and2 advanced. Conditional advancement among assessed is1/4 in both, but overall advancement is1/10 versus1/5 because assessment access differs. These are invented sourcing categories, not protected attributes. They cannot establish discrimination, equal opportunity or applicant quality; inspect actual job relevance, self-selection, access and stage decisions before explaining the difference.

Do not validate a selection instrument solely from outcomes among the people it selected. Rejected candidates' counterfactual job performance is generally unobserved, and selected applicants may have a restricted score range, different assignments or unequal support. Record what the later comparison actually estimates. Likewise, a manager's rating of an employee may partly reflect the assignments that manager provided. Capability, opportunity, incentives and measurement are related but distinct. Use the resulting uncertainty to improve evidence and process, not to make every evaluation impossible or to excuse a documented critical error.

A complete decision record states role/version, evidence examined, permitted support, criteria/weights, independent ratings, calibration reasons, unresolved facts, decision owner and next review. Give specific feedback within appropriate confidentiality and institutional limits; do not reveal another candidate's private record. A correction should propagate to every affected decision, preserving the original and reason. Actual hiring, promotion, data handling and employment obligations depend on the relevant institution and jurisdiction; this chapter makes no universal legal claims.

Complete solutions and evaluation criteria

1. The observed difference can coexist with zero effect

The full means are6+4/4=7 and3+8/2=7, giving0. The tuple is (7,7,0). Correction: “Random assignment removes assignment selection under the maintained assumptions, but the reported means condition on retention. With the supplied missing means4/8, both full means are7 and the effect is0, despite the retained difference2. Without that extra information, the original sharp interval remains−2to5.5. Its endpoints are attainable by placing missing outcomes at the support endpoints while preserving observed means/retention and randomizing independently.” Full credit requires the full-population target, arithmetic, feasible construction and a complete corrected statement; saying only ‘there may be bias’ is incomplete.

2. A stronger assumption changes identification

Full means are4+u/2 and3+v/2, soτ=1+(u−v)/2. Bounds are (-4,6), attained at(0,10) and(10,0). Underu=v, τ=1 exactly, although the common missing mean remains unknown. The assumption removes the difference of missing means because their coefficients now match; it is not implied by equal retention rates or random assignment. The retained difference remains2 and is still not the full effect under that added restriction. Credit both bounds/witnesses and the specific cancellation argument, not a blanket claim that equal attrition solves missingness.
