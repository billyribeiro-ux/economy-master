Turn a risk report into an accountable, funded decision

Capability. Defend an institution's lending and funding plan against a defined set of joint stresses, specify who can authorize it, and revise it when evidence changes. The immediate prerequisite is financial crises and systemic mechanisms. We use dated liquidity, control and model failure, consistent stress accounting, and governance and incentives. The necessary distinction is between a feasible calculation, reliable evidence that its conditions hold, and authority to execute the action.

Meridian's lending committee wants to expand. Its chief financial officer says more term funding solves the problem; its risk officer says the proposed balance sheet remains fragile; its business head offers a better risk presentation. The board needs a decision that survives those competing accounts. Meridian, its contracts, its decision rights, all numbers and all probability laws below are original teaching constructions. They are not observations about a named bank or recommendations for a real institution.

1. Capacity, appetite and authority answer different questions

Capacity describes a physical, financial or operational boundary under stated conditions: cash that can arrive before a payment deadline, assets that may be pledged, or the loss that an identified equity base can absorb. Risk appetite is a chosen restriction inside the assessed capacity, such as a reserve floor or concentration ceiling. Limits translate that choice into quantities, measurement times, owners and responses. An appetite statement cannot supply cash, change another party's contract or improve a price forecast. A limit without its numerator, denominator, time and breach response is not yet an executable rule.

Authority concerns the right to approve or perform an action. A committee can approve a proposal only within its actual mandate; a model cannot confer a collateral right or resolve a conflict of interest. Distinguish a contractual condition from an internal board choice and both from a legal requirement. An internal exception may change a chosen limit if the proper decision maker permits it. It cannot waive an external payment obligation or make unavailable funds available.

The dated July 2015 BCBS governance principles define risk appetite and capacity and discuss board responsibility, an independent risk function, risk reporting and incentives. The inspected passages support assigning oversight and challenge, communicating material risks, and considering compensation's effects on risk taking. They do not provide Meridian's numbers, certify its control effectiveness, or grant national statutory powers. See the glossary and principles 1, 6–8 and 11 in the scoped source record. The 2023 OECD board recommendations also motivate informed review and conflict management; their nonbinding status is explicit. Neither reference replaces applicable law. The institutional procedures below are our proposed case design.

2. Freeze the institution, clocks and available actions

Use one currency, CU, and one legal entity. There are three contractual payment cutoffs, numbered 1, 2 and 3. They are not years; quoted interest amounts are per specified contract, not annualized rates. Initial security price is 1 CU per unit. The opening assets are cash 24, securities 60 units worth 60, and loans 116: total 200. Deposits 140 and short debt 30 total 170; equity is 30. Of the 60 owned security units, a separately identified 30 are eligible for the new secured facility. No other collateral use, sale, netting, intercompany transfer or emergency support is assumed.

Before the scenario becomes known, choose new lending x ∈ {0,10,20,30}, cash equity b ∈ {0,10,20}, and term replacement t ∈ {0,10,20}. These are actual executable commitments in the toy case. Growth deposits x arrive before new loans x are paid, leaving cash unchanged and adding x to loans and deposits. Outside equity b is received in cash, adding b to equity. A term lender pays t; the same amount of old short principal is immediately repaid. A fee 0.02t is then paid and expensed. The remaining old short principal is 30−t, due at cutoff 1. Term principal t is due at cutoff 3 and coupon 0.01t at each cutoff. Thus after date 0, cash is 24+b−0.02t and equity is 30+b−0.02t. The term transaction changes the debt's date; it does not create equity.

Each scenario jointly fixes the security mark p, collateral haircut h, loan impairment ℓ, operating cash expense o, and fraction ρ of the remaining old debt that a contracted rollover lender will fund before cutoff 1. Security and credit marks occur once at the beginning of the scenario and remain fixed; loans are carried at their post-impairment principal for this instructional account. The rollover principal R = ρ(30−t) is due at cutoff 2 with interest 0.02R. The facility is available before cutoff 1 in the reference contract; its maximum advance is min(100,30p(1−h)). Borrow only g against this distinct eligible pool, pledge g/[p(1−h)] units, and repay 1.03g at cutoff 2, releasing the pledge. No new facility is available at later cutoffs.

This denominator is the pre-impairment gross book, not a stressed accounting ratio. Nonnegative cash must also hold throughout any executed journal. The closing equity limit is not an intraday equity limit, a regulatory capital ratio or a guarantee against other scenarios. All future residual deposits remain liabilities after cutoff 3.

3. Derive feasibility before selecting a plan

Let rj, ij and wj denote the principal receipts, interest receipts and withdrawals at cutoff j. After every cutoff-1 payment but before a hypothetical secured draw, the signed cash projection is

A₁ = 24+b−0.02t−o+r₁+i₁+R−(30−t)−w₁−0.01t.

This is an algebraic projection. A negative A₁ is a funding need, not an executed negative banknote account. With cash floor c = 5, the smallest adequate draw is g* = max(0,c−A₁). Let F be the timely facility capacity, equal to zero if unavailable. If g* > F, the proposed plan cannot meet the first cash limit. For a rejected row the lab displays the capped draw min(g*,F) and signed full-payment projections to identify the missing funds. It does not execute that row or assume that unpaid creditors disappear.

C₁ = A₁+g; C₂ = C₁+r₂+i₂−w₂−1.02R−1.03g−0.01t;
C₃ = C₂+r₃+i₃−w₃−1.01t.
E₁ = 30+b−0.02t+60(p−1)−ℓ−o+i₁−0.01t;
E₂ = E₁+i₂−0.02R−0.03g−0.01t; E₃ = E₂+i₃−0.01t.

These equity expressions do not subtract withdrawal or principal repayments as losses. At every cutoff, check cash plus the marked security and remaining loan books against deposits plus outstanding debts plus equity. The selected feasible plan additionally receives an event-by-event execution journal with nonnegative asset and debt accounts. This second check reveals mistakes hidden by a correct aggregate total, such as counting collateral as sold while also borrowing against it.

Minimum-draw proposition. For a fixed (x,b,t), fixed scenario and the stated funding menu, test g* if it is within F. If it fails a later cash or equity floor, no larger secured draw can repair the failure. To prove this, an extra draw Δ > 0 raises C₁ by Δ, then requires repayment 1.03Δ, reducing both C₂ and C₃ by 0.03Δ relative to g*. It reduces E₂ and E₃ by the same interest expense and leaves E₁ unchanged. A smaller draw violates the first floor whenever g* > 0. When g* = 0 there is no smaller nonnegative borrowing. Hence g* is sufficient to test feasibility of every allowed draw. This proof depends on nonnegative interest, no later borrowing or profitable investment opportunity, and unchanged withdrawals. With zero interest, extra borrowing can tie later cash; with negative interest or behavioral responses the stated dominance argument changes.

Selection is a finite constrained decision, not an expected-profit forecast. First maximize lending among plans satisfying every stated scenario and limit. Then minimize the declared commitment score k = 0.08b+(0.02+3×0.01)t = 0.08b+0.05t. The first term is a board-imposed opportunity charge, not a cash dividend; the second counts the term fee and three coupons. This score deliberately excludes scenario-dependent rollover and secured interest from its ranking; those actual expenses remain in every financial constraint. It is not total funding cost, welfare or a market price. Exact score ties prefer lower b, then lower t. Publishing this rule before calculating prevents selecting whichever criterion happens to justify a favorite plan.

Worked example 1 — Follow the selected joint-stress money

Consider x = b = t = 20. After date 0, cash is 43.6, securities 60 and loans 136; deposits 160, old short debt 10, term debt 20 and equity 49.6 reconcile assets 239.6. Under joint stress, securities lose 15, loans lose 14 and the operating expense costs 2. Before the first interest receipt, equity is 18.6. Collect principal 5 and interest 2. With no rollover, pay old short debt 10, withdrawals 46 and coupon 0.2. The signed cash before the secured draw is −7.6. Borrowing 12.6 produces closing cash 5 and requires 28 units of the 30-unit eligible pool, since 12.6/(0.75×0.60) = 28. The loan is available and arrives before those payments.

Closing assets are cash 5, securities 45 and loans 117, totaling 167. Deposits are 114, secured debt 12.6 and term debt 20, totaling 146.6. Equity is 20.4, so the balance sheet reconciles. At cutoff 2, collect 45+3, pay withdrawals 22, secured principal and interest 12.978, and coupon 0.2. Cash becomes 17.822 = 8911/500; equity becomes 22.822 = 11411/500. At cutoff 3, collect 20+2, pay withdrawals 11, term principal 20 and coupon 0.2. Cash is 8.622 = 4311/500 and equity 24.622 = 12311/500. Assets now total 105.622: cash 8.622, securities 45 and remaining loans 52. Residual deposits 81 are still owed. The three-cutoff test has not demonstrated ability to pay all of them on an unmodeled fourth date.

The pre-receipt equity of 18.6 also demonstrates a measurement boundary: the closing 20-floor passes, but an all-events 20-floor would fail. A dashboard that reports only closing observations must disclose that difference. The data do not justify the phrase “equity stayed above 20.”

Worked example 2 — Prove the finite decision is globally selected

In joint stress A₁ = b+0.97t−41−0.30x and F = 13.5. The first cash floor therefore requires b+0.97t−0.30x ≥ 32.5. If b ≤ 10, its left side is at most 29.4. If t ≤ 10, it is at most 29.7. Thus any feasible grid plan must have b = t = 20. The cash restriction then gives x ≤ 23. The first equity floor is 24.4−0.20x ≥ 20, giving x ≤ 22. Consequently only x = 0,10,20 can possibly qualify; x = 30 cannot be rescued by a better score.

For b = t = 20 and these three x values, the joint draw is 6.6+0.30x. Later joint cash is C₂ = 26.002−0.409x and C₃ = 17.802−0.459x; later equity is E₂ = 27.002−0.209x and E₃ = 28.802−0.209x. These remain above the limits through x = 20. The other scenarios also pass, as verified by their own accounts. Concentration at x = 20 is 56/136 = 7/17, below one half. Of all 36 candidate plans, exactly three are feasible, and maximum lending uniquely chooses (20,20,20), with commitment score 2.6 = 13/5. No probability was assigned to a scenario and no expected-return optimum was established.

4. Put evidence, ownership and escalation around the calculation

A model input is a claim that someone must substantiate. For Meridian, treasury must document received equity, funded term replacement, available bank balances, pledge eligibility, settlement route and facility cutoff. Credit staff must reconcile the loan book and supply the repayment and impairment assumptions. Risk staff must independently recompute the joint cash paths, test omitted mechanisms and record disagreement. Operations must verify that the collection and payment sequence can actually be executed. The board, within its fictional mandate, sets the risk limits and the lending objective. Internal audit examines whether the process followed its rules; it does not become the business owner of the risk.

The rule for this case is to stop uncommitted new lending when a material condition fails, notify the risk head and authorized committee immediately, and complete a new decision before the affected payment cutoff. Existing obligations remain payable under their contracts. Notification is not a remedy by itself. A contingency must name an amount, source, owner, earliest arrival time, collateral and authority, plus its effect on later obligations. If no feasible authorized response exists, escalate the actual gap and the required legal or operational response; do not label the institution safe because an exception was requested.

An auditable decision record contains the observation time, measurement dates, input and contract versions, candidate set, chosen objective, full rejected alternatives, calculation and independent review references, unresolved assumptions, conflicts, approval scope and conditions, execution evidence, and the trigger for reconsideration. Keep the earlier record and add a superseding record instead of rewriting history. File hashes can bind a reviewed calculation to particular bytes. They do not prove that the inputs are true, the signer is authentic, authority exists, or a receipt actually arrived. A model pass and a signed-looking form are separate evidence objects.

Worked example 3 — New evidence changes the decision

Tighter concentration. The committee changes the approval-time ceiling to 0.4. Solve (36+x)/(116+x) ≤ 0.4: 0.6x ≤ 10.4, so x ≤ 52/3. The grid now permits at most x = 10. With b = t = 20, the joint draw falls to 9.6, cash closes at 5, 21.912 and 13.212, and first equity is 22.4. The feasible set has two plans and selects lending 10. The lower concentration figure is a board choice; it is not a discovered universal safe ratio.

Late facility. Suppose the joint scenario's facility cannot pay before cutoff 1. Set its timely capacity to zero. Even x = 0, b = t = 20 gives A₁ = −1.6, below the 5-floor, so no plan in the original grid is feasible. Calling the facility “committed” does not cure lateness. If a separately verified additional equity commitment expands b's grid to include 30, the condition becomes 30+19.4−41−0.30x ≥ 5, or x ≤ 34/3. The best grid plan is now (10,30,20). It borrows no secured cash in joint stress, and its cash closes at 5.4, 32.2 and 23.5. This remedy requires ten extra units of received equity beyond the old maximum, not a relabeling of available capital.

Changed repayment burden. Keeping facility timing unchanged but raising its specified interest charge from 0.03g to 0.40g leaves the first cash test unchanged. For the old selected plan, cutoff-3 cash falls to 3.96 and cutoff-2 equity to 18.16. A plan that solves today's funding can therefore fail the later test. Recomputing all constraints selects (10,20,20). The minimum-draw proof explains why borrowing more cannot fix this failure.

5. Make truthful escalation privately worthwhile

A procedure can be technically sound while rewarding concealment. Consider a manager who already observes a reportable red condition and chooses truthful disclosure or concealment. Common salary cancels. Undetected concealment earns a private bonus g = 4; detected concealment loses that bonus and pays penalty f = 6. The penalty is fully funded by the manager's separately escrowed resources and collectible under the hypothetical contract. It is not Meridian capital and cannot fund Meridian's payments. Honest reporting has no bonus or penalty in the initial model.

The audit system is unavailable with probability z = 0.2. Conditional on being available it detects concealment with probability d = 0.5. These are stipulated physical probabilities, not measured control effectiveness. Detection is p = (1−z)d. Concealment's expected incremental payoff is (1−p)g−pf = g−p(g+f); disclosure pays zero. Thus truthful reporting is weakly preferred exactly when p ≥ g/(g+f), and strictly preferred when the inequality is strict. This is a conditional two-action incentive result, not proof of a truthful organizational culture. Misclassification, retaliation, hidden side benefits, risk preferences, uncollectible penalties and missing reports can change it.

Worked example 4 — An indifferent manager has not been deterred

With g = 4 and f = 6, the threshold is 4/10 = 0.4. Actual p = 0.8×0.5 = 0.4, so concealment and disclosure both pay zero in expectation. A claim that the manager will report honestly requires a selection assumption and is not warranted. Raising conditional detection to 0.75 gives p = 0.6, concealment payoff −2, and a strict truthful advantage of 2. Merely installing two checks does not establish this: their joint availability and dependence must justify the stated detection probability.

If an honest report is falsely sanctioned with probability α, its payoff is −αf. Concealment is weakly deterred when p(g+f) ≥ g+αf, or p ≥ (g+αf)/(g+f). With α = 0.05 the threshold is 0.43, not 0.4. Under z = 0.2 this needs d ≥ 0.5375 = 43/80; the 0.75 design meets it. False sanctions make truthful disclosure less attractive even when the penalty is nominally severe. An appeal and evidence process affects behavior as well as fairness; its actual effectiveness requires evidence beyond this equation.

Compensation must also be consistent with the business constraint. If the business head earns for loans originated while treasury bears delayed funding losses, reporting an attractive closing ratio may be privately useful even when total institutional value declines. One possible policy in this case is to defer a portion of the origination award until the dated funding conditions are verified, with independently reviewed adjustments. This is a proposal requiring its own incentive and legal analysis. Arbitrarily increasing punishment or suppressing a dissenting report is not a model-derived governance improvement.

6. Reproduce, challenge and bound the decision

The laboratory contains inputs, standard-library Python, saved output, a payload manifest, and a reproduction guide. Download them to one directory and run python3 v11-c12-analysis.py. The quiet, nonmutating run(data) API returns exact rational strings; Invalid input and protected-output failures leave an existing output untouched. The supported contract is deliberately bounded. None of its files authenticates an approver or fetches funds.

The lab enumerates the 36 initial policies, derives the minimum secured draw in each scenario, checks all closing limits and concentration, and then applies the declared ranking. Only the chosen feasible plan receives actual account journals. Candidate projections after a financing failure are counterfactual full-payment schedules and must not be described as a continuation after default. Source and checker hashes establish correspondence between reviewed artifacts, not real-world correctness. The finite scenario menu omits uncertain arrival times, additional withdrawals, market feedback and model misspecification unless the user explicitly changes the supplied contract.

Another is a genuinely agreed rescheduling with the entitled creditor or a legally authorized process, whose availability cannot be invented. Waiting for noon without an agreement does not satisfy an 11:00 payment. Grade out of 12: accurate chronology/evidence 2; honest changed risk status and independent escalation 2; existing obligations and stop boundary 2; two distinct concrete contingent responses with amounts/times/authority 4; accountable retest and closure record 2. A claimed on-time payment from 12:00 cash or retrospective alteration of the 09:00 record requires correction regardless of score.

Teach-back and delayed retention

Teach a new director why the plan needs both cash equity and term replacement, why the secured borrowing is a dated obligation, and why neither a hash nor a model score approves the transaction. A sound answer connects the 32.5 cash inequality, 20 equity floor, collateral units, later debt service, received resources and the actual mandate. It identifies one omitted risk and the evidence needed to assess it. Merely naming a committee or saying “diversify” does not explain the decision.

After a week, answer without the worked examples: (a) what is the joint first cash bound for x = 10?
