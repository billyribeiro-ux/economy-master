A rule changes what an institution can do, what it reports, who bears losses and how it responds. Evaluating regulation therefore requires a mechanism, a resource ledger, a stated objective and evidence about legal scope. Passing a reported ratio alone answers none of those questions.

1. Capability and the policy problem

After this chapter you should be able to evaluate prudential, conduct and resolution interventions using explicit incentives, dated cash and a defensible welfare comparison. Prerequisites are the banking ledger and liquidity distinctions in Chapter 2, the operational and legal evidence discipline in Chapter 6, and the entity, currency and jurisdiction boundaries in Chapter 9. Expected values use physical scenario probabilities, not risk-neutral pricing probabilities.

Consider Rowan, a fictional bank whose owners can increase their expected residual by choosing a strategy that transfers losses to a deposit guarantor and harms other institutions. A rule may restrict that strategy, induce a differently reported substitute, or preserve productive assets after a loss. Which response is desirable depends on the alternatives, their real costs and the rights and resources needed to execute them. Every numerical probability, screen, fine, guarantee, priority and capital floor below is an original instructional assumption. None is an observed institution or an actual regulatory calibration.

Prudential regulation concerns the safety and soundness of institutions and relevant stability consequences. Conduct regulation concerns behavior toward customers and markets, including information and incentives. Resolution concerns an orderly response to a failing institution, including continuity, exit and allocation of losses. These purposes overlap, but an institution can have ample measured capital and still mis-sell a product; a customer restitution payment can improve conduct outcomes while consuming liquidity; and an orderly failure can be preferable to preserving an unviable firm indefinitely.

A systemic externality is a cost imposed on others that the decision maker does not fully face: for example, a stipulated disruption of another firm's payment activity. It is different from the private loss of a creditor who knowingly contracted for risk. Some creditor losses can generate additional external effects, but counting the same monetary loss twice does not establish that effect. Regulatory arbitrage here means adapting activity or its legal/reporting location to differences in rules. Whether a real transaction is permitted, abusive or unlawful requires the applicable legal facts; our numerical example establishes only a possible incentive mechanism.

Use a deliberately historical legal example. The UK Financial Services Act 2012, chapter 21, as enacted received Royal Assent on 19 December 2012. The inspected inserted sections 1B–1E distinguish the FCA's market-functioning strategic objective and consumer protection, integrity and competition objectives. A second example is the FSB Key Attributes, revised version dated 25 April 2024. Its twelve Key Attributes are an international resolution standard; the foreword separates them from annex implementation guidance and explains the 2024 revision to the financial-market-infrastructure annex. The inspected preamble and Key Attributes 1–6 discuss continuity, authority, loss allocation, safeguards and funding. They do not themselves confer a power on a particular national authority. For a dated guidance example, the US 17 April 2026 SR 26-2 letter supersedes SR 11-7 and SR 21-8. Its interagency attachment describes model risk management as supervisory guidance rather than prescriptive enforceable standards and scopes its model definition, excluding some simple arithmetic and deterministic tools. A correct implementation of Rowan's arithmetic does not prove that this particular calculator falls within that definition, nor that its economic assumptions or policy use are valid.

3. Derive the private and social ledgers before choosing a rule

All money is in arbitrary currency units (CU), all terminal values below are on one date, and discounting is zero. Rowan initially has assets A0=100, deposits D=85 and owner capital K=15, so A0=D+K. Deposit interest and guarantee premiums are zero by assumption. Two states have physical probabilities 3/4 and 1/4. Owners choose one of three portfolios before the state is known. Terminal assets include all realized bank resources; there is no additional hidden cash account.

Routed H has the same state risk structure as H with a real routing cost of 1 CU already deducted from assets in each state. Do not subtract it again. A separate guarantor has an initial liquid reserve of 40 CU, sufficient to pay every supported deposit shortfall. It receives no premium and has no other cash flows. Its reserve is an explicitly endowed resource, not an unlimited public promise. Each depositor is fully paid under this stipulated guarantee.

For terminal assets A≥0, owners receive (A−D)+, the bank pays depositors min(A,D), and the guarantor pays (D−A)+, where x+=max(x,0). Two statewise identities are

(A−D)++min(A,D)=A,
min(A,D)+(D−A)+=D.

Define owner profit π=E[(A−D)+]−K and expected insurance payment I=E[(D−A)+]. Subtracting the two identities and using D+K=A0 gives π−I=E[A]−A0. Depositors' net return is zero; the guarantor loses I relative to its initial reserve. Insurance is therefore a transfer between the parties in this aggregate ledger, not a second destruction of assets. The initial reserve cancels in the guarantor's change in wealth. Premiums, taxation costs, different welfare weights or constraints on the guarantor would require additional accounts.

In this experiment only, bank default means A<D and causes a separate real harm of L=20 CU to outsiders. This is a primitive scenario assumption, not an estimated causal effect of every bank failure. With equal monetary welfare weights and no other distortions, social surplus relative to initial resources is

W=E[A]−A0−L P(A<D)=π−I−L P(A<D).

The resource and stakeholder formulas agree. Subtracting I once more from the first formula would count the guarantee transfer twice. Conversely, ignoring I when starting from owner profit would treat the subsidy as newly created social value. An actual distributional analysis may assign different weights to different households; it must state those weights and preserve the transfer accounts.

Worked example 1 — A privately attractive risk can have lower surplus

C gives equity 23 in either state, so πC=23−15=8, IC=0 and WC=8. H gives equity 45 or 0, so πH=(3/4)45−15=18.75. Its expected assets are 110, insurer payment is (1/4)35=8.75, and expected external harm is (1/4)20=5. Thus WH=110−100−5=5, also 18.75−8.75−5.

Routed H gives equity 44 or 0. Its expected assets are 109, private profit 18, insurance payment 9 and surplus 4. The worst guarantor payout is 36, leaving reserve 4. Without an intervention, H uniquely maximizes private profit, although C has the highest surplus among these three alternatives. This is a statement about the specified menu and welfare objective, not a universal theorem that low risk is socially best.

The experiment holds asset payoffs fixed when changing financing. If capital rose to 50 and deposits fell to 50 at the same total opening assets 100, H's bad-state assets would exactly cover deposits. It would have no default, no insurance payment and no stipulated external harm; private profit and surplus would both be 10. H would still beat C's private profit of 8. More capital can change loss bearing without eliminating the chosen risky portfolio. Such a funding change is not an unexplained cash injection: it means a different initial funding mix, or a fully specified issuance and deposit repayment transaction.

4. Rules change choices, including where activity is reported

Consider the fictional screen K≥τX with τ=.20 and measured exposure X. The narrow policy uses reported exposure; the broad policy uses the stipulated look-through exposure. Neither measure is asserted to be a legally correct risk-weighted asset calculation. The no-rule policy has no screen. Owner capital remains 15. The narrow policy costs .5 CU and the broad policy costs 2 CU to implement; no rule costs zero. These are real resources from a separately endowed policy budget, not unrecorded bank withdrawals or fees. They reduce social surplus but do not change owner profit in this experiment.

For policy p, let Fp be its feasible choices. The bank's best-response set is Bp=arg maxj∈Fₚπj. A policy with an empty feasible set does not imply that the bank has secretly chosen a missing safe option. If Bp contains several choices, retain all of them unless an additional behavior rule is justified. Policy surplus for a selected j is Wpj=Wj−cp. This order matters: evaluate the induced choice, not the portfolio the regulator hoped to induce.

The narrow rule excludes H but admits its costly reported substitute. It lowers surplus below the no-rule outcome by 1.5: routing consumes 1 and implementation .5. The broad rule raises surplus by 1 relative to no rule, after its higher resource cost. With external harm L left symbolic, no-rule surplus is 10−L/4, narrow surplus 8.5−L/4 and broad surplus 6. The broad rule beats no rule precisely when L>16; they tie at 16. The physical failure law, resource costs and feasible menus drive this boundary.

Now suppose an official's decision score is J=W+λπ, where λ≥0 is an extra weight on incumbent owner profit beyond its existing place in aggregate W. This is an original possible conflict mechanism. Broad minus no-rule score is 1−10.75λ, so broad is preferred when λ<4/43≈.0930233, the two tie at 4/43, and no rule is preferred above it. Narrow has both lower W and lower π than no rule, so it is never a maximizer for nonnegative λ.

The score calculation is a proof about assumed preferences, not evidence that a real authority is captured. Regulatory capture concerns influence that redirects a public decision toward a regulated interest; identifying it requires evidence of the process and a defensible alternative explanation. Consultation can reveal genuine implementation costs. Different legitimate distributional objectives, information limits, a flawed harm estimate or legal constraints may produce the same observed policy. To investigate, state the authorized objective, compare documented evidence and revisions, examine access and conflicts, and test whether the decision follows a favored interest after relevant alternatives are accounted for. A disliked result alone does not identify the mechanism.

Perimeter reform also has possible costs omitted here: lost beneficial entry, duplicated compliance, migration to a less observable entity, diminished competition or impaired credit supply. Add those mechanisms to the resource and behavior model before claiming a general optimum. Disclosure, supervision, prices for risk, activity restrictions and orderly exit may be complements or substitutes. Each changes a different margin and requires evidence that the margin responds as assumed.

5. A conduct rule must change the feasible incentive, not just the printed penalty

Use a separate service transaction, not another cash flow of Rowan's portfolio. A customer pays a fee of 6 for a service with gross benefit 12. The seller can take care at a real cost of 2 or take a shortcut at zero effort cost. Harm of 30 occurs with probabilities .05 under care and .30 under the shortcut. These are synthetic marginal probabilities. A random inspection occurs with probability a independently of the chosen action; conditional on a shortcut, inspection detects that procedural breach even if no customer harm happens. Care is never fined. Inspection costs 1 whenever it occurs, financed by a separate authority budget of 1.

The paper fine is F, but only Q=min(F,M) is collectible from an explicitly pledged seller reserve M. The reserve's initial and remaining balances cancel when measuring seller profit except for the fine actually paid. Care cost is funded from the fee. The seller's expected profits are 6−2 under care and 6−aQ under the shortcut. Thus care is a weak best response exactly when aQ≥2, uniquely best when aQ>2, and both actions are best responses at equality. With a=0 and positive care cost, increasing a nominal fine cannot deter. With M finite, a fine above M adds no deterrence in this contract.

Worked example 3 — Sum all parties before calling a fine a social cost

Let a=.25, F=10 and M=10, so Q=10. Seller profit is 4 with care and 3.5 with a shortcut, making care unique. The deterrence margin is .25(10)−2=.5. A fine of 8 would give equality, not unique compliance.

There is no need to assume that an unlucky careful seller is procedurally noncompliant. The inspected object is the chosen process, not the realized harm alone. If inspection instead detects only realized harm, expected sanction changes and the inequality must be derived again. Likewise, a delayed investigation, a contested legal interpretation, limited collection or an unfinanced inspection changes the feasible mechanism. A documented legal power and a credible operational capacity are separate requirements.

6. Allocate losses, then fund the dated continuation

The FSB's inspected Key Attributes 3.5–3.6 describe write-down and conversion within a resolution framework. Key Attributes 5.1–5.2 address creditor hierarchy and safeguards relative to the applicable liquidation counterfactual. Key Attributes 6.1–6.5 address resolution funding and loss recovery; they do not say that public temporary liquidity can never be used. A standard's loss-allocation principle is not a self-executing domestic power, and a claim of compliance with an actual creditor safeguard requires the applicable rights, valuations and compensation arrangements.

Our next ledger instead stipulates its own enforceable priority: deposits first, then senior uninsured debt, then junior debt, then old equity. The bank has one currency and no transfer restriction. All identified rights are effective in time. There is no actual national bail-in eligibility claim. Long assets have a known collectible value at cutoff 2; an earlier liquidation realizes less but provides cash before cutoff 1. This separates valuation uncertainty from the timing issue being taught. The BCBS September 2008 liquidity principles support attention to available funding, restrictions and execution; they do not promise this bank access to a central bank or lender.

Worked example 4 — Five units of new equity still need four units of cash

This is a new balance sheet. Opening cash 6 plus long assets 94 equals deposits 60, senior debt 20, junior debt 10 and equity 10. A realized loss of 30 on long assets leaves assets 70 and accounting equity −20 before liabilities are adjusted. Under the stipulated waterfall, old equity absorbs 10, junior debt absorbs 10 and senior debt absorbs 10. After write-down, deposits are 60, senior debt 10 and equity zero.

Convert 5 of remaining senior debt into new equity owned by those senior creditors. The resulting balance sheet is cash 6 plus long assets 64 against deposits 60, senior debt 5 and equity 5. No asset or cash was added. A deposit payment of 10 is due at cutoff 1, so the cash gap is 4.

A separate lender has 4 cash and commits that amount before cutoff 1, with repayment 4.2 at cutoff 2. This is a 5% rate over the stipulated horizon, not an annualized quote. Receiving the bridge and paying the deposit produces cash zero, long assets 64, deposits 50, senior debt 5, bridge principal 4 and equity 5. Deposit payment reduces an asset and a liability; it is not an expense.

At cutoff 2, long assets realize 64. Accrue bridge interest .2, reducing equity to 4.8. Pay bridge 4.2, deposits 50 and senior debt 5: remaining cash and equity are 4.8. Senior creditors receive debt 5 plus new equity worth 4.8, totaling 9.8. Depositors receive 60 across the two dates. The lender ends with 4.2 after initially advancing 4, so its net receipt is .2. The aggregate identity is 60+9.8+.2=70; do not count the lender's returned principal as additional income.

A fictional capital floor of 5 is passed immediately after conversion but fails after the known interest expense. Converting 5.2 instead leaves senior debt 4.8 and initial new equity 5.2; the same bridge and payments leave final equity 5. Old senior total value remains 4.8+5=9.8. More conversion reallocates that class's surviving claim between debt and equity but neither removes the cash gap nor pays the interest.

The stipulated immediate-liquidation alternative destroys 8 of asset value and realizes 62 before cutoff 1. Depositors receive 60 and senior creditors 2; junior and old equity receive zero. Continuation preserves 8 real resources but transfers .2 to its lender, leaving old creditors collectively 7.8 better off than this comparator. That result does not certify a legal safeguard in any jurisdiction. It uses known future realization, zero discount, no other costs and the stated creditor rights.

More generally, within this restricted loss region, let surviving senior debt after write-down be S′, conversion x≤S′, required early advance g, and bridge rate r. A funded continuation has terminal equity x−gr and remaining senior debt S′−x. Consequently the original senior class retains total value S′−gr, independent of x when the continuation is solvent. A terminal capital floor k requires x≥k+gr, so a feasible conversion exists exactly when k+gr≤S′, alongside the separate cash condition g≤committed funded bridge capacity. These are necessity and sufficiency statements within the given deterministic ledger, not a general resolution theorem.

If the bridge capacity were only 3, one CU would remain unfunded at cutoff 1. Long assets worth 64 at cutoff 2 cannot meet that earlier payment. The model reports this failed branch and stops its continuation ledger; it does not assume an unauthorized loan or silently postpone a due obligation. If cash is funded but conversion is too small to cover interest, it records the maturity shortfall without inventing a second default-allocation rule. A supported calculation can fail a policy objective; failure is not automatically malformed input.

7. Evaluate an intervention as a testable decision

A defensible policy file connects five propositions. First identify the legal entity, activity, jurisdiction, effective date and authority. Next state the market failure or distributional purpose and the counterfactual. Then explain the behavioral response, including migration and strategic reporting. Reconcile private transfers, real resource costs and dated funding. Finally identify what evidence would change the decision, who can revise it and how implementation will be monitored.

For Rowan, the broad policy's welfare gain disappears when the stipulated failure harm falls to 16, and may disappear sooner if a new compliance burden exceeds the modeled one. Observing fewer defaults after adoption is not sufficient evidence of the gain: the portfolio mix, state probabilities, reporting perimeter, funding and wider economy may all have changed. A study should specify comparable exposed and unexposed units where feasible, pre-policy trends and simultaneous interventions, reporting changes and migration, and uncertainty about counterfactual losses. No design can infer a clean causal effect from a single before-and-after count without further assumptions.

Likewise, a policy can be legally applicable but poorly implemented, or numerically effective under a fictitious power that nobody possesses. For a new national application, collect the actual law or final rule, effective dates, scope and delegated authority before using it as a constraint. A consultation, international standard and enforcement outcome answer different questions. Preserve unresolved legal questions in the decision file and obtain an accountable interpretation rather than turning an unknown into a favorable model input.

8. Reproducible regulation and resource lab

The portable files are original inputs, standard-library Python implementation, exact saved results, four-file integrity manifest and domain and execution guide. Copy them into one directory and run python3 v11-c10-analysis.py; success quietly regenerates the neighboring v11-c10-output.json, or use --output to select a different destination. All numerical input fields are bounded rational strings, such as "1/4"; results retain exact fractions. There is no random simulation or external data download.

The portfolio module lists every private best response. If a tied action changes a policy's score, it reports that policy ranking depends on tie behavior instead of choosing a favorable response. A screen with no feasible option is reported as such. The conduct module caps collectible sanctions at pledged resources and preserves equality cases. The resolution module supports a disclosed loss region in which old equity and junior debt are wiped and deposit principal remains covered; it separately reports early funding and later capital conditions. It does not solve arbitrary insolvency cases.

Use meaningful interventions: change L across 16, change λ across 4/43, hold assets fixed while changing funding, reduce fine collectibility, restrict the cash bridge, and include its interest in the continuing capital requirement. A file hash proves byte identity, not legal truth, a causal estimate or a correct objective. The author checker independently recomputes state transfers, choice inequalities, inspection states and journal vectors; separate peer review is still required.

  9. Interest, feasibility and transfers. Give the resolution bridge a horizon rate of 15%, keeping capacity and advance at 4.
