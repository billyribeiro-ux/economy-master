Judge the claim, explain the obstacle, propose a proportionate response

Capability. Write a fair referee report that separates an obstacle to the central claim from a useful improvement. The prerequisite is replication and reproduction: a result can be computationally reproducible and still answer the wrong question. Use literature mapping to judge contribution against actual nearby work. The two submissions, evidence packets and reports in this chapter are original fictional teaching materials. They are not confidential manuscripts, actual publication decisions or evidence that a learner has served as a referee.

A referee advises an editor about a particular submission. The author owns the research question; the editor owns the decision; the reviewer owns the accuracy and relevance of the report. A useful report reconstructs what the paper claims, checks the argument needed for that claim, and explains the consequence of a weakness. It does not merely list everything the reviewer would have done differently.

Distinguish correctness, whether a stated result follows; identification, whether the information and assumptions determine the target; evidence, what was observed or established; contribution, what changes relative to specified existing knowledge; and fit, whether that change meets the venue's remit. A correct calculation can accompany a failed identification argument. A correct theorem can be familiar. A valuable paper can be outside one journal's scope. These judgments need separate reasons.

1. Reconstruct the argument before ranking concerns

Read the invitation and the venue's stated criteria, then identify the paper's central question and strongest claimed answer. Write that answer in two sentences without adjectives about the authors. Trace the dependence chain: observation or primitive assumption, transformation or proof, estimate or proposition, interpretation, and policy or theoretical implication. Locate the weakest bridge that matters to the main conclusion. Check units, target population, denominator, timing, model domain and the exact version of the manuscript and supplement.

For an empirical submission, an execution check asks whether the code produces the reported statistic; a measurement check asks what that statistic measures; an identification check asks which counterfactual it supports. For theory, check definitions, quantifiers, existence, admissibility, boundary cases and the link from a lemma to the final proposition. A counterexample needs to satisfy every stated assumption. Violating a premise does not refute the theorem. If a proof appears incomplete, distinguish a missing step you can supply from a false conclusion.

Keep a private working ledger with four columns: claim and locator; evidence checked; issue and consequence; minimum response needed. “Missing table” is not a severity judgment until you say what the table could establish. “Endogeneity” is not a diagnosis until you identify a competing explanation compatible with the observations. In an inaccessible-data case, say which parts you could assess and what remains unverified. Do not infer that inability to reproduce means misconduct or that matching an output establishes validity.

A major concern threatens a central claim, its interpretation or the contribution required by the venue. A minor concern is a bounded correction that leaves those judgments intact. Severity and repair cost are different. One changed sentence may repair a mathematically important domain error; a new dataset might be essential for the original causal question and infeasible for this submission. Classify the problem by its consequence, then judge whether a revision can reasonably address it.

2. Write a report the author can act on

Open with a neutral reconstruction and the main strength. State the overall assessment with its scope. Organize substantive concerns by importance, not page order. Each concern should identify the claim, explain the failure, show evidence or an explicit logical witness, and specify the minimum response that would resolve or narrow it. Separate necessary changes from optional extensions. A request for a different country, population or entire mechanism must explain why the submitted claim requires it.

Use a contribution comparison rather than a novelty slogan: name the closest inspected work, its relevant result, and the difference the submission establishes. If you have not checked the closest work, say so. “I have not seen this before” does not prove originality; “someone must have done this” does not refute it. Distinguish an influential benchmark from an exact antecedent. A newly useful measurement, a clean negative result or a clarified boundary may contribute without introducing a new estimator.

Recommendations should follow the argument. A request to revise and resubmit means the central contribution could plausibly survive specified changes under the venue's standards; it is not a promise of acceptance. Rejection can be appropriate when the current design cannot sustain the main claim and the needed replacement would be a different project. Acceptance need not wait for every attractive extension. In an illustrative workshop, a pedagogical model may fit; that does not establish suitability for a journal requiring a substantial new research contribution.

When a submission is revised, compare the response with the original concerns and the new manuscript. Mark each concern resolved, partly resolved or unresolved with a reason. Do not quietly replace a resolved essential requirement with an unrelated wish. New problems can legitimately arise from changed evidence or arguments; identify their origin. A polite response is not proof of a repair, and disagreement with a reviewer can be well justified.

Worked case 1. A replicated statistic does not support the submitted causal claim

Complete mock submission packet A. The paper is titled “Working-capital support raises investment.” It studies four firms, two participating voluntarily in a facility and two nonparticipants, before and after its introduction. The single prior date is date 0; date 1 is after participation. Investment is measured in the same real CU per firm and period. The packet states that definitions and reporting coverage are unchanged and gives the complete observed table below. There is no randomized assignment, instrument, earlier outcome history or evidence restricting untreated group trends. Firm participation was chosen, and no assignment justification is supplied.

The ordered input (T1before,T1after,T2before,T2after,C1before,C1after,C2before,C2after) is (20,32,30,42,20,22,30,32). The paper subtracts the control mean change from the participant mean change and reports (participant,control,difference)=(12,2,10). It calls 10 the causal effect of the facility and says that a resource cost of 3 CU per participant makes the policy socially worthwhile. No benefit valuation, displacement analysis or welfare criterion accompanies that final statement. The code exactly reproduces the table arithmetic. The hypothetical venue requires a credible causal contribution for this submission category.

Reviewer's reconstruction. Let τ be the average effect on the two participating firms at date 1. Let δ be their mean untreated investment trend minus the control mean trend. Algebra gives the observed difference 10=τ+δ. This is a decomposition, not an identifying assumption. If their unobserved untreated date-1 values were (22,32), then δ=0 and τ=10. If they were (27,37), then δ=5 and τ=5. Both preserve all observed values, voluntary assignment and the stated units. The paired effects and differential trends are (tau1,delta1,tau2,delta2)=(10,0,5,5). Therefore the supplied information does not identify τ. More precise computation of 10 cannot choose between the compatible explanations.

Completed report to the author. “The paper documents a ten-CU difference between the average investment changes of participating and nonparticipating firms. The complete table and reproducible arithmetic are useful strengths. My assessment concerns the supplied version and evidence packet; I have not verified any additional records. The present evidence does not establish the paper's central causal interpretation.

“The main concern is the untreated comparison for participants. Voluntary participation and one pre-period do not explain why their untreated mean trend would equal the controls' trend. Untreated participant outcomes of (22,32) and (27,37) are both compatible with the table, yet imply effects of ten and five. The paper needs a substantive assignment/comparison argument with evidence bearing on that restriction, or a narrower descriptive claim. Adding fixed effects to the same two-date table does not provide the missing assumption. Earlier outcomes could inform the assessment but would not by themselves prove the future counterfactual. I cannot recommend a causal conclusion from the current packet.

“The welfare claim is also unsupported. Investment expenditure is not itself a measured social benefit, and subtracting the facility's three-CU cost from the ten-CU comparison would mix an unidentified effect with an incomplete welfare account. If the paper retains a policy recommendation, it must define benefits, opportunity costs, relevant parties and the treatment of displaced activity. Otherwise remove that conclusion. Finally, label the reported statistic as a group mean-change difference and state that no inferential procedure or sampling population has been supplied. A clear definition of each group and period is essential; a more polished figure is optional. A descriptive revision may have value, but its contribution and fit would need assessment under a different submission category.”

Consistent editorial note. “I recommend declining this version in the stated causal-research category. Its numerical execution is correct; the central identification bridge is unsupported, and the remedy may require a different research design. The report identifies the exact observational ambiguity and a possible descriptive reframing. This is not a finding of fabrication, nor a claim that the true effect is zero. I have no basis to certify novelty beyond the supplied packet.” The author-facing reasons and editorial judgment agree. Rejection here is a conditional judgment about this version and venue, not a verdict on the topic or researcher.

3. A serious error can still have a narrow repair

Ask what survives after a correction. If a result fails at a boundary but a corrected statement preserves the economic mechanism over the intended domain, a focused revision may be sufficient. If the central effect is not identified by any supplied restriction, correcting a standard error will not rescue it. The severity label alone does not choose between revision and rejection.

Worked case 2. Repairing a buffer-choice proposition

Complete mock submission packet B. A short explanatory model note chooses buffer b from the closed interval [0,4]. Buffer is measured in model resource units. Its stipulated gross service benefit is 6b−b² CU and its opportunity cost is cb CU, with c≥0 measured in CU per buffer unit. The objective is J(b;c)=(6−c)b−b². There are no uncertainty estimates or empirical calibration. The note claims: “For every c≥0, the unique optimal buffer is (6−c)/2, obtained by the first-order condition; therefore the optimizer is always interior.” The closest supplied fictional predecessor solves only the c=0 case. No search beyond that predecessor is represented as complete. The hypothetical workshop accepts clearly bounded explanatory models whose mechanism and limits are useful, rather than requiring a major new theorem.

Independent correction. Complete the square: J(b;c)=−[b−(6−c)/2]²+(6−c)²/4. For 0≤c<6, the vertex lies in (0,3], within [0,4], and uniquely maximizes the strict concave quadratic. For c≥6, J(b;c)≤0 for every b≥0, with equality only at b=0, including c=6 because J=−b². Thus b*(c)=max{0,(6−c)/2}; the upper bound never binds for the stated nonnegative c. The domain matters: a subsidy represented by c<0 could make the upper bound bind, but that is outside this proposition.

For the displayed cost menu (0,2,5,6,8), the true buffers are (3,2,1/2,0,0) and their objective values are (9,4,1/4,0,0). At c=8 the submitted formula gives −1, outside the choice set. This is a valid counterexample to the claimed universal proposition because c=8 satisfies its premise. At c=6 the first-order equality does hold at zero, but the conclusion of interiority still fails. The optimal buffer decreases with c until it reaches zero, then remains zero; a claim of strict decrease over all c≥0 would also need correction.

Completed report to the author. “The note uses a transparent objective to explain how a buffer's opportunity cost changes the preferred quantity. Its strength is the separation of a stipulated service benefit from the cost of tying up resources. Relative to the supplied cost-free predecessor, allowing positive cost makes an additional comparative-static question available; I have not established novelty against the wider literature. For this workshop's stated explanatory remit, I recommend a focused revision.

“The central proposition needs a boundary correction. At the admissible c=8, its formula gives b=−1, which is infeasible. Completing the square shows that the corrected optimizer is max{0,(6−c)/2}; the upper capacity does not bind for c≥0. State the interior condition c<6, treat c=6 explicitly, and replace the global strict-decrease claim with a decrease followed by a zero-buffer region. This is essential because the paper currently presents an infeasible policy as its main result. It is a narrow repair: the objective and its conditional economic interpretation survive.

“Also explain the units of c and why the concave service-benefit function is a modeling assumption rather than an estimated response. The result describes this objective and does not establish that actual intermediaries should hold the derived quantity. A comparison with the supplied predecessor should state the precise added scope; broader novelty remains unresolved. Extending to random withdrawal needs or estimating the benefit function would be interesting subsequent work, but neither is necessary for a correct note within the present remit. The revised text should include the corrected proof and boundary example; a new decorative graph is optional.”

Editorial note and revision test. “A revision is plausible under the stipulated workshop criteria because the main mechanism survives an explicit correction. Verify the corrected domain, proof, units and contribution statement before acceptance.” A successful response would replace the proposition, recompute any dependent examples and remove unsupported global language. Merely adding a footnote that b must be nonnegative while leaving the original formula as the recommendation would not resolve the concern. If the venue instead required a substantial new research theorem, the same correct result might still fail its contribution threshold.

4. Make the review accountable

Record which materials and versions you actually checked. Declare expertise limits and possible conflicts through the venue's process. Keep confidential material within the authorized review process; obtain the required permission before involving another reviewer. Describe suspected irregularities to the editor as observations and uncertainties, not proven misconduct. These principles are supported by COPE's 2017 peer-review guidelines, whose sections on conducting and preparing reviews also distinguish necessary changes from extensions. This chapter's public synthetic packets require no access to confidential submissions.

The American Economic Review's reviewer guidance, inspected on 16 October 2026, asks reviewers to assess execution and contribution and distinguish essential from optional revisions. It also addresses anonymity and conditions on AI assistance; a real reviewer must check the applicable current venue policy and their authorization before using outside assistance. Those journal-specific instructions are not treated as universal law or as permission to share a confidential manuscript here.

In practice, a report should make its evidentiary limits visible: “I checked the proof and supplied code but could not access the restricted data” is more informative than “the paper is validated.” A reviewer must own each judgment and verify every cited claim. Do not request citations merely to promote your work. If an overlooked source matters, identify the overlapping result and explain how it changes the contribution. Distinguish advice to improve this paper from a suggestion for the authors' next paper.

For further study, Berk, Harvey and Hirshleifer's 2017 article on effective referee reports discusses costs and common reviewing mistakes. Here its publication record and abstract were inspected, not its full text. It is a reading route, not evidence that this chapter reproduces its argument or empirically measures review quality.

Complete answers and evaluation criteria

1. Separate the reproduced statistic from the claim

“The paper compares investment changes for two voluntary participants and two nonparticipants over two dates. It reports a ten-CU difference in mean changes and interprets it as the facility's effect.” Participant changes are 12 and 12, mean 12; controls 2 and 2, mean 2; difference 10. The code reproduces those descriptive operations on the supplied table. The participants' untreated mean trend, and hence τ, remains unidentified without an additional restriction. Full credit requires both neutral reconstruction and the distinction; neither “the program ran” nor “the effect is zero” is sufficient.

2. A compatible explanation is a logical witness

The proposed untreated participant changes are 10 and 10. Their observed minus untreated outcomes are 2 and 2, giving (tau,delta)=(2,8); 2+8=10. These counterfactuals preserve the observed table, so they show another compatible explanation, not evidence that it is true. Under the extra restriction δ∈[−2,3], τ=10−δ lies in (7,12). Every value in that interval corresponds to an allowed δ and can be implemented by setting both participant untreated changes to 2+δ. Thus the interval is sharp under the stipulated restriction, not a sampling confidence interval. No such restriction was provided in the original packet.

3. Rank by what the claim needs

The untreated comparison is essential to the causal interpretation. Benefit definition is essential if the welfare recommendation remains. Exact version identification is essential to an auditable execution claim, even though it may be easy to supply. Colors are optional if the figure is already legible. A new-continent estimate is outside the stated local claim; it would become relevant only to an explicitly broadened transport claim. A costly new design can be essential and infeasible, while an easy wording change can address a major error. Ask whether the requirement supports the actual conclusion, then assess repair feasibility.

4. State the whole domain

For 0≤c<6 the completed-square vertex lies in (0,3], so strict concavity yields the unique feasible maximum b=(6−c)/2. For c≥6, J=(6−c)b−b² is negative at every b>0 and zero at b=0, so zero is uniquely optimal. For c=5 choose 1/2 with J=1/4; for c=8 choose 0 with J=0. At c=6 the derivative6−c−2b vanishes at b=0, a boundary point, so a zero derivative does not imply interiority. A negative cost violates the premise c≥0 and cannot be a valid refutation on that domain; it would instead motivate an explicitly extended theorem with the upper bound considered. Full credit requires existence/admissibility, uniqueness and the boundary argument, not just differentiation.

5. Make criticism testable

“The ten-CU comparison is reproducible, but the packet does not justify equal untreated trends. The two counterfactual histories in the worked example preserve all observations while changing the estimated effect. Please explain the assignment/comparison restriction and provide evidence relevant to it, or narrow the conclusion to a descriptive difference. Additional regressions on the same table do not by themselves resolve the ambiguity.” A citation request is justified when a specified source bears on the assumption, method or claimed contribution; explain that connection and verify the source. The reviewer's authorship of a paper is neither a reason to require nor to exclude it. Full credit replaces personal judgment and an unlimited demand with evidence, consequence and a bounded response.

6. Correctness survives the novelty comparison

“The corrected proposition is valid, but the newly inspected predecessor already establishes its result and interpretation on the same domain. The note should cite that work and stop claiming this proposition as a new contribution. It may serve as an exposition or training note if the venue values a demonstrably better explanation or application; that value must be argued separately. For a category requiring original theory, the current contribution is insufficient.” A changed notation or newly drawn graph is not automatically a consequential advance. Full credit identifies the exact overlap, preserves mathematical correctness and conditions fit on the venue rather than asserting a universal publication decision.

7. Resolve the question the reports address

“Both reports can be correct about their inspected components: the table reproduces, while its causal interpretation requires an unsupported restriction. Execution is necessary but does not settle identification. Under the stipulated causal-research remit, the second concern prevents acceptance of this version. A credible assignment argument with relevant evidence could change that conclusion; alternatively the authors could reframe and establish a descriptive contribution for an appropriate category. I would seek clarification about the identification bridge, not average the recommendations.” The synthesis must not invent a third reader's data audit or treat the number of favorable reports as evidence about counterfactuals.

8. A complete short report

“The note documents nominal income rising from 100 to 110 and provides code reproducing those values. Its complete small input table is a useful starting point. However, the corresponding price indices imply base-period-price incomes 100/(100/100)=100 and 110/(110/100)=100. The reported real gain is therefore 0, not 10%. Please correct the real-income variable, computation and every dependent statement, and identify the index base and measurement population. The policy attribution is separately unsupported without an assignment/comparison argument; remove it for the proposed descriptive note. I recommend a focused revision under the stated measurement remit, conditional on whether the corrected zero-change description has sufficient value. Acceptance should require a rerun producing the corrected real series and an accurately bounded conclusion. The available packet supplies no sampling design, so I make no claim about statistical significance.” Full credit includes all six requested components and keeps arithmetic repair separate from causal identification and contribution.

6. Teach-back and delayed retention

Teach-back. Explain in three minutes why packet A and packet B can both contain major problems while justifying different recommendations. Show one exact witness for each and name the minimum credible response.

Teach-back guide

Packet A's compatible untreated histories change τ while preserving observed data; the missing causal bridge needs evidence or a narrower claim. Packet B's c=8 gives an infeasible proposed choice, but the corrected corner formula has a short global proof and preserves the stipulated mechanism. The comparison concerns repair feasibility and venue fit, not whether one kind of author deserves harsher treatment.

Delayed retention. After a week, review a short note that calls a precisely computed association causal, while its main table silently changes population between columns. Write one essential comment and one optional suggestion; then state what you would inspect in a revision.

Criteria and illustrative answer

“Please specify the population in each column and reconstruct a common-population comparison or explain the changed target; otherwise the apparent change may reflect composition. Even a corrected association needs an explicit assignment/counterfactual argument before causal language is justified.” An optional suggestion is a clearer caption once the target is correct. In revision, inspect population definitions, inclusion/exclusion counts, regenerated comparisons and the claimed identification bridge. A smoother figure alone resolves none of those concerns. Full credit distinguishes the two essential mechanisms and makes the follow-up observable.
