Volume 17 · Chapter 12

Teaching and supervision portfolio

Capability. Defend a coherent teaching program by linking its purposes, materials, assessment, observed delivery and supervision to specific evidence and explicit limits. Prerequisite: observed teaching and reflective practice, with the course, assessment and supervision designs developed throughout this volume. Both dossiers below are entirely fictional calibration cases; even their stipulated observations are invented teaching specimens, not observations of the reader or of an actual instructor.

1. A portfolio makes a claim that someone else can test

A directory of polished files does not by itself establish teaching competence. A syllabus can show a coherent plan; it cannot show that the plan was delivered. A student's corrected solution can show a correct answer after feedback; it cannot alone show independent transfer. A portfolio is an argument connecting a bounded claim to materials a reviewer can inspect. The first question is therefore not “How many pages?” but “Which capability, in which setting, does this evidence support?”

A claim is a proposition about the instructor's design, practice or development. An artifact is a dated, attributable object such as an assignment, learner response, observation note or supervision exchange. A warrant explains why the artifact bears on the claim. A limitation states what the evidence leaves unresolved. An external evaluation here means an actual assessment by an appropriately qualified person other than the portfolio's author; it is not created by writing a section with that heading.

The CMU Eberly Center's portfolio guidance distinguishes framing material from supporting evidence, asks authors to consider audience and purpose, and recommends explaining selected materials and preserving earlier versions. The Cornell teaching-portfolio guide likewise connects collecting materials with reflection and annotation. These are bounded professional design references. Our examples, criteria and decisions are original; neither source certifies this library or establishes the rules of a particular appointment process.

Build the portfolio around a small number of substantial claims. For example: “I designed a sequence from equilibrium calculation to an allocation-sensitive welfare judgment”; “I identified a false uniqueness claim in a student's proof and asked for a new argument”; “I revised a task after observing a recurring error.” Each is more inspectable than “I am an excellent teacher.” A teaching statement should explain what you value and connect it to actual choices. When the evidence is only a proposed lesson, write “I propose” or “This design asks students to”; do not change the tense to “My students learned.”

2. Assemble a complete program with a visible evidence chain

For this library, a complete program contains both undergraduate and graduate designs, substantive sample materials, original examinations with keys and rubrics, an actual teaching demonstration and observation when delivered, a supervision plan, and a documented independent evaluation. This is an authored learning requirement, not a universal university application format. Follow the actual recipient's requirements for a real submission. Separate a compact argument from an indexed evidence archive so a reviewer can locate the full prompt and response behind a summary score.

The links supply complete instructional models, not a learner's completed submission. Attribute borrowed structures and adapted problems. A peer's observation of a ten-minute explanation does not establish delivery of an entire semester. A course plan can be reviewed before delivery; its delivery claim remains pending. A portfolio may therefore have strong evidence for design and incomplete evidence for implementation at the same time.

Keep a simple evidence register: identifier, version/date, creator and role, setting, claim supported, support allowed, permission/access condition, limitation and reviewer disposition. For actual learner work, use authorized handling, necessary redaction and a reviewer route that respects the applicable agreement. Do not invent permission or assume that removing a name resolves every privacy issue. Retain enough context to interpret the work: the prompt, denominator, timing, available help and grading key are often indispensable.

Use four qualitative review outcomes: supported within scope; supported with a specified limitation; revision required because the argument is wrong or incomplete; and not yet evaluable because essential evidence is absent. These are proposed portfolio judgments, not a validated scale. Do not average a fabricated observation into an otherwise high score. A missing item can be repaired with actual work or an honestly narrowed claim; it cannot be repaired by a more confident paragraph.

3. Two complete portfolio audits

Dossier A — welfare instruction with an observed allocation error

Context and opening statement. Fictional instructor Eva submits a designed three-session undergraduate unit, one stipulated observed twenty-minute demonstration, four learner responses and a revised task. Her statement reads: “I want students to separate a market calculation from the allocation assumptions needed for welfare. My first task did not elicit that distinction adequately. The attached response and observation records explain the revision.” The larger course plan is a proposed application of Chapter 3, not a completed course. The demonstration and responses are stipulated only within this fictional dossier.

Complete demonstration task. Demand is Qd=100−2p and supply Qs=2p, with nonnegative quantities over the relevant price range. A unit of quantity is one service unit per period; price is currency per unit. Consumers have quasilinear surplus represented by inverse demand Pd(q)=50−q/2; inverse supply Ps(q)=q/2 represents marginal resource cost. There are no externalities, search costs or enforcement costs. First find competitive p and Q. Then impose a ceiling p̄=20 and assume supplied quantity is sold, with the units assigned to the highest willingness-to-pay buyers. Find shortage, consumer surplus, producer surplus, total surplus and lost gains from trade. Explain which result would change if allocation were inefficient. Inputs: (100,2,2,20), ordered demand intercept, demand slope magnitude, supply slope, ceiling.

Full key and economic argument. Equating demand and supply gives100−2p=2p, hence p*=25 and Q*=50. At the ceiling, supply40 and demand60 leave shortage20; traded quantity is40 under the stated trading rule. Efficient allocation of those40 units gives CS=∫₀⁴⁰(50−q/2−20)dq=800, PS=∫₀⁴⁰(20−q/2)dq=400 and total1200. Without the ceiling each surplus is625 and total1250. The lost surplus is ∫₄₀⁵⁰(50−q)dq=50, equivalently one half times the missing10 units times the willingness-to-pay/marginal-cost gap10 atq40. Results(p*,Q*,Qd at cap,Qs at cap,shortage,CS,PS,total,loss) are (25,50,60,40,20,800,400,1200,50).

The integral of inverse demand is total willingness to pay only when the buyers with the highest values receive the units. Demand alone does not tell us how rationing assigns the40 units. Transfers from consumers to producers cancel in total surplus, but who receives units affects gross value. The computed50 is the loss under the efficient-rationing assumption, not a universal loss from a ceiling. With omitted search/enforcement costs it also need not equal total social loss. A lower price does not by itself establish a welfare improvement for every consumer.

Dossier B — a graduate proof and supervision record with a missing demonstration

Context and syllabus connection. Fictional instructor Dev submits a proposed two-session auction module within the research-seminar structure of Chapter4. Session1,90 minutes: assumptions and mechanism20, individual proof attempt20, counterexample discussion25, corrected proof defense25. Session2,90 minutes: alternative payment rule20, changed task25, referee-style criticism25, proposal scope20. Each session assigns three independent hours for reading mechanism and incentive reasoning, reconstructing the supplied proof and writing a one-page critique. Total workload is 9 hours. The module is proposed; no delivery is recorded.

Teaching statement and supervision agreement. “I assess whether a learner can state the mechanism before claiming an incentive result. My feedback should identify a failed implication and leave the next proof with the student.” Student Inez owns a two-week proof note on a single-item second-price auction; Dev reviews one draft and one revision in a thirty-minute meeting each week. Four student hours per week include those meetings. Use private valuev≥0, risk-neutral quasilinear payoff, nonnegative bids, no participation cost and no external effect from another person's win. Highest bid wins and pays the highest other bid; tied highest bids are broken by a declared lottery. Nonwinners pay zero. Work excludes empirical auction outcomes, common values, collusion, budgets and a claim of scholarly novelty. Record assistance and credit; unresolved scope or credit concerns can go to an actually verified independent adviser without claiming confidentiality in advance.

Supplied student draft. “Bidding one's value is strictly dominant, so there is a unique equilibrium and every winner makes positive profit. Since the second price does not depend on my bid, any bid has the same payoff.” The draft's conclusion contradicts its last sentence: one's bid can change whether one wins. The task is to replace it with a precise proposition and full proof, then identify the limits of the inference.

Complete supervisor response. “Fix the other bids and call their maximum m. Distinguish m below, above and equal to v. Your bid changes allocation even when it does not set the payment conditional on winning. Compare each possible allocation's payoff, including a tie lottery. Replace ‘strict’ with the property the comparison actually proves, and test the claims about equilibrium uniqueness and winner profit separately. I have supplied the cases; your revision must write the argument. For a new task I will change the payment rule, not only a number.”

Full revised proof. Conditional on the other bids, a win yieldsv−m, a loss0, and a highest-bid tie with win probabilityα∈[0,1] yieldsα(v−m). If m<v, truthful bidv strictly exceedsm and secures the largest feasible payoffv−m>0; any alternative win is equally good, a tie weakly worse and a loss worse. If m>v, truth loses with0, while a win is negative and a tie is nonpositive. If m=v, every possible allocation has payoff0, so truth is again no worse. These exhaustive comparisons prove truthful bidding weakly dominates every alternative for any fixed other bids. No probability distribution or independence of values is needed for this ex-post comparison. The proof does not establish strict dominance, unique equilibrium, positive winner surplus in ties, or the same result with another payment rule.

Concrete proof witnesses. Forv6,m4 (input (6,4)), truth6 and alternative5 both win and yield2; bid3 loses and yields0. Forv6,m8, truth6 loses with0 and overbid9 wins with−2. Forv=m6, a tie gives0 regardless of its lottery probability. The payoff tuple is (2,2,0,0,-2,0). A separate complete-information two-player example shows why a uniqueness claim fails: values(6,4), bids(5,4) is a Nash equilibrium under second-price rules. Player1 wins and earns2; any winning deviation still pays4, a tie yields at most2, and losing yields0. Player2 loses with0; outbidding5 wins at price5 and gives−1, a tie has nonpositive expected payoff, and lower bids still yield0. Thus neither can improve. The truthful profile(6,4) is also an equilibrium; different bid profiles exist. This example concerns that complete-information game and proves no classification of all equilibria.

Independent changed-rule task and supplied response. “Keepv6 and the other bid4, but make the winner pay their own bid. Is bidding6 still weakly dominant? Supply a profitable deviation and state the scope of the result.” Inez's stipulated independent written response, with only the mechanism statement available, is: “Bidding6 wins but pays6, giving0. Bidding5 also wins but pays5, giving1, so truth fails to be weakly dominant in this first-price mechanism. This single profile disproves the universal dominance claim; it does not solve equilibrium for a distribution of private values.” Full key agrees, with (0,1). On a six-point rubric award2 for both correct payoffs,2 for the dominance counterexample and2 for the limit:6/6. The supervisor supplied the original case structure, whereas this changed-rule response is stipulated as independent; the portfolio must preserve that distinction.

External review specimen and disposition. A fictional independent theory reviewer is stipulated to have read the proof note, feedback and changed-rule response, but has not observed any class. Full report: “The corrected weak-dominance proof handles ties and does not rely on distributional assumptions. The changed-rule counterexample demonstrates a correct local extension under its declared support conditions. The supervision exchange supports a bounded claim about feedback and this learner's response. It does not establish that Dev can lead the proposed seminar or that Inez can independently produce the original proof. I require an actual observed demonstration and appropriately handled learner work before evaluating delivery. The proposed course and correct proof are reviewable design artifacts now.”

Portfolio correction and defense. Dev initially labels the dossier “Observed graduate teaching, completed.” The corrected cover reads: “Graduate module designed; proof and supervision specimens reviewed; classroom observation absent. An actual demonstration is the next required activity.” Register entriesB1(module),B2(draft/proof),B3(feedback),B4(changed task/response),B5(reviewer report) support distinct claims. EntryB6(observation) is missing, not pending evidence that may be presumed favorable. At defense, the question “Does the report satisfy the observation requirement?” receives the answer: “No. A reviewer can validate an argument without observing my interaction with learners. I will arrange an actual session, agree the observation purpose and data handling, retain the observer's notes and full learner responses, and write a reflection. Until then I can defend design and the supplied supervision record only.” This is a complete audit decision, not an invented completion of the missing activity.

4. Conduct a defense that can change the conclusion

Before the defense, reviewers inspect full artifacts and identify consequential questions: a disputed calculation, an unexplained score, a missing observation, an alternative explanation or a mismatched course objective. The candidate answers by locating evidence and solving a changed task, not by repeating the teaching statement. Reviewers record what help they gave, whether the answer repaired the claim, and which dispositions remain. A correction is valuable evidence of judgment, but it does not retroactively make an earlier false claim true.

After review, produce a short revision log: original claim, criticism, exact change, evidence added, and unresolved limit. Version the supporting task and key together so an old score is not silently interpreted under a new rubric. A real portfolio's actual external evaluation, dates and signatures must come from the people who perform it. The library provides the protocol and calibration; it cannot award itself the reader's teaching experience. Continue development by selecting the most consequential missing evidence, rather than adding another polished statement to an already documented design.

5. A tie does not create positive profit

Truth5 loses against7 and earns0. Overbid8 wins, pays7 and earns−2. Bid7 ties and earns expected payoff(1/2)(5−7)=−1. The tuple is (0,-2,-1). Since any win againstm7 loses2 and any tie is a nonpositive mixture, losing with truth weakly beats every possible allocation. Ifm=v5, any win pays5, yielding0; loss and every lottery also yield0. Thus all reports have the same payoff in that equality case, directly refuting strict dominance and guaranteed positive winner surplus. Credit the three payoffs, conditional allocation comparison and equality boundary.

Teach-back and delayed retention

Teach-back. Explain the difference among a correct key, a correct learner response, an observed lesson and an identified teaching effect. Full guide: a key supplies a benchmark; a response supplies performance under specified support; observation supplies evidence of actual interaction; a causal teaching-effect claim needs a credible counterfactual design beyond those objects. Locate one actual example or missing item in each dossier. Do not use a score as a substitute for its underlying response.

Sources, deeper study and the next stage

The two official portfolio guides support only the narrow organization-and-reflection principles cited above. The full courses and specialist economic prerequisites are linked within the library; all dossier data, proofs, responses, reports and tasks are original. The judgment categories and workloads are design choices, not empirically calibrated hiring criteria. The volume assessment rehearses integrated design and judgment; the separate teaching portfolio capstone requires the learner's actual work and human observation. Volume 18 then extends responsibility to institutional strategy, resources and governance. A sound teaching portfolio states the boundary between preparation and demonstrated practice as carefully as an economist states the boundary between a model and evidence.
