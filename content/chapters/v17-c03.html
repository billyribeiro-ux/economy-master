A course is a sequence of opportunities to learn and demonstrate something. A calendar full of important topics is not yet that sequence.

Capability. Build an undergraduate course whose entry assumptions, learning objectives, reading, practice, feedback and cumulative assessments fit together within a feasible workload. Prerequisites are objective and prerequisite design and faithful explanation across representations. The numerical examples use the accepted foundation chapters. All courses, student records and classroom decisions below are original proposed teaching cases, not courses that have been approved or taught.

1. Begin with the exit task and the entering learner

A syllabus communicates the course’s purpose, expected work, sequence, assessment and operating arrangements. A course objective specifies what the learner should be able to do under stated conditions. A learning activity provides practice; an assessment elicits evidence. A prerequisite is a capability needed for a later task, not merely a topic that appeared earlier. Alignment means that the intended capability is actually practiced and assessed. It does not mean that every assignment repeats the same question.

Work backward from a concrete exit performance. For example, “prepare a defensible project-choice memo” requires defining a target, reconciling units and accounts, valuing dated cash flows, distinguishing value from cash availability, comparing scenarios and qualifying the claim. Put those dependencies into a sequence. Ask what a student must already know, which missing pieces the course will teach, and which ambitions the available weeks cannot support. Specify the expected audience before selecting difficult readings. Expertise in economics does not imply that entering students can read its compressed notation.

The Bok Center’s learner-centered design guidance places learning evidence and students’ prior knowledge before exhaustive content selection.[1] Carnegie Mellon’s alignment guidance connects objectives, evidence tasks and instructional activities.[2] These are professional design recommendations. They do not establish that the particular course below will cause a specified learning gain.

Use two ledgers. The capability ledger links each objective to prerequisite practice, feedback and an independently completed task. The time ledger accounts for contact time, reading, practice and assessment work. If a new project requires three hours, those hours must fit somewhere; renaming it “formative” does not remove its workload. Estimated hours are planning hypotheses to revise using actual student work and reported difficulties, not precise measures of learning.

2. Worked case: a complete foundation course

Case 1 — Economic and financial reasoning for undergraduate decisions

Course identity and audience. This proposed twelve-week introductory course is for undergraduates who can perform arithmetic, read a simple table and solve a one-step equation. It does not require calculus, coding, prior economics or prior finance. The course teaches the necessary algebra, unit conventions and discounting. It is a foundation for later microeconomics, accounting, statistics and finance; it does not replace those full courses. No university credit or accreditation is asserted.

Delivery and support. Each week has two one-hour lessons and a one-hour problem/discussion meeting, plus six planned independent hours. Before actual enrollment, the instructor must fill in name, contact route, room or meeting link, dates, office-hour schedule and the responsible institutional support route. Those fields are not invented here. Offer one scheduled question period each week and a written question route; give an equivalent written way to demonstrate understanding when spontaneous public speech is not the assessed capability. Materials use text, labeled tables and explicit units. Determine actual accommodations and local policies through the appropriate process before launch.

Learning objectives. By the end, a student should independently: O1 reconcile units, percentages and simple stock/flow accounts; O2 value stated dated cash flows and distinguish value from financing feasibility; O3 analyze a feasible choice and a simple incentive/equilibrium model under explicit assumptions; O4 compare probability scenarios and identify what an expected value omits; O5 explain a bounded price-level or financial-intermediation mechanism without confusing nominal and real quantities; and O6 write and defend a claim whose evidence, assumptions and uncertainty can be inspected. Assessment conditions vary deliberately: written calculations are completed independently, while the project permits documented resources.

Entry diagnostic, before week 1. Give four ungraded questions: convert 250 thousand into millions; find the percent increase from 80 to 88; solve 3x+2=14; and explain why a balance of 50 is not a monthly income of 50. The answers are (1/4,1/10,4) for the first three; the fourth must identify a stock measured at a date versus a flow measured over an interval. Ask for reasoning, not just a total. Unit or percentage difficulty routes to week 2’s preparatory examples; equation difficulty receives a short guided practice before week 3. These responses inform support and pacing; they are not a claim that one short quiz precisely measures ability.

Workload. The planned weekly vector (contact, reading, practice, assignment preparation) is (3,2,2,2) hours, totaling 9; twelve weeks total 108. Assessments replace scheduled practice or meeting time rather than being added invisibly. In week 11, use the six independent hours as one hour of targeted reading, one of changed practice and four of project revision. In week 12, use them for two hours of spaced review, two of unfamiliar problems and two of reflection/repair. The first allocation is a target, not a promise about each learner’s pace. Check actual workload at the first two checkpoints and adjust scope or support rather than penalizing students for an inaccurate estimate.

Assessment and feedback policy. Proposed course-mark weights are practice 10%, midterm 15%, cumulative final 25%, project 40% and oral transfer 10%. Practice is submitted individually after permitted discussion; credit depends on a reasoned attempt and a substantive correction, not merely attendance. The midterm and final use unseen numbers and require independent work with a basic calculator; no external solutions or generative assistance. The project is open resource: identify sources, collaborators and tools, retain calculations and submit your own defended reasoning. The oral task changes an assumption rather than requesting a memorized script. These are specimen rules to communicate and reconcile with actual local policy before teaching.

Return the midterm and project-checkpoint feedback within the next teaching week in this proposed schedule; assign an instructor or teaching-team owner to each response. Give specific next actions: “your repayment was subtracted twice; redraw the cash timeline” is more useful than “be careful.” A missed assessment needs a documented equivalent reassessment arrangement under the actual course policy; no automatic zero or exception is prescribed for an unknown institution. State deadlines and late-work rules before enrollment. Publish rubrics with the tasks, preserve first attempts, and distinguish corrected practice from independent reassessment. Proposed internal mastery review requires at least 85% overall, no essential objective-domain result below 75%, and correction of critical unit, feasibility or evidence errors. A weighted course mark and demonstrated capability are separate records; thresholds are uncalibrated teaching choices.

A complete sample project assignment and instructor answer

Prompt. A fictional cooperative’s (opening cash, immediate required reserve) are (100,20) currency units. It can choose at most one machine. For each machine, (cost now, certain inflow at year 1, certain inflow at year 2) is given in currency units: A has (90,55,55) and B has (70,42,42). Payments occur at year end; use the stated risk-free annual discount rate 1/10 (=10%). There are no taxes, other expenses, salvage value or subsequent cash commitments in this case. First assume borrowing is unavailable. Then allow a contract with (loan now, repayment at year 1)=(10,11), with no other fee. Calculate both project NPVs and immediate cash positions; make the feasible choice in each regime; reconcile A’s equity cash-flow NPV under borrowing; and write a short conditional recommendation. Finally distinguish this stipulated-value calculation from a claim that machines cause higher community welfare. These data are assumptions, not market quotations.

Answer. The discount factors are 10/11 and 100/121. For A, NPV=−90+55(10/11)+55(100/121)=60/11. For B it is −70+42(10/11)+42(100/121)=350/121. Thus the two values are (60/11,350/121). Immediate cash without borrowing is (10,30) for A and B. A violates the 20 reserve; B satisfies it and has positive NPV, so choose B among the supplied feasible alternatives, including doing nothing at NPV zero. A’s higher unconstrained NPV does not make its payment feasible.

With the permitted loan, A leaves 100+10−90=20 and becomes feasible. The equity cash-flow vector is (-80,44,55): initial net payment 80, then 55−11=44, then 55. Discounting at the same 10% gives −80+44(10/11)+55(100/121)=60/11. The loan’s own NPV is 10−11(10/11)=0, so consistent accounting preserves project value. It changes immediate financing feasibility. The value advantage of A over B is 310/121. The reserve condition is specifically immediate; an actual multi-date liquidity requirement would need its own cash schedule.

Complete recommendation. “Choose B if borrowing is unavailable: it leaves cash 30 and has positive NPV 350/121, whereas A breaches the immediate reserve. With the stipulated 10-unit loan, A is feasible at cash 20 and has the higher NPV 60/11. This comparison relies on the stated certain cash flows, matched borrowing/discount rate and complete expense list; it does not establish real demand, social welfare or an effect in a population outside this case.” A defensible alternative recommendation must identify a changed objective or assumption, not silently replace the stated model.

Additive project rubric (20 points for this specimen). Units/timing/assumptions 3; both NPVs with calculation 4; immediate cash and feasible selection without borrowing 4; borrowing cash-flow reconciliation and value/feasibility distinction 5; complete bounded recommendation 4. Award each criterion once. A carried arithmetic error can retain correct later reasoning credit, but an unfunded choice requires correction even if the total is high. The full course project adds the explicitly taught scenario extension from week 8; this specimen establishes its valuation core rather than pretending to assess all six objectives by itself.

3. Worked redesign: preserve evidence by narrowing scope

Case 2 — Nine weeks available, with the same weekly ceiling

The course loses three weeks before enrollment. Available student time is now 9×9=81 hours, a loss of 27. An attractive but invalid response is to assign all twelve weeks’ work unchanged and call the last three chapters “independent reading”: that would require 12 hours per remaining week on average. It would exceed the declared ceiling by 3 hours each week and remove planned practice and feedback while preserving the original breadth claim.

Decision. Offer a narrower course titled “Foundations of project decisions.” Retain O1, O2, O4 and O6. Narrow O3 to feasible individual choices and elementary algebra; remove its market-equilibrium performance requirement. Remove O5’s assessed purchasing-power and intermediation breadth. Move the original weeks 6, 7 and 9 to optional future study that is genuinely ungraded and unnecessary for any remaining assessment. The 27-hour reduction now reflects an explicit change in promised learning. Do not describe this as an equivalent twelve-week course.

Contact/independent hours remain 3/6 each week. The project and feedback dates move with the new sequence; they are not squeezed into the same old calendar dates.

Assessment repair. Retain the component weights 10/15/25/40/10, but rewrite the final blueprint to the five retained objective domains. Remove purchasing-power, intermediation and market-equilibrium items; replace them with unfamiliar units, valuation, feasible-choice, uncertainty and bounded-argument tasks already practiced. Have a second instructor solve those tasks and check their prerequisites before use. Numerical scores from this revised exam are not equated to the original exam. Keep the same internal readiness principle—85 overall, at least 75 within each retained essential domain and critical-error repair—without pretending it empirically establishes equivalence.

Complete student-facing notice. “The nine-week course focuses on project decisions: units and cash accounts, dated value, feasible choices, uncertainty and evidence-based communication. It will not assess the original course’s market-equilibrium, price-index or banking-mechanism modules. The revised calendar shows all required work and feedback dates within a planned nine hours per week. Optional foundation chapters remain available for later study but are not prerequisites for the revised final. We will revisit these workload estimates after the first checkpoint and publish any justified adjustment with its assessment consequences.”

Why this is a defensible redesign. Each retained task still has preparation and an opportunity to correct errors before the final performance. The design reduces breadth rather than claiming that faster speech preserves learning. It remains a proposal: learner work, workload feedback and independent observation would be needed to judge its actual execution and educational effects. If an institution requires the removed content for progression, the narrower course cannot silently substitute for it; obtain an actual curriculum decision or change the scheduling constraint.

4. Turn a syllabus into an operating course

Before launch, solve the assessment yourself under the stated conditions, then ask another qualified instructor to inspect alignment and ambiguity. Check that readings are available, notation is introduced before use, deadlines leave time for feedback, and each marked skill has been practiced without giving away the final task. Put the instructor and support information into the actual syllabus. The Bok Center’s syllabus guidance identifies logistics, objectives, schedule, assessment, responsibilities, materials and support as communication needs.[3] The specimen’s actual policies and numerical workload are our proposed choices.

During teaching, inspect the reasoning in a sample of student responses. A low score can reflect a prerequisite gap, ambiguous wording, poor alignment, insufficient practice or an incorrect solution key; investigate before attributing it to effort. Separate an access problem from a disciplinary objective. If rapid oral calculation is not the objective, a documented format change can preserve the intended reasoning demand. Where an assessment’s conditions change, record the change and avoid assuming old and new scores are comparable.

After the course, retain anonymized or otherwise appropriately handled work only under actual authorized practice. Compare objective-specific evidence, changes in task difficulty, missing responses and assistance conditions. A higher average score after a redesign is not by itself a causal teaching-effect estimate: students, selection and questions may have changed. The later chapters develop assessment, feedback and observed-teaching protocols; the present task is to make the design coherent enough to implement and evaluate.

5. Exercises

  1. Repair a course promise. A syllabus promises “master all finance” in nine weeks and grades only a terminology quiz. Write two complete replacement objective sentences and a corresponding evidence task for each. State a boundary that the course should disclose.
  2. Sequence actual dependencies. Let U denote units, A accounts, P present value, C project choice, R risk scenarios and M a memo. Required edges are U→A, U→P, A→C, P→C, C→M and R→M. Give a valid order and explain why U,A,C,P,M,R fails. Add a legitimate feedback loop without making the prerequisite graph cyclic.
  3. Repair a weekly workload. A week contains 3 contact hours, 3 reading, 3 practice and 2 project preparation, against a ceiling of 9. Calculate the excess and give one exact revised allocation. Explain what must be checked before calling the revised plan feasible.
  4. Check a changed financing contract. In the machine project, keep the discount rate at 10% but change the permitted loan to 10 now and repayment 12 in year 1. Compute A’s equity NPV, its difference from the original project NPV, and whether A still beats B when both are feasible. Explain the teaching point.
  5. Distinguish a mark from readiness. A learner scores (100,90,60,95,100) on practice, midterm, final, project and oral transfer. Use weights (10,15,25,40,10) percent. The separately scored essential valuation domain is 50%. Apply the proposed course readiness rule and write a specific next action.
  6. Interpret incomplete course evidence. Six of eight learners complete a ten-point exit task, with mean 8. Two outcomes are missing and can lie anywhere in [0,10]. The whole group’s entry-task mean was 6. Bound the full exit mean and the difference from 6. Write a short evaluation note; do not assume the entry and exit tasks are equivalent.
  7. Write a real transfer check. An instructor says a confidence survey is enough to establish valuation mastery. Replace it with a complete short task and full answer using these numbers: opening cash 70, reserve 20, one payment 50 now, certain receipt 60 in one year and discount rate 20%. Then change the payment to 51. Explain what the survey can still contribute.
  8. Communicate a narrowed exam. After removing unpracticed breadth items worth 15 from a 100-point exam, 85 possible points remain. A student earns 75. Compute the rescaled percentage, then write a complete notice explaining what it means and what cannot be inferred from comparison with the original exam.
Complete solutions and evaluation criteria

  1. “Given a supplied two-date cash-flow schedule and discount rate, compute NPV and reconcile it with the timing of payments. Given a project with a cash reserve and permitted financing terms, identify feasible choices and justify a conditional recommendation.” For the first, supply an unfamiliar cash timeline and require the calculation plus unit/date explanation. For the second, require a cash-account table and a short memo that separates value from liquidity, with a changed reserve during defense. The boundary is that this is a project-decision foundation, not mastery of all finance, a credential or evidence of real investment skill. Full credit requires observable actions, conditions, matched evidence and an honest scope statement; simply replacing “master” with “understand” is insufficient.

  2. One valid order is U,A,P,C,R,M. The proposed order places C before P even though valuation is needed for the project comparison, and M before R even though its recommendation must address scenarios. Feedback may return a draft memo to an already taught units exercise: distinguish that practice/revision process from a prerequisite edge asserting that units cannot be learned until the memo is complete. Keep the first-teaching dependency graph acyclic and schedule remedial revisits explicitly. Full credit requires every supplied edge respected, both violations located and a workable distinction between teaching dependency and revision.

  3. The original total is 11, exceeding the ceiling by 2. One revision keeps contact 3 and project preparation 2 while narrowing reading and practice to 2 each: (11,2,3,2,2,2) gives total, excess and the new allocation. Identify which reading sections and repetitive practice items are removed, while retaining an example and an independently attempted task for each assessed capability. Test the revised estimate with actual work and student feedback; a smaller number on a timetable is not evidence that unchanged assignments take less time. Full credit requires the arithmetic, a nonnegative exact budget and an explicit scope/learning check.

  4. A’s equity vector becomes (−80,43,55), so NPV=−80+430/11+5500/121=50/11. The financing contract has NPV 10−120/11=−10/11, exactly the decline from 60/11. A’s advantage over B is 50/11−350/121=200/121, still positive. Thus (50/11,-10/11,200/121). Immediate reserve feasibility is unchanged because the initial loan remains 10, but financing is no longer value-neutral at the stipulated discount rate. This is a conditional calculation, not a rule that financing never matters or that a higher loan rate should be ignored. Full credit: cash timing 2, equity value 2, financing decomposition 2, feasible comparison 2 and interpretation 2, on an illustrative ten-point rubric.

  5. The weighted mark is 10+13.5+15+38+10=86.5, exceeding 85. Valuation at 50 is below the essential 75 floor, so readiness is not established. Inspect the learner’s timeline and discounting steps, assign a focused correction explaining each cash-flow date and denominator, and administer a new independently solved valuation/feasibility task after practice. Do not relabel the learner ready merely because the project or oral marks compensate numerically. Full credit requires the correct weighted score, correct domain decision, targeted remediation and changed independent reassessment; the record should distinguish this internal criterion from any institution’s grade policy.

  6. The observed total is 48. Full exit mean is (48+x+y)/8, giving [6,8.5]; subtracting 6 gives [0,2.5]. The checked endpoints are (6,8.5,0,2.5). Endpoint values occur when both missing scores are 0 or both are 10. A note is: “The completers averaged 8, but the full-group exit mean is compatible with 6 through 8.5 under the stated score bounds. Entry and exit tasks may differ in difficulty, and missingness and other changes prevent interpreting this difference as an effect of the course redesign.” Do not convert the descriptive bound into a causal interval. Full credit requires weighted accounting, attainability, missingness disclosure and task-comparability/causal limits.

  7. Task: “For each payment, calculate NPV and immediate reserve feasibility, then state the decision under a positive-value requirement.” At cost 50, NPV=−50+60/1.2=0 and cash is 20: reserve feasible but not strictly positive value, so no strict value-based preference for purchase over doing nothing is established. At cost 51, NPV=−1 and cash 19: both the positive-value requirement and reserve fail. The result vector is (0,20,-1,19). A confidence response can identify perceived difficulty or a mismatch between confidence and demonstrated reasoning; it cannot replace the independent calculation. Full credit requires the complete prompt, boundary treatment, changed-case answer and a bounded role for self-report.

  8. The rescaled mark is 100×75/85=1500/17 percent, approximately 88.24%. A notice is: “Your score is 75 of the 85 possible points on the revised exam, or approximately 88.24%. The revision assesses the retained project-decision objectives and excludes the announced breadth modules. Its percentage is not an equated score on the former 100-point exam, and it does not demonstrate the removed capabilities. We will review the retained objective domains and any critical errors separately before recording readiness.” Full credit requires the correct denominator, exact or sensible rounding, complete scope/comparability explanation and preservation of domain-based evidence. A claim that the learner gained 13.24 points of knowledge would be unjustified.

Teach-back and delayed retention

Teach-back. Explain in two minutes why a coherent nine-week course can be better justified than an unchanged twelve-week syllabus squeezed into nine weeks. The answer must name the changed capability promise, the workload reduction, preserved practice/feedback and the non-equivalence of scores. “Less is more” without those decisions is not an explanation.

Delayed task. After a study interval, a planned ten-week course at 8 hours per week loses two weeks and retains the same weekly ceiling. State the lost time and give a defensible redesign rule. The answer is (80,64,16) for original time, available time and loss. Remove or narrow objectives with their dependent readings and assessment demands, or obtain an explicitly agreed change in capacity; do not hide 16 hours as unbudgeted homework. Name a retained capability and verify that its prerequisites, practice, feedback and unseen evidence remain. If the specimen answer has been studied, an evaluator must supply a new constraint and check the response independently.
