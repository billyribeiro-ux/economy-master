Specify the decision before the result can influence it

Capability. Plan an empirical study that separates design choices from choices made after inspecting outcomes. The prerequisite is data construction, measurement and ethics; also bring the question and identification contract. A technically correct estimator cannot rescue an unavailable comparison, a poorly measured outcome or a study too imprecise to resolve its economic question. This chapter supplies a prospective calculation and a complete hypothetical analysis plan, not an authorized intervention or an actual registry submission.

A research design connects the target to assignment, measurement and inference. A preanalysis plan specifies how the evidence will be analyzed before the relevant results are inspected. Preregistration records a study and its intended choices with a verifiable time and version. These can make later deviations visible. They do not prove identification, validate measurements, confer ethics approval or guarantee that a study can detect a meaningful effect.

The two worked artifacts extend the fictional early-refinancing offer from Chapter 5. No bank, access arrangement, variance estimate or policy effect here is real. The planning numbers are explicit assumptions to be challenged. For an actual study, a defensible literature and measurement audit, appropriate authority and review, and usable data would all be needed before launch.

1. Ask what the study could learn at a meaningful effect

A type I error rejects a true null hypothesis; a level α test bounds its rejection probability under the specified null model. Power is the probability of rejection at a specified alternative effect, under a specified data-generating process and test. It is not the probability the hypothesis is true after observing the data. The minimum detectable effect at a chosen power is a property of that design and test; it is not the smallest economically important effect, the smallest possible estimate or a guarantee that every effect above it will be detected.

Choose the economically important effect before solving for a convenient sample size. Record its units and whose decision it would change. Then investigate plausible dispersion, the independent assignment units, take-up, spillovers, missing outcomes and the planned hypothesis family. A stated 80% target is a design choice, not a universal quality certificate. Power for each outcome separately is different from the probability of detecting effects on all outcomes together.

The J-PAL quick guide to power calculations motivates early feasibility checks, explicit inputs and sensitivity to uncertain inputs and implementation. We use that bounded planning perspective. The normal model below is derived directly and uses invented values; it is not an estimate from that guide or a claim that its assumptions describe bank data.

Assignment and analysis units must agree. If whole banks receive an offer, adding thousands of loan observations does not create thousands of independently assigned banks. Repeated observations may improve measurement but can remain correlated within a bank. Clustered or network assignment, unequal allocation, covariate adjustment and different outcome distributions require their own variance and power calculations. Simply putting the number of transactions into an independent-bank formula can dramatically overstate precision.

Keep uncertainty about inputs visible. A pilot variance estimate is itself uncertain, and the effect used for planning is not a known future result. Explore plausible alternatives rather than quoting a single decimal as certainty. Do not compute “observed power” using the final estimated effect and treat it as new evidence that a nonrejection proves zero; the estimate and its uncertainty already describe the realized evidence. Prospective power is useful because it can still change the design.

2. Fix assignment, outcomes and the family of claims together

Document who is eligible before assignment, what each assigned alternative delivers, the unit of randomization, the allocation probabilities and the records needed to audit the draw. Uniformly choosing a fixed number of banks without replacement is different from independently flipping a coin for each bank. Either can be legitimate, but the planned estimator and inference should reflect the actual procedure. Do not repeat a draw because its baseline balance or anticipated result is unattractive unless a valid restricted-randomization procedure was specified beforehand.

Define intention-to-treat outcomes for every randomized unit, including non-takers and exits. Actual use of refinancing is a post-offer decision; switching to a take-up comparison changes the estimand and may introduce selection. A change in sample size caused by missing outcomes is also more than a loss of power: if observation depends on treatment or potential outcomes, it can change what the comparison identifies. Increasing the initial roster does not by itself fix selective missingness.

Multiplicity starts with the claims, not the software menu. If either of two outcomes can support a confirmatory headline, both belong to the declared family. In the worked design, test each null at α/2=0.025 to keep familywise false-rejection probability at most 0.05 when both individual tests have their stated validity. For any subset of true nulls, the probability of at least one false rejection is at most the sum of their rejection probabilities, which is at most two times0.025. This union-bound argument does not require independent outcomes. It cannot protect undisclosed extra tests or invalid individual p-values.

Exploratory outcomes, subgroups and alternative specifications can be valuable. Label them, report their construction and avoid describing them as prespecified confirmation. There is no honest escape from multiplicity by moving inconvenient confirmatory outcomes into an exploratory appendix after seeing them. A registration can expose such a move; it cannot prevent every selective claim.

Worked case 1. A prospective power calculation with a real decision boundary

Planning question. A fictional team is considering an offer to a fixed eligible roster of160 banks, with80 offers and80 controls. The two confirmatory outcomes are ninety-day net borrower advances, in millions of a fixed currency, and liquid assets at day90, also in millions. The first economically relevant offer effect is1 million; the second is2 million. These thresholds are invented judgments for this example, not thresholds estimated from policy benefits. Both hypotheses are two-sided, so an adverse effect is not made invisible by the test direction.

Explicit probability model. For each outcome j separately, assume independent bank observations Yij=μj+δjZi+εij, with εij normal with mean0 and known variance σj2, independent of the offer assignment. Outcomes within a bank may be correlated with each other. There are no missing outcomes or cross-bank spillovers. The effect δ is a constant mean shift in this planning model. Conditional on any fixed balanced assignment, the difference in sample means has normal distribution with meanδ and variance2σ²/n, where n is banks per arm. This follows by adding independent normal errors; each arm mean has varianceσ²/n and their errors are independent.

The known-variance assumption makes this an exact probability calculation within the model. It does not make σ known in a real project. The actual analysis plan below estimates variances and uses an explicitly asymptotic test; its finite-sample power is not certified by the exact benchmark. Nor is this a finite-roster randomization distribution: it is a prospective outcome model used to evaluate a candidate design.

Test and calculation. Let s=σ√(2/n), c=Φ−1(1−αj/2) and λ=|δ|/s, where Φ is the standard normal cumulative distribution function. Reject the zero-effect null when |δ̂|/s>c. Standardizing δ̂ gives the power

π(n,σ,δ,αj)=Φ(λ−c)+Φ(−λ−c).

The first term is the upper rejection tail under a positive alternative; the second is the lower tail. Symmetry makes the result depend on |δ|. At δ=0 the two tails sum to αj. This expression is exact under the stipulated normal law; evaluating Φ numerically introduces only calculation approximation. Keep both tails rather than calling a convenient one-tail sample-size shortcut exact.

For net lending, inputs (n,σ,δ,αj) are (80,2,1,0.025). The critical value is 2.241403; s is 0.316228 million and λ is 3.162278. Power is 0.821442, about82.1% for that effect and outcome. The second outcome's assumed σ=4 and effect2 give the same standardized effect and power; this is not a claim that either variance has been measured. The chance of detecting both effects is not82.1% merely because each has that power.

Take-up interpretation. The half-effect row could arise in a very specific planning story: a constant two-million effect of actual receipt, zero direct offer effect, no receipt in controls, and a drop in offer-induced receipt from one half to one quarter. Then the mean offer effect falls from1 to0.5 million. Those exclusion and constant-effect restrictions are additional assumptions, not consequences of random assignment. For this numerical power comparison, σ=2 is held fixed as a separate planning assumption; changed receipt could also change outcome dispersion. A more complex pattern requires another model. Analyze assignment under the planned intention-to-treat target; dropping non-takers does not restore power for that target.

Reproduce the arithmetic. This complete standard-library program computes the displayed table and integer boundaries. It was executed during chapter preparation. Copy it into a separate file and run Python3; it needs no package installation, data download or simulation seed. Printed values are rounded to six decimals, while the integer search uses unrounded power.

from math import sqrt, isfinite
from statistics import NormalDist

normal = NormalDist()

def power(n, sigma, effect, alpha=0.025):
    if type(n) is not int or n < 1:
        raise ValueError("n must be a positive integer per arm")
    if any(type(x) not in (int, float) or not isfinite(x)
           for x in (sigma, effect, alpha)):
        raise ValueError("finite numeric inputs required")
    if sigma <= 0 or not 0 < alpha < 1:
        raise ValueError("positive sigma and alpha in (0,1) required")
    c = normal.inv_cdf(1 - alpha / 2)
    shift = abs(effect) / (sigma * sqrt(2 / n))
    return normal.cdf(shift - c) + normal.cdf(-shift - c)

def minimum_n(sigma, effect, target=0.8):
    if type(target) not in (int, float) or not isfinite(target) or not 0 < target < 1:
        raise ValueError("target must be in (0,1)")
    for n in range(1, 10001):
        if power(n, sigma, effect) >= target:
            return n
    raise ValueError("target not reached through 10000 per arm")

if __name__ == "__main__":
    for n, sigma, effect in [(40,2,1), (76,2,1), (77,2,1),
                             (80,2,1), (80,3,1), (80,2,0.5)]:
        print(n, sigma, effect, f"{power(n,sigma,effect):.6f}")
    print("minimum per arm:", [minimum_n(s,e) for s,e in [(2,1),(3,1),(2,0.5)]])

Completed planning decision. Retain160 banks as a candidate only under a defensible baseline dispersion and effect assessment. Before allocation, audit the eligible independent-bank count, outcome construction, plausible variances and take-up. If evidence supports σ=3 for net lending, do not launch this design while promising80% power for a one-million effect: its benchmark power is44.7%. Discuss an achievable design or a narrower descriptive/pilot objective, explicitly changing the claim. None of the synthetic calculations alone establishes a real study's feasibility or authorization.

3. Preserve what was decided and when it was known

A plan should distinguish choices made before assignment, before outcome collection, before unblinding treatment labels and after inspecting effects. “Before analysis” is ambiguous when an analyst has already seen treatment-group plots. State access and knowledge directly. With existing data, disclose prior exposure to outcomes and separate genuinely prospective choices from exploratory ones. A time stamp cannot erase what the team already knew.

The J-PAL preanalysis-plan resource distinguishes the analysis document from trial registration and discusses recording prespecified versus other analyses. Its overview is not a universal mandatory template. The complete plan below is our original design, with assumptions and decisions selected for its fictional question.

Amendments are sometimes necessary. Preserve the original, date the new version, give the reason, state what data and labels had been accessed, and classify the effect on confirmatory claims. The AEA RCT Registry FAQ, inspected16 October2026, describes retained edit history and recommends clearly versioned uploaded plans with change notes. It also warns that readers need to compare uploaded document versions to locate their changes. These features support an audit trail; they do not themselves establish the validity of a design. No registry entry has been created for this chapter.

Planning-to-analysis connection. The power model used known σ and a constant normal shift. This plan replaces σ with sample estimates and relies on a large-sample approximation. Worked application with complete records. Consider a fictional completed-data branch in which all160 outcomes are recovered and no identification failure is found. Suppose the prespecified two tests produce raw p-values (0.018,0.041). Bonferroni-adjusted values are (0.036,0.082). The first null is rejected at family level0.05; the second is not. A report stating “both outcomes are significant because each raw p is below0.05” would violate the plan. Report both effect estimates and intervals as well; p-values alone do not describe magnitude or benefit. These p-values are stipulated to illustrate the decision rule, not calculated from hidden bank data.

For comparison, if two independent true-null p-values were exactly uniform and each were tested at0.05 without adjustment, the chance of one or more false rejections would be 0.0975, because1−(1−0.05)²=0.0975. Testing each at0.025 gives 0.049375 under that independence illustration. The general0.05 upper bound needs only valid individual tests, not independence. Neither illustration establishes actual error rates if the bank-level inference assumptions fail.

4. Make deviations informative rather than invisible

A useful plan allows justified corrections while preventing them from erasing the original question. Distinguish a deterministic unit conversion from a new outlier rule, an access failure from a negative finding, and a safety stop from an efficacy stopping design. Preserve the evidence behind each distinction. If an unforeseen event invalidates the primary analysis, a transparent narrower report is more informative than a mechanically completed table with an unsupported interpretation.

At reporting time, map every confirmatory output back to the version that specified it. Show deviations, nonexecuted analyses and exploratory work. Do not infer no effect from a nonsignificant test; examine the estimate and interval against economically relevant alternatives. Do not infer useful policy from a statistically detectable effect without costs, implementation and external-validity evidence. The next chapter examines how conclusions change under plausible alternatives to the assumptions.

Complete solutions and evaluation criteria

1. Marginal power concerns one specified test

The standard error is4√(2/80)=0.632456 million. The standardized shift2/0.6324555… equals the first outcome's1/0.3162277…, so the same c and two tails give 0.821442. Both effects have |δ|/σ=1/2. The probability both tests reject depends on their joint distribution. With only the two marginal powers, the union bound on failures supplies the lower bound 0.642884, not82.1%; this is a bound, not the joint probability. Independence would justify multiplying the marginal probabilities, but it is not assumed for outcomes in the same bank.

2. Preserve the decision boundary

The unrounded powers round to0.799771 at76 and0.805385 at77, so the first integer meeting the rule is77 per arm, or 154 banks. The160-bank capacity allows80 per arm, with six banks more than the minimum total. Rounding a value below the threshold before comparing it changes the rule. The narrow numerical margin also does not eliminate uncertainty about σ, implementation or the normal approximation.

3. Input uncertainty can change feasibility

Atσ=3, the80-per-arm power is0.447017 and the minimum is172 per arm, or 344 banks. Under an inviolable160-bank capacity, the stated80% requirement for a one-million effect cannot be met in this model. Do not launch under a promise that it can. Investigate a genuinely more precise measurement or defensible design and recalculate, or change the project to an explicitly limited pilot/descriptive purpose after discussing its value. A larger target effect chosen merely to get a favorable calculation does not answer the original economic question.

4. The family belongs to the claims

Independence is one possible joint structure, and two independent uniform null p-values give false-rejection probability1−0.95²=0.0975 at0.05 each. Thus an unspecified correlation claim cannot guarantee a0.05 family error rate. Bonferroni bounds the probability of any false rejection by the sum of valid individual levels, here0.025+0.025=0.05, for any dependence. The supplied pair gives adjusted values (0.05,0.052). With the declared p≤threshold convention, reject the first only. A rounded printed p-value at the boundary should not replace the unrounded value in an actual decision. The guarantee also requires that the declared family is the family used and that the individual p-values have their claimed null validity.

5. Offer effects and receipt effects are not interchangeable

Under the stated exclusions and constant receipt effect, offer effects are2×1/2=1 and2×1/4=0.5 million. At80 per arm withσ=2 and0.025 testing, their powers are (0.821442,0.254608). The original target averages assignment effects across the eligible roster, including non-takers. Conditioning on take-up selects a post-assignment group and can reflect demand or risk; it does not convert the same data into a better-powered estimate of the same intention-to-treat target. A receipt-effect analysis needs its own identification assumptions and inferential design.

6. A registry entry is not a sequential-testing rule

The day45 request depends on an inspected effect and violates the fixed ninety-day outcome window and no-efficacy-peeking rule. Continue the authorized planned collection if safe and feasible, disclose the unplanned look, and obtain an appropriate inferential review rather than pretending the registered fixed-horizon test automatically absorbs the deviation. If implementation was changed because of that look, the original guarantee cannot simply be asserted. A mandatory safety or authorization interruption can stop the study, but the plan then reports a truncated study without its original fixed-horizon confirmation claim. A blinded, documented unit conversion follows the already specified measurement rule and need not change the estimand or testing schedule. Credit all three distinctions and disclosure of what was known.

Answer guide

With80 per arm,σ=2, an offer effect1 and a two-outcome Bonferroni family, benchmark power is82.1%; withσ=3 it falls to44.7%, and with effect0.5 it falls to25.5%. The inputs encode dispersion and implementation assumptions that need evidence. Registration records choices and changes; it cannot establish those inputs, fix spillovers, guarantee the approximate test or authorize the intervention. A nonsignificant realized result must be interpreted through its effect estimate and interval, not declared zero because a plan once reported80% power.
