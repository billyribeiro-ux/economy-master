Capability and prerequisites

After this chapter, you can build an income statement, a balance sheet and a cash-flow statement from one adjusted ledger; explain the changes in equity; and reconcile operating cash by both direct and indirect methods. Read Transactions and double-entry accounting. Retain its debit-positive ledger convention: a debit increases a debit-normal account, while a credit increases a credit-normal account. Neither word is a synonym for cash received or paid.

The chapter's numerical company is fictional. All displayed amounts are thousands of currency units, kCU; 1 kCU = 1,000 CU. A CU has 100 minor units, so the downloadable program converts 1 kCU to exactly 100,000 integer minor units. The reporting entity is Harbor Instruments, a single trading corporation distinct from the preceding chapter's service company; the period is 1 January–31 March 2025, with opening balances at 31 December 2024. These are original instructional facts, not a historical company's reported results or a claim of compliance with a particular jurisdiction.

1. Three statements ask different questions about the same entity

A balance sheet, also called a statement of financial position, reports recognized asset, liability and equity stocks at a date. An income statement reports recognized revenues, gains, expenses and losses over an interval. A cash-flow statement reports cash receipts and payments over that same interval, grouped by activity. Comparing a year of profit with one day's cash balance without the opening balance confuses a flow with a stock.

An asset is a recognized economic resource controlled by the entity, not necessarily an object it can sell immediately. A liability is a recognized obligation: it may require cash, delivery of goods or another settlement. Equity is the residual of recorded assets after recorded liabilities. Book value is the amount recognized under the accounting measurements being used. Market value is a value in an actual or specified market transaction. Book equity is therefore not a promised liquidation payment or a quotation for the company's shares.

At each balance-sheet date: A = L + E.
With no other equity movements: E₁ = E₀ + owner contributions + profit − distributions.
With no other changes to cash: Cash₁ = Cash₀ + operating cash + investing cash + financing cash.

The equity bridge is essential even though the title emphasizes three statements. A complete reporting package can also include a statement of changes in equity, comprehensive income, comparative information and explanatory notes. The SEC's dated introductory guide distinguishes the principal statements and explains their connections. Here we explicitly supply the equity bridge and case notes rather than suggesting that three totals constitute a complete formal filing.

Retained earnings accumulates recognized profit less distributions and any separately identified adjustments. It is not a designated bank account. A firm can have positive retained earnings while cash is tied up in receivables or equipment. Owner contributions increase equity without being revenue; distributions reduce equity without being expenses. Borrowing increases cash and a liability without producing profit. Paying loan principal reduces that liability without creating an expense. Interest is a separate cost of financing under our case assumptions.

2. Establish the measurement and classification conventions first

Harbor Instruments recognizes revenue when the stipulated goods or services have been delivered and accepted. Credit sales are certain collectible; a collection delay does not change that maintained fact. Inventory purchases become an asset until the stated goods are sold, when their supplied carrying cost becomes cost of goods sold. Prepaid insurance is an asset until coverage is consumed. Wages and interest become expenses as incurred, whether paid yet or not. Customer cash received before performance creates a liability. These rules define the case; detailed contractual revenue judgments, inventory methods, impairments, leases and tax accounting belong to later chapters.

Equipment is carried at historical cost less accumulated depreciation. Depreciation expense allocates the stipulated equipment cost to the period; the amount is supplied, so no useful-life estimation is hidden in the exercise. Accumulated depreciation is a credit-normal contra asset deducted from equipment cost, not a liability or a reserve of cash. On disposal we remove the equipment's original cost and its accumulated depreciation; proceeds minus the remaining carrying amount are a gain or loss. Period depreciation amounts already allow for acquisition and disposal timing, so do not add an extra convention.

The case has no tax expense, foreign currency, cash equivalents, overdraft facility, restricted cash, other comprehensive income, revaluations, business combinations or unlisted transactions. Tax expense is set to zero for the model, not asserted to be zero under real tax law. All bank cash is available for payment. Transactions occur in the listed order. Dividends are declared and paid together at the final event, with no dividend payable at either reporting date.

For the cash statement, maintain this classification: receipts from customers and payments for inventory, wages, insurance, rent and interest are operating; cash acquisition and disposal of operating equipment are investing; cash share issues, loan proceeds, principal repayment and dividends are financing. A transaction without cash has no cash-flow amount. Asset-financed borrowing is disclosed separately. Current versus noncurrent presentation on the balance sheet does not choose the cash-flow category: a short-term loan is still financing in this convention, while buying inventory is operating.

Classification depends on the reporting framework and its applicable version. The IFRS Foundation's public IAS 7 overview explains the activity categories, direct and indirect methods, and exclusion of noncash investing/financing transactions from cash flows. Its overview also records amendments associated with IFRS 18. The April 2024 issuance announcement sets IFRS 18's effective date at annual periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027, with earlier application permitted. The April 2024 Effects Analysis, page 27, explains changes to the indirect starting subtotal and interest/dividend classification. Harbor Instruments' simplified presentation is an explicit teaching convention, not a representation that every IFRS or U.S. GAAP entity must use these subtotals and categories.

5. Derive the indirect operating bridge rather than memorizing signs

The indirect method starts from the specified profit measure and removes accrual timing differences and items whose cash belongs elsewhere. It must produce the same CFO as the direct method under the same classification. Let Δ mean closing minus opening, using normal positive balances, not raw credit-negative ledger entries.

Write R for revenue, AR for receivables, U for customer advances, I for inventory, AP for inventory trade payables, P for prepaid insurance, W for wages payable and J for interest payable. These symbols refer only to the explicitly described account contents. Assume no writeoffs, refunds, returns, acquired balances, revaluations or cross-account transfers. Then the account roll-forwards give:

Customer receipts = R − ΔAR + ΔU.
Inventory purchases = cost of goods sold + ΔI.
Supplier cash payments = purchases − ΔAP = cost of goods sold + ΔI − ΔAP.
Wage payments = wage expense − ΔW.
Insurance payments = insurance expense + ΔP.
Interest payments = interest expense − ΔJ.

For example, AR₁ = AR₀ + credit revenue − collections, so collections = credit revenue − ΔAR. Customer advances add cash before revenue and release revenue without current cash. Combining those accounts with cash sales gives the first equation. AP₁ = AP₀ + credit inventory purchases − creditor payments; adding cash purchases yields total supplier payments. The signs therefore follow from the specific account, not a universal slogan that every liability increase produces operating cash.

Let Dep be depreciation and G the equipment disposal gain, positive for a gain and negative for a loss. Since profit = R − cost of goods sold − wages − insurance − rent − interest − Dep + G in this case, subtracting all direct payments from receipts yields

CFO = profit + Dep − G − ΔAR − ΔI − ΔP + ΔAP + ΔW + ΔJ + ΔU.

The gain is removed because the complete equipment-sale cash receipt belongs in investing; subtracting it here does not assert that no cash was received. A disposal loss would be added back, because subtracting a negative G reverses the loss included in profit. Depreciation is added back only because it reduced the starting profit without being a current cash outflow. This arithmetic does not manufacture cash or make the use of equipment economically costless.

Define operating noncash net working capital for this convention as N = AR + I + P − AP − W − J − U. Then CFO = profit + Dep − G − ΔN. This is a precisely defined subtotal. It differs from balance-sheet working capital, current assets minus all current liabilities, because N excludes cash and borrowings. If interest paid is classified as financing, its payable also leaves this operating subtotal and the profit bridge must change consistently.

The simplified bridge fails if an account contains movements outside its assumed activity. Suppose a firm puts equipment creditors into the same AP account as inventory suppliers. Adding the whole AP change to CFO would treat an investing acquisition as an operating timing item. Separate the underlying transactions. A receivable writeoff, foreign-exchange movement or acquisition of another business can likewise change AR without a customer cash flow. Comparative balance sheets alone do not identify all cash receipts and payments.

The SEC Chief Accountant's December 2023 statement emphasizes classification and noncash disclosure as substantive issues, even when cash totals reconcile. It is a staff statement with its own stated legal limits, not a new accounting rule. The practical implication for this lab is direct: test the meaning of each posting and cash tag as well as the final sum.

6. Profit is compatible with a payment shortfall

Worked example 3 — The same sales, a different collection calendar

Change only event 7: collect 20 instead of 85 during the quarter. All sales remain delivered, collectible and recognized at the same amounts, and every other event is unchanged. Receivables end at 30 + 100 − 20 = 110, cash at 69 − 65 = 4. Profit stays 25, retained earnings 43 and total assets 286: cash has been replaced by a receivable, not by an expense.

Operating cash falls from 21 to −44. Investing cash stays −19 and financing cash 27, giving cash change −36 and closing cash 40 − 36 = 4. The indirect bridge agrees because ΔAR is now 80, larger by 65. The firm is profitable under the case measurements but operating activities consumed cash, and its cash buffer is small.

If collection instead falls to 10, the unfinanced ending calculation becomes cash −6. Under the explicit no-overdraft assumption, this is an infeasible payment plan, not a legitimate negative cash asset. It cannot be repaired by adding an unnamed liability to force balance. The firm must actually arrange funding, reduce a not-yet-declared distribution, reschedule a payment with the counterparty's agreement or otherwise change a specified event. Even a nonnegative closing cash balance does not prove every earlier payment was possible; the ordered cash ledger must also stay nonnegative.

A positive CFO is not by itself proof of strong economics: it can reflect delayed supplier payments, customer advances for future work or inventory reduction that cannot repeat indefinitely. A negative CFO can arise from expanding receivables and inventory, but the company still needs financing and eventual recovery. Assess the cause, collectibility, contractual dates and available funding rather than awarding a good/bad label from one sign.

Similarly, book equity 143 is not 143 available for dividends. It includes noncash assets and retained accounting results. A distribution must meet actual cash, contractual and applicable legal constraints. This chapter supplies the case's cash constraint; it does not determine a real corporation's lawful distribution capacity.

7. An audit trail that connects all three statements

Use this sequence to make the statements reviewable. Establish the reporting entity, currency, interval, opening balances and recognition policies; inspect the evidence for each transaction; post journals and adjustments; and prepare the profit calculation before closing temporary accounts. Then close profit and dividends to retained earnings, prepare the balance sheet and classify actual cash movements. Independently reconstruct the operating bridge from the account movements and investigate any difference.

Do not substitute a single equality for this whole process. A balanced trial balance can contain a duplicated transaction, a missing transaction or an entry to the wrong account. Classifying a loan receipt as customer cash leaves total cash unchanged but misstates operating performance. Crediting revenue instead of a receivable when a customer pays can keep both the accounting equation and the indirect cash reconciliation balanced while overstating profit. Exercise 7 makes that failure concrete.

Useful supporting schedules are the receivable/customer ledger, inventory quantities and carrying costs, prepaid coverage schedule, unpaid expense schedule, equipment cost and accumulated-depreciation roll-forward, debt maturities, share transactions and dividend authorization. The cash statement and closing bank balance need the same cash perimeter. In a richer case, separately explain exchange effects or other non-cash-flow changes rather than forcing them into CFO.

These are supplied sale values, not estimates justified merely by the balance sheet. The first comparison concerns payment timing without sales; the second concerns a different liquidation scenario. Both need information beyond recorded totals.

Teach-back and delayed check

Teach-back. Explain Harbor Instruments' profit 25, operating cash 21, total cash increase 29 and equity increase 37 to a colleague who thinks they must be identical. Use a stock–flow bridge and two specific transactions; explain why the noncash note 18 appears in the asset and debt schedules but not as a cash receipt or payment.

Teach-back guide

Profit measures recognized performance, while operating cash adjusts timing and removes depreciation/gain effects. Investing and financing then convert CFO 21 into total cash change 29. Equity rises by contribution 20 + profit 25 − dividend 8 = 37. The receivable collection and share issue distinguish cash from revenue; depreciation distinguishes expense from a current payment. Equipment for note 18 exchanges an asset and liability without bank cash. A complete explanation also states the case's currency, reporting period and interest/dividend classification.

Delayed check, after one week. Return to Harbor Instruments' baseline and collect an additional 7 of existing receivables immediately before the dividend. Nothing else changes. Find closing cash, receivables, profit, CFO, total assets and retained earnings. Then explain the effects of reclassifying 5 of borrowings from noncurrent to current without repayment.

Delayed-check answer

Cash becomes 76 and receivables 38; profit stays 25 and CFO rises to 28. Total cash change rises to 36, while assets remain 286 and retained earnings 43. The indirect method agrees because receivable growth falls by 7. Debt reclassification changes current debt 21 → 26 and noncurrent debt 72 → 67; total liabilities, profit, equity, cash and every cash-flow amount remain unchanged. A maturity presentation change is not principal repayment.
