Capability and prerequisites

You can derive a fixed-income position's first and second rate sensitivities, build a funded asset–liability hedge, and identify the rate movements and cash events that the hedge does not cover. Begin with bond cash flows and yield curves: prices are dated cash flows times discount factors, the dirty price is the settlement payment, and a bond's yield to maturity is a cash-flow-specific quotation. Refresh derivatives and Taylor approximation, linear systems and portfolio exposures. Every additional result used below is derived here. No stochastic calculus is assumed.

A reserve committee is told that its assets and liabilities both have duration three. That statement leaves several questions unanswered. Do their present values match? Is duration measured against a nominal yield, an annual effective rate or a particular spot-curve movement? Can early proceeds actually be reinvested, and can later assets be sold before the liability is due? Who funds trading costs? The useful output is a set of signed exposures and a feasible dated cash plan, together with a precise statement of the disturbances it withstands.

All numerical contracts are original and synthetic, in currency units (CU). Unless a case explicitly changes the contract, cash flows are fixed, default-free, in one currency, with no options, taxes or costs; valuation uses a common settlement date. A basis point is 0.0001 in the stated decimal annual rate coordinate. A scenario is a conditional calculation, not a rate forecast or an assigned probability.

1. Derive the sensitivity before naming it

At settlement, a claim has nonnegative future payments Ci at positive times ti, measured in years, with at least one positive payment. Let y be its nominal annual yield compounded m times per year. Under this explicitly chosen quotation convention, write q=1+y/m>0 and

P(y)=ΣiCiq−mtᵢ,   pi=Ciq−mtᵢ/P(y).
Σpi=1,   pi≥0.

P is the dirty price. The pi are present-value shares, not physical probabilities of receiving the payments: the payments are already stipulated. Coupon-date periodic bonds have mti integer. A fractional-time power can also be a defined quotation convention, but it must not replace the actual settlement/day-count contract silently. For m=1, y is an annual effective yield. The domain y>−m permits negative yields; it excludes nonpositive periodic gross factors.

Differentiate with respect to the annual quotation y, keeping the settlement date and each Ci, ti fixed. The factor m in the exponent cancels the derivative 1/m of q:

P′(y)=−ΣitiCiq−mtᵢ−1.
P″(y)=Σiti(ti+1/m)Ciq−mtᵢ−2.

Define Macaulay duration DM=Σpiti, in years. Define modified duration Dmod=−P′/P, the proportional exposure to this annual yield coordinate. The derivatives establish the conversion, rather than requiring a memorized convention:

Dmod=DM/q.
C=P″/P=Σpiti(ti+1/m)/q².

Here C denotes convexity; the payment Ci always carries a subscript. This convention includes P″/P but does not include the factor one-half used in a Taylor expansion. Positive future cash flows imply P′<0 and P″>0. Macaulay duration lies between the earliest and latest positive payment dates. For a zero-coupon claim paid at T, DM=T, Dmod=T/q and C=T(T+1/m)/q². Modified duration is convention-dependent even when the dated claim is unchanged.

A useful decomposition follows by adding and subtracting DM² inside the second moment:

C=[VarPV(t)+DM²+DM/m]/q²,
VarPV(t)=Σpi(ti−DM)².

At a common yield and frequency, two positive cash-flow streams with the same Macaulay duration have different convexities exactly because their present-value timing dispersions differ. This is a statement about a specified price function, not proof that the more dispersed investment is preferable after curve changes, costs or liabilities. The inspected MIT fixed-income notes provide background for duration terminology and dollar-exposure matching; the signs and convention conversions used here follow from the displayed derivatives.

Dirty price, clean price and rate units

If accrued interest AI is fixed while the yield is perturbed at the same settlement date, Pclean=P−AI and Pclean′=P′. The dollar sensitivity is unchanged, but dividing by clean price gives −P′/(P−AI), a different percentage sensitivity. A funded position costs the dirty amount. Multiplying a dirty-price duration by clean price understates its dollar exposure when AI>0. If time passes, accrual, remaining maturities and coupon entitlement can change; that is a different derivative and a different cash ledger.

DM and Dmod are conventionally expressed in years; convexity uses squared time units consistent with the annual rate coordinate. Operationally, −P′ is CU per unit change in the decimal annual quote and P″ is CU per squared unit change. A move from 5% to 6% is Δy=0.01, not 1. If a vendor differentiates with respect to the per-period yield y/m, its first derivative is m times our annual-quote derivative and its second is m² times ours. Convert the coordinate before comparing numbers.

2. Dollar exposures, approximation and a remainder

Define dollar duration K=−P′=PDmod and dollar convexity G=P″=PC. We report DV01=10−4K as the positive approximate loss for a one-basis-point upward move when K>0; the signed predicted price change is −DV01. Some reporting systems use other signs, so the label is part of the calculation. Taylor's theorem gives

P(y+h)−P(y)=−Kh+(1/2)Gh²+R3.
ΔP/P≈−Dmodh+(1/2)Ch².

The first-order tangent is below the true price on either side of y because P is convex. A second-order approximation is more informative locally, but it is still an approximation. Differentiating once more yields

P‴(y)=−Σti(ti+1/m)(ti+2/m)Ciq−mtᵢ−3.

Suppose the entire segment from y to y+h remains above −m. Let M bound |P‴| on that segment. Then |R3|≤M|h|³/6. For these nonnegative payments, evaluate the displayed absolute sum at the segment's smallest q to obtain such a bound. This proves an error statement under explicit assumptions; describing a shock as “small” does not by itself specify an acceptable currency error. P‴<0 also means that the quadratic estimate is above the exact price for h>0 and below it for h<0. The statement concerns the quadratic estimate, not the first-order tangent.

A note pays 4 CU at years 1, 2 and 3 and 104 at year 4. Settlement is a coupon date immediately after that date's payment; AI=0. Add exposures before taking ratios

With fixed signed holdings nj, portfolio value V=ΣnjPj and sensitivities KV=ΣnjKj, GV=ΣnjGj. If V>0, DV=KV/V is the value-weighted average of individual modified durations. Negative positions create signed weights; a zero-value spread has no useful percentage duration but can have substantial dollar exposure. Holdings and financing must still be executable.

Aggregation requires one common perturbation. Moving each bond's own yield by the same ε defines one family even if their initial yields differ. It is generally different from moving every point of a nonflat spot curve by ε. A dollar-duration sum answers the question posed by its parameter, not every possible question called “rates up.” For asset value A and liability value L, the surplus S=A−L has KS=KA−KL. First-order surplus protection requires KA=KL, not equality of normalized durations when A≠L.

3. A curve has several directions

Let z(t) be a continuously compounded zero rate, so a payment at t has price C exp[−tz(t)]. Choose key maturities τ1<⋯<τK and define a particular interpolation rule. Here, between adjacent keys, z(t) is linear in the two neighboring node rates. There is no extrapolation in the lab: every cash date must lie within the keys. Write

z(t)=ΣkBk(t)zk.
For τj≤t≤τj+1:
Bj(t)=(τj+1−t)/(τj+1−τj),
Bj+1(t)=(t−τj)/(τj+1−τj); other Bk(t)=0.

The basis values are nonnegative and sum to one. At an exact key, that key's basis value is one. A one-basis-point change in node k changes every intervening rate according to Bk; it does not move just one isolated payment date. This bump definition matters as much as the number of keys. Interpolating discount factors, par yields or log discount factors would define other directions.

For the chosen continuous-zero coordinates, differentiating the actual price produces a vector and a Hessian:

P(z)=ΣiCiexp[−tiΣkBk(ti)zk].
Kk=−∂P/∂zk=ΣitiPViBk(ti).
Hkℓ=∂²P/(∂zk∂zℓ)=Σiti²PViBk(ti)Bℓ(ti).

Kk/P is the key-rate duration under this exact convention; 0.0001Kk is its dollar basis-point exposure. For a vector δ of node changes, ΔP≈−Kᵀδ+δᵀHδ/2. Mixed terms are real: an off-key payment depends jointly on its neighboring nodes. With nonnegative payments, uᵀHu=Σti²PVi[ΣukBk(ti)]²≥0. A surplus Hessian subtracts the liability Hessian and need not be positive semidefinite.

Because the basis sums to one, ΣKk=ΣtiPVi is dollar sensitivity to a parallel shift of the continuous zero curve; ΣkℓHkℓ=Σti²PVi. These sums are not automatically an annual-YTM duration or convexity. Under a flat curve z=ln(1+y), the chain rule gives Ky=Kz/(1+y) and Gy=(Gz+Kz)/(1+y)². Even at an identical initial price, a +100-bp continuous-zero move is a different scenario from +100 bp in annual effective yield.

Matching ΣKS,k=0 removes the parallel component but leaves other directions. Matching every KS,k=0 removes first-order risk only in the span of the declared bumps. It does not remove higher-order terms, cash-flow changes, spreads, interpolation error or movements outside that span. The CFA Institute's inspected key-rate summary motivates distinguishing maturity-segment exposure from a single duration; this chapter supplies its own basis, derivatives and numerical hedges.

4. What a local immunization theorem actually proves

Let A(y) and L(y) be twice continuously differentiable values under the same scalar rate family near y0. Hold contracts, settlement and asset quantities fixed while perturbing y. Suppose

S(y0)=A(y0)−L(y0)=0,
S′(y0)=0,
S″(y0)>0.

Local result. There is a neighborhood of y0 in which S(y)≥0, with strict inequality away from y0. To prove it, continuity of S″ implies S″ remains positive on some open interval around y0. Taylor's theorem to second order gives S(y0+h)=S″(ξ)h²/2 for a ξ between the two rates. Both signs of sufficiently small nonzero h therefore yield positive surplus. The theorem supplies a local neighborhood, not its operational width; an explicit derivative bound or exact repricing is needed to establish a particular stress range.

For a common annual yield and positive equal initial values, the conditions become equal dollar duration and strictly greater asset dollar convexity. Since A=L at that point, they can also be written as equal normalized modified duration and greater normalized convexity. If both use the same compounding factor, equality of Macaulay durations is equivalent. These shortcuts fail when value bases or perturbation coordinates differ. If initial surplus is already positive, continuity can preserve solvency locally even without these equalities; duration matching is then a separate attempt to stabilize that surplus. If S″=0, the second derivative alone says nothing about the sign of higher-order terms.

The primary Redington paper develops a common-rate asset–liability argument and explicitly discusses practical complications, including nonuniform rates and changing cash flows. Our finite theorem uses a strict curvature condition and states its neighborhood limitation. Equal duration is not an assurance that every liability can be paid along every future rate path.

Worked example 2 — A funded hedge and its horizon interpretation

A reserve owes exactly 125 CU at year H=3. At date 0 the annual curve is flat at 5%, and it may buy default-free zero-coupon claims maturing at years 1 and 5. All trading is divisible and costless. Its initial cash budget equals the liability PV, V=125/1.05³=107.97969982. No additional contribution is available. Let the two asset PVs be V1 and V5. The budget and duration equations are

V1+V5=V,   V1+5V5=3V.
V1=V5=V/2.
F1=(V/2)1.05=56.68934240,
F5=(V/2)1.05⁵=68.90625000.

The F values are purchased face amounts, not initial expenditures. Their dirty purchase prices add to the actual budget. Both asset and liability modified duration are 3/1.05=2.85714286. Asset convexity is [(1×2+5×6)/2]/1.05²=14.51247166; liability convexity is 3×4/1.05²=10.88435374. Surplus curvature is their difference multiplied by V, which is strictly positive. This proves the local valuation result.

For a separate horizon claim, suppose the rate shifts immediately to a new flat annual y>−1 and stays there. At year 1, reinvest the short zero's entire receipt until year 3 at that same known annual rate. At year 3, sell the long zero at the price implied by its remaining two years, with no fees or market closure. These are additional trading and reinvestment assumptions. Writing q=1+y and q0=1.05, horizon wealth is

W3(q)=F1q²+F5/q²
= (125/2)[(q/q0)²+(q0/q)²].

For x=(q/q0)²>0, x+x−1−2=(x−1)²/x≥0. Thus this particular barbell has W3≥125 for every constant post-shift rate in the domain, a stronger result than the general local theorem. At 4% the wealth is 125.02289430; at 6% it is 125.02246232. The asymmetry comes from compounding. A(y)−L(y)=[W3(y)−125]/(1+y)³ links the valuation and horizon ledgers under exactly these assumptions.

The general first-order horizon argument is also instructive. If every earlier payment is reinvested to H and every later payment is valued at H using one constant q, then WH(y)=qHA(y). Hence WH′/WH=H/q−Dmod,A=(H−DM,A)/q. Matching Macaulay duration to H makes horizon wealth locally stationary. It does not license using unobserved future reinvestment rates as if already contracted.

5. Parallel matching leaves a visible vector of risks

Worked example 3 — A curve twist breaks the scalar match

Keep the same purchased faces and liability from example 2. Define continuous-zero keys at years (1,3,5), initially all ln(1.05). The zeros and the liability fall exactly on keys. In units of the original V, asset dollar key durations are (0.5V,0,2.5V); the liability vector is (0,3V,0). Thus the surplus vector is

KS=V(0.5,−3,2.5)
≈(53.98984991,−323.93909945,269.94924954).

Its entries sum to zero, confirming first-order neutrality to a common continuous-zero move. Now lower the one-year zero rate by 100 bp, leave the three-year rate fixed, and raise the five-year rate by 100 bp. The linear surplus prediction is −KSᵀδ=−0.02V=−2.15959400. Exact same-date repricing gives

Stwist=V[(1/2)e0.01+(1/2)e−0.05−1]
=−2.09050904 CU.

The liability remains worth V while the assets fall to 105.88919078. Including the Hessian gives −2.08940719, a close local estimate whose remaining error is still measurable. This is a loss under a specified nonparallel valuation shock; no probability of that shock has been estimated.

The coupon note from example 1 also illustrates the interpolation choice. At year 2, the (1,3,5) basis is (1/2,1/2,0); at year 4 it is (0,1/2,1/2). A key bump therefore changes more than payments at an exact key and produces nonzero mixed Hessian entries. If the reserve can buy only its one- and five-year zeros, no rearrangement of those two faces can create the liability's positive middle-key exposure. An exact three-key first-order match is infeasible with that asset menu. Buying the actual three-year zero for face 125, if offered under the same rights, would instead match the liability cash flow itself.

A larger asset timing dispersion produced additional parallel convexity in example 2 while exposing distant curve segments in example 3. Those statements are compatible. Evaluate the hedge against the actual perturbation set and the liability, rather than maximizing a single convexity statistic without a mandate.

6. Limits are mathematical and operational

Worked example 4 — Positive local curvature does not cover a large move

Consider separate fixed cash-flow portfolios. Assets pay 120 at year 1 and 320 at year 3. Liabilities require 340 at year 2 and 100 at year 4. The baseline annual yield is zero. Both PVs are 440; both first derivatives are −1,080. Asset second derivative is 120×2+320×12=4,080; liability second derivative is 340×6+100×20=4,040. Thus S=0, S′=0 and S″=40>0 at the baseline.

Let d=1/(1+y). Direct valuation factors the surplus exactly:

S(y)=120d−340d²+320d³−100d⁴
=100d(d−1)²(1.2−d).

For a sufficiently small move from y=0, d remains below 1.2 and S is nonnegative. But at y=−20%, d=1.25, assets are worth 775 and liabilities 775.390625, so S=−0.390625=−25/64. The failure region is −1<y<−1/6. All contractual payments remain positive; only the rate parameter moved beyond the local neighborhood. The extreme negative rate is a synthetic mathematical stress, not an observed quote. As in Chapter 1, negative rates are within the defined price domain; no costless physical-currency storage or other unprovided arbitrage facility is assumed.

This counterexample concerns date-0 valuation. It does not assert that the asset proceeds can meet every earlier liability without a separate funding plan. A positive local PV surplus is not a substitute for checking the dates of payments.

A later path, a changed payment and a fee

Return to example 2's original faces. Suppose there was no immediate rate shift. When the short claim pays at year 1, the actually available two-year reinvestment rate is zero; at year 3 the long zero's remaining two-year yield is 10%. No earlier contract locked either quote. Horizon wealth is now

56.68934240×1²+68.90625/1.10²=113.63665645.
Funding gap=125−113.63665645=11.36334355 CU.

The algebraic identity for a single constant q no longer applies: two dated rates entered different cash operations. Rebalancing might change the outcome, but its positions, information, prices and funding must be specified before claiming a repair. Likewise, if the long zero's contractual payment is changed to 90% of its promised face while the original 5% rate remains, horizon wealth becomes 118.75, leaving 6.25 unpaid. Original fixed-cash-flow derivatives do not insure a changed claim.

Finally introduce an entry fee of 0.2% of purchased security value, paid from the original cash budget V. Buying the full original hedge requires additional initial cash 0.002V=0.21595940. Without that contribution, proportional holdings must be scaled by 1/1.002 so that security value plus fee equals V. At the original constant rate they deliver 125/1.002=124.75049900, leaving 0.24950100 unpaid. Their normalized durations remain unchanged. The missing protection comes from an unfinanced cost and insufficient purchased value, not a mistake in the derivative formula.

Other failures have the same logical form: different discounting or credit-spread movements for assets and liabilities, call or prepayment responses, basis changes, stale inputs, binding collateral rules, and trading after the valuation snapshot. Identify which price function, cash contract or feasible-action assumption changes. More frequent rebalancing can alter exposures; it also creates costs and cash demands and cannot be assumed perfect.

7. Model-based bumps and controlled interpretation

For a fully specified repricer V(θ), symmetric finite differences estimate its local sensitivity:

Deff,b=[V(θ−b)−V(θ+b)]/[2bV(θ)],
Ceff,b=[V(θ−b)−2V(θ)+V(θ+b)]/[b²V(θ)].

The parameter θ, bump direction, held-fixed inputs and treatment of changed cash flows are part of the definition. In the fixed-cash-flow yield model these estimates converge to the analytic modified duration and convexity as b tends to zero, subject to numerical precision. A security with contractual rate-dependent payments requires an appropriate repricer; merely inserting its promised payments into a fixed-payment formula does not account for that response. If a value function has a kink, a smooth Taylor expansion across it may not be justified. These statements use ordinary calculus, not an unintroduced option-pricing model.

A practical review records the settlement price and accrual basis, rate units, cash-flow ownership, the curve and interpolation, signed dollar and key exposures, exact scenario repricing, approximation error, and dated liquidity. For a model comparison, change one assumption at a time and retain a case where the models give different answers. A historical hedge-profit regression would also need an information cutoff, actual holdings and costs, and a separate evaluation period; positive fitted performance does not establish protection against untested scenarios.

Reproducible duration and immunization laboratory

The original offline package consists of inputs, standard-library analysis, complete reference output, a four-file hash manifest and a reproduction and interpretation guide. Download all five into one directory. Run python3 v10-c02-analysis.py to print strict JSON, or use --input edited.json --output result.json. Importing the script and calling run(data) are quiet.

The lab computes analytic and finite-difference yield sensitivities, exact repricing and a third-derivative remainder bound; solves the two-zero funded hedge; closes its constant-rate and altered-path horizon ledgers; builds the stated key-rate gradient and Hessian; and checks the local-only counterexample. It preserves complete outputs, including small floating-point residuals rather than rounding a near-zero budget into a false exact claim. The checker independently reconstructs exact baseline cash arithmetic, derivatives, exercises and interventions.

Try doubling the liability, changing a key bump, increasing the entry fee, or changing the later reinvestment rate. Explain whether the experiment changes scale, valuation risk, funding or the fixed-cash-flow contract. Negative or zero periodic gross factors, negative contractual payments in this positive-payment lab, an unbracketed liability, out-of-range cash dates, duplicate keys or scenarios, and altered contract labels are rejected. It is a bounded teaching implementation; the README states the supported edits and numerical limits. It neither estimates rate probabilities nor simulates future market liquidity.

Thus H=P[[1,1],[1,1]], a positive semidefinite rank-one matrix. Opposite equal node bumps leave z(2) exactly unchanged, so exact price remains P; both the linear and quadratic changes vanish. This follows from the chosen linear basis and the single payment date. Actual intermediate rates or a different curve interpolation can behave differently; zero modeled exposure is not evidence that the modeling choice is correct.
  7. 7. Equal PV means asset PV weights sum to one. With nonnegative weights, asset Macaulay duration is between two and five, and cannot equal eight. Formally w2+w5=1 and 2w2+5w5=8 yield w2=−1 and w5=2. That requires a short two-year position and financing/settlement rights excluded by the mandate; an algebraic solution does not authorize it. More generally, a convex combination of maturities all strictly below the liability date is also strictly below it. A new eligible maturity, a changed liability or authorized signed financing would be a changed problem.
  8. 8. Both PVs are 400. First derivatives are −(300×2+100×4)=−1,000 and −(100×1+300×3)=−1,000. Second derivatives are 300×6+100×20=3,800 and 100×2+300×12=3,800. The surplus is 300d²+100d⁴−100d−300d³=100d(d−1)³. For y>0, 0<d<1 and S<0; for −1<y<0, d>1 and S>0. Thus equal value, duration and convexity fail to provide two-sided local nonnegative surplus. The theorem's strictly positive S″ cannot simply be replaced by S″=0. One must inspect higher-order terms or establish an exact identity such as cash-flow matching.
  9. 9. A costs the original budget only if all faces are scaled by 1/1.01. Horizon wealth is 125/1.01=12,500/101≈123.76237624, so the gap is 125/101≈1.23762376. Funding the full original hedge requires extra initial cash 0.01V≈1.07979700, paid as the fee; it is not investment profit. In B, at the unchanged 5% rate each original leg contributes 62.5 at the horizon. The reduced long payment contributes 0.8×62.5=50, giving total wealth 112.5 and gap 12.5. These are independent experiments; do not deduct both losses from one unexplained ledger. A rescales value while leaving normalized timing unchanged; B changes the contractual cash flow on which the original hedge was based.

  10. 10. A defensible design states the liability payments, objective and loss metric first; fixes settlement and dirty-price cash budgets; identifies which rate coordinates and curve basis are bumped; and records any separate spread, credit or option assumptions. Preserve the original key grid and decision cutoff before opening test outcomes. Compare dollar and key-rate exposures, exact repricing and approximation errors across parallel, twist and curvature scenarios, with a documented rule for a call-capable repricer rather than unchanged promised cash flows. Record beginning holdings, coupon receipts, actual available sale/reinvestment quotes, collateral, fees and authorized trades at each date; test intermediate funding as well as terminal surplus. Compare the proposed hedge with feasible cash-flow matching or a declared alternative, accounting for their different costs and opportunity sets. Use holdout information for evaluation rather than selecting a favorable new grid, and report uncertainty under a stated data/dependence model if making empirical claims.

Either is a determinate failure of the report's universal language. Neither estimates how often markets create such an outcome. Full credit requires an explicit valuation coordinate, an actually funded strategy, a predeclared information boundary, a meaningful comparison and evidence that could reject the claim. Equivalent coherent protocols are acceptable; unsupported certainty, retrospective grid selection or assumed free rebalancing is not.

Teach-back and delayed retention

Teach-back. Explain in two minutes why two portfolios can have the same reported duration but different funding and curve risks. Include one budget equation, one rate coordinate and one assumption whose failure matters.

Teach-back benchmark

Start from S=A−L and KS=ADA−LDL. Equal normalized durations cancel the first derivative only when the relevant values and coordinates also align. A scalar match against a common annual yield does not imply KS,k=0 at every continuous-zero key. In the 125-CU liability case the short and long zeros cost the available PV budget, but a twist loses value and a later reinvestment/sale path can leave the liability unpaid. The horizon claim requires actually reinvesting early receipts and selling the long zero under the stated rates and trading rights. Fees need funding even if the portfolio's duration number does not change. A complete explanation separates local sensitivity, a conditional future cash plan and empirical evidence.

Retention. Without the chapter open: (a) derive the modified duration and convexity of a two-year zero at annual yield 4%; (b) a liability value 80 has modified duration three, while assets are worth 100—what asset modified duration removes first-order surplus risk? (c) under a continuous-zero basis that sums to one, a surplus key vector is (20,−50,30); calculate the linear surplus change for node moves (0.01,0,−0.01), and decide whether zero parallel exposure proves protection; (d) state the strict local immunization conditions and one missing cash-flow condition they cannot supply.

Retention answers

(a) Dmod=2/1.04=25/13≈1.92307692 and C=6/1.04²=3,750/676≈5.54733728; Macaulay duration remains two years. (b) 100DA=80×3 implies DA=2.4. (c) The sum is zero, but −[20(0.01)+30(−0.01)]=+0.10 CU for this twist; its opposite produces a first-order loss. Parallel neutrality does not control each node. (d) For the same C² scalar valuation family with fixed holdings and contracts, require S=0, S′=0 and S″>0 at the base point. Continuity then proves nonnegative surplus in some neighborhood. It does not ensure that an early bill can be paid before later asset receipts, that a future reinvestment quote is locked, or that rebalancing costs have been funded.

Sources and continuation

The MIT fixed-income recitation is used for duration and exposure terminology; the chapter independently derives convexity rather than adopting the source's apparent sign inconsistency. Redington's original paper, sections 3–4, supplies the historical common-rate immunization framework and its practical qualifications. The CFA Institute's 2017 key-rate summary distinguishes segment sensitivities from a single duration. These are narrowly inspected method sources, not certifications of the original examples or claims about current market conventions.

The successor term-structure models and rate risk adds explicit dynamics and pricing probabilities. It must show which future prices follow from a model and what today's curve leaves unidentified. This chapter deliberately stops before that additional assumption: a sensitivity is a derivative of a declared price function, not a probability law for tomorrow's rates.
