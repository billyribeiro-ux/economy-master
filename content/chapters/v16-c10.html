Write an argument whose claims a reader can trace

Capability. Organize a scholarly paper around its question, contribution, evidence and limitations. The prerequisite is robustness, sensitivity and external validity. Bring the distinction between changing an estimand and checking an estimate, together with the measurement contract from data construction. A paper should let a reader identify what was learned and reconstruct why. A polished narrative cannot repair an undefined population, an unsupported counterfactual or an unreported change in the analysis.

The two worked artifacts below are original teaching constructions. The first is a complete compact paper specimen with invented bank records; the second revises an overclaimed account and then propagates a source correction. Neither is a real submission, a completed empirical investigation or a claim of research novelty. Their small size makes the connection between every number and sentence visible. A real paper would require substantially more evidence and a comparison with the actual closest literature.

An abstract is a concise statement of the question, approach, central result and its scope. An introduction explains why the result changes the reader's understanding relative to a specified starting point. The methods make the operations and inferential assumptions inspectable. The results describe what those operations establish. An appendix supplies supporting material without hiding information necessary to interpret the central claim. These are functions, not a mandatory sequence of identical headings for every journal or theoretical paper.

1. Build the claim before polishing the prose

Write a claim ledger before a flowing draft. For each intended conclusion, record its target, evidence location, assumption and limitation. “Short funding reduces lending” requires a counterfactual argument; “the high-share group has lower mean growth in these records” requires a definition of the group, mean, records and period. The first cannot replace the second simply because it sounds more interesting. Keep a failed or inconclusive result visible when it changes the answer to the question.

Choose the main table or proposition and explain it in ordinary language. Then ask what the reader must know to interpret it: the observation unit, outcome, comparison, scale and uncertainty. Introduce those objects before their symbols. A reader should not need to reverse-engineer a column label such as “baseline specification.” In theory, state the result's domain and why it matters before giving a proof whose notation depends on that domain. In empirical work, distinguish the descriptive calculation from the design that might justify causation.

John Cochrane's June 2005 writing guide offers useful advice on making the central contribution concrete, explaining results early, documenting calculations and making tables interpretable. We use those bounded writing ideas, not its page limits or journal-specific conventions as current rules. The examples and revision procedure here are original. Follow the target venue's actual requirements when preparing a real submission; a historical guide cannot establish its present word limits.

Draft the abstract after the result and its boundaries are clear. Include the substantive finding, not a promise that results will be discussed. Use a numerical scale when it helps, and say whether a contrast is a percentage change or a difference in percentage points. Avoid an adjective such as “significant” unless the intended meaning and evidence are explicit. A result can be economically relevant without a p-value, but an exact arithmetic difference is not automatically evidence about a wider population.

An introduction should connect four things: the unresolved question, the concrete answer, the basis for that answer, and the contribution relative to relevant prior work. The order may vary. Explain a comparison with the closest papers accurately; do not claim that every previous author ignored an issue because your search was incomplete. If the document is an exposition, replication or synthetic demonstration, identify that genre directly. Adding a new dataset or changing notation does not by itself establish an important contribution.

2. A complete paper specimen

Worked case 1. Whose loan growth? Weighting in a synthetic funding comparison

Document identity. Original teaching note, version A, 16 October 2026. All four banks and values are invented. No outside data, actual research participants, empirical estimates or confidential records enter this specimen.

Abstract

Does a comparison of bank loan growth depend on the unit receiving equal weight? This synthetic note compares four banks over two dates, grouping them by initial short-term funding share. Mean bank growth is −5% in the high-share group and 5% in the low-share group, a −10-percentage-point contrast. Growth of group loan totals instead differs by about +2.27 percentage points. The reversal occurs because the growing high-share bank is much larger initially than its shrinking peer. The records therefore illustrate why weighting defines the reported comparison. They provide no causal effect of funding structure, sampling inference or finding about an actual banking system.

Introduction and contribution

A statement that one group of banks “grew faster” can refer to the average bank or to the stock of credit represented by the group. Those questions differ when initial loan sizes differ. This note constructs a fully disclosed example in which the direction of a funding-group comparison reverses between those two targets. The distinction matters for interpretation: a statement about a typical bank's proportional change cannot silently become a statement about the growth of total credit.

The contribution of this document is pedagogical: it supplies the inputs, two explicit targets and an exact decomposition linking them. It does not claim that weighted means are a new result or that a real funding mechanism has been discovered. The relevant conceptual starting point is that growth of a sum weights individual growth by initial amounts, whereas an equally weighted mean gives every bank the same influence. The specimen demonstrates the consequence for a particular headline. It is not positioned as an original empirical contribution against a literature it has not surveyed.

Data and methods

The population is the fixed set H1,H2,L1,L2, observed at dates 0 and 1. The economic outcome is the stock of outstanding loans, in millions of one fixed currency on a common accounting perimeter. Every initial stock is positive and both dates are observed for every bank. There are no entrants, exits, missing cells or price conversions. No claim about new advances follows from these stocks. Initial short-term funding share is a stipulated ratio: funding with remaining contractual maturity at most one year divided by positive total funding. Group H has a share at least one half; group L has a share below one half. Grouping uses date-0 information and is held fixed.

Both targets describe this finite constructed population. The note supplies no sampling mechanism, random treatment assignment or model for extrapolation. It reports no standard errors or significance stars. That choice does not assert zero uncertainty about a real economy; it identifies what this demonstration does and does not contain. Changing the weights changes the target rather than testing whether a single causal estimate is robust.

Results and interpretation

Individual loan-growth rates in the table's order are (1/10,-1/5,1/20,1/20). H1 grows by 10%, H2 shrinks by 20%, and both L banks grow by 5%. The following table keeps the population, units and weighting visible.

In fractional rate units, the two group means and their contrast are (-1/20,1/20,-1/10); the two loan-total growth rates and contrast are (4/55,1/20,1/44). The H loan total rises from 110 to 118 million even though its mean bank growth is negative. The L total rises from 100 to 105 million. H1 receives half the weight in the mean-bank calculation but 10/11 of the weight in H's loan-total growth. H2 receives half versus 1/11. In L, the initial-loan weights are 4/5 and 1/5, but both banks have the same growth, so reweighting does not change the group value.

The sign reversal answers the specimen's question. It does not establish that one weighting rule is always preferable. An equally weighted target describes the average bank's proportional change in this roster; a loan-total target describes the growth of its combined stock. Neither target reveals the causal effect of funding maturity: lending demand, bank characteristics, write-offs and other processes have not been separated. Nor does either target establish a change in borrowers' investment or welfare.

Limitations and conclusion

The construction deliberately selects unequal sizes and heterogeneous growth to expose a reporting issue. It cannot establish the prevalence or magnitude of that issue in real data. The fixed population avoids coverage changes by design; it does not show that actual reporting panels are complete. With four invented observations and no sampling design, the exercise provides no evidence about statistical precision in a banking population. Its conclusion is a writing requirement grounded in the computation: identify the unit and weights before interpreting a growth comparison, and keep the same target in the title, abstract, table and conclusion.

Appendix A. Exact reconstruction and provenance

Table 1 is the entire source input. H growth rates are (110−100)/100=1/10 and (8−10)/10=−1/5; their mean is −1/20. L growth rates are (84−80)/80=1/20 and (21−20)/20=1/20. H loan-total growth is (118−110)/110=4/55; L growth is (105−100)/100=1/20. Hence the total-growth contrast is 4/55−1/20=1/44. The rate differences become −10 and 25/11 percentage points after multiplication by 100.

The weighting identity follows directly: ΣLi 1−ΣLi 0=ΣLi 0gi. Divide by the positive initial total to obtain Gh=Σwigi, where wi=Li 0/ΣLj 0, all weights are positive and sum to one. Equal initial sizes make these weights equal; identical growth rates also make the two averages coincide. Otherwise equality is possible but is not guaranteed. The identity does not identify a causal coefficient.

Version A consists of the four rows above, the threshold, both formulas and the stated rounding rule. All inputs were authored for this chapter. No external data processing or real-paper replication is claimed. A reader can reconstruct every reported quantity with rational arithmetic. The chapter's writing references support the presentation lesson, not an external estimate or priority claim for this synthetic example.

Why the specimen is organized this way. The abstract identifies the question, finite construction, numerical contrast and boundary. The introduction explains why the two targets matter and names the document's limited contribution. The methods supply what the tables need; the results explain the weighting mechanism rather than narrating every cell. The appendix contains the full reconstruction, while the main text retains the sign reversal and the changed-target warning. Burying either warning in the appendix would change what a skimming reader believes the paper established.

3. Make methods, tables and appendices work together

A methods section is an instruction for understanding and checking the result. State inclusion rules, timing, transformations, denominators and the role of missing data. For an estimate, identify the target and inference procedure, including the independent units and hypothesis family where relevant. For a simulation, state the model, parameters, randomization, repetitions and output definition. For a proof, give the assumptions needed for the proposition and enough steps to verify the conclusion. Methods are incomplete when a reasonable reader must guess a choice that changes the answer.

Tables should answer specific questions. Use a title and note that identify the outcome, population, period, scale, weights and uncertainty convention. Keep the number of displayed digits consistent with the purpose. Exact fractions are useful in this teaching specimen; a real measured quantity rarely warrants arbitrarily many digits because software prints them. Explain a pattern in the prose and why it matters. Do not describe an unadjusted and a reweighted target as “the same result with controls” if they answer different questions.

Decide appendix placement by interpretive importance. A long derivation, dictionary or full specification inventory can go in an appendix with a clear route from the main text. A limitation that overturns the headline must remain visible in the main argument. If only one subgroup supports the claim, the target restriction belongs in the main text. If a planned outcome fails to support it, relabeling that outcome as an appendix exercise does not erase its role. Concision means reducing unnecessary explanation, not concealing contrary evidence.

The AEA data and code policy retained from Chapter 3 provides a bounded professional reference for connecting inputs, transformations and outputs. A real package also needs its applicable access and release arrangements. In the specimen, all raw inputs and formulas fit in the document; a larger project should map each table to its data vintage and computation. Neither a clean appendix nor an exact match to a table establishes the paper's causal or novelty claims.

4. Revise the argument and propagate the change

Worked case 2. From an unsupported headline to a checkable paper

Complete problematic passage. “Short-term funding causes banks to lend 10% less. Our statistically significant results survive weighting by loan size, confirming that regulation should discourage short funding. Table 2 presents the evidence. Technical caveats and alternative estimates are relegated to the appendix.” This passage refers to version A's four synthetic rows and supplies no additional design or uncertainty information.

Diagnosis. The −10 is a percentage-point difference between mean growth rates, not a proportional fall in loan levels. The funding group is observed, not randomized. No sampling law or test makes the contrast statistically significant. The loan-weighted comparison reverses direction and changes the target. The regulatory conclusion would additionally require benefits, costs, equilibrium and implementation evidence. “Technical caveats” hides restrictions necessary to interpret the main result. Editing adjectives alone cannot fix these failures.

Complete revised passage, version A. “In this four-bank synthetic population, equally weighted mean loan growth is 10 percentage points lower in the high initial funding-share group. Growth of group loan totals is instead about 2.27 percentage points higher in that group. The difference arises because the growing high-share bank holds most of its group's initial loans. Table 2 reports both targets and their weights. These calculations describe the supplied records; they do not estimate a causal funding effect or support a regulatory recommendation.”

Revision decisions. The title now names a weighting comparison. The abstract retains both targets and removes significance language. Methods define the group threshold and weights; the table note states units and the absence of an inferential design. The main discussion explains the reversal and causal boundary. The appendix documents the algebra rather than storing a hidden contradictory result. A response to a reader can now say exactly which interpretation changed and where to verify it.

A subsequent source correction. Suppose the author of the fictional inputs declares that H2's date-1 stock should be 12 rather than 8, with every other field unchanged. This stipulated version B is an input correction, not evidence collected from a real bank. H2 growth becomes 1/5, H mean growth becomes 3/20, and the equal-bank contrast becomes 1/10. H total loans become 122, its total growth is 6/55, and the loan-total contrast becomes 13/220. The exact revised results are (1/5,3/20,1/10,122,6/55,13/220). The corresponding contrasts are 10 and 65/11 percentage points, the latter about 5.91. Both comparisons are now positive. A version-A claim about an observed reversal is false for version B even though the general distinction between targets survives.

Completed correction notice. “Version B changes H2's date-1 input from 8 to 12. The original input and version-A note are retained. Recomputed individual growth, both group summaries and both contrasts replace the affected cells; the abstract, results paragraph and conclusion no longer report a sign reversal. In version B the high-share group has higher growth under both targets:10 percentage points for mean bank growth and about 5.91 for loan-total growth. The two targets still differ. No change supplies causal identification or sampling inference. We checked the raw correction, fixed group membership, denominator rules and every dependent statement.”

Revised abstract for version B. “This synthetic note compares mean bank growth with growth of loan totals in four banks grouped by initial funding share. After correcting one date-1 loan stock, the high-share group exceeds the low-share group by 10 percentage points under equal bank weights and about 5.91 under initial-loan weights. The targets differ, but both contrasts are positive. The documented correction removes the earlier sign-reversal claim. The note illustrates how a data revision changes numerical and verbal conclusions; it establishes no real-world funding effect.”

Why this is a substantive revision. The first repair changes an interpretation that the unchanged data never supported. The second changes the inputs and therefore the result. Keep these operations separate in the log. A journal decision, colleague's preference or desired sign is not an input correction. When revising a real paper, preserve enough provenance to distinguish corrected evidence, exploratory new analysis and rewritten explanation, while respecting the data's access constraints.

Complete answers and evaluation criteria

1. A short abstract with an actual result

“How does weighting change a funding-group comparison? In four synthetic banks, equal-bank mean loan growth is 10 percentage points lower in the high initial funding-share group, while growth of loan totals is about 2.27 percentage points higher. A large growing bank dominates the latter group's initial loans. The example distinguishes two descriptive targets; it supplies no causal funding effect or evidence about real banks.” This is below 90 words. Full credit requires the question, fictional population, both contrasts with percentage-point units and a substantive boundary. Stylistic alternatives are welcome; omitting the positive contrast changes the result.

2. Explain the weights rather than choosing a preferred sign

The four rates are 1/10,−1/5,1/20,1/20. Group means are −1/20 and 1/20; loan-total growth rates are 4/55 and 1/20. H's initial weights 10/11 and 1/11 favor its growing bank, whereas equal bank weights put one half on the shrinking bank. L's weights 4/5 and 1/5 have no effect because both rates equal 1/20. If all initial stocks are a common positive amount, the final stocks must be adjusted to preserve the stipulated growth rates. Each group's loan-total growth then equals its mean growth; both H-minus-L contrasts are (-1/10,-1/10). Merely replacing initial values without changing finals would be another experiment. Full credit gives all calculations, the mechanism and the precise equal-size condition.

3. Write the table's interpretation into its note

“The table describes loan-stock growth from date 0 to 1 for the complete fixed synthetic roster H1,H2,L1,L2, with two banks in each group. H is defined by initial short-term funding share at least one half; L is below one half. Row 1 averages each bank's proportional stock change equally. Row 2 uses initial-loan weights and equals proportional change in the group's total stock. Group entries are percentages; differences are percentage points. Fractions are exact and decimals rounded to two places. No sampling model, causal design or standard errors are supplied.” The full algebra can go in Appendix A. The sign reversal and changed target must stay in the main results because they determine the headline's meaning. Full credit includes every requested note component and a reasoned placement choice.

4. Describe the method that was actually used

“We retain all four supplied banks at both dates, require positive initial loan stocks and fix groups using the initial funding-share threshold of one half. We calculate each bank's stock change divided by its initial stock. We average these rates equally within each group, and separately divide each group's change in total loans by its initial total. We report H minus L for each target, retaining exact fractions and rounding only display decimals. No regression or inferential procedure is used.” A regression on a group indicator with an intercept could reproduce a descriptive mean difference under appropriate unweighted coding, but it would not create assignment, remove arbitrary confounding, or turn loan stocks into firm investment. Full credit reports the actual operations and their target without inventing estimation or identification.

5. Propagate a correction through claims

Version B changes H2 growth from −1/5 to 1/5. Mean H growth is 3/20, mean L growth remains 1/20, and the contrast is 1/10. H total growth is 6/55, so the loan-total contrast is 13/220. Multiplying by 100 gives (10,65/11) percentage points. Check the input/dictionary version, individual-growth display, results table, abstract, introduction's result preview, results explanation, conclusion and appendix reconstruction. Example notice: “Version B corrects H2's final stock from 8 to 12, giving positive H-minus-L contrasts of 10 and about 5.91 percentage points under the two stated targets; version A is retained and all dependent claims have been revised. The correction removes the previous sign reversal but supplies no causal interpretation.” Full credit gives both values, at least four affected locations and a transparent two-sentence notice.

6. Match inference language to supplied evidence

“The specimen exhausts its invented four-bank population and supplies no sampling or assignment distribution. Adding conventional standard errors would introduce an unprovided probability model, so we report exact descriptive quantities and state the limitation. We do not infer that a failed test would establish a zero effect.” A real study would need a defensible target, sampling or assignment process, independent units or dependence model, estimator, variance/inference method and treatment of selection and the tested family. A zero arithmetic difference is a statement about the supplied values. Nonrejection can reflect weak information or imprecision; evidence against an economically important effect needs credible uncertainty small enough to exclude the relevant effect sizes. None of these calculations alone identifies a causal effect. Full credit distinguishes these claims rather than asserting that small samples can never support any inference.

7. Keep a changed answer visible

“Under equal bank weights, version A's high-share group has mean growth 10 percentage points below the low-share group. Under initial-loan weights, its combined stock grows about 2.27 percentage points faster. These are different descriptive targets, and their signs differ because group H contains a large growing and a small shrinking bank. Neither identifies a funding effect.” Hiding the second target while asserting broad robustness makes the main sentence unsupported; the result's central interpretation belongs in the body even if detailed derivation is appended. An extra comparison selected after inspection should be labeled exploratory, with the selection reason and target disclosed. Do not retroactively describe it as prespecified or use it to replace an inconvenient planned result. Full credit corrects both the numerical account and the status of the analysis.

8. Transfer the writing discipline without manufacturing findings

Title: “Measuring advertised rents on one platform: a documented descriptive design.” Introduction: “This proposed note asks how advertised rent varies with observed dwelling size in one platform's listings during a specified month. It defines the listing population, records the deduplication rule and separates asking prices from completed transactions. Its intended contribution is an auditable description of what this source can measure, conditional on comparison with the closest existing work. No numerical result has yet been supplied.” Limitations: “Platform listings are selected offers, not a census of dwellings or residents. Duplicate removal does not correct every unobserved quality difference or validate the recorded sizes. Without transaction prices, incomes and displacement outcomes, the proposed description cannot establish residents' welfare or the effect of a housing policy. Novelty and empirical findings remain to be demonstrated.” Full credit supplies all three requested artifacts, a defined question and contribution boundary, and no invented estimate. Equivalent bounded descriptions earn credit.

6. Teach-back and delayed retention

Teach-back. Explain to a colleague why the paper's abstract, methods, main table and appendix must agree on the target. Use one exact version-A contrast and distinguish an interpretive repair from an input correction.

Answer guide

A complete explanation identifies the −10-percentage-point equal-bank contrast and the+25/11-percentage-point loan-total contrast, with their different weights. Removing causal language repairs an unsupported interpretation without changing those data. Changing H2's final stock to 12 is a new input vintage that changes the arithmetic and removes the reversal. The appendix documents each result, but cannot excuse an incompatible headline.

Delayed retention. After a week, a collaborator discovers that a caption uses the full sample while the reported number uses only complete cases. Write a revision plan and a replacement claim before changing any styling.

Criteria and illustrative answer

Preserve the current manuscript and computation, identify the actual complete-case population and exclusion reasons, and determine whether the original target can be estimated with defensible missingness assumptions. Recompute a common target if justified; otherwise label the narrower quantity and revise the abstract, table note, methods and conclusion consistently. A replacement claim might be: “Among the records observed at both dates, the stated descriptive comparison is X; the full-population comparison is not established by these records.” X must come from the corrected computation, not a guessed value. Record affected outputs and verify the revision. Full credit changes the substantive claim before cosmetic edits and distinguishes missing observations from zeros.

Next step. Carry the one-paragraph argument, exact main result and limitation into seminars, discussion and academic communication. An audience should be able to recover the same question and answer from a talk and from the paper, even though each uses different space and pacing.
