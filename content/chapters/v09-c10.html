Capability and prerequisites

You can compare nontraditional investments by reconstructing their underlying claims, financing, valuation conventions and cash deadlines. You can distinguish a smooth reported return from low economic risk, reconcile a private fund's gross proceeds with its investor's net experience, and test whether an apparently attractive allocation can actually meet its commitments. You can explain which conclusions depend on a model rather than observed evidence.

Use strategic and dynamic allocation for objectives and dated constraints, covariance and diversification, performance measurement, and private equity and venture claims. The latter develops enterprise, preferred-equity and fund waterfalls; here the focus is the outside investor's portfolio and liquidity. CU denotes fictional currency units. Dates within each case are equally spaced. A simple return R compounds wealth by 1+R; an IRR is per stated period. All cases, probabilities, prices, contracts and return paths below are original synthetic constructions. They are not current market observations.

The Rowan investment committee sees a property with a high equity IRR, a private fund with an attractive multiple, and a fund whose reported volatility is half its underlying volatility. It also owes cash before some distributions arrive. Choosing the largest headline return would leave the central decision unanswered: which claims can meet Rowan's objectives after fees, financing and cash restrictions?

1. Start with the claim, not the label

Alternative is a classification relative to an investor's conventional public-stock and bond menu, not a common payoff law. A warehouse, a senior private loan, a venture share and a futures strategy can have very different exposures. Look through any fund wrapper to the assets, senior claims, trading obligations and decision rights. Then add the wrapper's fees, redemption terms, valuation rules and governance. Economic exposure and access to cash are separate dimensions.

Four quantities should never be used interchangeably: economic value conditional on a model; reported net asset value (NAV); the cash available from a sale now; and the amount contractually withdrawable now. An accurate valuation of an illiquid claim does not create a buyer. A contractual gate may defer withdrawals even when the portfolio contains valuable assets. Conversely, a quoted security can be easy to sell while its price is very volatile.

An illiquidity premium is a hypothesis about compensation for a restriction under particular prices and preferences. It is not a return increment attached automatically to every private asset. An investor with costly future cash needs may require more compensation than one with secure external income. Competition, purchase price, manager selection, fees and exposure to bad aggregate states can consume or explain an apparent premium. A portfolio decision needs comparable net claims and a feasible cash policy before it needs a slogan about asset classes.

2. Real-estate valuation must retain capital expenditure and debt

Let NOIt be property operating income after operating expenses but before debt interest, depreciation and the separately stated capital expenditure Kt. Our case has no taxes, changes in working capital or additional cash items. Its unlevered cash is At=NOIt−Kt, plus net sale proceeds at exit T. Acquisition costs are paid at date 0. Conditional on a supplied discount rate appropriate for those cash flows,

NPV = −(purchase + acquisition costs) + Σt=1…T At/(1+r)t + net saleT/(1+r)T.

A terminal capitalization convention instead writes VT=NOIT+1/cT, where c is the quoted forward-NOI capitalization rate and V is gross sale price. The numerator's date matters. This ratio is a valuation assumption, not a separate source of cash and not generally the required total return r. The familiar perpetuity identity c=r−g requires a compatible distributable-cash numerator and constant-growth assumptions; NOI that omits capital expenditure does not automatically satisfy it. Net sale is (1−s)V when the stipulated selling-cost fraction is s. MIT's real-estate DCF teaching material supports keeping operating cash, capital costs, terminal assumptions and discounting distinct.

Cap-rate risk is nonlinear: ∂V/∂c=−NOI/c². For small changes, dV/V≈dNOI/NOI−dc/c; large changes require recalculating the ratio. A higher quoted cap rate lowers price holding forward NOI fixed. Stressing only NOI while preserving a favorable sale multiple can miss a joint deterioration in operating cash and exit conditions.

Debt creates a senior cash schedule. If Bt−1 is opening principal, i is the contractual interest rate and Pt is principal repayment, debt service is iBt−1+Pt, with Bt=Bt−1−Pt. Equity receives the remainder only after this payment. A negative remainder cannot be silently ignored: the model needs an equity injection, a funded reserve, a workout or a default rule. Our property fixture permits only fully performing debt, positive initial equity and a positive exit residual. A loan quote at par is supplied; it is not derived from the expected asset return.

For a cash sequence with negative initial cash and nonnegative later receipts, at least one strictly positive, NPV(r) is continuous and strictly decreasing for r>−1. Scale, cash timing and risk can make IRR rankings disagree with value rankings. More general sequences with repeated sign reversals can have multiple roots or no economically useful root; never report a solver's first root without checking the cash pattern.

3. Appraisal smoothing changes measured risk

Consider an explicitly assumed reporting rule, not a claim that every appraisal system behaves this way. Let rt be the latent one-period economic simple return and at the reported return:

at = θat−1 + (1−θ)rt,   0≤θ<1.

Assume rt are iid with mean μ and finite variance σ², and the filter has been running in its stationary distribution. Then E[a]=μ. Iteration gives the mean-square convergent representation at−μ=(1−θ)Σj≥0θj(rt−j−μ). Independent shocks eliminate the cross terms, so

Var(a)=(1−θ)²σ²Σj≥0θ2j=[(1−θ)/(1+θ)]σ²;   Cov(at,at−k)=θkVar(a), k≥0.

The covariance result also follows by iterating the recursion k times: the intervening innovations are independent of at−k. Thus the reported lag-one correlation is θ under these assumptions. Smoothing lowers the one-period variance and moves some recognition of a shock into subsequent reports.

Now let xt be a market simple return. Assume the pairs (rt,xt) are jointly iid across dates, though the two returns can be correlated within a date. Write their contemporaneous covariance as C and market variance as σx²>0. Only the current innovation contributes to Cov(at,xt), giving

Cov(at,xt)=(1−θ)C;   βreported=(1−θ)βlatent;   ρreported=√(1−θ²)ρlatent.

The last equality divides covariance by the two standard deviations. A small contemporaneous beta can therefore reflect timing rather than weak underlying exposure. If the market or latent returns have serial dependence, the omitted lagged covariances enter this calculation. The displayed correction is then not generally valid.

Over T periods, the variance of the sum of simple returns is Var(a)[T+2Σk=1…T−1(T−k)θk]. Dividing by T and taking the limit yields Var(a)(1+θ)/(1−θ)=σ². This statement concerns an additive return sum, not the compounded wealth return ∏(1+at)−1. It shows why treating every smoothed report as an independent observation understates aggregate variation. It does not prove that reported and economic wealth agree at every exit date.

With known θ and the previous reported value, the exact inverse is rt=(at−θat−1)/(1−θ). At θ=0 the report is the latent return. At θ=1 current latent returns disappear from the report and cannot be recovered. Values close to one amplify small reporting errors through division by 1−θ. An estimated autocorrelation is not proof of a known smoothing parameter: changing expected returns, genuine persistence, stale prices, fees and measurement noise can also matter. Getmansky, Lo and Makarov's 2004 paper develops a broader moving-average smoothing model and its measurement consequences. Our geometric filter, numerical path and derivations are a separate teaching construction, not an estimate of their historical sample.

Worked example 2 — Half the reported volatility does not mean half the economic exposure

Suppose the stationary population has μ=8%, latent σ=20%, θ=0.6, market σx=15% and latent market correlation 0.5. Then latent variance is 0.04 and reported variance is (0.4/1.6)0.04=0.01: reported volatility is 10%. Latent covariance with the market is 0.5×0.20×0.15=0.015; reported covariance is 0.006. Latent beta is 0.015/0.0225=2/3; reported beta is 4/15. Reported correlation is 0.006/(0.10×0.15)=0.4.

With a same-period safe simple return of 2%, latent mean-excess-to-volatility is (0.08−0.02)/0.20=0.3; the uncorrected reported statistic is 0.6. Neither calculation establishes manager skill. For four periods, the correct variance of the reported return sum is 0.01[4+2(3×0.6+2×0.6²+0.6³)]=296/3,125=0.09472, rather than the independence calculation 0.04.

Separately illustrate the recursion with a fixed initial report a0=0.08 and four latent returns (−0.20,0.20,−0.10,0.30). The reported returns are (−0.032,0.0608,−0.00352,0.117888). Inverting, for example, the second return gives (0.0608−0.6×(−0.032))/0.4=0.20. Starting both wealth indices at one, latent wealth ends at 0.8×1.2×0.9×1.3=1.1232; the reported index ends at approximately 1.143868. That difference is not spendable cash. This four-observation, fixed-initial-value path is not a stationary sample estimate of the population moments above.

4. Private-fund returns belong to dated investor cash flows

A commitment is an obligation to contribute under specified terms; it is not the same as already invested capital or liquid NAV. A call requests part of that capital. An investor's total contribution may also pay fees and expenses. Define exactly which amounts count against the commitment. Distributions may return capital, distribute operating proceeds or realize gains. Residual NAV is a valuation of investments still held, not another cash distribution.

Separate the portfolio-company level, the fund level and the limited partner (LP). A company can service debt while its fund makes no LP distribution. Fund-level borrowing can delay LP calls without improving the underlying assets; the financing cost and repayment still belong somewhere. A fund's gross return before fees cannot be compared directly with an LP's after-fee return. The Kaplan–Strömberg institutional discussion provides a historical account of the firm, fund and portfolio-company distinction. The ILPA reporting definitions make cash-flow categories and fee perimeters explicit. Neither source makes our fictional fee contract universal.

For a fully liquidated investment, net DPI is total net distributions divided by total LP paid-in capital; RVPI is residual net value divided by paid-in capital, and TVPI=DPI+RVPI. These multiples do not discount or annualize. A long wait and a short wait can have the same multiple. Net IRR solves Σ(Dt−Ct)/(1+r)t=0 using actual investor dates and consistent net distributions D and contributions C. Suppose initial net cash is negative, final net cash positive, and all negative net cash precedes all positive net cash, ignoring zeros. There is one positive gross root x=1+r. To see why, let k be the last negative-cash date and multiply NPV by xk. Each later positive cash flow then has a negative exponent of x; each negative cash flow has a nonnegative exponent. Their signed sum is strictly decreasing on x>0, positive as x approaches zero and negative for sufficiently large x. It therefore crosses zero exactly once. More complicated cash sequences need a separate root analysis.

A transparent benchmark-relative comparison compounds each contribution and distribution to a common terminal date using the same supplied public benchmark. With Bt,T its cumulative gross return from t through T, compute FV(C)=ΣCtBt,T, FV(D)=ΣDtBt,T, ratio FV(D)/FV(C), and excess FV(D)−FV(C). This cash-flow-matched public-market-equivalent ratio is one defined comparison, not a universal PME convention. It adjusts timing relative to that benchmark but does not equal risk-adjusted alpha. Different leverage, sector concentration, currencies, selection and liquidity can remain. Separately compounding both cash legs also does not prove that a nonnegative self-financing benchmark account could have honored every private distribution.

Worked example 3 — A profitable private fund with an unfunded investor deadline

Rowan makes a commitment of 100 that covers investment capital only. Calls are 40, 30 and 20 at annual dates 0, 1 and 2. The remaining 10 is formally released at final liquidation, date 3. Fees are 2% of the original commitment, paid separately at dates 1, 2 and 3: 2 each time. Gross fund distributions are 8, 12 and 120 at those dates. The stipulated carry is paid only at date 3 and equals 20% of positive total gross distributions less total investment capital. Management fees are excluded from that carry base; there is no hurdle, catch-up or interim carry.

5. Translate illiquidity into a cash constraint

For the case's no-interest, no-borrowing account, let Ct be contributions, It income, St spending and Dt distributions. Immediately before the date-t distribution,

Lt− = L0 − C0 + Σj=1…t(Ij−Sj−Cj) + Σj=1…t−1Dj.

Require every Lt−≥0, including the initial payment. With nonnegative distributions, these are the restrictive deadlines. The additional initial reserve required for a specified path is max(0,−mintLt−). This follows because an extra CU held initially raises every later balance by exactly one. Interest, a secured line, withdrawal penalties or contingent sales would change the recursion and would require their own costs and limits.

A lockup forbids withdrawal for a specified period under the stipulated contract; a notice period requires advance notice; a gate limits or defers otherwise requested withdrawals. A secondary-market exit requires a buyer, an executable price, settlement before the deadline and any needed transfer permission. These mechanisms are not interchangeable. The existence of a valuation report, or a history of distributions, establishes none of those exit rights. Model the exact terms that are supplied rather than assuming a legal rule that applies to all funds.

Private debt makes a further distinction important. Seniority ranks claims on available assets; it does not guarantee enough assets to pay the senior claim. If the only creditor is owed F at date T, has first priority, and the entire available enterprise payoff is X≥0 with no enforcement cost, debt receives min(F,X) and equity receives max(X−F,0). Their sum is X state by state. A quoted loan at 100 with an 8% promised coupon can have an expected return below 8% because recovery can be less than 108. Fees, uncertain recovery dates, collateral realization costs, covenants and additional senior creditors would change the calculation. A stable carried loan mark does not eliminate that credit exposure or create an early redemption right.

The allocation implication is conditional: compare the compensation for a particular claim with the investor's liability dates and feasible reserve. A long horizon alone does not remove short-run calls, spending or collateral obligations. Conversely, a well-funded investor may rationally accept a lockup when the net claim fits the mandate. Neither choice follows from the word “alternative.”

6. Commodity exposure requires a contract and a collateral ledger

A physical commodity has a spot price, storage and other holding costs, and possibly operational benefits from availability. A producer's equity adds operating leverage, management, debt and firm-specific risks. A futures contract instead exchanges cash through changes in its quoted settlement price. These are different investments even when they concern the same commodity.

For q contracts with multiplier u, variation cash over a settlement interval is qu(Ft−Ft−1). A newly entered futures contract has zero initial contract value under the stipulated fair quote, while collateral is transferred or reserved separately. Notional quF measures exposure; it is not the purchase payment. Margin is cash or eligible collateral supporting the position, not a fee and not a cap on losses. The CME mark-to-market explanation describes settlement of futures gains and losses and the need to replenish inadequate margin. Our dates and required reserve below are fictional rather than an exchange's actual contract specifications.

Rolling means closing one maturity and opening another. Each old contract's change from its preceding mark is settled; the new contract starts its subsequent mark-to-market history from its own entry quote. The CME expiration discussion illustrates that change of maturity. A new quote above the expiring quote does not by itself create an immediate cash loss equal to their difference. That difference can influence the later exposure and realized return relative to spot, but subsequent prices matter. Transaction costs, if stipulated, are separate cash charges. “Roll yield” needs a specified return decomposition and maintained exposure rule.

Worked example 4 — Futures gains, a high-water fee and a positive-NAV margin failure

Rowan's fictional commodity strategy starts with 1,000 cash and ten contracts, multiplier one, at a near-maturity quote of 100. Thus initial cash equals initial notional; fixed units do not maintain constant leverage later. There are three model periods, with no external subscriptions, withdrawals, borrowing, trading costs or taxes. All opening cash, including reserved margin, earns 1% per period, credited at the end. The management fee is separately 1% of opening NAV. A performance fee is 20% of after-management NAV above the previous net high-water mark H, initially 1,000. Update H to the larger of its old value and ending NAV after the performance fee.

At each modeled date, credit cash interest, settle variation cash, pay management fees, pay any performance fee, then test a required cash margin reserve of 200. That reserve is part of cash, not an expense. This contract tests margin only at those specified points; it makes no claim to capture actual intraperiod price paths or exchange procedures. If cash is insufficient, the model stops rather than inventing a loan. At date 3 the final test occurs before the position closes; releasing the reserve then creates no income.

The old near contract settles and closes at 98 at date 1. After that settlement, ten contracts in the deferred maturity are opened at 105 with zero initial contract cash price. That same deferred contract marks at 104 at date 2 and settles/closes at 112 at date 3. Variation cash is therefore 10(98−100)=−20, 10(104−105)=−10, and 10(112−104)=80.

7. Evaluate the strategy inside a hedge-fund wrapper

A high-water mark is a path-dependent fee rule, not a guarantee that an investor cannot lose money. The example's rule charges only above a previous net peak and has no external cash flows. Subscriptions, redemptions, hurdles, crystallization dates, loss carryforward changes or a different fee base require a fuller share-accounting contract. A simple flat subtraction from gross return cannot reproduce every such rule. In addition, management fees can accrue during losses.

These are analytical descriptions of stipulated mandates, not claims about every manager in a category. Inspect holdings and contracts, not just a strategy name. An investor lockup may reduce the manager's need to sell for redemptions, but it does not eliminate asset losses or lender demands. A strategy's funding horizon should be compared with the horizon needed for its trade to work. Concentrated counterparties, valuation discretion and weak reconciliation can add operational risk alongside the investment thesis.

A defensible comparison reports net realized cash, residual valuation uncertainty, gross and net exposures, tail scenarios, fee bases, and the timing of cash needs. It also checks sample selection, surviving funds, backfilled histories, stale marks and changing strategies before interpreting a performance sample. Reuse the inference discipline from market efficiency and empirical evidence: a fitted pattern is not a credible estimate of implementable expected outperformance. For an actual mandate, specify independent valuation checks, custody and cash reconciliation, authority for changing leverage, and a response when funding assumptions fail. Those controls do not promise returns; they make the delegated decision assessable.

9. Teach-back and delayed retention

Teach-back. In three minutes, explain why a high reported private-fund return can coexist with an unsuitable allocation. Use one cash identity, one measurement assumption and one funding deadline. An adequate answer distinguishes asset, fund and investor cash; states that appraisal smoothing can reduce reported variance under a maintained model without removing economic risk; and shows that a distribution received after a call cannot fund it without an explicit bridge. An excellent answer adds a net fee perimeter and explains why a benchmark-relative surplus is not risk-adjusted skill.

Return after one week, without the tables. (1) For θ=0, what happens to the smoothing correction? For θ=1, can latent returns be recovered? (2) In the original private case, why are 14/9 and 65/48 both correct yet different? (3) With cash 150 and a required margin reserve of 200, is the strategy feasible merely because NAV is positive? (4) What happens to the property gross sale if forward NOI stays 105 but the cap rate rises from 7% to 8.4%?

Retention answers

(1) At zero the report equals the latent return; at one the report contains no new latent information and the inverse is undefined. (2) The first is gross fund realization 140 divided by investment capital 90; the second is LP net distributions 130 divided by all paid-in capital 96, including fees. Neither is annualized. (3) No: margin is short by 50, so the specified strategy needs a funded remedy or must stop. (4) Gross sale falls from 1,500 to 1,250, a decrease of 250 or 16⅔%; dividing by the new cap rate, rather than using a linear approximation for a large change, gives the exact result.

10. Reproduce, change and defend

Download the lab instructions, synthetic inputs, standard-library Python model, complete reference output and file integrity manifest. The model accepts --input and optional --output; an output path must be new. It uses exact rational cash calculations and finite numerical IRRs and square roots. It runs offline. Import and run() are quiet; the CLI prints JSON only when no output file is requested.

Reproduce all four cases, then change a permitted numeric assumption in a copied input: an exit cap rate, a known smoothing parameter, the amount of a delayed distribution, or the management-fee rate. Explain the economic consequence before running the model. Timing and claim conventions are fixed; unsupported changes are rejected rather than silently reinterpreted. Infeasible private liquidity is labeled as a diagnostic with no implemented terminal cash, and futures execution stops at the first failed modeled margin test. Property defaults require a different model and are rejected.

The focused checker independently reconstructs cash schedules, moments, all determinate exercise anchors, interventions, rejection boundaries and portable execution. That supports reproducibility; it does not validate a real asset valuation, estimate an illiquidity premium or substitute for external expert review. The chapter uses no observed investment dataset. The successor, execution, trading costs and market microstructure, examines how an intended trade becomes an actual fill and cash cost. Deeper derivatives valuation and credit models belong to the planned fixed-income and derivatives volume; they are not supplied by the deliberately bounded futures ledger here.

Sources and extension

Method sources are MIT's property DCF and capitalization lectures, Getmansky, Lo and Makarov's original smoothing paper, Kaplan and Strömberg's institutional private-equity discussion, ILPA's scoped cash-flow and performance definitions, and CME's explanations of mark-to-market and contract rolling. Their historical examples, empirical magnitudes, product specifications and dated regulatory statements are not imported as current facts. The original case contracts and conditional derivations above state the assumptions needed for their conclusions. A useful extension is to add a priced, limited credit line to the private liquidity problem and compare its commitment fee and stress capacity with the opportunity cost of a larger reserve; the line must be funded and its draw dates explicit.
