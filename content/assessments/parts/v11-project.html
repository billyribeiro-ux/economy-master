Institution stress project — Portlight's two currencies and one missed pension payment

Capability. Build and defend an institution-wide funding decision whose accounts, creditor rights, currency locations, valuation and evidence agree. This original open-resource project is designed for 14–20 hours, an uncalibrated planning estimate. Complete all twelve Volume 11 chapters first. Submit an independent implementation and initial decision before reading the reference output or instructor guide. Your task is a coherent bank–pension payment case, not a collection of chapter screenshots or a dashboard with unrelated risk scores.

Portlight's finance director proposes reporting all resources in home currency H and treating a positive balance sheet as evidence that payments will settle. Treasury wants to preposition foreign currency F. The pension trustee needs benefits paid at a specific time, and the credit officer says the borrower's collateral will eventually cover the gap. Determine what each statement establishes, which funded response works, and what new evidence would withdraw its approval. The institutions, money, priorities, probabilities and powers below are fictional. No actual law, observed market quote or empirical default frequency is being asserted.

Files and the information flow

Download the original inputs, standard-library reference model, baseline output, four-payload manifest and standalone reproduction guide. Keep input and model together and run python3 v11-assessment-analysis.py; it silently writes the neighboring output file. Explicit --input and --output paths and python3 -O work from another directory. The quiet, nonmutating run(data) API returns exact rational strings. Invalid input and protected-output failures occur before output is opened. Hashes bind bytes; they do not verify a beneficiary receipt, a source claim or an approver's authority.

The information flow is funded opening positions and rights → dated H/F receipts and payments → actual retained claims and marked equity → all-scenario policy test → finite loss and reverse-stress evidence → conditional decision. Borrower recovery changes both asset value and the amount arriving at the final cutoff. A delayed F transfer changes where cash is usable without changing the bank's total F asset. The pension's bank deposit is Portlight's liability and the pension's asset; consolidation must eliminate that internal claim once.

Opening accounts, clocks and claims

H and F are distinct currencies; e is H per F. Reference e0 = 2. The early and final dates are contractual cutoffs, not years. Within the early date, F deposits settle at 09:00, home payments at 10:00, and pension benefits at 10:15. On the later final date, F recovery and F deposits settle at 17:00; home receipts, conversion and payments settle at 18:00; the pension applies late receipts at 18:15. Receipts specifically listed before a cutoff are usable there. No subsequent receipt repairs the historical fact of a missed earlier deadline.

Before stress, the sponsor may subscribe b ∈ {0,10,20} H of actual bank equity and contribute z ∈ {0,2} H of actual pension cash. In the supplied states, prepositioned F is confirmed in the bank's foreign account before 09:00. A transfer task changes that fact: H has still been paid, but the bought F sits in a titled in-transit receivable until final 17:00. The dealer has already funded that F into the settlement route. Neither its H-equivalent mark nor an acknowledgment makes it foreign payment cash before delivery. F already in the foreign account may pay F creditors even when conversion or repatriation is restricted.

One joint state determines the whole institution

Long-bond price is q(y) = 1/(1+y)² H per unit. This is the value of a unit H payoff two specified discount periods away under the stipulated flat yield quote. The remaining-maturity convention and scenario mark are held fixed over both settlement dates; the long bonds do not mature during the project. The maturing bill is a different asset. The nonlinear price rule is a teaching revaluation model, not a forecast or a claim that the displayed yields are annual market observations. All 20 long-bond units are initially separately eligible collateral; the bill is not pledged.

Collateral is sold only at the final date; costs and the stated prior creditor are paid before Portlight. Thus final bank receipts are operating cash plus net collateral. At scenario recognition, carry the domestic asset at its total stipulated actual receipts and write down the difference from face once. The nominal unpaid claim survives the accounting writeoff. Likewise carry the offshore claim at its specified final F recovery, retaining its unpaid nominal balance as a separate credit record. No favorable recovery date is inferred from collateral value.

Immediately after the funded opening transactions and before early payments, recognize the new FX mark, long-bond mark and both credit impairments. Values remain fixed afterward except cash collections, payments and secured interest. The offshore impairment is translated at the scenario e. Revalue the original net F position and then deduct that impairment once; do not also expense the smaller recovery when received. Marked equity is assets minus the remaining nominal liabilities. It is neither a cash account nor a promise that all assets can be sold at their marks.

Actual settlement and the declared decision rule

  1. Early F payment. At 09:00 buy only the F needed to meet the 15 F deposit call, limited by the early conversion cap and available H cash. Conversion is delivered before payment at the scenario e. Pay min(available F, 15 F); retain any unpaid part. The 10:00 secured lender cannot retroactively fund this deadline.
  2. Early H payment. Receive the borrower's first cash. Attempt the smallest secured draw that would leave 2 H after the 18 H household call and the 12 H pension call. Actual draw is capped by min(20 H, eligible units × q × (1−h)). Pledge g/[q(1−h)] units; the remainder is not also pledged elsewhere. Pay households first, then the pension from actual remaining cash. The reserve is a monitoring criterion, not a creditor: a failing policy still uses its available cash to pay the stated claims. No cash is held back merely to display 2.
  3. Pension benefit payment. At 10:15 the pension pays as much of its 15 H benefit obligation as cash 1+z plus its actual bank receipt allows. Retain unpaid benefits. A sponsor's unused cash and the bank's positive marked equity are not automatically the pension's property.
  4. Final foreign settlement. Receive any delayed prepositioned F and the actual offshore recovery. Pay remaining F deposits, including early arrears, as far as foreign cash permits. A late cure is recorded as late.
  5. Final H settlement. Receive the bill's 20 H and the borrower's final cash. Accrue the secured interest expense 0.05g and add it to the secured claim. Convert only the F needed to cover that claim, all remaining H deposits and the 2 H reserve, capped by the late conversion limit and F left after foreign creditors. The dealer's H arrives before home payments. Pay secured principal and interest first, remaining household deposits second and pension arrears third. Release collateral only when the secured claim is fully paid. Retain every unpaid balance. The pension applies any late bank receipt to its retained benefit arrears at 18:15.

There is no long-bond sale, additional borrowing, dividend, interest on deposits, late-payment penalty, automatic FX netting, emergency state funding or cancellation of a claim. These priorities and rights are stipulated contracts, not assertions about actual insolvency law. The scenario model values the pension's remaining bank claim at face for its accounting reconciliation; that is not a certified economic recovery value after a missed payment. Investigating an impaired claim or a subsequent legal process requires a new model and evidence.

A policy qualifies only if every bank and pension obligation is paid at its specified time, H cash is at least 2 at both home-payment closes, and bank marked equity is at least 20 at both closes. No extra F buffer is required beyond nonnegative actual cash. The 20 H equity floor is a chosen board rule, not regulatory capital or an intraday rule. Evaluate all 18 policies in all three states without probability-weighting feasibility. Select least b+z, then least f, then least b and z for any remaining exact ties. This certifies a finite menu under one payment policy; it does not identify the best possible institution or policy in every future world.

1. Reconcile ownership, lending and actual resources

Reference bank assets are 20 + 2×6 + 20 + 20 + 60 + 2×20 = 172 H. Liabilities are 78 + 12 + 2×25 = 140 H; equity is 32 H. Pension assets are 1 + 12 = 13 H against benefits 15 H, so its deficit is 2 H even before a bank delay. Consolidated bank–pension equity is 32−2 = 30 H: the internal 12 H claim cancels, rather than generating an extra asset for the pair. Received b and z raise this combined equity by b+z; buying f F exchanges 2f H for a distinct F asset without generating reference profit.

For b = 20, f = 10, z = 2, sponsor cash falls from 30 to 8 H. Portlight's H cash becomes 20 H and F cash 16 F; bank equity is 52 H. The pension's cash becomes 3 H and its funding deficit closes at face. Neither the sponsor's remaining 8 H nor the bank's marked equity can be spent by the pension without another authorized transfer. The new bank investor owns 20/52 = 5/13 of the residual equity and bears its subsequent loss; old owners retain 8/13. The secured lender has its own cash and a senior claim, not that equity risk.

Operating service ratios at the first date are 1, 3/5 and 2/5 against scheduled 20; final operating ratios are 1, 4/5 and 7/10 against scheduled 40. Final net collateral is 0, 10 and 12 H. Thus bank final receipts are 40, 42 and 40 H, total actual domestic receipts are 60, 54 and 48, and recognized credit losses are 0, 6 and 12. First contractual arrears are 0, 8 and 12. The credit state's extra 2 at the final date cures only 2 of its earlier 8; terminal nominal unpaid amounts are 0, 6 and 12. The carried asset becomes zero after collecting the impaired book; the residual legal claims do not cease to exist. Offshore unpaid face is 0, 2 and 4 F. No underwriting conclusion should substitute gross collateral or an average cash flow for these rights and dates.

3. Loss distribution and a scoped reverse boundary

For any policy, a useful reconciliation is Efinal = 32+b + (1+f)(e−2) + 20(q−1) − domestic loss − e×foreign loss in F − 0.05g. Principal receipts, withdrawals and currency exchanges at e do not add another expense. For the selected plan, loss measured from received-capital-adjusted reference equity 52 is 0, 127/10 and 1094/45 H. Its mean under the stipulated physical weights is 2237/270 H. At α = 3/4, cumulative probability is 1/2 after the zero-loss state and 5/6 after the credit state, so the lower quantile is 127/10.

ES₃⁄₄ = [(1/6)(1094/45) + (1/12)(127/10)]/(1/4) = 5519/270 H.

The worst quarter consists of all the joint state and only 1/12 probability from the credit atom. Conditioning on all losses at least VaR would include too much of that atom. The finite ES method and atom issue are taught in Chapter 4 and the scoped general-loss source. None of these invented physical weights is a pricing probability or an estimate validated by backtesting. The policy rule requires each state to pass and does not average a missed payment against another state's surplus.

For the selected plan, varying only joint y leaves the required draw at 4 as long as the facility can supply it. Capacity is 15q, so reserve feasibility requires q ≥ 4/15. With g = 4, final equity is 94/5 + 20q, so the 20 H floor requires only q ≥ 3/50. All other receipts, payment rights and conversion limits are unchanged; the required late conversion remains 7/5 F. Consequently the first failure boundary is

q ≥ 4/15 ⇔ y ≤ √15/2 − 1. Equality is feasible; at equality Efinal = 362/15 > 20.

Below q = 4/15 the capped draw cannot preserve the early 2 H reserve. A creditor need not yet miss payment: H available before borrowing is 28, so full early H claims 30 still pay if q ≥ 2/15. A reserve breach occurs first. The formula for final equity with fixed g = 4 is used on its feasible-draw region; do not extrapolate it through a capacity failure while pretending borrowing remains 4. This is a conditional one-factor boundary with a proved equality case, not a probability of crisis or a global search across all stress mechanisms.

4. A funded conditional decision and its limits

A defensible memo conditionally approves the funded (20 H,10 F,2 H) response only after independently confirming the sponsor's 22 H transfers, the 10 F beneficiary credit before 09:00, the separate lender's available 20 H, unencumbered title to the eligible long bonds, the conversion dealer's funded capacities and the stipulated receipt/priority rights. The forecast is not execution evidence. Treasury owns the currency ledger; credit owns the borrower cash and seniority evidence; operations verifies delivery and payment identifiers; independent risk challenges valuation, aggregation and the finite boundary; the actually mandated committee decides the limits and any new action. Preserve rejected policies and objections.

A manager paid only for a green consolidated-H cash total can have an incentive to omit the F account's deadline or count a transfer acknowledgment as delivery. An acceptable response defines observable cash-at-beneficiary evidence, separates originating and confirming roles, gives risk an independent escalation route, and makes compensation eligibility contingent on verified timely performance with a review/appeal process. Those design choices do not establish truthful incentives without assumptions about preferences, information, collectibility and actual control dependence. Two approvals based on the same failed data feed are not two independent observations. Record a control's tested failure mode and evidence rather than inventing a numerical effectiveness probability.

The sponsor absorbs the separate 2 H pension funding cost and the risk of its purchased bank shares. In joint stress, its marked bank stake is (5/13)(1246/45) = 1246/117 H; its remaining cash is 8 H. The private secured lender receives 4.2 H from a funded draw of 4 H and retains its original undrawn cash; this is a particular full-payment state, not a promise of universal recovery. No public treasury is secretly paying. A proposed debt conversion may allocate creditor losses or change rights; it creates no cash. Do not describe it as an interchangeable substitute for the actual F prefunding or pension grant.

The short governance framing reuses dated 2008 liquidity guidance on available resources and execution, the inspected 2018 stress principles on coherent objectives and challenge, and the scoped 2015 governance passages on oversight and incentives. They are international guidance, not Portlight's legal mandate, a current national ratio or evidence that the invented states are likely. This assessment imports no new legal power or empirical calibration.

6. What constitutes a completed project

An adequate evidence package contains independently implemented cash logic, all candidate outcomes, the selected three full journals, original and changed contracts, exact arithmetic anchors, invalid-domain tests, and the source/evidence register. A valid alternative implementation need not mimic the reference file structure. Its oracle must derive expected values from rights and resources, not simply call the same function twice or compare a saved report. Explain why optimization over 18 policies is exhaustive for this menu, while uncertainty about the scenarios, quoted marks, actual delivery and legal rights remains.

The oral defense should connect every chapter: intermediation and maturity transformation; capital versus usable cash; primary repayment and senior collateral; tail atoms and loss definitions; dated funding and collateral; delivery, controls and model scope; joint and reverse stress; benefit funding and guarantee limits; currency and cross-border access; allocated losses versus funded continuity; transmission through a payment network; and accountable decisions and revision. Name one important omitted mechanism and the evidence or new contract needed to model it. Simulating many arbitrary states, asserting a crisis probability, or claiming real supervisory authority does not compensate for a missing dated resource.
