Question 1 — Cash matching and a curve that does not identify dynamics · 20 points · D1

Dates 0, 1 and 2 are years. A default-free bill Z pays 100 at year 1 and costs 99 today. An amortizing instrument A pays 50 at year 1 and 52 at year 2 and has dirty price 102.0096. There are no other cash flows, accrued coupons, fees or taxes. Both instruments can be held in signed real quantities with immediately reusable short-sale proceeds. Lending and borrowing follow the same dated prices; there is no separate costless currency-storage right. Let D₁ and D₂ be prices of one certain CU at the respective dates.

A liability pays 40 at year 1 and 60 at year 2. Define independent continuously compounded zero-rate perturbations by Dᵢ(εᵢ)=Dᵢe−tᵢεᵢ, with t₁=1 and t₂=2; the other discount coordinate and all contractual cash remain fixed. Positive key-date exposure means Kᵢ=−∂PV/∂εᵢ at zero.

Two alternative models for the future one-year discount P(1;2) are proposed. Model U takes values 1.06 in state L and 0.98 in state H, with stipulated pricing probabilities Q(L)=Q(H)=1/2. Model C makes P(1;2)=1.02 certain. Both use the observed one-year discount D₁. Under Model U a separate physical forecast has P(L)=3/4 and P(H)=1/4. A new claim pays max(P(1;2)−1,0) at year 1. No quote for this claim or rate-volatility data is supplied.

  1. Bootstrap D₁ and D₂. Derive the effective one-year forward rate from year 1 to year 2 by matching dated cash. Give its sign and explain why that sign does not by itself establish arbitrage under the stated trading rights.
  2. Compute the liability's PV and two key-date exposures. Find holdings x of A and y of Z that match both exposures. Give the portfolio's price and its receipts at both dates. Prove whether this particular hedge continues to match the liability under every positive replacement pair (D₁′,D₂′), and distinguish the reason from a generic scalar-duration match.
  3. Show that Models U and C both fit the observed two-year zero. Price the year-1 claim under each. Does exact initial-curve calibration identify its price? Show what would happen to the two-year zero price if Model U's physical probabilities replaced its supplied Q probabilities, and interpret the failure.
