Oral transfer: answer an unfamiliar challenge

After the written form, use a proposed 30-minute session with a human evaluator. The five prompts below are practice specimens, 4 points each. For a recorded advancement attempt, the evaluator must change the case or assumption before the learner sees it, retain the exact new prompt and independently verify its answer. Use one follow-up to test a weak assumption. Record whether each point was demonstrated independently, only after a cue, or not demonstrated; a cue-dependent point does not count as independent success. Require at least 17/20, at least 3/4 per task and correction of every critical error. These are proposed instructional criteria. A script read aloud is practice, not evidence of spontaneous defense.

  1. Two papers, one evidential base. An unfamiliar panel member says: “Two studies confirm the mechanism.” You are given only these descriptions: Note P regresses bank stock changes on funding access in ledger L; Note Q uses the same banks, dates and ledger L but divides changes by opening stocks. Both are observational, and their full methods have not been supplied. Give a bounded oral response, a useful next question and a decision about whether the evidence establishes independent replication.
  2. Carry a correction through the argument. During a defense, a verified correction changes one bank’s closing stock; (opening, original closing, corrected closing) are (80,96,88). The original abstract claimed “20% growth proves improved credit access.” The program faithfully reproduced 96. Calculate the corrected growth and its change in percentage points, then give an immediate response and release plan.
  3. Respond to selective significance. A study predeclared two outcomes as one family and a Bonferroni familywise level and valid unadjusted p-values (0.05,0.03,0.04). A discussant calls both confirmatory discoveries because each is below 0.05. Correct the decision and explain what the p-values do not establish about power or identification. Assume the stated p-values are valid under their respective nulls.
  4. Transport a result under challenge. There are two observed types. Source type shares are (1/2,1/2) and causal effects are (1,3). Target shares are (3/4,1/4). Compute source and transported effects if type-specific effects are stable. Then allow target effects (-1,1), with the same support and shares. Give the actual target effect and an oral response to “overlap guarantees transport.”
  5. Defend an incomplete portfolio honestly. A candidate has a runnable code archive and a friendly practice talk. The literature review includes only abstracts; the paper’s central proposition has no checked proof; no qualified external reader has evaluated novelty. The candidate asks the panel to certify an independent original contribution. Give a complete decision, a prioritized revision request and a defensible continuation gate.
Complete answers and additive scoring

  1. A complete response is: “These are two analyses of one underlying set of observations. The denominator change may answer a useful different measurement question, but it does not create independent data. Neither description states an assignment mechanism or sufficient adjustment conditions, so I cannot yet infer causality. I would obtain both full methods and exact sample/variable definitions, trace their common ledger and compare estimands and identifying restrictions. A feasible question is whether the funding-access association survives a predeclared measurement correction within the common sample. I cannot yet claim an original mechanism result or independent replication.” Even matching signs may result from shared data errors. A changed formula can be an extension of an analysis while leaving the evidence dependent.

    Rubric (4 points). Shared evidence 1; causal/source limits 1; concrete next question and inspection 1; bounded contribution/replication decision 1.

  2. Original growth is 16/80=1/5; corrected growth is 8/80=1/10, a change of −10 percentage points: (1/5,1/10,-10). A response is: “The correction halves the measured growth to 10%. Our program reproduced the old source but could not validate its value. I withdraw the 20% claim and the causal access language: a bank stock increase by itself does not establish improved access. I will preserve the original release, document the verified source correction, rerun affected tables and figures, revise the abstract and conclusion, and label the corrected version clearly.” Check other records derived from the same source and whether sample membership or weights change. Do not silently overwrite the input or claim that agreement with old output refutes the correction.

    Rubric (4 points). Rates/change 1; bounded immediate statement 1; complete propagation/version plan 1; reproduction and source-validity distinction 1.

  3. Each comparison uses 0.05/2=0.025; neither rejects. The adjusted p-values are min(1,2p), giving (0.025,0.06,0.08). Under either or both true nulls, the probability of at least one false rejection is at most the sum of the two valid marginal bounds, at most 0.05; independence is unnecessary. A suitable response is: “Both point estimates may be interesting, but neither clears the declared confirmatory rule. Report all outcomes, their intervals and the family definition; label further exploration. These realized p-values do not tell us the design’s prospective power, and valid testing of a statistical contrast does not supply its missing causal identification.” Do not retroactively split the family to rescue a result.

    Rubric (4 points). Threshold and decisions 1; adjusted values 1; union-bound logic 1; prospective power/causality and honest reporting 1.

  4. The source mean is 2; stable-effect transport yields (3/4)1+(1/4)3=3/2. With changed target effects it is (3/4)(−1)+(1/4)1=−1/2. Thus (2,3/2,-1/2). A response is: “Overlap means both target types occur in the source. It allows the weighting operation but does not ensure that interventions, mechanisms, implementation or effect modifiers are comparable. The same overlap is consistent with a target effect of −1/2. I need a justified conditional effect-stability argument, correct type measurement and target shares, and a common intervention/outcome definition; otherwise I would present the 3/2 estimate as conditional and analyze departures.” This counterexample changes conditional effects, not merely population composition.

    Rubric (4 points). Source/conditional transport 1; counterexample value 1; overlap versus stability 1; concrete conditions and qualified response 1.

  5. A defensible decision is: “The archive and talk are useful evidence of preparation, but originality and correctness remain unestablished. I would not certify the contribution yet. First state the exact proposition, domain and assumptions, finish its proof or revise it around a counterexample, and reconcile the paper’s claims with that result. Read the closest full papers and build a claim-by-claim comparison. Then submit a versioned paper, source map, proof and reproduction materials to qualified readers who can challenge both novelty and validity. Keep their actual comments and a response memo, and conduct a defense with unseen changes of assumption. Continue only when material objections are answered or the contribution is narrowed honestly.” A favorable private conversation is not a documented evaluation of the unresolved tasks. Passing the synthetic exercise records rehearsal success only.

    Rubric (4 points). Evidence-based decision 1; prioritized correctness and literature work 1; exact external submission/response 1; honest continuation/defense gate 1.

Delayed retention and changed transfer

After a proposed three-week interval, attempt these five new tasks in 90 minutes without notes or code, using a basic calculator. Each carries 8 points. Require at least 34/40, at least 6/8 per task and correction of critical errors. If the learner has already studied these answers, administer materially changed cases with independently checked solutions. Preserve the delay, exact task, first attempt, assistance and correction rather than representing familiarity as retention. Thresholds and timing are not calibrated.

  1. Reconstruct a literature decision. After a study interval, use only these complete fictional notes. Note U: “For four banks with opening stocks 10 each and closing stocks (11,11,9,9), mean individual growth is zero. We describe one period without treatment variation; the observation cannot estimate a funding-policy effect.” Note V: “Using the same records as U, total opening and closing stocks are both 40. Aggregate growth is zero. This confirms a new causal law: funding policy never changes lending.” Write a small evidence map and a concise referee reply to V, including a feasible follow-up question. Do not invent other literature or an actual policy assignment.
  2. Rebuild an aggregate from units. Bank A reports opening and closing stocks (1.2,1.3) million. Bank B reports (400,440) thousand of the same currency. Construct common-unit totals, aggregate growth and equal-bank mean growth. State a unit test and write a two-sentence results note including what is not identified.
  3. Repair a design before observing outcomes. For a prospective equal-arm independent-sample normal-mean comparison, use the supplied approximate planning formula n≈2σ²(zα/2+zpower)²/δ² per arm. Assume (common known σ, target difference δ, zα/2, zpower)=(2,1,1.96,0.84), for two-sided 5% significance and target power 80%. Compute the integer per-arm and total target. Capacity is only 120 completed observations. State a feasible prospective response and two reasons this calculation may not describe an actual field design.
  4. Report bias separately from sampling error. A normal benchmark estimate of an effect and its known standard error are (0.9,0.2). Suppose the estimator’s mean is θ+b, where unknown bias b lies in [0,B]. Use the supplied rounded 1.96 quantile. Derive an interval that covers θ whenever the usual sampling-error event holds. Compute it for B=0.4 and B=0.6; explain whether a positive effect is robust in each case and whether the bias limit was estimated.
  5. Choose the next research step. A paper’s code reproduces its tables. Its theoretical counterexample is correct on a documented parameter domain. Two closest papers have now been read fully, but no new data have been collected. A qualified reader writes: “The counterexample may be useful; I cannot yet tell whether the claimed mechanism is distinct from the second paper.” A separate reviewer identifies a restricted dataset whose access has not been granted. Write a response memo, a next-month research plan with exact hours totaling at most 80, and a stop or revision rule. Do not claim that an encouraging comment establishes originality.
Complete answers and additive scoring

  1. The map has two rows: U uses four equally sized banks, estimates mean individual growth, reports zero and explicitly limits its conclusion to a descriptive period; V reuses exactly the same data, estimates aggregate stock growth, also obtains zero, and adds an unsupported causal law. Both share one evidence base. The individual rates are (1/10,1/10,−1/10,−1/10); both reported zeroes are correct because opening weights are equal. (0,0) checks the means. A full reply is: “Your aggregate accounting calculation is correct, but it neither provides independent evidence nor estimates a policy effect. With no assignment or counterfactual, many policy effects can coexist with zero observed aggregate growth. Replace the causal-law claim with the descriptive reconciliation, explain why the two estimands agree here, and retain the shared-data provenance. A useful follow-up is whether changed opening weights make bank-average and aggregate growth diverge in a documented panel. A causal extension requires a separately defended design and actual evidence.” This comparison supports a limited measurement contribution in the supplied dossier; a broader novelty claim requires reading and searching the real literature.

    Rubric (8 points). Two accurate map rows 2; arithmetic and equal-weight explanation 2; major concern with counterfactual reasoning 2; constructive repair and bounded follow-up 2.

  2. Express B as (0.4,0.44) million. Total stocks are 1.6 and 1.74 million; growth is 0.14/1.6=7/80. Individual rates are 1/12 and 1/10, whose equal mean is 11/120. The checked tuple is (1.6,1.74,7/80,11/120). Opening-stock weights (3/4,1/4) applied to individual rates recover 7/80. A unit test converts both records instead to thousands and verifies unchanged growth and totals multiplied by 1000; rejecting missing or contradictory unit labels prevents silent addition of incompatible scales. A results note is: “Outstanding stock for the two recorded banks rose from 1.6 to 1.74 million, or 8.75%; equal-bank growth was approximately 9.17%. These descriptive stock measures do not establish new originations, a causal intervention effect or growth outside the two-bank population.” Preserve the raw unit labels and transformation log.

    Rubric (8 points). Normalization/totals 2; two growth estimands and weights 2; meaningful unit test/provenance 2; bounded two-sentence writing 2.

  3. The continuous target is 2×4×(2.8)²=62.72, so round up to 63 per arm, 126 total. Capacity is short by 6: (62.72,63,126,6). The formula is a planning approximation, not an exact guarantee even in its benchmark model. One defensible response is to seek capacity for six more completed observations before launch; if unavailable, retain balanced 60-person arms and explicitly revise the feasibility/power claim, or narrow the proposed question using a separately justified effect target and replan before looking at outcomes. Do not silently describe 120 as the originally calculated target or change the primary outcome after inspecting significance. Clustered assignment changes effective information; differential attrition can lower sample size and threaten identification; noncompliance changes the estimand or detectable contrast. Document assignment, outcome timing, exclusions, familywise rule and contingencies prospectively. A larger sample cannot repair an invalid comparison.

    Rubric (8 points). Formula substitution 2; rounding/total/capacity 2; honest prospective repair 2; two concrete design limitations with identification distinction 2.

  4. On the event |estimate−(θ+b)|≤1.96×0.2=0.392, θ lies in [estimate−b−0.392, estimate−b+0.392]. Taking the union over b∈[0,B] gives [0.508−B,1.292]. For B=0.4 it is [0.108,1.292]; for B=0.6 it is [−0.092,1.292]. Thus (0.108,1.292,-0.092,1.292). The first excludes zero positively under its maintained bias bound and normal sampling model; the second includes zero and negative values. The bias-adjusted point-effect sets [0.5,0.9] and [0.3,0.9] are both positive, but they omit sampling uncertainty. Neither B value was learned from these three numerical inputs: each is a substantive sensitivity restriction requiring justification. The union argument ensures at least the sampling event’s approximate 95% coverage for any fixed permitted b; it does not assign probabilities to bias values or claim exact 95% coverage of the wider union.

    Rubric (8 points). Sampling event and union derivation 2; both intervals 2; point versus interval/sign distinctions 2; assumption and coverage scope 2.

  5. A complete response memo is: “Thank you for separating the valid counterexample from its contribution. I will add a side-by-side comparison with the second paper, including its maintained assumptions, mechanism and claimed domain. I will ask you to review the precise distinction and revise or withdraw the novelty statement if the example is already implied there. The unapproved dataset will not enter the analysis; I will investigate authorized access and document feasibility without claiming results. The current paper remains a theory-based candidate contribution with reproducible illustrative tables.” A feasible 80-hour plan assigns 25 to the mechanism/assumption comparison, 20 to proof and boundary extensions, 15 to the revised paper and response, 10 to qualified feedback and preparation for unseen defense, and 10 to access feasibility or a public-data alternative. (25,20,15,10,10,80) records the allocation. End the month with a versioned revised proposition, comparison and response, not a promised acceptance. If the closest paper subsumes the result, narrow to a clearly labeled pedagogical counterexample or replace the research question; if access is denied, drop or redesign the empirical extension. Do not interpret a delayed response as approval. Preserve actual feedback and acknowledge which gates remain incomplete.

    Rubric (8 points). Specific response and contribution comparison 2; honest access/evidence status 2; feasible exact plan/deliverables 2; substantive stop/revision and external-review gate 2.

From research rehearsal to real scholarship

The central chain is question → evidence and assumptions → argument → challenge → revision. A failure at one link changes what the whole paper can claim. A correct proof need not be novel; a reproducible table need not measure the intended object; a credible source-population effect need not transport; and a clear talk need not withstand an unfamiliar objection. Preserve these distinctions in the final paper and portfolio rather than hiding them in appendices.

To continue, choose a narrow question, read the closest full research, establish feasible and warranted access, complete the appropriate proof or empirical design, and maintain a reproducible claim-to-output record. Submit actual work to qualified readers, respond to consequential objections, and defend a changed assumption. The later research capstone organizes this submission and examination; the teaching volume then asks you to teach these reasoning processes and evaluate another learner’s work. Leadership later adds responsibility for people and resources. The library supplies instruction and protocols; those human achievements remain to be demonstrated.
