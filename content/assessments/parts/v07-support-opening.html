Volume 7 assessment — Accounting and business analysis

Turn business events into reconciled statements, explain the gap between cash and profit, and test whether a proposed decision can actually be financed.

Complete Chapters 1–12 before the examinations. This package supplies a prerequisite diagnostic, two distinct examinations, a historical reconstruction and forecasting project, full solutions, scoring criteria, oral transfer and delayed retention. All companies, transactions and contractual terms in the assessments are original fictional teaching cases. Stipulated measurement and tax rules govern each case; they are not an assertion of universal reporting requirements.

Prerequisite diagnostic

Before starting the volume, allow 30 minutes, without notes, using a basic calculator. There are eight four-point items: two points for the calculation or decision and two for the explanation. Aim for 28/32 and repair every missed distinction. These questions require the foundations in Volume 1, not accounting methods that this volume has yet to teach.

  1. A business has sales of 300 and total costs of 240 in Year 1, then sales of 360 and costs of 270 in Year 2, all in thousands of currency units. Calculate both profit margins, sales growth and profit growth. Distinguish percentage points from percentages.
  2. Cash starts at 70. Payments of 90 fall due on Monday and receipts of 110 arrive on Friday. Calculate closing cash and Monday's funding gap if no borrowing is arranged. Does a positive Friday balance establish that every payment can be met on time?
  3. An entity has assets of 140 and liabilities of 95. It then borrows 20 in cash, with no fees or other events. Find its new assets, liabilities and residual equity. Is the borrowed cash an increase in owners' net wealth at that instant?
  4. A room can be rented out this week for 12. A proposed workshop would instead generate receipts of 20 and additional cash costs of 11. Renovations paid for last year cost 50 and cannot be recovered. Compare the workshop with renting, using relevant incremental amounts.
  5. Solve 15q − 9q − 120 = 0 for q, with q measured in units and monetary amounts in currency units. Interpret the solution and explain why it is not a forecast of demand.
  6. A certain payment of 121 arrives exactly two years from now. At a constant annual effective discount rate of 10%, find its present value. State the timing and compounding assumptions.
  7. Company A reports sales of 120 for twelve months; Company B reports 100 for nine months. Can one infer that A's annual sales capacity is greater? What does multiplying B's amount by 12/9 assume?
  8. After a new manager arrives, customer collections improve. Give an alternative explanation to managerial skill, one useful evidence request and one distinction between a historical observation and a forecast assumption.
Diagnostic answers and remediation
  1. Profits are 60 and 90. Margins are 60/300 = 20% and 90/360 = 25%, an increase of five percentage points or 25% relative to the old margin. Sales grow 20%; profit grows 50%. A larger percentage change in profit is consistent with these costs, not evidence of a calculation error. Review units and percentage changes.
  2. Closing cash is 70 − 90 + 110 = 90. Monday's gap is 20. Friday's receipt cannot settle Monday's obligation without an agreed bridge or changed payment schedule. A period-end cash total loses intraperiod timing. Review stocks and flows and credit.
  3. Initial equity is 45. New assets are 160 and liabilities 115, so equity remains 45. The cash comes with an equal claim; it is financing rather than an immediate gain. Review balance sheets.
  4. The workshop nets 9 versus rent of 12, so its incremental advantage is −3. Renting is preferable on these stated monetary facts. The irrecoverable 50 is sunk and common to both choices; the foregone rent is a current opportunity cost. Unstated educational or strategic benefits would need to be named and valued separately. Review opportunity cost.
  5. 6q = 120 gives q = 20. Each unit contributes six toward the fixed 120. This is the sales quantity needed to break even within the assumed cost and price model; no evidence about customers' willingness to buy has been provided. Review algebra and marginal decisions.
  6. 121/(1.10)² = 100. The payment arrives at the end of two annual periods, the rate compounds annually, and the stated certain-payment discount rate is applicable. Confusing 10% with a gross factor of 0.10 would produce the wrong value. Review dated value.
  7. The periods differ, so the ranking does not follow. Straight annualization gives 400/3 ≈ 133.33 for B but assumes its nine-month average is representative of the missing months. Seasonality, acquisitions or capacity changes can invalidate that assumption. Obtain comparable periods and entity boundaries. Review comparable units and conditional arguments.
  8. A change in customer mix, an older invoice reaching its normal payment date, or a seasonal collection cycle could explain the improvement. Request invoice-level due dates and receipts across comparable periods, including customer composition; this improves diagnosis but does not alone establish causality. A dated receipt is an observed event; assuming that the same collection rate will persist is a forecasting choice. Other specific, coherent answers receive full credit. Review evidence and explaining analysis.

Examination rules, coverage and advancement

Each form is 100 points and 180 minutes, closed notes and closed solutions, with a basic nonprogrammable calculator. No code or browsing is needed. Five questions each contain four five-point parts; each question assesses one essential domain. Show the reconciliation, relevant assumptions and interpretation. Exact fractions are acceptable; keep intermediate precision and round final displayed amounts consistently. Save your answers before opening solutions. Use Form B after remediation without rehearsing its answers. The forms differ in transaction structure, financing timing and decision problems; equal nominal scores do not establish empirical difficulty equivalence.

The library's default criterion is at least 85/100 overall, at least 15/20 in each domain, a satisfactory project and oral transfer. Apply the same numerical threshold to the project, with at least 75% in each rubric category. A material unbalanced statement, unsupported financing plug, double-counted cash flow, unsupported fraud conclusion, or fabricated source requires correction even if the aggregate score passes. A single arithmetic slip earns carry-through credit when subsequent logic is consistent; do not penalize the same mistake repeatedly. Equivalent correct methods and well-supported alternative decisions earn equal credit. These are author-designed learning thresholds and estimated workloads, not institutional standards or psychometrically calibrated examinations.

Use each question's stated currency scale, dates, entity boundary and accounting rules. A separately introduced case is a separate company; do not import opening balances or taxes from another question. A cash shortfall against a minimum balance differs from inability to make any payment at all. Annual closing feasibility does not certify monthly liquidity. The project and oral work explicitly test those distinctions.
