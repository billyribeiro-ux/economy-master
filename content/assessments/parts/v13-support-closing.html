Oral transfer and proof defense

Prepare without the solutions, then explain each answer aloud to a reviewer. Allow roughly four minutes per prompt and follow-up discussion; this is a practice design. Each prompt has four points. Seek 17/20 with at least three points on each, and repair every critical error. A reviewer should change one assumption and ask which step survives. If studying alone, record the explanation, compare it with the benchmark and identify what would still require an independent expert to judge.

  1. Representations and social comparisons. Explain the admissible utility transformations for deterministic ordinal choice and for expected-utility preferences over lotteries. Show how multiplying one person's utility by a positive constant can change an interpersonal sum without changing that person's choices. Give a two-allocation numerical witness.
  2. Existence, clearing and welfare. Use diagnostic 4 to show why value-balanced excess demand is weaker than market clearing. Distinguish a price normalization from a general uniqueness theorem. State the assumptions that would have to be audited before applying a welfare theorem.
  3. Best replies and credibility. Give an explicit game with unique own best replies and multiple Nash equilibria. Then explain how a normal-form Nash threat can fail sequential credibility. Distinguish existence, uniqueness and a selection or adjustment rule.
  4. A globally checked hidden action. A risk-neutral agent chooses hidden L or H, with costs zero or one and success probabilities 1/4 or 3/4. Nonnegative wages w₀,w₁ depend on failure/success; outside utility is zero. The agent accepts employment when indifferent to its outside option. A principal has four cash units before payroll and receives value four after success. Find the lowest expected wage that weakly implements H, compare profit with inducing L, and explain what strict implementation and an enlarged action set would change.
  5. Defend the matching theorem. A referee asserts that proposer optimality of deferred acceptance proves truthful reporting. Locate the logical gap and give the actual noncircular proof route. Explain the roles of outside options, recipient reports and restrictions that allow only a first-choice report.
Oral benchmarks and criteria

  1. A strictly increasing transformation preserves a deterministic ordinal ranking. A von Neumann–Morgenstern expected-utility representation over lotteries allows positive affine transformations, not arbitrary nonlinear transformations. Positive scaling of one person's utility belongs to both classes. Consider allocations with utility vectors (3,0) and (0,2): their equal-weight sums are (3,2). Double only the second person's utility; the sums become (3,4). Individual rankings remain unchanged, but the chosen sum reverses. Interpersonal weights or a justified common money metric add structure to individual preferences. Award one point each for the ordinal domain, the lottery domain, an exact reversal witness and the interpersonal interpretation. A different valid witness earns full credit.

  2. At r=2, excess demands are (−1/2,1): their value is zero but neither quantity clears. In that particular economy, solving both optimization and clearing equations yields a unique normalized relative price and allocation. Multiplying every price by a positive constant changes only units; normalization does not eliminate economically different relative-price equilibria in other models.

    A first-welfare-theorem argument needs price-taking optimizing choices, feasible resource accounts and ownership, an appropriate local nonsatiation condition, and the absence of unpriced effects in its stated version. Supporting a chosen efficient allocation instead requires its own convexity/separation and transfer assumptions, and a quasiequilibrium support must be upgraded to full choice optimality under the conditions actually proved. Existence, efficiency and decentralization are distinct conclusions. Award one point each for the nonclearing witness, normalization distinction, scoped first-welfare reasoning and separate support/transfer requirements.

  3. In the two-agent substitute game uᵢ=xᵢ−xᵢ²/2−xᵢxⱼ with xᵢ≥0, each player has the unique best reply max(0,1−xⱼ), yet every (z,1−z), 0≤z≤1, is Nash. A best-response function need not have a unique fixed point. In diagnostic 6, (Out,Fight) is normal-form Nash but the incumbent would accommodate after entry, so it is not subgame perfect. Existence establishes that the solution set is nonempty; uniqueness makes it a singleton. Selection or a convergent adjustment law needs further structure even when the equations are solved correctly. Award one point for the game and action domain, one for its complete continuum, one for the actual profitable continuation deviation, and one for the three distinct claims.

  4. High effort's global IC inequality is (3w₁+w₀)/4−1≥(w₁+3w₀)/4, equivalent to w₁−w₀≥2. Expected high-effort wage is w₀/4+3w₁/4≥w₀+3/2. Limited liability therefore gives the minimizer (0,2), expected wage 3/2. Both H and the deviation to L then give agent utility 1/2. Weak implementation uses an H-at-indifference convention; it does not force H uniquely. For strict IC, w₁>w₀+2, so the same cost is an unattained infimum, approached by w₀=0,w₁=2+ε with small positive ε. These wages can be prefunded from four.

    Under the weak implementation convention, high effort yields principal expected profit 3−3/2=3/2. With zero wages, L is strictly better than H conditional on accepting employment. L gives zero utility, equal to the outside option, so actual employment also uses the stated accept-at-indifference convention. Under that convention the principal expected profit is 1. The comparison covers exactly the two declared actions. If another hidden action is available, its IC inequality must also be checked; a local derivative or two-action calculation cannot exclude it. Award one point for global IC/IR, one for the optimal wage and funding, one for the profit comparison, and one for both tie and action-domain limits.

  5. Proposer optimality compares stable outcomes for one fixed reported profile. A lie changes that profile and its stable set, so the comparison alone does not cover the lie's outcome. For finite strict one-to-one matching, first prove that every stable matching leaves the same agents unmatched, using the alternating path and its no-cycle argument. Run institution-proposing DA with applicant i rejecting every offer to define a menu independent of i's report, including outside.

    A singleton report preserving i's actual partner, unmatched-set invariance and a coupled first offer put each attainable partner in this menu. Truncate the report strictly above its actual partner and reproduce the applicant-DA prefix: i exhausts the list and remains unmatched. The lemma makes it unmatched in institution DA too; a receiver who accepts an acceptable proposal cannot later become unmatched. Coupling therefore rules out every reported-better menu offer. Every report chooses its reported-best menu member, and truth chooses its true best. The proof covers false acceptability because outside remains available. It holds the institution reports fixed; recipients have a different strategic role, and a report cap may exclude the full true preference list. Award one point for the logical gap, one for the fixed-menu/unmatched lemma, one for both inclusion and exclusion, and one for outside/report-domain limits. A citation or finite example alone is insufficient.

Delayed retention with changed problems

Return after a study interval, such as one to two weeks, without consulting your earlier solutions. Use paper and a basic calculator; propose 90 minutes and extend it when a proof needs more time. The interval and allowance are study choices. Each domain carries ten points, totaling fifty. The retention criterion is 43/50 and at least eight points per domain, with critical errors repaired. A remembered answer without its derivation is not sufficient; explain how the new primitives affect the conclusion.

  1. Recover the dual problem. For u(x,y)=√(xy), prices (2,1) and wealth twelve, derive Marshallian demand. For a positive target utility ū, derive the expenditure function and Hicksian demands at arbitrary positive prices. Specialize to ū=3, verify cost, and check the expenditure derivative with respect to the first price.
  2. Preferred outcomes still need incentives. Two agents choose nonnegative efforts with uᵢ=2(x₁+x₂)−xᵢ²/2. Utilities are comparable, and effort cost is disutility rather than a cash invoice. Find every pure Nash outcome and the global sum-utility maximum. Compute individual payoffs at both. Does mutual preference for the planner allocation make that allocation Nash?
  3. Check every repeated-game history. A publicly observed stage game pays (3,3) at CC, (5,0) at DC, (0,5) at CD and (1,1) at DD. Repeat it infinitely with common discount δ∈[0,1) and the unnormalized sum of discounted payoffs. Grim trigger plays C until any D has occurred, then D forever. Derive the exact weak incentive threshold, justify optimality against arbitrary randomized history-dependent unilateral deviations, and evaluate δ=3/5.
  4. Truthfulness is not a revenue certificate. A risk-neutral buyer has value θ∈[0,4], outside utility zero and cash four. A seller owns one object with zero cost. Report r∈[0,4] yields allocation probability r/4 and a deterministic fee r²/8, charged whether or not the buyer receives the object. Participation occurs before the lottery. Prove dominant truthful reporting in expected utility and interim participation. Under uniform values on [0,4], find expected revenue and compare a posted price of two. State the limit on an ex-post participation claim.
  5. Consent and a funded network. In the chapter's three-node connections model, take ρ=1/2 and per-endpoint maintenance c=9/16. Classify pairwise-stable and efficient graphs and their welfare, using the chapter's both-weak/one-strict addition convention. For target path {12,23}, each leaf pays node 2 a transfer 1/8 whenever its designated edge exists; edge 13 carries no transfer. Everyone starts with two cash units and pays maintenance/transfers before benefits. Check the target's every-link incentives and funding at all graphs. Can balanced bilateral transfers make an improving first link from empty?
Retention solutions and scoring

  1. For positive prices and wealth, maximizing √(xy) is equivalent to maximizing xy. The budget binds; substitution or the first-order condition with global concavity of log utility gives x=m/(2pₓ), y=m/(2pᵧ). At the stated prices and wealth the bundle is (3,6). For expenditure minimization, xy≥ū² and the arithmetic–geometric mean inequality gives pₓx+pᵧy≥2√(pₓpᵧxy)≥2ū√(pₓpᵧ). Equality requires xy=ū² and pₓx=pᵧy, yielding

    hₓ=ū√(pᵧ/pₓ),   hᵧ=ū√(pₓ/pᵧ),   e(p,ū)=2ū√(pₓpᵧ).

    At ū=3 and prices (2,1), Hicksian demand is (3/√2,3√2) and expenditure is 6√2. Direct spending 2(3/√2)+3√2 equals 6√2. Differentiating the expenditure formula gives ∂e/∂pₓ=ū√(pᵧ/pₓ)=hₓ, as required. Positive prices and a positive target ensure the interior formula and equality conditions used here. Credit: Marshallian derivation and value 2; global expenditure lower bound 2; equality and Hicksian formula 2; specialization/account 2; derivative and domain 2.

  2. With the other action fixed, own utility is 2xᵢ−xᵢ²/2 plus a constant. Its unique nonnegative maximizer is two, so the complete Nash set is the singleton (2,2), with payoffs (6,6) and welfare 12. Aggregate welfare satisfies

    W(x)=4(x₁+x₂)−(x₁²+x₂²)/2=16−[(x₁−4)²+(x₂−4)²]/2.

    This identity proves the unique global planner allocation (4,4), payoffs (8,8) and welfare 16. Both players prefer it to Nash. Nevertheless, when the other chooses four, reducing one's own action to two raises own utility to 10, above eight. A Pareto comparison among allocations does not establish that the desired allocation survives individual deviations. Credit: global best replies/complete Nash 2; Nash accounts 2; global planner certificate 2; planner accounts 2; profitable deviation and interpretation 2.

  3. After a defection, the opponent plays D forever. Against D, one's own D strictly improves the stage payoff from zero to one, so every punishment history is credible. At any still-cooperative history, complying gives 3/(1−δ); a first defection can give at most 5+δ/(1−δ), because optimal continuation after the trigger is D. Cooperation is a weak best reply exactly when δ≥1/2.

    This comparison also covers full history-dependent plans. Conditional on any possible first deviation time, the continuation gain is the same displayed difference multiplied by that time's discount. After the deviation no plan improves on the per-period best reply D. When the inequality holds, each conditional gain is nonpositive; averaging over randomized first-deviation times preserves this conclusion, while never deviating has zero gain. Bounded payoffs and δ<1 make discounted tails vanish. When the inequality fails, an immediate defection at a cooperative history profits. Thus the condition is necessary and sufficient for the proposed public grim strategies to be subgame perfect. At equality some choices tie; uniqueness is not claimed.

    At δ=3/5, compliant value is 15/2, immediate-deviation value is 13/2, and the difference is 1. Perfect public monitoring, an infinite horizon and the stated common discount are substantive assumptions. Credit: punishment histories 2; exact cooperative inequality/threshold 2; all-history/randomized sufficiency and converse 3; numerical values 2; monitoring/boundary scope 1.

  4. The expected utility from report r is θr/4−r²/8=θ²/8−(r−θ)²/8. Every feasible report therefore loses (r−θ)²/8 relative to truth. This is a global report comparison, with truthful expected utility θ²/8≥0. Fees are at most 2, below the buyer's endowed four. Under the uniform prior, E[θ²]=16/3, so expected revenue is 2/3. A price of two sells to values above two with probability one half; the boundary type has zero probability, and revenue is 1. Hence dominant truthfulness and interim participation do not establish revenue optimality.

    For a positive type below four, a losing lottery realization still incurs a positive fee and negative realized utility. The model permits participation before the lottery under risk neutrality. It does not give ex-post individual rationality, and the expected-utility square is not a proof for an arbitrarily risk-averse buyer. Credit: exact square/global IC 3; interim IR/funding 2; uniform expected revenue 2; posted-price comparison 1; participation timing and preference limits 2.

  5. The stability thresholds are ρ−ρ²=1/4 and ρ=1/2; the path–empty efficiency threshold is ρ+ρ²/2=5/8. Since c=9/16 lies strictly between the last two, only empty is pairwise stable, while all three labeled paths are efficient. Welfare by architecture—empty, single edge, path, triangle—is (0,−1/8,1/4,−3/8). These statements concern the original no-transfer game.

    At the designated path, each leaf's benefit net of maintenance is 1/2+1/4−9/16=3/16; the center's is 1−18/16=−1/8. Applying the two conditional transfers gives payoffs (1/16,1/8,1/16), still summing to one quarter. A center deleting one edge retains one direct benefit, one maintenance charge and one receipt, giving 1/2−9/16+1/8=1/16; it loses 1/16. A leaf deleting its edge becomes isolated and loses 1/16. Adding edge 13 changes no transfer and gives each leaf gain −5/16. Every allowed deletion/addition at the target is unprofitable, so it is pairwise stable under the stated contract.

    At any graph a leaf's maximum pre-benefit obligation is 2c+1/8=5/4, and the center can pay maintenance at most 2c=9/8 before any receipts. Both are below the initial two. Transfers sum to zero at every graph; maintenance consumes resources. A first isolated edge creates aggregate surplus 2ρ−2c=−1/8. No balanced transfer between its endpoints can make both weakly gain, because their sum remains negative. All three can prefer the complete path package to empty, but jointly creating two edges is a different permissible move. Credit: original complete classifications/welfare 3; exact transfer payoffs 2; all target link incentives 2; every-graph prefunding and transfer/resource distinction 2; first-link impossibility/coordination 1.

Remediation and reassessment map

Diagnose the failed step before repeating a complete form. Rewrite the assumptions and the feasible set, solve a smaller counterexample, then attempt a fresh transfer without the solution. Record which error was corrected and why the new argument covers the original claim. A higher aggregate score does not erase an unresolved critical-domain error.

From individual choice to a defensible theory contribution

The volume's arguments form a chain. Preferences and information define what agents value and know. Optimization and duality describe their feasible choices. Equilibrium connects those choices through resources and beliefs; existence does not imply uniqueness. Welfare analysis adds its own distributional and measurement assumptions. Contracts and allocation rules change which deviations are attractive, while network design adds consent, feedback and formation constraints. At every stage, a valid theorem can support an invalid interpretation if its quantifiers, rights or empirical premises are silently enlarged.

A completed project should make that chain inspectable: original primitives, a precise result, a complete proof, an informative failure case, reproducible calculations and a limited economic interpretation. The actual-paper transfer requires engagement with a real theoretical contribution and honest reporting of access and proof limits. Passing these internal exercises is preparation for research and teaching; originality and professional judgment still require work assessed by independent people.

Carry the same discipline into the planned Graduate macroeconomics and macro-finance. A recursive problem needs a state and admissible history, a numerical solution needs a certificate at a stated domain, and a policy experiment needs resource accounts and defended invariant primitives. Similar-looking equations do not supply those assumptions automatically.

Distinguish a checked synthetic calculation from observed evidence, a proof within a model from proof of its real-world premises, and an internal assessment from independent evaluation of a research contribution.
