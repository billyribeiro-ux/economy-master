Form B — Investments and asset pricing reassessment

100 points; 240 minutes; closed book; nonprogrammable calculator permitted. Answer every question. Each question has four parts worth 5 points each and constitutes one 20-point essential domain. Allow about 48 minutes per question. Show cash timing, units, derivations and the assumptions behind an interpretation. Exact fractions or sensibly rounded decimals are acceptable; retain precision in intermediate work.

Every case, probability, quote, contract and observation below is original and synthetic. CU means currency units. Returns are simple total returns unless explicitly called excess returns, and all covariance entries use decimal-return units, not percentage-point units. There are no taxes, fees, distributions, cash flows or trading rights other than those stated. Different questions, and expressly separate panels within a question, are independent. A statistical expectation, a pricing weight, an appraisal and available cash are different objects.

The advancement rule is 85/100 overall and at least 15/20 in each domain D1–D5, followed by the separate transfer and teach-back assessment. These are the library's instructional thresholds, not an externally calibrated credential. An unresolved critical pricing, funding, timing or evidence-integrity error requires remediation even if the point totals pass.

Question 2 — Insurance in an incomplete market and a proxy pricing claim · 20 points · D2

Parts (a)–(c), one period. Exactly three terminal states L, M and H are possible, each with positive physical probability. A unit bond pays (1,1,1) and costs 19/20 at date 0. A traded asset S pays (1,2,4) and costs 41/20. There are initially no other traded claims. Holdings may be signed real quantities; short-sale proceeds can be used immediately; the bond can be bought or shorted at the same price; there are no transaction costs, position limits or intermediate margin calls. All payoffs settle at the same terminal date. State prices q must be strictly positive. Their sum is a discount factor rather than one.

A proposed insurance claim X pays (2,0,3). In part (c) it is offered for 12/5 with both buying and shorting permitted under the same frictionless contract. An initial cash receipt may be invested in the unit bond. Arbitrage must be demonstrated with an actual initial budget and a nonnegative terminal payoff that is positive in at least one possible state.

Part (d), a separate annual CAPM diagnostic. A safe simple return is 2%. A public proxy portfolio P has expected simple return 6% and variance 0.04. Asset A has expected simple return 9% and covariance 0.06 with P. These supplied population moments are not the preceding three-state market. For the theoretical derivation, the frictionless CAPM's market-clearing relation is given as μ−rf1=λΣwM, where M is the true aggregate risky market, 1′wM=1, λ>0 and Var(RM)>0. No assertion that P equals M is supplied.

  2. Find the complete interval of no-arbitrage prices for X, including whether its endpoints are allowed. Explain why every interior price has a strictly positive pricing extension and why a physical expected payoff alone does not choose one price from the interval.
  3. At the quoted price 12/5, construct an explicit zero-initial-cost arbitrage using X, S and the bond. Include the investment of any initial surplus, give every signed holding and show the terminal payoff in all three states.
  4. Starting from the given market-clearing relation, derive the true-market beta pricing line. Calculate A's beta and alpha relative to P. Explain why this proxy alpha alone neither refutes the true-market CAPM nor proves an executable arbitrage.

Question 3 — Omitted risk, net performance and an evaluation clock · 20 points · D3

Parts (a)–(b), synthetic monthly population moments. M and Z are excess returns on two specified traded funds relative to the same safe asset. Their means are E[M]=0.004 and E[Z]=0.006; variances are 0.0004 and 0.0009; covariance is 0.0003. A manager's gross excess return is Y=0.001+0.6M+0.4Z+ε, with E[ε]=0, Cov(ε,M)=Cov(ε,Z)=0 and Var(ε)=0.0001. These moments describe a projection exercise; no SDF or assertion that either observed mean is a correctly priced risk premium is supplied.

The manager charges a fixed fee of 0.002 per CU of beginning NAV at each month-end, so net excess return is Ynet=Y−0.002. The benchmark was specified in advance as 60% of the M fund and 40% of the Z fund, rebalanced at the start of this one-month measurement interval. Its excess return is B=0.6M+0.4Z, with no additional fee. Define monthly tracking error as the standard deviation of the net active return Ynet−B and information ratio as its mean divided by that tracking error. Do not annualize.

Part (c), separate construction audit. A purported January 2 characteristic sort uses financial accounts dated the previous December 31 that were first released publicly on March 20. No earlier release or private access existed. Separately, for cash accounting, a genuinely executable later one-period long–short position buys a 100 CU long leg using the reusable proceeds of a 100 CU short leg, and also posts 20 CU of investor collateral. At final close, total execution charges are 0.15 CU and stock-borrow charges are 0.35 CU, both paid from collateral. All positions close and collateral is returned; there are no other payments or financing costs.

Part (d), separate evaluation cohort. Before opening a one-year holdout, a protocol invests 100 CU in each of managers U, V and W and holds without reallocating.   1. Derive the slope and intercept when gross Y is projected on a constant and M alone. Compare the intercept with that of the full two-factor projection. Explain the omitted-factor mechanism without equating a projection intercept with causal skill.
  2. Find the net full-model intercept, net market-only intercept, net mean active return against B, monthly tracking error and information ratio. Explain why the market-only intercept's sign does not determine performance relative to the predeclared benchmark.
  3. Identify the information-timing error in the January sort and give a valid prospective repair. For the separately executable long–short position, compute gross CU profit, net CU profit and the investor's collateral return. Explain why zero net cost of the two security legs is not a denominator for an investment return.
  4. Compute the original three-manager cohort return and the database's survivor-only return. Diagnose both the sample-membership change and the claim about U's retuned holdout. Specify how a subsequent evaluation could preserve a meaningful information boundary.

Question 4 — A gated endowment, trading costs and a shared cash reserve · 20 points · D4

Mallow has 60 CU of liquid financial wealth at date 0, initially held as 45 CU in a risky fund and 15 CU cash. Cash earns zero. A chosen risky holding x pays x(1+Z) at date 1. There are two equally probable states, Good and Bad, with Z=(0.30,−0.10). Mallow also owns a separate nontradable gated investment with terminal payoff H=(34,26). Its last reported NAV is 30, but it cannot be sold, pledged or redeemed before date 1. These terminal payoffs already include all proceeds from that investment, conditional on honoring its contractual call of 22 CU at an intermediate date τ, with 0<τ<1. It makes no earlier distribution. No other liabilities exist.

All financial rebalancing is allowed only at date 0; neither risky holding nor gated investment can fund the call by a sale at τ. Mallow cannot borrow. Remaining date-0 cash must therefore cover the call. The objective is the stipulated currency-wealth score J=E[T]−(γ/2)Var(T), with γ=0.05 per CU and T total date-1 wealth after the call. This is a one-period commitment criterion, not a sequence of conditional mean-variance optimizations.

In part (a), temporarily ignore trading costs and the interim cash restriction, but keep the 22 payment in terminal wealth; impose only 0≤x≤60. In part (b), retain that relaxation of the interim restriction but introduce a date-0 cash fee K(x)=0.02|x−45|, with nonnegative post-trade cash. Part (c) restores the actual interim funding restriction. Apart from this fee, there are no costs or taxes.

Part (d), a proposed replacement for cash reserved for the call. Independently of the risky holding, an officer proposes using the 22 CU reserve as the collateral account for two long futures contracts. Each has multiplier one, enters at quote 50 with zero initial contract value, and marks at 40 at τ in the proposed stress. Immediately at τ, variation cash is settled, then the private call of 22 must be paid, and 5 CU must remain reserved as margin to keep the futures position open. Cash yield and all fees are zero in this proposal. The futures must remain open through the deadline; closing them early is not permitted in this proposal. No top-up, borrowing, early sale of the risky fund or gated redemption is authorized. The quoted NAV of the gated claim is not a settlement receipt.

  1. Express terminal wealth, derive its mean and variance as functions of x, and solve the relaxed frictionless allocation. Show how the covariance of the nontradable payoff with Z changes risky demand and why the stated optimum is global.
  2. Solve the cost-aware problem while still temporarily ignoring the call's cash deadline. Derive the relevant one-sided objective derivative, identify the optimal trade, and reconcile the post-trade risky holding, fee and cash. Explain why trading to the cost-free target need not maximize this score after costs.
  3. Restore the call's actual timing. Find the globally optimal funded date-0 risky holding, the sale quantity in CU, the fee and cash reserve. Prove why the relaxed solution from part (b) fails, and why the last NAV of the gated investment cannot repair it.
  4. For the officer's futures proposal, calculate variation cash, cash remaining after settlement, and the additional funding needed to both pay the private call and maintain margin in the stated order. Is the proposed strategy feasible under the supplied rights? Explain why treating margin as a separate asset or using the gated NAV as immediate cash would give a false answer.

Form B solutions, analytic scoring and error carry-forward

Form B — Complete solutions

Each answer below allocates exactly 5 points. Accept equivalent valid derivations and consistent rounding. Carry forward a student's earlier numerical error without a second arithmetic penalty when the later method is correct and the resulting problem remains meaningful. Keep method and interpretation credit available for an independently corrected result. Error carry-forward does not excuse using an impossible price, an unauthorized funding source, a future information set, or an undefined return denominator; require the student to recognize and repair such a contradiction. Do not award a policy-compliance point to a portfolio that fails a stated hard constraint.

Question 1 — Returns, covariance and allocation

  1. The first return is 240/200−1=20%. The withdrawal leaves 120, so the second is 110/120−1=−1/12≈−8.3333%. Linked gross growth is (6/5)(11/12)=11/10: two-year TWR is 10%, whose annualized equivalent is √1.1−1≈4.880885%.

    The investor's dated cash is (−200,+120,+110). With x=1+r, the IRR equation is −200+120/x+110/x²=0, or 200x²−120x−110=0. Its roots are x=1.1 and x=−0.5. Only x>0, equivalently r>−1, is admissible, so annual IRR is 10%. Its numerical equality to the two-year TWR is accidental; compare it with the annualized TWR when discussing rates per year. More capital was withdrawn before the second-period loss.

    Credit: both flow-correct subperiod returns, 1 point; linked two-year TWR, 1 point; annualized TWR, 1 point; correct investor IRR equation and root, 1 point; admissible-root and horizon interpretation, 1 point.

  2. Means are 3% and 4%. Centered A returns are (−0.05,−0.01,0.01,0.05), and centered B returns are (−0.05,0.03,−0.03,0.05). Their sums of squares are 0.0052 and 0.0068, and their cross-product sum is 0.0044. Dividing by three gives

    S = (1/7,500) [[13,11],[11,17]].

    For any vector z, z′Sz=(1/3)Σt[z′(Rt−R̄)]²≥0, proving PSD for common-date sample covariance. Here the first diagonal entry is positive and det(S)=(13×17−11²)/7,500²=100/7,500²>0, so the two-by-two symmetric matrix is positive definite. Neither property proves it is the population covariance.

    Credit: both means, 1 point; two variances with correct divisor, 1 point; covariance and symmetric matrix, 1 point; quadratic-form PSD reasoning, 1 point; strict positive-definiteness test, 1 point.

  3. With the common factor 1/7,500 retained, variance is

    v(w) = [13w²+22w(1−w)+17(1−w)²]/7,500 = (8w²−12w+17)/7,500.

    Thus v′=(16w−12)/7,500 and v″=16/7,500>0. The unconstrained minimum is w=3/4. The policy caps it at w=2/3, with B weight 1/3, because variance is still decreasing up to that bound. Its estimated variance is 113/67,500≈0.001674074. Strict convexity and the derivative sign certify the global constrained optimum; there is no hidden target-return requirement.

    Credit: correct variance function, 1 point; derivative and unconstrained weight, 1 point; both constrained weights, 1 point; variance at the choice, 1 point; convexity and boundary certificate, 1 point.

  4. Shrinkage leaves the two diagonals unchanged and changes the off-diagonal numerator from 11 to 11/2. The variance polynomial becomes (19w²−23w+17)/7,500. Its unique minimum is w=23/38 in A and 15/38 in B. Because 23/38<2/3, the cap no longer binds. The reported variance is 763/570,000≈0.001338596.

    The selected portfolio and the matrix used to measure it both changed. Comparing this number with 113/67,500 under the raw estimate is not a same-population risk comparison. Shrinkage is a predeclared estimator choice that can trade variance of estimation against bias; four observations do not certify its true-risk superiority. A common evaluation law or properly separated future evaluation, with uncertainty, is needed.

    Credit: correct shrunk covariance/polynomial, 1 point; both weights, 1 point; estimated variance, 1 point; interior-versus-binding explanation, 1 point; honest estimation/comparison limitation, 1 point.

Question 2 — State-contingent pricing and CAPM interpretation

  1. Pricing the bond and S gives qL+qM+qH=19/20 and qL+2qM+4qH=41/20. Set qH=t. Subtraction and substitution yield

    q(t) = (2t−3/20, 11/10−3t, t),   3/40<t<11/30.

    The lower bound makes qL positive; the upper makes qM positive; the lower also ensures t>0. For example t=1/5 gives q=(1/4,1/2,1/5), a strictly positive pricing vector. The two payoff columns are linearly independent but span only a two-dimensional subspace of three states. Thus no arbitrage is compatible with this menu, but it is incomplete and state prices are not unique.

    Credit: both pricing equations, 1 point; correct family, 1 point; both strict bounds, 1 point; positive-vector/no-arbitrage justification, 1 point; rank/completeness distinction, 1 point.

  2. The insurance price is 2qL+3qH=7t−3/10. The exact admissible interval is

    9/40 < pX < 34/15.

    Every interior quote corresponds to t=(pX+3/10)/7 in the interval from part (a), and hence a strictly positive state-price extension. X is not affine in S; adding it produces rank three. At an endpoint one state price becomes zero and an arbitrage exists. Explicitly, L=1.5S−3 bonds pays (−1.5,0,3)≤X and costs 9/40; U=(1/3)S+(5/3) bonds pays (2,7/3,3)≥X and costs 34/15. At the lower endpoint, X−L is a free nonzero nonnegative payoff; at the upper, U−X is one. Physical probabilities alone do not supply the risk adjustment needed to select an interior price.

    Credit: affine price expression, 1 point; both bounds, 1 point; strict endpoints with a valid span/hedging reason, 1 point; every interior price admits a positive extension, 1 point; physical-versus-pricing distinction, 1 point.

  3. Short one X and buy its superhedge U=(1/3)S+(5/3) bonds. This produces initial cash 12/5−34/15=2/15. Invest it in an additional (2/15)/(19/20)=8/57 bonds. Final holdings are −1 X, +1/3 S, +103/57 unit bonds. Their date-0 cost is

  4. The i-th component of the market-clearing relation is μi−rf=λCov(Ri,RM). Premultiplying by wM′ gives μM−rf=λVar(RM). Dividing yields μi=rf+βi,M(μM−rf).
