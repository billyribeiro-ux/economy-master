Cumulative examination — Form A

100 points; suggested 240 minutes; nonprogrammable calculator and blank paper only. Answer all five questions. Notes, internet and code are not permitted. Each question is one essential 20-point domain. This timing is an authored design target, not a calibrated student completion time. The common advancement rule is at least 85/100, at least 15/20 in every domain, and no unresolved critical error, followed by the separate project and oral transfer.

The fictitious Vale desk must value dated rights, finance its trades and decide what its models justify. All inputs are original synthetic teaching cases. The questions use separate markets unless a connection is stated; do not carry one question's curve, measure or trading rights into another. CU means currency units; a unit rate change is 1.00 in decimal units, and 0.01 is one percentage point. Show intermediate reasoning and cash dates. Exact expressions earn full credit; harmless final rounding is acceptable. No normal-CDF lookup is needed. For calculator convenience, e−0.015 = 0.9851119396, e−0.08 = 0.9231163464, e−0.12 = 0.8869204367 and e−0.20 = 0.8187307531.

Question 1 — A curve fit and a duration match are different restrictions (D1, 20 points)

Settlement is date 0. Safe cash claims pay at 0.5, 1.5 and 2.5 years. All instruments below have the same currency, credit and settlement rights and can be bought or shorted without costs. A claim paying 100 at 0.5 costs 98. A second bond pays 4 at 0.5 and 104 at 1.5; its dirty settlement price is 100.744. A third bond pays 5 at 0.5, 5 at 1.5 and 105 at 2.5; its dirty price is 101.955. The third bond pays annual coupons of 5, its previous coupon was at −0.5, and the stipulated accrued-interest convention allocates the annual coupon in direct proportion to elapsed time. Define D(t) as the price now of one certain CU at t and z(t) = −log D(t)/t.

  1. Bootstrap all three discount factors from the dated cash equations. Find the third bond's accrued interest and clean price. Explain why its clean quote is not the cash needed to buy it at settlement.
  2. A liability pays 100 at 1.5. Hedge it with x units of the claim paying one CU at 0.5 and y units paying one CU at 2.5. Choose x and y so hedge and liability have equal present values and equal first derivatives with respect to a common continuously compounded zero-rate shift z(t) ↦ z(t)+ε. Derive both matching equations and solve for the holdings.
  3. Now increase only z(1.5) by 0.01, holding the two other zero-rate coordinates and all cash rights fixed. Compute the first-order and exact change in hedge-minus-liability surplus for the holdings in (b). Explain why the initial duration match neither forces this surplus to stay zero nor makes this deterministic scenario a physical forecast.
  4. For the interval from 0.5 to 1.5, a separate two-branch rate model has one-year node discounts dL = 0.99 and dH = 0.91. At date 0 its pricing branch probabilities are Q(L) = Q(H) = 1/2, while physical probabilities are P(L) = 3/4 and P(H) = 1/4. The first half-year discount is the bootstrapped D(0.5). Verify the fitted D(1.5). Compute the effective one-year forward f = D(0.5)/D(1.5)−1, EQ[r] and EP[r], where r = d−1−1. Decompose f−EP[r] into a nonlinear averaging component and a measure-change component, and explain why the curve alone does not reveal the physical forecast.

Question 2 — Recovery and collateral are paid on dates (D2, 20 points)

Parts (a–b) concern a zero-coupon note with face 100, maturity T = 2 years, constant continuously compounded safe rate r = 0.04 and stipulated pricing-law default intensity λ = 0.06. Survival is Q(τ > t) = e−λt. Default terminates the note; there are no coupons. In contract I, default before maturity pays 50 CU immediately at τ; survival pays 100 at T. In contract M, the same default recovery is paid at T without further credit risk. These are separate contractual rights.

  1. Obtain the first-default density from survival. Derive and evaluate both prices by dating survival principal and recovery separately. Give the price difference and explain its sign without treating the recovery as a reduction in the promised face on every path.
  2. An analyst labels 0.06 “the observed two-year physical default probability” and says that the note's price uniquely reveals it even if recovery and a liquidity wedge are unknown. Correct both inferences. Compute the two-year pricing default probability supplied by this model and state what additional evidence would be required for the physical or separately identified claim.
  3. In a separate frictionless single-curve market, a payer-fixed swap begins at a reset date, has notional 100, pays annually at dates 1 and 2, and exchanges no principal. Its floating coupons reset to the same default-free deposit rates used by its discount curve: D(1) = 0.96, D(2) = 0.90 and D(0) = 1. Derive the discounted floating-coupon telescoping identity, calculate the annuity and par fixed rate, and value an existing payer-fixed contract with fixed rate 0.05. Show its two discounted net coupon amounts using discount-ratio forward rates. Would an unrelated floating index automatically have the same telescoping value?
  4. A separate, fully specified margin-financing experiment starts with zero desk cash. At date 0 borrow 12 and post all 12 in segregated collateral. The collateral earns 1% over each annual interval; bank debt compounds at 6%. At date 1, after both interests accrue, the required collateral principal falls to 8. Release everything above 8 and immediately repay that amount of bank debt. No other trade, loss, cash injection or margin call occurs. At date 2 the remaining collateral and its interest are returned and all remaining debt is due. Reconstruct both accounts at dates 1 and 2 and find terminal net cash. Explain why charging the posted principal as a permanent expense, or assuming it was freely reusable while segregated, changes this experiment.

Question 3 — Replication must survive the next payment (D3, 20 points)

Parts (a–b) use dates 0, 1 and 2. A non-dividend-paying stock starts at 100 and at each step is multiplied by u = 1.2 or d = 0.8. The bank's gross return is R = 1.05 per step. Stock/cash positions may be signed; there are no fees or constraints. Every branch is physically possible. A put has strike 110 and may be exercised at dates 0, 1 or 2, receiving (110−S)+ then and ending. Decisions at date 1 can use only that node's information.

  1. Derive the pricing up probability from the traded stock and bank. Compute all terminal payoffs, date-1 continuation and exercise values, the optimal adapted policy, and the initial American value. Compute the European value when exercise is permitted only at date 2 and report the early-exercise premium. Explain why the comparison holds under this same tree and does not establish convergence across different trees.
  2. Replicate the American value up to date 1 using stock and an initial bank balance. At the up node, show how to finance the new stock/cash holdings that replicate the two remaining terminal put payoffs. At the down node, show how the incoming hedge pays the exercise obligation. Display the actual trade cash and bank balances; replacing incoming wealth by a fresh option mark is not a funding argument.
  3. For a separate continuous-time model, let S > 0 satisfy dS = μSdt+σSdW under P on a finite horizon, where W is Brownian relative to the trading filtration and μ, σ > 0, r and q are constants. The stock's continuous proportional dividend cash is qSdt per share and the bank earns r; allow q ≥ 0. Let v(t,S) be C1,2 on [0,U]×(0,∞) for each U < T. State its Itô differential, identify the quadratic-variation term, and derive the PDE required for a self-financing stock/cash hedge to track v, including dividend cash. Explain why μ disappears. Give the terminal condition for a European put. Distinguish this local derivation from a unique global terminal price: name the regularity, terminal-limit and admissibility/growth requirements needed before making the latter claim. General Girsanov or martingale-representation theorems are not required.

Question 4 — A small numerical residual is a claim with premises (D4, 20 points)

Part (a) concerns fixed-input Black–Scholes Greeks with S = 100, pricing volatility σ = 0.20, fixed r,q, and vega measured per 1.00 change in σ. The desk's existing option book has delta −70 shares, gamma −4 per CU and vega −5000 CU per unit volatility; it also already holds 10 stock shares. Each candidate is a fixed hedge package of signed European vanilla option holdings on that stock, possibly with different strikes; all options within a package share its stated maturity and volatility. Per package, A has remaining maturity 0.5 year, delta 0.50, gamma 0.030 and vega 30. B has the same remaining maturity and pricing volatility, gamma 0.020 and vega 20. C instead has remaining maturity 1 year, delta 0.40, gamma 0.025 and vega 50. These are supplied aggregate exposures, not proposed Greeks of a single unscaled call. Quantities below mean signed package units; all constituent quantities and multipliers are already included.

  1. Can any combination of A and B neutralize both the option book's gamma and vega? Use the identity ν = S²στΓ or the two exposure equations to prove your answer. Replace B by C and find quantities of A and C that neutralize both. Find the additional stock trade needed to neutralize total delta, including the 10 shares already held. State why these local neutralities alone do not prove a self-financed or profitable trading strategy.
  2. For a smooth option mark v(t,S,σ), a report uses ΔδS+½Γ(δS)²+νδσ+Θδt and asserts a remainder bounded by a constant times ‖(δt,δS,δσ)‖³ for every sufficiently small joint move, merely because v is C³. Identify all omitted second-order terms and give the condition needed for a genuine cubic Taylor bound. Demonstrate why smoothness alone does not make the abbreviated claim valid, using the local coordinate function f(x,y)=xy along x=y=ε. Explain why a spot-only third-derivative bound cannot certify the joint report.
  3. An explicit finite-difference step on a fixed bounded spatial grid is Ujn+1 = 0.20Uj−1n+0.65Ujn+0.10Uj+1n. Derive its interior maximum-norm contraction and positivity property, including the role of boundary values. Suppose the initial error is at most 0.04, every boundary error is at most 0.01, and the already-justified absolute one-step value defect is at most 0.001. Prove a global bound after n steps and evaluate it at n = 20; a table of adjacent-grid prices is not the supplied premise. The stencil comes from weights α− = ak/h²−bk/(2h), α0 = 1−2ak/h²−rk, α+ = ak/h²+bk/(2h). Holding a,b,r and k fixed, halve h. Find the new weights and decide whether the same positivity proof survives. Give a concrete nonnegative input demonstrating any failure.
  4. For a separate ideal IID Monte Carlo estimator, one simulated path provides a discounted payoff Y and a control X whose mean m is known exactly. Population moments are Var(Y) = 16, Var(X) = 9 and Cov(Y,X) = 6, all in compatible CU units. A fixed coefficient β is chosen before the 400 evaluation paths, giving Z = Y−β(X−m). Derive the variance-minimizing β, the resulting observation variance and the standard error of the mean. Give an approximate 95% half-width using 1.96 and compare with raw Y at the same path count. Identify the independent sampling unit and two errors this interval does not cover. Explain what fails if β or the reported rule is selected from the same evaluation outcomes without accounting for that selection.

Question 5 — Exact conditional valuation does not identify a missing price (D5, 20 points)

Dates are 0 and T only; intermediate trading is unavailable. A stock costs 95 and has terminal values (70,100,160). A claim paying one certain CU at T costs D = 0.95. All three states are physically possible; the stock has no dividends. Signed stock/cash positions and both sides of the new claim are available without costs. The new claim pays g(ST) = min((ST−90)+,10), hence (0,10,10). No option price is supplied to complete this market.

  1. Parameterize every strictly positive pricing probability vector consistent with stock and cash. Derive the complete no-arbitrage interval for the capped claim. Explain why assigning all three physical states equal probabilities is not a pricing argument, and why checking one positive calibrated law proves consistency of its value without establishing unique identification.
  2. Find stock/cash sub- and superhedges attaining the limiting lower and upper costs. Check their payoffs in every state. For a claim quoted at either endpoint, specify a zero-initial-cost portfolio with nonnegative terminal cash and strictly positive cash in a physically possible state. Explain why strict positivity excludes those endpoints even though the hedge costs themselves are attained.
  3. In a separate local jump diagnostic, a smooth marked claim has v(S)=S²/100 and the stock jumps from 100 to 160 at a scheduled announcement. There is no elapsed interest or dividend cash during the jump. Compute the marked claim's jump, the gain of the pre-jump delta holding and their difference. Explain why applying only the continuous diffusion cancellation would miss the residual; do not claim that v is the capped claim from (a).
  4. A model team chooses an interior pricing law that gives the capped claim value 5.000 CU. Its implementation returns 5.006; the independent conditional benchmark tolerance is 0.010. The desk wants to carry 20 short claim units at reference mark 5.000. An invented internal policy additionally requires the supremum over every strictly positive calibrated law of the upward liability-value deviation from that reference mark to be at most 60 CU. A valid exception raises the permitted position count to 30 but waives only that count limit. All other prerequisites have been checked. Assess the numerical and model-uncertainty gates separately; calculate the maximum whole-number short position that meets the 60-CU gate. Write a scoped decision for 20 units and a defensible restricted-use alternative, naming a responsible remediation and independent closure test. Do not describe the valuation range as a sampling confidence interval or as a prediction of terminal trading losses.

Solution 1 — Bootstrap, hedge coordinates and conditional expectations

  1. The first instrument gives D(0.5)=98/100=0.98. For the second, 4D(0.5)+104D(1.5)=100.744, hence D(1.5)=(100.744−3.92)/104=0.931. The third equation is 5(0.98)+5(0.931)+105D(2.5)=101.955, so D(2.5)=(101.955−4.9−4.655)/105=0.88. Each coupon is discounted at its own payment date.

    Half of the annual coupon period has elapsed, so accrued interest is 5(0.5/1)=2.5 CU and the clean quote is 101.955−2.5=99.455 CU. Settlement requires the dirty 101.955; the clean quote removes the specified accrual for quotation, not from the seller's settlement entitlement. Scoring, 5: first discount 1; second dated equation/result 1; third dated equation/result 1; accrued/clean amounts 1; settlement interpretation 1.

  2. The liability is worth L=100(0.931)=93.1 CU. A common continuous zero shift multiplies each dated discount by e−tε, so the value and first-derivative equations are

    0.98x+0.88y=93.1,
    −0.5(0.98x)−2.5(0.88y)=−1.5(93.1)=−139.65.

    Put X=0.98x and Y=0.88y. Then X+Y=93.1 and 0.5X+2.5Y=139.65, yielding X=Y=46.55. Thus x=47.5 and y=4655/88≈52.897727273 terminal-CU units. Both sides have duration 1.5 relative to this parallel coordinate. This is a two-constraint match, not equality of dated cash vectors. Scoring, 5: liability PV 1; dated derivative/matching equations 2; both holdings 1; coordinate interpretation 1.

  3. The hedge has no 1.5-year payment and its two discount coordinates are unchanged, so its value remains 93.1. The liability becomes 93.1e−1.5(0.01). Initial surplus is zero; first-order surplus change is +1.3965 CU, while its exact change is

    93.1(1−e−0.015)≈+1.386078423 CU.

    The positive sign comes from the liability falling while the hedge is unchanged. Matching one common-shift derivative does not match the middle key-rate exposure. Nor does the scenario come with a physical probability or rate transition law. It is a revaluation under a specified curve coordinate change. Scoring, 5: hedge unchanged and liability repricing 1; first-order amount 1; exact signed amount 1; nonparallel explanation 1; scenario-versus-forecast distinction 1.

  4. The next-period average discount is EQd=(0.99+0.91)/2=0.95, so D(1.5)=0.98(0.95)=0.931, as required. The effective forward is f=1/0.95−1=1/19≈0.052631579. The short rates are rL=1/99 and rH=9/91. Therefore

    EQr=½(1/99+9/91)=491/9009≈0.054501055,
    EPr=¾(1/99)+¼(9/91)=97/3003≈0.032301032.

    The required decomposition is

    f−EPr = (f−EQr)+(EQr−EPr)
    = −0.001869476+0.022200022=0.020330547.

    First invert the Q-average discount, rather than averaging the inverse discounts: 1+f=1/EQ[1/(1+r)]. Its harmonic averaging makes f smaller than EQr here. The second term changes the probability law. The curve restricts the pricing average discount; it does not select physical branch frequencies or a term premium convention. Scoring, 5: fitted discount and forward 1; both expected rates 2; decomposition 1; nonlinear/physical interpretation 1.

Solution 2 — Same nominal recovery, different dated rights

  1. Differentiating survival gives the first-default density λe−λt=0.06e−0.06t. Survival principal has value 100e−(r+λ)T. Immediate recovery has value ∫0T50λe−(r+λ)tdt, so

    BI=100e−0.20+50(0.06/0.10)(1−e−0.20)
    =30+70e−0.20≈87.311152715 CU.

    Recovery paid only at T instead has value 50e−rTQ(τ≤T). Thus

    BM=e−0.08[100e−0.12+50(1−e−0.12)]
    =50(e−0.08+e−0.20)≈87.092354973 CU.

    Contract I is worth 0.218797742 CU more. On each default path, positive discounting values 50 at τ<T more highly than 50 at T. Both survival paths still pay 100; neither contract replaces that face by 50 on every path. Scoring, 6: density 1; correctly dated immediate principal/recovery derivation 2; maturity-recovery derivation 1; both numerical prices/difference 1; pathwise timing interpretation 1.

  2. The 0.06 is a per-year Q intensity, not a two-year probability. The supplied cumulative Q default probability is 1−e−0.12≈0.113079563. A physical probability requires a separately justified P law or evidence about physical default frequencies and its relation to Q. Unknown recovery and a possible pricing/liquidity wedge can offset hazard changes in a single observed price; one price does not uniquely separate them. Relevant added evidence includes recovery bases and settlement dates, independently supported liquidity/execution information, multiple suitable prices and physical default data with uncertainty. Such evidence must support a stated identification argument rather than merely add more fitted parameters. Scoring, 3: intensity/probability and Q/P correction with numerical probability 1; nonidentification argument 1; concrete relevant additional evidence 1.

  3. Let fi=Di−1/Di−1 for annual accrual. Then Difi=Di−1−Di, so the floating coupons' PV is NΣDifi=N(1−D2)=10 CU. This uses the matched deposit reset and discount assumptions; the swap itself exchanges no principal. The fixed annuity is A=0.96+0.90=1.86 years, giving

    kpar=(1−0.90)/1.86=5/93≈0.053763441 per year,
    Vpayer=100[0.10−0.05(1.86)]=+0.70 CU.

    The discount-ratio rates are f₁=1/24 and f₂=1/15. The net coupon amounts are 100(1/24−1/20)=−5/6 and 100(1/15−1/20)=5/3. Their PVs are −0.80 and +1.50, summing to +0.70. The second rate is a pricing forward, not a known future fixing. A different floating index needs its own justified projected coupon values; its leg need not telescope to 1−D₂. Scoring, 6: telescoping derivation 2; annuity/par rate 1; existing signed value 1; dated net-coupon check 1; projection/rights qualification 1.

  4. At date 0 the loan receipt 12 exactly funds collateral 12, leaving zero free cash. Just before the date-1 release, collateral is 12.12 and debt is 12.72. Release 12.12−8=4.12 and pay it to the lender: collateral becomes 8 and debt becomes 8.60. At date 2, collateral returns 8(1.01)=8.08 while debt is 8.60(1.06)=9.116. Terminal net cash is therefore −1.036 CU.

    Both principal and accrued collateral interest are included once. The cost is the interest/remuneration mismatch with the stated release timing, not a permanent loss of the original 12. Segregation prevents investing those posted funds elsewhere while they are locked. This separate loan experiment also does not prescribe a universal discount rate or automatically amend the swap's reference value in (c). Scoring, 5: date-1 accrued balances 1; release and debt reduction 1; date-2 balances 1; net cost 1; principal/reuse interpretation 1.

Solution 3 — Adapted exercise and explicit hedge cash

  1. Matching the discounted stock gives p=(R−d)/(u−d)=(1.05−0.8)/(1.2−0.8)=5/8, strictly between zero and one because d<R<u. Terminal stocks are 144, 96 and 64 and put payoffs are 0,14,46. At the up node S=120, continuation is [(5/8)0+(3/8)14]/1.05=5, exceeding immediate exercise 0. At the down node S=80, continuation is [(5/8)14+(3/8)46]/1.05=520/21≈24.761904762, below immediate exercise 30.

    Thus wait at the up node and exercise at the down node. The time-0 continuation value is [(5/8)5+(3/8)30]/1.05=575/42≈13.690476190, exceeding immediate exercise 10, so continue initially. European exercise removes the down-node early payment and gives

    VE=[(5/8)5+(3/8)(520/21)]/(21/20)=3475/294≈11.819727891.
    VA−VE=275/147≈1.870748299 CU.

    The admissible exercise set expands under the same transition law and cash discounting, so its optimum cannot fall. Changing a tree changes more than the exercise set and requires separate convergence/error evidence. The node decisions use current information, not future branches. Scoring, 6: pricing probability and admissibility 1; terminal/date-1 calculations 2; American value/policy 1; European value/premium 1; same-law/adapted interpretation 1.

  2. The date-1 required values are 5 at S=120 and 30 at S=80. With initial stock Δ₀ and bank b₀, solve 120Δ₀+1.05b₀=5 and 80Δ₀+1.05b₀=30. Thus Δ₀=−5/8, date-1 bank cash is 80, and b₀=1600/21≈76.190476190. Initial cost is −62.5+1600/21=575/42.

    At the up node the short stock is worth −75 and cash is 80, leaving wealth 5. To match remaining payoffs 0 at 144 and 14 at 96, solve 144Δ₁+1.05b₁=0 and 96Δ₁+1.05b₁=14. This gives Δ₁=−7/24 and b₁=40; future bank cash is 42. Changing stock from −5/8 to −7/24 means buying 1/3 share for 40 CU. Deducting 40 from incoming cash 80 leaves the required bank 40, with unchanged wealth −35+40=5. At date 2 the hedge pays −42+42=0 or −28+42=14.

    At the down node the short stock is worth −50 and bank cash is 80. Buy back 5/8 share for 50, leaving 30 to pay immediate put exercise, after which stock, cash and claim obligations are zero. No value is inserted by resetting a mark; every trade and exercise payment is funded by incoming holdings. Scoring, 6: root matching equations/holdings 2; up-node terminal replication 1; explicit 40-CU rebalance cash and unchanged wealth 2; down-node settlement/closure 1.

  3. For t≤U<T, the scalar Itô formula, localized away from S=0 and large S, gives

    dv = [vt+μSvS+½σ²S²vSS]dt+σSvSdW.

    The correction is ½vSSd[S], with d[S]=σ²S²dt. Brownian squared increments contribute at order dt; replacing stochastic calculus by the ordinary chain rule would omit that drift.

    For a self-financing hedge H with Δ shares and bank value H−ΔS, funded gains are

    dH = Δ(dS+qSdt)+r(H−ΔS)dt
    = [rH+(μ+q−r)SΔ]dt+σSΔdW.

    Matching diffusion terms requires Δ=vS. If H=v, equating drifts cancels μSvS and gives

    vt+½σ²S²vSS+(r−q)SvS−rv=0,
    v(T,S)=(K−S)+ for the European put.

    The physical expected return disappears because the same stock exposure carries it on both sides; it was not assumed to equal r. If v solves the PDE, the same subtraction for a possibly unequal H gives d(H−v)=r(H−v)dt, so equal initial values track on each localized preterminal interval.

    A local identity alone is not a unique terminal valuation theorem. Apply the taught finite-horizon class: positive GBM, constant finite coefficients and σ>0; v is C1,2 before T, has the correct continuous terminal/boundary limits and at most linear growth uniformly over the horizon; hedging gains are predictable and satisfy the localized integrability plus the square-integrable/admissible terminal extension justified for the vanilla hedge. The c08 uniqueness argument uses this growth control and GBM moment bounds to take limits of stopped discounted solutions. A put's kink means C1,2 is not required at T itself. Arbitrary explosive PDE solutions or unrestricted doubling strategies are outside that theorem. No general measure-change result follows solely from cancelling μ. Scoring, 8: Itô differential and quadratic variation 2; dividend-inclusive funded gains 2; diffusion/drift matching, PDE and μ cancellation 2; terminal condition and explicit global scope 2.

Solution 4 — Rank, Taylor order, stability and sampling units

  1. For A and B, S²στ=100²(0.20)(0.5)=1000, so ν=1000Γ for every combination. Removing gamma −4 would add gamma 4 and necessarily add vega 4000, leaving vega −1000. Neutralizing both requires vega 5000, hence the two equations are inconsistent. More digits or another strike at the same σ,τ do not restore rank.

    Replacing B by C gives 0.030x+0.025y=4 and 30x+50y=5000. Subtracting 1000 times the first equation from the second yields 25y=1000, so y=40 and x=100. Resulting option delta is −70+100(0.50)+40(0.40)=−4. Including 10 existing stock shares gives total delta +6; the required additional trade is sell 6 shares, leaving 4 shares. Vega quantities are per 1.00 volatility change; a one-point move multiplies them by 0.01. These are local mark sensitivities. Initial option purchase costs, bank funding, future trades, dividends, fees and higher/model exposures still determine a financed strategy and its outcome. Scoring, 6: A/B rank proof 2; A/C equations and quantities 2; incremental stock/sign 1; units and financing limitation 1.

  2. Besides the retained ½vSS(δS)², the full second-order expansion contains vSσδSδσ, vStδSδt, vσtδσδt, ½vσσ(δσ)² and ½vtt(δt)². For a compact neighborhood wholly inside the smooth positive-maturity/positive-volatility domain, bounded third partials control the remainder after retaining the whole Hessian. C³ gives such a bound on a suitable compact neighborhood, but does not eliminate any quadratic term that was omitted.

    At (0,0), f(x,y)=xy has zero value, gradient and pure second derivatives. The abbreviated pure-coordinate approximation is zero, whereas f(ε,ε)=ε². With Euclidean norm √2|ε|, the ratio |ε²|/‖(ε,ε)‖³=1/(2√2|ε|) diverges. No fixed local cubic constant works for this abbreviated expression. A spot-only bound holds σ,t fixed; it cannot control a move that changes them. Scoring, 4: complete omitted Hessian terms 1; compact-domain/full-Hessian premise 1; explicit quadratic counterexample/order 1; spot-only scope 1.

  3. All weights are nonnegative and their sum is 0.95. A nonnegative old grid with nonnegative supplied boundaries stays nonnegative at interior nodes. For two old grids, the interior sup-norm difference contracts by at most 0.95; independently imposed new boundary differences must also be included. With En the maximum error including boundaries, the given value defect implies

    En+1≤max{0.01, 0.95En+0.001}.

    The scalar sequence b₀=0.04, bn+1=0.95bn+0.001 has explicit solution bn=0.02+0.02(0.95)n. It is always at least 0.02, so the 0.01 boundary term cannot exceed it. Induction gives En≤bn. At n=20 this is E₂₀≤0.027169718448. The one-step defect was supplied as justified; small differences between two approximate answers would not establish that premise.

    From the original weights, ak/h²=(0.20+0.10)/2=0.15, bk/(2h)=(0.10−0.20)/2=−0.05 and rk=0.05. Halving h gives 0.60, −0.10 and 0.05 respectively. New weights are (0.70,−0.25,0.50). They still sum to 0.95 but lose nonnegativity; old values (0,1,0) update to −0.25. The positivity/contraction-by-positive-weights proof no longer applies. Refining space alone can break the step restriction. Scoring, 6: positivity/interior contraction and boundaries 2; recurrence, induction and numerical bound 2; rescaled weights and counterexample 2.

  1. Let p₁=a. Pricing stock requires EQST=95/0.95=100. Using Σp=1 gives −30p₁+60p₃=0, so
