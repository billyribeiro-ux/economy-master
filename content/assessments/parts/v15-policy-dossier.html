Required policy dossier — connect the laboratory to evidence and institutional history

The numerical laboratory establishes conditional decisions. Completing this volume's project also requires a literature map, an institutional history, a causal-evidence assessment, a distributional account and an implementation memo. Submit this dossier with the seven laboratory tasks. The laboratory retains its 100-point rubric; this companion has a separate 40-point rubric. Pass both: at least 85/100 with the stated laboratory domain floors, and at least 34/40 with no dossier dimension below 6/8, plus correction of every critical integrity or identification error. Scores cannot compensate across the two components. These are authored study thresholds, not externally calibrated standards. Allow another 6–8 hours, open resources, after completing the laboratory.

Use the United States Moving to Opportunity housing experiment as the historical evidence case. The question is deliberately narrower than whether neighborhoods matter: What can an offer of housing assistance establish about outcomes for participating families, and what additional evidence would a new local opportunity program require? The laboratory's service-linked work program is a different intervention. Its four groups, costs, effect grid, forecasts and welfare weights remain synthetic. Historical housing evidence does not calibrate those inputs. A complete answer must identify this mismatch rather than attach a real citation to invented quantities.

The four source cards below are sufficient for the bounded assessment. Their claims and access limits are supplied here so that the dossier remains usable offline. Links allow further primary-source reading when connected. No restricted household records are provided or required. Do not present this exercise as a replication of an empirical paper. For a quantitative empirical extension, first obtain lawful access, a data dictionary, the exact specification and a reproducible output; unavailable evidence should be recorded as unavailable.

Five dossier tasks

  1. Literature map and claim ledger. Create one row for each source: author/institution, document date or vintage, population, intervention, comparison, outcome horizon, design, admissible claim and unresolved limitation. Mark unavailable details explicitly. Draw the dependency map and classify two statements as institutional description, causal interpretation or normative judgment. Explain why repeated use of one experiment does not provide independent replication.
  2. Institutional history. Build a timeline separating assignment, offer, counseling, completed move, outcome measurement and publication. Include the verified dated milestones supplied in the cards, while leaving family-specific events undated. Name the delivery actors and one bottleneck supported by the institutional description. Explain why a later child-outcome finding need not logically contradict an earlier adult-outcome finding. Give one claim the short source cards cannot settle.
  3. Causal evidence. Define an intention-to-treat estimand for one voucher-offer comparison, and distinguish it from an effect among movers. State the observation and assignment assumptions required for identification. Derive the binary instrumental-variable ratio under additional assumptions, and explain why it need not isolate neighborhood poverty from counseling or other bundled services. List the population, outcome, horizon and interference conditions needed before transporting any conclusion to the local work-service laboratory.
  4. Distribution and a decision. Select either expected weighted welfare, expected resources or deterministic minimax regret in the laboratory. Report its policy, objective value, funding and who may lose. Contrast its decision assumptions with what the historical cards actually establish. Then propose a bounded real-program decision memo: state the target group, outside option, budget information still needed, an implementer and a measurable revision trigger. “Defer scale-up while resolving a specified missing fact” is a legitimate decision; an invented benefit-cost ratio is not.
  5. Feasible implementation and adversarial handoff. Supply a responsibility and evidence table for authorization, recruitment, counseling or service delivery, payments, outcome follow-up and complaints. Separate historical actors from proposed roles. Set a funded pilot stage gate without inventing legal permission or treating future benefits as current cash. Write a 150-word skeptical referee note on your own recommendation and a response identifying what you changed or still cannot establish. Preserve a source/version log and identify what a reader can reproduce offline.
Instructor guide, illustrative answers and grading boundaries

1. Build a map of claims, not a vote count

A complete ledger records D1 as a March 1996 administrative account with implementation activity as its object; its supplied text supports delivery processes, not an income treatment effect. D2 is a November 2011 official summary of evaluation outcomes; exact coefficients and uncertainty are “not supplied.” D3 is an author research summary identifying a 2016 article and later childhood outcomes; the publication year must not be mistaken for assignment year. D4 provides program chronology and assignment-group descriptions; its separate Family Options discussion is outside this case. State sample size only if separately verified at the intended precision; it is unnecessary for this assessment.

The dependency graph has one experiment feeding several records. Agreement among its publications can increase coherence, but common participants, assignment and measurement histories create dependence. A statement about an agency's counseling partners is institutional description. A difference attributed to randomized offer assignment is a causal interpretation requiring an estimand and valid observation. Giving extra welfare weight to low-income groups is a normative judgment. Publication by a respected institution does not collapse these categories.

Award 2 points for four identifiable source/vintage rows; 2 for explicit unavailable fields and admissible claims; 2 for a correct shared-cohort graph; 2 for the classifications and replication distinction.

2. Establish the sequence before telling a success story

A valid skeleton is: 1994–1998 assignment window; March 1996 implementation report, inside that window; follow-up through 2010 and the November 2011 evaluation announcement; later child-outcome analysis identified as the 2016 article. The cards do not give each household's counseling dates, move date or observation date. Those belong in separate event columns marked unknown, not filled with the publication date. Some early administrative observations were made while later families were still entering the experiment, so a single common “before” date would erase the program's staggered history.

HUD's 1996 account supplies public housing agencies and nonprofit counseling organizations as delivery actors. Its sequence from outreach through applications and enrollment motivates a proposed recruitment or delivery-capacity bottleneck, but does not estimate which stage caused an outcome. A new dossier should seek dated waiting-list, offer, counseling and lease records to test such a hypothesis. These records would also help distinguish eligibility, offer and take-up; they would not themselves randomize take-up.

An earlier adult economic-outcome result and a later childhood-exposure result concern different ages and potentially different horizons. They can both be true. A careful comparison holds outcome, sample and follow-up fixed before calling results contradictory. The short cards cannot establish the precise size, uncertainty or mechanism of either effect; nor do they show that a particular counselor caused a household to move. An evidence gap is an appropriate conclusion.

Award 2 for correctly ordered verified milestones; 2 for distinct undated household events; 2 for actors plus a testable rather than asserted bottleneck; 2 for the age/horizon reconciliation and one explicit unresolved claim.

3. An offer is a defined intervention

For a chosen pair of assignment arms, let Z be the randomized offer, Y(z) the outcome at a prespecified horizon under that assignment, and D(z) a precisely defined binary take-up event. The intention-to-treat target is E[Y(1)−Y(0)] in the eligible randomized population. Under assignment independence, consistency, positive assignment probabilities and valid observation of that population, it equals E[Y|Z=1]−E[Y|Z=0]. Differential loss to follow-up, failed linkage or outcome definitions that depend on assignment require additional treatment; random assignment alone does not repair them. If site-specific assignment probabilities differ, compare within design strata and aggregate with declared target weights.

To interpret the offer as an instrument for D, additionally assume exclusion of direct offer effects, no defiers D(1)≥D(0), a positive first stage, and a coherent treatment version and interference definition. Write Y=Y(0)+D[Y(1)−Y(0)] using treatment-indexed potential outcomes under exclusion. Subtract assignment-arm expectations. The reduced form is E[(D(1)−D(0))(Y(1)−Y(0))]; under monotonicity it equals the complier share times the complier average effect. The first stage E[D|Z=1]−E[D|Z=0] is that share. Their ratio identifies the complier effect, not automatically the effect on all movers or all eligible families. In this paragraph Y(d) is treatment-indexed; it differs from the assignment-indexed Y(z) above.

The derivation is a conditional identification argument, not a claim that every assumption holds for an MTO mechanism. If assignment changes counseling, expectations or assistance before a move, exclusion for a variable defined merely as “moved” can fail. Even a valid effect of a bundled move intervention need not identify the isolated effect of neighborhood poverty. A continuous exposure measure, multiple assignment arms or several move types needs its own estimand and assumptions.

Transport to the laboratory would require comparable populations and take-up constraints, comparable interventions and outside options, measured outcomes that map to its resource objective, a justified horizon and price conversion, and an interference/scaling model. None is supplied by fitting the laboratory's four invented means. The laboratory sensitivity restriction on selection, finite state weights and group welfare weights remain assumptions. A disciplined answer refuses to replace θ with an unverified housing estimate.

Award 2 for the ITT target and assignment/observation conditions; 2 for the reduced-form/first-stage derivation; 2 for the complier and bundled-treatment limits; 2 for explicit transport requirements and rejection of false calibration. Treat an unsupported movers-versus-nonmovers causal comparison as a critical error.

4. Carry the decision's losses into the memo

One acceptable laboratory recommendation is capacity one, fee zero, subsidy one and abatement one under expected weighted welfare. Its initial reserve is 1(3+1)+1=5. The supplied probabilities imply weighted gain 3/16 and resource change −5/4. Expected group changes are (23/8,−23/16,−43/32,−43/32); only P1 gains. Their sum is −5/4 and weights (2,2,1,1) give 3/16. Thus the recommendation buys a distributional objective while losing total goods in this model. A proposal that reports only its positive objective omits the central tradeoff.

Two alternative recommendations earn equal credit if argued consistently. Under expected resources, no program uniquely maximizes the objective at zero, with no program levy or model-induced losses. Under deterministic minimax regret, capacity two with the same fee, subsidy and abatement needs reserve 9 and minimizes worst regret at 3, despite expected weighted change −1/8. Its low-effect state already exhibits losers in the laboratory's manual account. These are different objectives, not three estimates of the same empirical welfare effect.

An illustrative real-program memo might propose gathering a costed design for a voluntary offer targeted to a clearly defined eligible group, preserving the current assistance option. It would specify an accountable local delivery organization, seek per-offer and per-completed-service cost records, distinguish up-front cash from discounted future benefits, and require an evaluation protocol before scale-up. It cannot recommend a precise MTO-based subsidy or an actual city's legal action from these cards. A revision trigger could be verified inability to fund promised services or a prespecified adverse outcome boundary; the threshold and monitoring design must be fixed before inspecting results. Health, earnings, family disruption, rents, movers and nonparticipants belong in separate outcome accounts rather than a single unexplained index.

Award 2 for a correct objective/policy/value; 2 for reserve and group/resource accounts; 2 for separating historical evidence from invented calibration; 2 for a bounded decision, outside option and measurable revision trigger.

5. Assign responsibility without inventing authority

A defensible proposed responsibility table assigns: authorization to the relevant public decision-maker, with a dated authority/funding document still required; recruitment to an eligibility team, with an auditable offer log; counseling or service delivery to a contracted provider, with capacity and completion records; payments to a finance officer, with commitments and a cash ledger; follow-up to an evaluation team, with consent, data permissions and an attrition plan; complaints to an identifiable review channel, with resolution records. These are proposed functions. Only the historical agencies and counseling organizations named in D1 are source-supported actors; the cards do not establish any current jurisdiction's powers.

The first stage gate is a verified budget and feasible delivery plan. Specify which commitments can be made before enrollment, the reserve required under low take-up and high take-up, and who may stop new offers while honoring outstanding obligations. The second is a feasible evaluation with assignment and outcome definitions, protected data handling, funding for follow-up and a declared stopping rule. The third is a documented review of benefits, losses and implementation against that protocol. A future earnings projection is not cash available to pay today's provider. A pilot also consumes resources and can change behavior; it is not free information.

An illustrative skeptical note is: “The preferred numerical policy depends on assumed forecasts, a privileged access order and an explicitly unequal welfare criterion. Historical housing offers do not identify the productivity effect of this service. The proposed recruitment process could exclude the people with the largest gains, and the budget does not yet price follow-up. The memo should retain the current outside option and postpone irrevocable expansion until it has a costed delivery protocol and a defensible comparison.” A good response accepts the valid limits, adds follow-up costs and a recruitment audit, and states what remains unknown. It need not capitulate to an unsupported criticism or pretend that qualitative risks have been quantified.

The source log should retain URLs, document vintages, access dates, the bounded claims actually inspected and the difference between author summaries and full reports. Offline readers can reproduce the synthetic laboratory, inspect the provided claim cards, recompute the hypothetical accounts and evaluate the written identification argument. They cannot reproduce confidential empirical estimates from this package. That boundary is part of the deliverable.

Award 2 for six assigned functions with evidence requirements; 2 for funded stage gates and treatment of obligations; 2 for a substantive skeptical note and responsive revision; 2 for the source/version log and honest reproduction boundary.

Defense and delayed transfer

In an oral defense, ask the learner to replace a randomized offer with voluntary enrollment while holding the observed outcome gap fixed. A satisfactory answer withdraws the random-assignment interpretation, names selection into enrollment and proposes a credible design or explicit sensitivity analysis. Then change the lab reserve ceiling from 10 to 8: the two-slot minimax policy's reserve 9 fails, so its old optimality certificate no longer applies. The correct next step is to re-enumerate the changed feasible set, not finance the gap with hoped-for benefits. Two weeks later, require a new source card from a different policy setting and a short explanation of which treatment, population and institutional assumptions do or do not transfer.
