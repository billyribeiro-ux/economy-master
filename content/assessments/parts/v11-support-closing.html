Oral transfer and teach-back

After a passing written form and project, conduct a 25–35 minute discussion without the model open. Use the five prompts below. Each carries four points: identify the changed assumption or claim (1), reconstruct the mechanism (1), give a resource-consistent implication (1), and state evidence or a limit needed for the decision (1). Pass with at least 17/20 and three points in every prompt, with all critical errors repaired. A peer or instructor should record the learner's explanation and follow-up. This protocol supplies an assessment; it does not claim that an external human evaluation has occurred.

  1. D1 — Explain a profitable screen that is not a funded loan. A score improves the mix of accepted applicants. Management concludes that every accepted loan can be paid out and serviced. Identify the additional questions. Then explain how a covenant or monitoring action can change the payoff distribution that made the screen attractive.
  2. D2 — Hold loss risk fixed and change a date. Construct two plans with identical terminal loss distributions but different ability to pay an early obligation. Give actual dated cash amounts, then explain why a loss quantile or an economic-value hedge cannot by itself choose the executable plan.
  3. D3 — Challenge two green indicators. Both a primary and backup payment route pass their individual tests. A totals-only reconciliation also balances. Explain two different ways a shared incident can still leave an obligation unpaid, and specify evidence that distinguishes the mechanisms.
  4. D4 — Trace a promise through borders. A guarantee covers a foreign-currency benefit that becomes due during a transfer restriction. Describe a concrete sequence in which the nominal guarantee exists but timely usable payment fails. Name the relevant claimant, guarantor, account, currency, rights and dates; do not invent a real country's law.
  5. D5 — Defend a conditional crisis decision. A recapitalized institution can pass a closing equity test but still disrupt a creditor's earlier payment. Explain one explicitly financed repair, who bears its downside, and a governance rule that makes bad news worth reporting rather than hiding. Distinguish an incentive inequality from legal authority and from actual implementation.
Illustrative oral responses and follow-up criteria
  1. A posterior repayment distribution is conditional on the screening process and the applicant's subsequent action. It must be paired with the loan amount, dated borrower cash available for debt service, the lender's own settlement cash and funding, and accessible collateral or recovery if service fails. A covenant can trigger information or enforcement; it is not cash on its own. Monitoring can make diversion or a risky project less attractive only if detection, penalties and rights actually change the borrower's feasible payoffs. A good response explains at least one such changed payoff rather than assuming the original posterior remains valid after behavior changes. Request evidence about test selection, borrower choices, enforceability, repayment sources and actual funding.
  2. For example, each plan has five CU initially, owes eight at cutoff 1, and eventually receives ten certain CU. In plan A, ten arrives before cutoff 1, so payment leaves seven. In plan B it arrives after cutoff 1, leaving an early gap of three without a new facility. Both can have identical zero ultimate credit loss if the late receipt eventually pays the claim, but B fails timely performance. That eventual continuation must be explicitly stipulated; it is not an automatic legal right. A loss law measured only at the later horizon omits the missed deadline. A genuinely committed advance of three arriving in time can close the gap, with its repayment and interest added to later cash tests.
  3. The two routes may depend on the same identity service, cash release or signing permission, so separate successful tests do not establish independence in the incident state. Separately, two debits can discharge only one of two obligations if the first is duplicated and the second omitted. Aggregate amounts can match while obligations do not. Inspect joint incident traces and shared dependencies for the first mechanism; reconcile immutable obligation IDs, beneficiary, amount, settlement status and deadline for the second. A later queue-clearance report does not prove timely delivery. A proposed repair must be exercised under the relevant failure and must show which actual obligation was paid.
  4. One possible fully fictional sequence is a forty-F pension benefit due to an offshore claimant at cutoff 1, with a solvent guarantor holding sixty-F-equivalent assets in another account. Its assets realize at cutoff 2, or the transfer permission applies only after cutoff 1. Nominal coverage and ultimate asset value therefore do not establish the claimant's timely forty-F receipt. The answer must say what pays first, who submits the guarantee claim, the covered trigger, whether and when the guarantor can convert and transfer, and who retains any unpaid obligation. A separately funded timely line might repair timing while creating a new later claim. Actual application requires the particular contract, eligibility, governing law, account access and an operationally tested transfer route.
  5. Debt conversion changes claims and equity, not usable cash. A cash advance from an identified provider, delivered before the creditor's deadline and backed by a separately available budget and eligible collateral, can fund payment. Its priority, interest, repayment source and stress loss determine who bears downside; a balance-sheet mark or unspecified public support cannot serve as the provider. Truthful escalation needs an actual manager payoff comparison, credible detection and collectible consequences, including any common control failure and false sanction. The board must also assign decision authority, limits, trigger, responsible owner, execution proof and revision record. A passing inequality shows only a conditional incentive result; it does not authenticate an approval or establish jurisdictional powers. Accept other coherent interventions with equally explicit resources and limits.

Delayed retention assessment

After two to four weeks without rehearsing these answers, allow 75 minutes with a basic calculator and no notes. Each of the five domain questions is worth ten points. Pass with at least 43/50 and eight points in every question, with no unresolved critical error. The rounded integer thresholds exceed the package's 85% overall and 75% domain minima. This is a study protocol, not a validated estimate of long-run retention. Explain each causal or accounting step before opening the solutions.

  1. D1 — Creation, settlement and credit. A bank starts with cash nine CU, loans 61 and securities 30, financed by deposits 80, senior debt ten and equity ten. It grants a new ten-CU loan by crediting a new deposit; the borrower then transfers seven to a different bank. A subsequent four-CU impairment reduces net loan assets without an immediate cash movement. Show all three balance sheets. The new borrower has five cash available for six of debt service; a covenant report has been filed but no third party has committed funds. Explain the borrower gap, the bank's remaining cash and what the report does and does not repair. Do not count the impairment as another cash payment.
  2. D2 — Tail loss and timely collateral. Loss is zero, eight or forty CU with probabilities 0.7, 0.2 and 0.1. Compute the 80% value at risk and 80% expected shortfall, filling exactly the worst 20% of probability. Separately, starting cash is six; receipts of three and nine arrive before payments of twelve and four at cutoffs 1 and 2. Derive the minimum early unrestricted cash contribution if its repayment is not due within these cutoffs. Ten eligible unpledged collateral units have price 0.8 CU and haircut 25%, but the facility can first deliver cash at cutoff 2. Calculate capacity and state whether it closes the first gap.
  3. D3 — Common failure and impossible aggregation. A common outage disables two routes with probability 0.1. Otherwise each fails with probability 0.2, independently conditional on no outage. Find joint failure and compare it with multiplying the two unconditional marginal failure rates. For a separate finite stress file, state X has credit/market/operational losses (8,2,0), while state Y has (1,7,4). Compute the worst joint total and the sum of component maxima. A reconciliation records two payments with obligation IDs (a,a) for obligations (a,b), of equal amounts. State the unresolved obligation and explain the three different errors these examples expose.
  4. D4 — A funded guarantee and an FX cap. In one fictional pension case, terminal assets are twenty or sixty CU with equal probabilities and promised benefits are forty. A separate guarantor begins with twelve CU of cash, receives no premium and pays the smaller of its cash and the benefit shortfall. Find the mean asset funding ratio, statewise and expected guarantee payments, unpaid benefits and sponsor residual. In a separate sovereign cash case, available H cash is 66, local H service of sixteen has priority, the quotation is two H per F, at most fifteen F can be bought before the deadline, existing usable F is eight and foreign service is thirty F. Calculate H/F balances after local service and permitted conversion, before foreign service, and the foreign-service gap. Contrast a timely seven-F grant with a seven-F loan that requires eight F later.
  5. D5 — Capital, network cash and escalation. A bank has cash four CU, loans 76, deposits fifty, senior debt twenty and equity ten. A loan loss of twenty is recognized. Assume effective authority to write senior claims down until equity is zero, then convert some surviving senior debt to equity. Deposits of nine are due at cutoff 1, before the remaining loans realize their full stated value at cutoff 2. A separately funded outside bridge can lend five at cutoff 1 and must receive 5.5 at cutoff 2. Find the write-down, the minimum conversion that leaves four equity after bridge interest, and original senior holders' total terminal value. If instead a stay delays the nine-CU receipt of a creditor with no cash and eight due to its own customers at cutoff 1, what happens without another funded action? Separately, a manager earns bonus three if concealed bad news is undetected, loses that bonus and pays a collectible five if detected, and gets zero for truthful reporting. Detection has a 25% common-failure probability and otherwise occurs with probability 0.5. Evaluate the incentive, then change conditional detection to 0.6. State one remaining implementation or authority check.

Remediation by mechanism

Preserve the unsuccessful answer and diagnose the first broken inference. Repairing the final number without repairing that inference is insufficient. For a timing failure, draw the dated cash ledger; for a double count, name the unique underlying claim and every holder; for a strategic failure, enumerate the changed action and its actual payoff; for a factual or authority claim, identify the exact source, date, jurisdiction and unverified condition. Then solve a fresh variant with one economically material assumption changed.

Synthesis and the next stage

The volume's common discipline is to follow a claim through information, choice, valuation, location, time and actual payment. Screening changes who receives credit; monitoring changes actions. Loss measures summarize a specified law, while a funding plan must survive each relevant cash cutoff. Insurance, pensions and guarantees transfer risk to another balance sheet. Cross-border holdings add currencies, permissions and settlement routes. Regulation and crisis interventions change incentives and priority as well as resource use. Governance must connect these conditional calculations to an accountable decision and its revision.

The finance-core assessment combines this institutional discipline with accounting, corporate valuation, investment choice and derivative pricing from Volumes 7–10. The advanced econometrics volume develops identification and inference tools for evaluating actual mechanisms and policies. A coherent scenario is not evidence that the scenario is likely, and a fitted historical relationship does not by itself identify the effect of a policy change. Later graduate theory and macro-finance study richer information, equilibrium and dynamic mechanisms.

Keep the written forms, code, inputs, decision memo, scoring record, corrected work and oral/retention feedback together. The material supports practice and evaluation; it does not award a credential, establish an institution's compliance or demonstrate that a learner has already performed research, teaching or leadership successfully. Those achievements require the learner's work and appropriate external evaluation.

Evidence and source scope

The diagnostic, oral illustrations and retention cases above are original finite constructions with fully stated cash and incentive assumptions. They introduce no observed institution, current national threshold or asserted legal power. Method and evidence discussions are taught and sourced in the linked chapters. The examination and project records identify any additional source use and its exact scope. A successful reproduction binds a computation to inputs; a file hash binds bytes. Neither alone validates empirical assumptions, legal applicability or an unmodeled financial decision.
