The time allocation is an instructional design target, not an empirically calibrated examination duration. Show dated cash, recipients and assumptions before rounding. Exact fractions or equivalent decimal answers receive credit. No specialist numerical tables are needed.

Every institution, probability, contract, rule and amount below is invented for this assessment. CU means one common currency within a panel; H and F in Question 4 are distinct currencies. Cash earns zero unless a stated loan carries interest. The panels are separate files: do not transfer one panel's resources, costs or probabilities into another without an explicit instruction. Question 1 — The monitor's incentive and a borrower's restructuring · D1 · 20 points

Monitoring panel. Larch services an existing loan. Verified monitoring costs Larch 1.5 CU and changes the borrower's default probability from 0.15 to 0.05. Each default causes a 20-CU total creditor loss; nondefault causes no loss. Larch bears 60% of either loss and a participation investor bears 40%. Both are risk neutral at a zero opportunity rate. The action changes only this loss probability; duplicate monitoring has no extra effect. Larch can fund the cost. The investor can pay a 0.4-CU fee from its own cash, but only upon independently verifiable completion. Consider the monitor's incremental choice after lending, not whether the loan was profitable at origination.

Repayment panel. A separate borrower owes principal 60. The current contract charges 8% annual interest on opening principal and requires principal 20 at year 1 and 40 at year 2. Unrestricted cash starts at 4. Cash available for debt service (CFADS), after every operating and investment cash expense, is 18 before the year-1 payment and 26 before the year-2 payment. A proposed amendment irrevocably forgives 12 principal, leaving 48; it charges 4% on opening principal and requires principal 18 then 30. Unspent cash carries forward at zero interest. No distribution, collateral realization or borrowing is available. A sponsor could make a nonrepayable cash contribution immediately before year-2 service, but none is yet committed. Analyze the supplied CFADS path; it is not a promise that every future state is safe.

Bank journal panel. Immediately before recording that 12-CU write-off, the lender's separate supplied balance sheet is cash 18, gross loans 182 and loan-loss allowance 10; funding is deposits 160, wholesale debt 12 and book equity 18. The 60-CU borrower claim is included in gross loans. For this isolated accounting exercise, first recognize an additional provision of 6, then charge off 12 against the now-sufficient allowance. No tax or other entry occurs. These recognition amounts are stipulated, not inferred from the monitoring probability panel or asserted to be a particular accounting standard. Do not post its monitoring costs or fees again into this supplied opening snapshot.

  1. Calculate the total creditor benefit and net surplus from monitoring, and Larch's incentive without the fee. Does the conditional 0.4 fee induce monitoring and benefit the investor? Explain why paying the same fee regardless of completion would not repair the incremental monitoring incentive.
  2. Construct opening principal, interest, principal repayment and total service for both contracts. Find the original contract's first cash failure. For the amendment, carry cash through both dates and derive the minimum year-2 sponsor contribution. Give a current credit decision and a concrete funding condition; explain why the smaller face amount alone is not proof of feasibility.
  3. Give the provision and charge-off entries, and reconcile the complete balance sheet after each. State their effects on current cash, allowance, net assets and equity. Explain why taking the same 12 as another income loss on charge-off would count the loss twice.

Question 2 — The missing joint law and opposite ALM signals · D2 · 20 points

Loss panel. Two positions each lose 6 CU with probability 1/4 and lose zero otherwise. Their marginal laws are known; their dependence is not. Let θ be the probability that both lose 6. No other loss amounts occur. Use VaRα=inf{l:P(L≤l)≥α} for total loss L. ESα is the probability-weighted average of exactly the worst 1−α mass, splitting a boundary atom when necessary. The illustrative risk limit is ES0.8≤9 CU. No distribution for θ is supplied.

Treasury panel. A different portfolio has fixed-rate assets worth 120 CU with modified duration 4 years and immediately resetting floating-rate assets worth 80. Its fixed-rate liabilities are worth 100 with modified duration 2 and its immediately resetting floating-rate liabilities are worth 50. The floating positions remain at par at unchanged balances when the common annual rate resets and then accrue a full year's interest at that new rate. Fixed coupons do not reset. Ignore options, credit changes, basis differences and convexity for the requested first-order calculation. Use ΔP≈−DPΔy for the fixed positions; a rise of 100 basis points is Δy=0.01. EVE here refers to these four listed positions, not an entire institution's balance sheet. All floating interest is paid one year later. A separately specified payment account has only 5 CU usable against 8 due today; its cash and funding are outside that partial EVE portfolio. No asset sale, transfer or new borrowing can settle before that cutoff.

  1. Derive the feasible interval for θ and the probabilities of total losses 0, 6 and 12. Show whether expected total loss depends on θ. Derive ES0.8 throughout the interval and the exact θ set satisfying the limit. At θ=0.2 calculate both VaR0.8 and ES0.8. Explain what the marginals alone fail to identify.
  2. Compute opening economic value of equity (EVE), the first-order EVE change under the 100-basis-point rise, and the change in the next full year's net interest income (NII). Give signs and units. Quantify today's payment gap. Explain how the value, income and cash results coexist, and identify one condition that would invalidate the simple sensitivity calculation.

Question 3 — A common control failure and a discontinuous funding boundary · D3 · 20 points

Route-audit panel. Route A fails with unconditional probability 0.08 and route B with unconditional probability 0.12. A common event C has probability 0.04 and always disables both. Conditional on C not occurring, the two route failures are independent. These are complete synthetic physical probabilities for this panel, not estimated operational rates. No extra event can disable both except the stated common event or their conditional joint failure.

Reverse-stress panel. In a separate deterministic stress path, severity x lies in [0,1]. A settlement desk has usable cash 10 and 60 previously unpledged eligible collateral units priced at one CU. A fully committed lender can deliver the permitted advance before the cutoff; haircut h(x)=0.1+0.2x applies to this entire pool. No second pledge or asset sale is allowed. A receivable of 12 arrives before the cutoff exactly when x<0.3; at x≥0.3 a stipulated processing gate delivers it only afterward. Payments due at the cutoff are 60+20x. The advance has no repayment inside this one-cutoff stress window; this does not establish later solvency. Negative projected cash denotes a missed payment. The operational probability panel above supplies no probability law for x.

A candidate model always books the 12 receipt before the cutoff. It fits every supplied training observation, all of which have x≤0.2. The desk requests approval to certify timely settlement for every x∈[0,0.5]. Under an invented use policy, certification requires correct contractual timing in all stated branches, nonnegative cash throughout that range under the proposed action, and independent evidence that any extra funding is deliverable. Extra unrestricted cash is allowed only as a separately funded nonrepayable contribution arriving before the cutoff.

  1. Infer each route's conditional failure probability given C does not occur. Calculate joint failure and P(C | both routes failed). Explain why the unconditional marginal product is not the relevant joint probability and which dependency the posterior makes important to investigate.
  2. Derive the actual piecewise cash headroom. Find the set of severities causing a shortfall, its first boundary, and whether that boundary itself fails. Compare the candidate model's failure boundary. Prove the minimum extra funded cash needed to meet every cutoff in x∈[0,0.5], including the timing jump.
  3. Decide the requested model use now. Specify a defensible restricted use, repair owner, independent challenge and concrete closure tests for the certification. Distinguish fit from valid deployed use, and explain why neither the reverse boundary nor the route-audit probabilities identify a frequency for x≥0.3.

Question 5 — Collectible sanctions and a creditor's continuity · D5 · 20 points

Conduct panel. Relative to compliant conduct, a risk-neutral provider gains 6 CU by mis-selling. Detection has probability 1/3. The stipulated fine has face F≥0, but at most 9 CU is collectible from this provider. When detected it pays min(F,9); there is no other clawback, private benefit, sanction or cost. This is a one-choice illustrative incentive contract, not an actual fine regime.

Resolution and network panel. A separate issuer has cash 6 and a noncash asset with current book amount 54, funded by a client's payable 12 due at cutoff 1, notes 40 due at cutoff 2 and equity 8. Stipulate effective authority before cutoff 1 to convert 12 of notes into equity, with no cash subscription. At cutoff 1 the issuer pays all its cash 6 to the client and retains the unpaid 6 claim until cutoff 2. No sale or new issuer borrowing is available. At cutoff 2, the asset realizes either 54 in the benchmark or 28.5 in the adverse scenario. Surviving notes have first priority, the remaining client claim second priority, and total issuer equity receives any residue. Each payment is capped by the remaining claim and available estate. Both issuer and client have limited liability: unpaid terminal claims are final creditor losses, with no further receipts, external recourse or negative shareholder distribution. No interest is due. No probability of either scenario is supplied. Only aggregate issuer equity is requested; its allocation between old shareholders and converted noteholders is not specified.

The client is a payment intermediary: it starts with cash 1 and the issuer claim 12, financed by supplier obligations 8 due at cutoff 1 and equity 5. It has no other receipts or debt. A public vehicle owns 1 CU of separately prepositioned cash. It may lend a bridge b to this client before the supplier cutoff; the bridge has zero interest, is senior on the client's entire cutoff-2 estate and cannot claim the issuer's assets directly. Supplier payments made at cutoff 1 are final. After bridge repayment, the client's shareholders receive the remainder. Keep public and client cash separate.

Decision policy. The invented board mandate requires every supplier to be paid by cutoff 1 and public principal loss to be at most 0.4 CU in the adverse scenario. The only further permitted action is a nonrepayable private cash contribution g∈[0,0.2] to the client before that cutoff. It must come from the sponsor's own separate cash, acquires no repayment or equity right and receives none of the client's eventual residual. Choose b=1−g so total new client funding is exactly 1; public cash not drawn stays with its vehicle. A sponsor has offered the contribution, but there is only an unexecuted email, with no received cash or independently confirmed settlement. Required approval records must identify a funding owner, independent risk challenge, effective rights and an execution cutoff. These are invented governance conditions.

  1. Derive the provider's incremental expected payoff as a function of face fine F. Can any face fine deter mis-selling at the given detection probability? With maximum collectible fine 9 fixed, find the minimum detection probability for weak deterrence and distinguish it from strict deterrence. State a relevant limit on interpreting this private incentive result.
  2. Reconcile issuer debt, equity and cash immediately after conversion; then quantify the client's early supplier gap. First take b=1 and g=0. Construct both entities' cutoff-2 waterfall in both scenarios and the public repayment/loss. In the adverse case, allocate the impairment of the client's issuer claim between lost client equity and public loss without counting it twice. Explain what the conversion did and did not accomplish.
  3. Derive the smallest private contribution satisfying both numerical board limits, the matching public bridge and its adverse repayment/loss. Make the approval decision on the evidence currently available. Give accountable owners and specific closure evidence that would permit reconsideration; do not count the email as executed cash or weaken the board's loss limit.
