Insurance, foreign funding and the price of a state

Research capability. Solve a small incomplete-market economy, certify a global finite policy, estimate a transition law from synthetic counts, distinguish a stationary distribution from a transition, price a marginal contingent transfer and compare a funded policy from common initial conditions. This open-resource project follows all twelve Volume 14 chapters. A suggested 18–24 hours and 2,500–4,000 words plus proofs, code and execution records are uncalibrated study-design choices, not validated completion times. All quantities and counts below are original synthetic teaching inputs. Nothing here estimates an actual economy.

The central question is deliberately narrow: can income insurance raise household welfare while lowering long-run assets, and what can a finite savings grid actually establish about prices and accuracy? Use the model's exact opportunities. Neither an Euler equality for continuous borrowing nor an endogenous world interest rate may be added silently.

Economy, timing and observations

A unit mass of households has a beginning-of-date foreign asset principal a and observed income state z∈{L,H}. Assets may be negative. Income is (1/2,3/2), the initial asset grid is (-1/4,1/4), discount β=19/20, and contractual safe gross return R=51/50. Households choose next principal a′ from that same grid, subject to strictly positive consumption:

c=y(z)+τ(z)+Ra−a′>0;   V(a,z)=maxₐ′{log c+βΣ_z′P(z,z′)V(a′,z′)}.

Current income and the payoff Ra are received before saving. Income then moves with P independently of the household's asset choice. There is no production, labor choice, domestic capital, default, aggregate shock or storage opportunity. The foreign safe claim is the only traded asset and has a finite set of permitted holdings. A positive return is not a universal arbitrage opportunity because consumption, borrowing and the upper asset bound constrain trades. State order is asset ascending, then L,H. Ties in computed action values choose the smaller asset index; an unresolved interval tie is reported as uncertified rather than called a strict optimum.

The conditional training-count matrix is ((80,20),(20,80)). Each row consists of 100 synthetic one-step transitions from households deliberately initialized in that income state. The separate held-out rows are ((28,12),(9,31)), forty transitions per starting state. These are stipulated panel counts, not a claim that a single path or a real survey generated them. The prespecified alternative is ((3/5,2/5),(2/5,3/5)). Fit P on training counts only; do not use the holdout to select a model and then call that same score untouched validation.

The common initial joint distribution is (1/4,1/4,1/4,1/4). Its income marginal is stationary under the fitted P. Foreign residents have per-date endowment W=2, receive new domestic investment A′, and pay RA on inherited aggregate domestic holdings. Their consumption is Cᴳ=W−RA+A′. They are a stipulated perfectly elastic counterparty at R, with sufficient resources verified below; their own optimality or a market-clearing world rate is not derived. Domestic aggregate consumption is C=Y+T+RA−A′. Thus C+Cᴳ=Y+T+W. Positive domestic assets are foreign liabilities; negative domestic assets are foreign claims. Both directions have counterparties.

The domestic fiscal authority levies income-state taxes and pays transfers, with no debt or real administrative cost. Baseline τ=0. The insurance counterfactual gives τL=1/5 and sets τH=−τL·πL/πH using the fitted invariant income shares. Because the common initial income marginal is π and income moves exogenously, net transfers are zero at every comparison date, even while wealth changes. The continuum uses a deterministic cross-sectional law of large numbers; a finite sample's realized imbalance would require a reserve or additional tax rule. Taxes change the private resource constraint, not the world resource total.

Two further comparisons are mandatory. Raise the contractual safe return to 11/10, keeping the same initial principal law, and separately refine the grid to (-1/4,0,1/4) with zero initial mass at the new middle asset. The rate comparison changes date0 inherited payoffs as well as future terms; the foreign account includes that difference. It is not a pure unexpected change only to future interest rates. Grid refinement preserves bounds and nests all old actions; it supplies a finite opportunity-set comparison, not a bound on a continuous-assets economy.

For conditional pricing consider a tiny auxiliary next-date consumption payoff x(z′)=(1/2,3/2). Under the strictly optimal existing policy g, define m(z′|a,z)=βc(a,z)/c(g(a,z),z′). Its marginal willingness-to-pay price is qₓ=ΣPmx, and the marginal price of a unit payoff is q₁=ΣPm. These are valuations of infinitesimal consumption perturbations under the locally unchanged finite policy. The auxiliary claim is not actually added to the household's trading menu.

  1. Specify and estimate. Give a complete dated household, foreign and fiscal ledger. Estimate both transition rows by maximum likelihood from the supplied conditional training counts. Prove the row estimator, compute invariant income shares and training/held-out log likelihoods against the fixed alternative. Explain what the counts do and do not identify.
  2. Solve and certify. Enumerate every feasible state-action consumption, solve the baseline Bellman problem and give its value, policy and all alternative-action gaps. Prove contraction, the residual bound and all-history optimality, including the tail. Build an explicit log-evaluation error bound; use it to certify the finite policy rather than rely on a plotted convergence curve.
  3. Distribution and world goods. Construct the baseline policy transition directly. Explain how to handle reducible, periodic or multiple-recurrent-class laws. Reconcile the counterfactual graph classifications in task 5.
  4. Price risk without inventing a market. Derive the conditional marginal-utility kernel and all four baseline risky-payoff and unit-payoff prices. Show the covariance decomposition and ordinary expected-return formula. Compare marginal unit-payoff prices with the traded finite-grid bond price1/R. Explain why a discrepancy does not refute the solved household optimum or establish an implementable arbitrage.
  5. Funded policy and rate sensitivity. Solve redistribution and the higher safe-rate regime from the common initial principal law. Complete instructor benchmark and proofs

  1. 1. Estimate only what was observed

    Receive y+τ+Ra, choose a′ and consume the remainder, then draw next income. A domestic saving purchase is foreign funding received now; its return is foreign payment later. Summing the two current budgets gives C+Cᴳ=Y+T+W, so fiscal transfers must separately satisfy T=0. Contractual return is applied once to inherited principal.

    For each training row with counts nL,nH, the parameter-dependent conditional likelihood is p^nL(1−p)^nH. Its log derivative nL/p−nH/(1−p) vanishes at p=nL/(nL+nH), and the second derivative is strictly negative when both counts are positive. The maximum is unique and interior here; zero counts require the appropriate boundary and log-likelihood convention rather than division by zero. Independent conditional panel rows make this a conditional MLE, not a stationary initial-frequency estimator. The fitted law is ((4/5,1/5),(1/5,4/5)). Invariance and normalization give (1/2,1/2).

  2. 2. Finite feasibility and a global policy certificate

    Baseline consumption by action, with columns a′=−1/4,+1/4 and rows in the declared state order, is((99/200,−1/200),(299/200,199/200),(201/200,101/200),(401/200,301/200)). The negative entry is excluded before applying log. Every state retains at least one action; the finitely many remaining log rewards are bounded. The certified asset-index policy is (0,1,0,1), giving consumption (99/200,199/200,201/200,301/200).

    For bounded vectors v,w, each action continuation changes by at most β∥v−w∥∞ because P is stochastic. The maximum operator cannot enlarge that bound, so ∥Tv−Tw∥∞≤β∥v−w∥∞. Iterates are Cauchy: successive distances are bounded by a geometric series, whose sum is finite. Their limit V satisfies TV=V by continuity. Two fixed points differ by at most β times their own distance and hence coincide. For any candidate v, the triangle inequality gives ∥v−V∥∞≤∥v−Tv∥∞/(1−β). This is a finite-model numerical bound, with no reference to a continuous asset space.

    Choose an action attaining TV at each state. Its policy matrix Q is stochastic, so the series Σβ^tQ^tu converges absolutely and equals the unique solution of(I−βQ)V=u. For any history-dependent feasible randomized alternative, condition on its complete history. The Bellman inequality holds for every current action and therefore for their mixture. Iterated conditional expectations imply E[Σₜ₌₀ⁿ⁻¹βᵗuₜ+βⁿV(sₙ)]≤V(s₀). The terminal term is bounded by βⁿ∥V∥∞ and tends to zero; the reward tail is bounded by βⁿmax|u|/(1−β). Taking limits proves global optimality after every possible starting history. Finite policy enumeration alone is a numerical cross-check, not this all-history proof.

    The value vector is (-3.9256186398,-1.2576366165,-3.2174335818,-0.8438311765). For the three states with a feasible alternative, chosen-minus-alternative Bellman gaps are0.0419085468,0.0713407136,0.1621961329; the first state has only one feasible action. The smallest rigorously bounded gap is 0.0419085468, strictly positive. These margins select a unique action in every state within this grid, not a unique general equilibrium.

    Reproducible logarithm bound. Write any rational c>0 as2ᵏz with1≤z<2, set t=(z−1)/(z+1), and use log z=2Σⱼ₌₀∞t^(2j+1)/(2j+1). This follows by integrating the geometric series for2/(1−t²) between0 and t; uniform convergence on |t|≤1/3 justifies integration. Truncating after N terms has absolute remainder at most2|t|^(2N+1)/[(2N+1)(1−t²)]. Apply the same formula to log2 and add |k| times its remainder. All centers and bounds are rational. The reader uses N=24 and solves the policy linear system exactly for these rational centers.

    If ε bounds every policy reward's log error, its value-center error is at most e=ε/(1−β), since ∥(I−βQ)⁻¹∥∞≤1/(1−β) by the stochastic series. For an alternative reward center with error εa, subtract at most(1+β)e+εa from its computed policy-value gap. Every resulting rational lower bound is positive here. The baseline value error is less than10⁻²², while its smallest gap exceeds0.04, so the conclusion does not rely on a tolerance-based tie. Output floats are readable summaries of that rational certificate. Iteration only locates a candidate; the certificate can succeed even if a deliberately short iteration has not reached its stopping tolerance, and any unresolved gap is reported honestly.

  3. 3. A distribution is another equation

    The next asset is fixed by the current policy and next income follows its current-income row. The baseline matrix is ((4/5,1/5,0,0),(0,0,1/5,4/5),(4/5,1/5,0,0),(0,0,1/5,4/5)). Solving wQ=w and Σw=1 gives (2/5,1/10,1/10,2/5), which directly verifies invariance. Wealth and income are correlated although each marginal is symmetric.

    Every entry of Q² is at least1/25. Thus Q²=(4/25)J+(21/25)K where J has every row uniform on four states and K is stochastic. For probability laws μ,ν, their difference has zero total mass, so(μ−ν)J=0 and ∥μQ²−νQ²∥₁≤(21/25)∥μ−ν∥₁. Iterating proves a unique invariant law and convergence from every initial distribution. Odd dates follow by the nonexpansiveness of Q. The argument supplies a genuine finite-state mixing bound, not only a small stationary residual.

    Stationary mean assets and next assets are both zero. Domestic consumption is 1, foreign consumption is 2, and world resources are3. From the common initial law the baseline also has mean current and next assets zero, but its joint distribution is not invariant: the first next law is already the stated invariant law because the policy depends only on current income and that income marginal is stationary. Budget reconciliation, rather than a numerical welfare average, establishes feasibility.

    For a general computed policy, build its directed graph from positive transition entries. Mutual reachability partitions communicating classes; a class is closed if no edge leaves it. Each finite closed irreducible class has a unique invariant law: a stationary law exists by a convergent subsequence of finite Cesàro averages, irreducibility forces positivity, and a maximum-ratio argument between two positive invariant laws gives uniqueness. The period is the gcd of cycle lengths, equivalently gcd of distance(i)+1−distance(j) over edges after choosing graph distances. Period1 permits convergence within the class; self-loops are a sufficient witness here. Transient states reach some closed class. With one closed aperiodic class, a finite path-length block has a strictly positive probability of reaching it from every transient state, making nonentry probabilities decline geometrically; combine this with within-class convergence. With several closed classes, invariant mixtures are not unique and initial absorption probabilities matter. With periodic classes, invariance need not imply date-by-date convergence. The reader lists all closed classes and their laws and refuses the project's convergence certification when its premises fail.

  4. 4. Value a small payoff at the stated opportunity set

    Reduce current consumption by an infinitesimal price p and add a next-date consumption payoff x. The first-order expected utility change is −p/c+βΣP x/c′. Strict policy gaps keep existing finite actions locally unchanged, so the willingness-to-pay threshold is p=ΣPβc x/c′. State probabilities and consumption differ by current state. A positive marginal kernel gives positive prices for positive payoffs, but it need not be the pricing kernel of a different enlarged market.

Adding a tradable risky claim requires a newly specified admissible portfolio set and a new optimization/clearing problem.

  5. 5. Insurance changes incentives, not the resource total

    Since π=(1/2,1/2), insurance pays1/5 in L and taxes1/5 in H. At date0 and every later date the income marginal is π: summing the joint transition over next assets gives μzP, independently of the asset policy. Induction therefore makes Tₜ=πLτL+πHτH=0 at every date. This proves balanced funding along the complete common-initial comparison path; balancing only at each regime's final wealth distribution would be insufficient in general.

    Insurance smooths income directly and weakens precautionary saving; the eventual domestic net debt creates interest payments, so stationary domestic consumption199/200 is below one even though common-initial domestic welfare rises. In the higher-rate regime long-run assets rise and stationary domestic consumption exceeds one, yet common-initial welfare falls. Initial low-asset households face costlier contractual debt, and transition welfare is not summarized by the new stationary flow.     For any feasible state/action in any supplied regime, foreign consumption is at least W−R·max A+min A, whose smallest supplied value is2−(11/10)(1/4)−1/4=59/40>0. The insurance table sums to3, as does the higher-rate table. The authority collects and pays equal aggregate transfers; foreign goods finance negative domestic saving. A household welfare gain is not a free world Pareto improvement: foreign consumption changes along transition and across stationary regimes, and their utility was not specified.

    No labor-supply response, search externality, sticky-price stabilization, default option or distortionary-tax cost was modeled. Those mechanisms from the rest of the volume cannot be inferred from this endowment experiment. Likewise, the rate stress is a change in the full contractual regime, including inherited date0 payoffs. An unexpected refinancing-only shock would require a separate inherited-rate state and newly dated budgets.

  6. 6. Three errors that must remain distinct

    After20 baseline value-iteration steps from zero the Bellman residual is 0.0452569154. Dividing by1−β gives a value-error bound 0.9051383077; the actual sup distance to the independently certified policy value is 0.9049852206, which lies below that bound. The final tighter residual is below10⁻¹⁰, hence the residual-based value bound is below2·10⁻⁹ before allowing numerical evaluation error. For a fully rational certificate use the explicit log and policy-system bounds in task2 rather than treating a floating residual as exact.

    On the refined grid the six-state policy is (0,1,0,2,1,2) and the invariant law is (1/3,1/12,1/12,1/12,1/12,1/3). Its graph is irreducible, includes a self-loop and is aperiodic. Direct multiplication verifies invariance. Mean assets remain zero and stationary domestic/foreign consumption remains1 and2, but finer smoothing changes conditional utility. Give the two new middle-asset states zero initial mass; common-initial welfare improves by 0.1816906558.

    Every old feasible history-dependent strategy is still feasible from an old initial state because its actions and inherited states belong to the refined grid; consumption and transition laws on those actions are unchanged. The supremum over the larger strategy set is therefore weakly higher at every old state. This proves the direction independently of a numerical table. The strict reported gain is certified only for this particular refinement. A small iteration residual bounds error relative to the finite-grid fixed point. A small distribution residual needs a mixing/invertibility bound before it bounds distribution distance. The difference between two grids is a sensitivity diagnostic, not an error bound relative to the continuous model: omitted interior actions, borrowing boundaries and policy discontinuities still matter. A credible continuous extension needs regularity, domain control, feasible interpolation and a justified operator discrepancy bound.

  7. 7. Execution is evidence with a limited scope

    The instructor package supplies a complete reproducible reference implementation and output; it does not certify the learner's independent implementation or oral defense. Run the files in a new five-file directory from a different working directory. Record the Python command, strict-JSON comparison and hashes. The normal and optimized checker gates separately reconstruct the economic quantities from primitives, compare all displayed values, and test the real CLI's protected writes. A small example of an invalid input is discount1 or a negative count; guards remain active under−O. A valid failure is setting foreign endowment to zero while retaining the private economy: the household problem can still be solved, but some foreign obligations cannot be funded, so the output reports a funding shortfall instead of certifying the project. Multiple closed classes or a periodic recurrent class likewise require explicit distribution diagnostics.

    Test each protected path as an output, then test a symbolic link and an existing hard link to it, in disposable copies. Verify every protected byte after each actual subprocess refusal; a successful call to a path-comparison helper is not a rejection test. Also test a normalized path containing a parent-directory segment. A manifest mismatch is a provenance failure, not automatically an economic error. Keep the distinction between author review, independent reconstruction and learner work.

    A defensible evidence memo would seek household panel observations of income transitions, asset choices, borrowing limits and consumption before and after an externally assigned insurance change. State the sampling unit, income measurement and attrition assumptions; quantify uncertainty in transition estimates rather than treating the synthetic sample size as real evidence. To estimate preferences or borrowing constraints, use additional moments and exclusion restrictions, check observationally similar parameter configurations and retain a separate validation sample. A possible reserved moment is the conditional saving response among initially low-income, low-asset households; do not fit that same response and advertise it as an independent test. A randomized or plausibly external policy assignment must still address take-up, labor incentives, spillovers and foreign funding effects. A common factor in observed interest rates is not itself causal identification.

    For the oral defense change one primitive, predict the affected feasibility or incentive inequality before rerunning, then explain any policy jump and altered recurrent class. Full credit requires a correct changed-model argument and truthful limits, not agreement with an insurance-policy conclusion. A successful numerical replication establishes the stated finite calculations; it does not establish real-world validity, unrestricted general equilibrium or research novelty.

Method sources and bounded transfer

The finite household problem is situated in the payoff-unit borrowing and stationary-distribution tradition of Huggett's revised1991 working paper, whose version and actual inspected scope were established in Chapter3. The conditional consumption-kernel context uses Sargent and Stachurski's finite-state asset-pricing lecture, inspected for Chapter7. Foreign-flow and price-taking distinctions connect to Uribe and Schmitt-Grohé's flow-collateral methods, inspected for Chapter10. Those sources motivate methods; none supplies these synthetic counts, policies, exact logarithm bounds, funded counterfactuals or project problems. Their continuous equilibrium, collateral or empirical results are not imported into this different finite endowment economy.
