Volume 8 assessment — Corporate finance and valuation

Build the cash flows, value the correct claims, identify who funds the decision, and defend a recommendation that survives its stated downside.

Complete all twelve chapters before the examinations and project. This package contains a prerequisite diagnostic, two distinct cumulative exams, an investment-committee project with a reproducible benchmark, oral transfer, delayed retention and a remediation index. Every assessment company, forecast, quoted price, tax rule and financing agreement is an original fictional teaching input. A supplied price or contractual term is an assumption to use within its case, not a verified market observation or statement of current law.

Prerequisite diagnostic

Before starting this volume, allow 40 minutes without notes, using a basic calculator. Eight five-point items give 40 points. Aim for at least 34/40 and at least four points each on Items 2, 3, 5 and 7. Repair every material units, timing, claim or funding error before proceeding. The diagnostic uses arithmetic, probability, cash accounting and evidence skills from earlier volumes; it does not require the corporate-finance models that Volume 8 will teach. Cases are independent. Unless stated otherwise, amounts are thousands of currency units, abbreviated kCU; a share count in thousands converts kCU per thousand shares into CU per share.

  1. Annual revenue is 250 kCU and total expense is 200 kCU, with 25 thousand shares outstanding throughout. Calculate profit, profit margin and profit per share. Next year's margin is forecast at 25%. Express that margin change in percentage points and as a percentage of the original margin.
  2. At a constant annual effective discount rate of 10%, compare a certain receipt of 121 at the end of Year 2 with a certain receipt of 105 at the end of Year 1. Find both date-0 values and state which is larger. Explain why comparing the two undiscounted receipts alone is insufficient.
  3. A company starts with receivables 20 and trade payables 10. Credit sales are 160, customer collections 140, credit inventory purchases 80, cost of goods sold 80, supplier payments 75, other cash operating expense 30 and depreciation 10. Ignore tax and all other transactions. Find profit, closing receivables/payables, direct operating cash flow and the equivalent indirect cash bridge. Inventory quantity and carrying value are unchanged because purchases equal cost of goods sold.
  4. Assets are 120 and liabilities 70. The company borrows 30 in cash, then pays an immediately expensed financing fee of two; ignore tax. Show assets, liabilities and equity after borrowing but before the fee, then after the fee. Which part of the cash receipt increases owners' net wealth at that instant?
  5. At one terminal date an estate has cash 70 or 130, each with physical probability one half. Debt has first claim to at most 100; common equity receives the remainder, with no other claims or costs. Compute both state-by-state payoffs and both expected payoffs. Do these expectations by themselves specify today's market prices?
  6. Two equally likely observations are (X,Y) = (−1,0) and (1,4). Calculate E[X], E[Y], Var(X), Cov(X,Y) and the population linear-projection slope Cov(X,Y)/Var(X). Does the numerical slope establish that changing X causes Y to change?
  7. Cash is 14 on Monday morning. A payment of 20 is due that day; a receipt of 16 arrives on Friday. The contract requires cash of at least five immediately after Monday's payment. At most eight of new borrowing can be drawn before the payment; there are no fees, interest or other flows this week. Find the draw needed to satisfy the reserve, Monday cash at the maximum permitted draw, the reserve gap and Friday cash. Distinguish the ability to make the payment from compliance with the reserve covenant.
  8. An analyst observes high returns in nine surviving investment funds selected because their managers supplied results. The analyst concludes that a new fund will deliver the same investor return. Identify two distinct reasons the inference is unsupported, one targeted data request, and how the conclusion should be worded while that evidence is missing.
Diagnostic answers and five-point scoring
  1. Profit is 50 kCU; margin is 50/250 = 20%; profit per share is 50/25 = 2 CU. The forecast change is five percentage points and 5/20 = 25% relative to the original margin. Credit: one point for each of these five results, with correct units. Review units and percentages.
  2. PV of the Year-2 receipt is 121/(1.10)² = 100. PV of the Year-1 receipt is 105/1.10 = 1050/11 ≈ 95.455. The former is worth 50/11 ≈ 4.545 more at date 0 under the stipulated certain-payment rate. Cash available earlier has a different investment opportunity. Credit: two per correct dated value; one for the ordering and timing explanation. Review dated value and powers and functions.
  3. Profit = 160 − 80 − 30 − 10 = 40. Receivables close at 20 + 160 − 140 = 40; payables at 10 + 80 − 75 = 15. Direct operating cash = 140 − 75 − 30 = 35. Indirect cash = profit 40 + depreciation 10 − receivables increase 20 + payables increase five = 35. No inventory adjustment is needed here. Credit: profit one; both closing balances one; direct cash one; signed indirect bridge and agreement two. Review statement reconciliation and working capital.
  4. Before the fee, assets are 150, liabilities 100 and equity 50. After the fee, assets are 148, liabilities 100 and equity 48. Borrowing adds an equal asset and claim, so the receipt itself creates no immediate owners' net wealth; the fee reduces it by two. Credit: each complete balance sheet two; financing-versus-wealth explanation one. Review transactions and liability claims.
  5. Debt receives (70,100), and equity receives (0,30). Each state's payments sum to its estate. Expected debt repayment is 85, expected equity payoff 15, and expected estate cash 100. Promised debt of 100 is not its expected repayment. Current market prices additionally require a pricing model or traded-claim/state-price information, including dates and risk compensation. Credit: both payoff pairs two; both expectations and conservation two; price limitation one. Review random variables, moments and claims.
  6. E[X] = 0, E[Y] = 2, Var(X) = 1 and Cov(X,Y) = E[XY] − E[X]E[Y] = 2. The slope is two units of Y per unit of X. A statistical projection is not a causal intervention argument; omitted common causes or selection can produce association. Credit: means one; variance one; covariance one; slope one; causal limitation one. Review covariance, regression and causal reasoning.
  7. The reserve requires 14 + draw − 20 ≥ 5, hence draw ≥ 11. The limit permits eight, leaving Monday cash two and a reserve gap of three. Friday cash is two + 16 = 18, with the eight borrowing still outstanding. The Monday payment can be made, but the stipulated reserve covenant is breached; Friday's receipt does not retroactively cure compliance. Credit: required draw and binding limit two; Monday cash/gap one; Friday cash one; payment-versus-covenant distinction one. Review financing constraints.
  8. Survival and voluntary reporting can select unusually favorable outcomes; the observed funds may also differ from the proposed fund in vintage, risk, leverage, fees or strategy. Request the complete defined investment universe, including failed/nonreporting funds, and dated investor contributions, distributions, fees and valuation policies on a comparable basis. These data would improve comparison; they do not ensure future performance or identify manager skill by themselves. State that the selected observations motivate a hypothesis whose generalizability and investor-net return remain unverified. Credit: two distinct mechanisms two; targeted universe and cash-flow request two; appropriately conditional conclusion one. Other specific, logically supported answers earn equivalent credit. Review data provenance, causal reasoning and forecast evaluation.

Examination rules, coverage and advancement

Each form is 100 points and 240 minutes, closed book and closed solutions, with a basic nonprogrammable calculator. Five questions each contain four five-point parts; each question is one essential 20-point domain. Show the dated cash flows or claims, the equation, the result and its interpretation. Exact fractions are welcome; keep intermediate precision and round only final displayed answers. Cases are independent unless explicitly linked. No code, internet lookup or unprovided market estimate is needed for either written form.

Advance after at least 85/100 overall and at least 15/20 in every written domain, plus the project criterion and satisfactory oral transfer below. The two forms use different economic structures, not merely changed company names or values. Use Form B after targeted remediation without rehearsing its solutions. Equal scores are a learning convention, not evidence that the forms have been empirically calibrated to equal difficulty. These thresholds are this library's design choices, not university rules.

Use the analytic credit allocations in each solution. Carry a single arithmetic mistake forward when the subsequent method is internally consistent; do not repeatedly penalize the same slip. Withhold credit for a result unsupported by the relevant reasoning. Equivalent valid methods and evidence-backed alternative judgments receive equal credit. A material claim/currency/date mismatch, double-counted cash flow, unlimited financing plug, nonexistent tax benefit, impossible ownership allocation, unstated covenant waiver or fabricated evidence requires correction even when the total passes. A defensible model value is conditional on its inputs; a defensible decision additionally requires executable funding and rights.

Keep physical probabilities, market pricing weights and personal decision criteria distinct. A quoted debt face is not automatically its market value. A projected annual cash surplus does not establish payment-date liquidity. A growing terminal cash flow requires a stated reinvestment mechanism. A fair share issue changes ownership without automatically destroying value. The project is open-resource, but its supplied benchmark is an instructor reference to inspect only after saving an independent attempt.
