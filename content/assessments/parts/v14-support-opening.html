Solve the economy, account for its claims, and test what the evidence can identify.

This package combines a prerequisite diagnostic, distinct written Forms A and B, a reproducible model project, oral transfer and delayed retention. All instructional numerical economies and datasets are synthetic unless a source record explicitly says otherwise. Complete the diagnostic before the volume; undertake the examinations and project after all twelve chapters. Keep original attempts, corrections and source logs. Passing an internally designed assessment does not certify research achievement or institutional credentials.

Prerequisite diagnostic

Use paper and a basic calculator, without notes or code. Proposed allowance75minutes is uncalibrated. Eight questions carry5points each. Readiness requires34/40, at least4on questions1,4,8, and repair of every critical budget, conditioning or feasibility error. Explain domains as well as numbers.

  1. Date consumption and debt. A household has two-date utility log c₀+log c₁, incomes(10,121/10), gross lending/borrowing return11/10, no initial debt and terminal debt zero. Derive its globally optimal consumption and date0 saving. State the foreign counterparty flow.
  2. Respect an active bound. Maximize4x−x² over0≤x≤1. Give the unique optimum, value and upper-bound multiplier, and derive the local value derivative if the upper bound B varies near1.
  3. Propagate a distribution. A two-state Markov chain has rows((4/5,1/5),(3/10,7/10)). Starting in low state with certainty, find high-state probabilities at dates1,2 and in the stationary law. If incomes are(1,3), find stationary mean income. Explain why a stationary probability is not the initial probability.
  4. Price state risk. Two equally probable states have positive SDF m=(4/5,6/5) and asset payoff X=(3,1). Calculate price, safe gross return, expected asset gross return and its expected excess return. Reconcile the sign with the payoff/SDF covariance.
  5. Keep marked equity and cash separate. A bank holds assets120 funded by debt100 and equity20. An exogenous asset loss10 occurs with debt face fixed. It must reduce assets/equity to5 by selling assets at their already-marked value and immediately repaying debt. Compute post-loss leverage, sales and final assets/debt/equity.
  6. Name the sampling law. Ten independent Bernoulli observations contain three successes. Derive the maximum likelihood estimate on(0,1) and its plug-in sampling variance. Explain what changes with zero successes or with a dependent sequence.
  7. Close a fiscal balance. Outstanding debt principal is10 at date0. Gross safe return is11/10 each period. With Bₜ=RBₜ₋₁−sₜ, let B₀=10, primary surplus s₁=3 and terminal B₂=0. Find B₁ and s₂ and verify the present-value backing identity. State why a finite equality alone is not an infinite-horizon debt theorem.
  8. Certify an iteration. On[0,1], T(x)=1/5+3x/5. Prove existence and uniqueness of a fixed point using contraction. For x̂=3/5 compute its residual, actual error and residual certificate.
Complete solutions and additive scoring

  1. The present-value budget is c₀+c₁/(11/10)=21. Substitute c₁=(11/10)(21−c₀); the strictly concave log objective has derivative1/c₀−1/(21−c₀), uniquely zero at c₀=21/2. Thus (21/2,231/20) are the two consumptions. Saving is −1/2: foreigners advance1/2 now and receive11/20 later, leaving terminal consumption121/10−11/20=231/20. Positive consumption and the negative-infinite boundary make the interior solution global. No unpaid terminal balance remains.

    Rubric (5 points). PV budget1; global derivative/domain1; consumption1; saving1; counterpart/timing1. Study routes: v06-c04, v02-c08.

  2. The objective is strictly concave and its derivative4−2x stays positive on[0,1]. Thus (1,3,2) gives choice, value and upper multiplier. For0<B<2 the optimum isB and V(B)=4B−B², so V′(1)=2. The unconstrained stationary point2 is infeasible. The multiplier equals the local value of relaxing the actual bound, not the unconstrained derivative evaluated at2.

    Rubric (5 points). Domain/global maximum1; choice1; value1; multiplier1; derivative/domain1. Study routes: v02-c08, v13-c02.

  3. Let h be the high mass. The next mass is1/5+(1/2)h. From zero it becomes (1/5,3/10,2/5), listing date1,date2,stationary. The unique fixed point2/5 follows from a contraction of factor1/2. Mean income is 9/5. The stationary law is invariant; it does not describe a specified nonstationary initial law before its transition has run. The two states are connected and both have self-loops.

    Rubric (5 points). Transition equation1; dates1; fixed point1; mean1; initial/stationary distinction1. Study routes: v03-c05, v06-c03.

  4. PriceE[mX]=(12/5+6/5)/2=9/5. E[m]=1, so the safe gross return is1. Mean payoff2 gives (10/9,1/9) for mean gross and excess returns. Cov(m,X)=9/5−2=−1/5: the claim pays poorly in the high-marginal-value state, so a positive premium is consistent with Euler pricing. Return covariance is payoff covariance divided by price.

    Rubric (5 points). Price1; safe return1; mean return1; premium1; covariance/sign1. Study routes: v09-c07, v09-c08.

  5. After the loss, assets110 and equity10 imply 11. Selling z and repaying z leaves equity10 unchanged. The cap requires110−z≤50; minimal sales are 60. Final accounts are (50,40,10). This assumes execution at the marking price and no fees or further price impact. Proceeds used to repay debt cannot simultaneously remain as additional cash assets.

    Rubric (5 points). Marked accounts1; leverage1; equity-preserving sale1; amount1; final accounts/assumption1. Study routes: v07-c02, v11-c03.

  6. The score is3/p−7/(1−p), positive below3/10 and negative above it, giving the unique global estimate 3/10. Independent indicators yield Var(p̂)=p(1−p)/10; its plug-in value is 21/1000, not an exact coverage statement. With zero successes, likelihood decreases on(0,1), so there is a supremum at the excluded boundary rather than an attained MLE; closing the domain changes that. Dependence requires its covariance terms and a suitable likelihood/sampling argument.

    Rubric (5 points). Likelihood/score1; global estimate1; variance1; open-boundary1; dependence/coverage1. Study routes: v12-c04, v03-c08.

  7. The dated recursion gives (8,44/5): B₁=11−3=8 and s₂=(11/10)8=44/5. Discounting gives3/(11/10)+(44/5)/(121/100)=30/11+80/11=10. Each surplus is resources transferred from taxpayers to creditors. An infinite extension requires convergence and a terminal condition; ignoring a nonvanishing discounted balance would change the identity. Here dates and terminal liquidation are explicitly finite.

    Rubric (5 points). Debt recursion1; B₁1; s₂1; PV check1; terminal/funding scope1. Study routes: v06-c07, v08-c02.

  8. The map sends[0,1] into[1/5,4/5] and has Lipschitz constant3/5. Successive iterate differences shrink geometrically, so their sum is Cauchy in a complete interval; the limit is fixed by continuity. Two fixed points differ by at most3/5 of their distance, forcing equality. The fixed point is 1/2. At3/5 the residual is|3/5−14/25|=1/25, hence error≤(1/25)/(2/5)=1/10, here exactly the actual error.

    Rubric (5 points). Invariance1; Cauchy existence1; uniqueness1; residual1; bound/actual1. Study routes: v02-c10, v13-c04.

Administration and advancement

Form A constructs economies and certificates; Form B diagnoses and reconstructs proposed solutions. Each has20scored subparts in five20point domains, total100. Suggested300minutes per form is an author-created allowance, not a measured equivalence to any university exam. Use paper and a nonprogrammable calculator, without solutions, notes or code, unless a prompt explicitly provides a tool. A separate open-resource project assesses implementation.

Essential domains shared by the two forms
DomainChaptersRequired reasoning
Dynamic and distributional methods1–3Optimization, terminal conditions, state distributions and aggregate clearing
Labor and policy4–6Values/entry, nominal rules, fiscal funding and equilibrium domains
Pricing and investment7–8SDF risk, marginal investment, incentives and balance-sheet constraints
Liquidity and international finance9–10Off-path incentives, dealer resources, currency units and cross-border accounts
Quantitative research11–12True-operator accuracy, identification, validation and a discriminating research question

The written criterion is85/100 overall and at least15/20in each domain. A critical error in resource feasibility, off-path incentives, terminal funding, identification or integrity requires remediation even if points pass. Grade derivations and explicit assumptions; equivalent valid approaches earn credit. Where a rubric permits partial credit, do not award the same reasoning point twice. After corrections, use the distinct form after a study interval, then complete transfer and teach-back. These thresholds are instructional choices, not validated predictors of professional success.

For the project, retain inputs, executable code, outputs and every failed diagnostic. The five downloadable files must run offline with standard-library Python. Synthetic estimates demonstrate a method; they do not validate the economy empirically. A human evaluator should inspect the learner’s independent work and oral defense before judging research readiness.
