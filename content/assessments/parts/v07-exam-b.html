Form B — Reassessment

100 points; 180 minutes; closed notes; calculator permitted. Answer all five questions. Each has four parts worth 5 points each; allow about 36 minutes per question. Show entries, reconciliations and assumptions where requested. All businesses and figures are original fictional cases. Monetary amounts are kCU unless a share price is explicitly stated in CU. There are no unlisted transactions, taxes or other comprehensive income unless the question supplies them. Exact fractions or clearly rounded decimals with the underlying calculation are acceptable.

Different questions describe different entities. Within a question, use its stated timing and measurement rules. A hypothetical comparison does not authorize a reporting-policy election or establish a legal remedy. The test assesses the methods taught in the twelve accounting chapters; its time and score design are author choices, not institutionally calibrated examination standards.

Question 1 — Advances, allowances and the complete close · 20 points · Domain D1

Hazel Service opens a quarter with cash 70, gross receivables 30, a credit allowance for losses 3, prepaid supplies 12 and equipment 60. Liabilities are trade payables 20 and customer advances 24; equity is 125. All receivables are from previously earned service revenue. During the quarter Hazel receives 48 of further customer advances, earns 60 of service revenue against the combined advance balance, earns and immediately collects 40 on other services, earns 50 on credit, and collects 44 of receivables. These categories do not overlap. It writes off 4 of existing receivables against the allowance; evidence requires a closing credit allowance 6. It pays 18 for supplies, consumes 20 of supplies, pays 85 for other current operating services and accrues another 8 of operating service expense unpaid at close. It pays 15 against opening trade payables. Depreciation is 6 and an owner dividend 5 is paid. There is no tax, borrowing, asset purchase, disposal or other flow. Other service expense excludes supplies, bad debts and depreciation; the accrued 8 enters trade payables.

  1. Calculate earned revenue and closing customer advances. Give the entries for the 48 advance receipt and the 60 service performance against advances. Compare total customer cash receipts with revenue and explain why they differ.
  2. Roll gross receivables and the allowance forward. Calculate the required bad-debt expense and closing net receivables. Give the write-off and allowance-adjustment entries, explaining why the write-off is not an additional new expense under the stated allowance method.
  3. Calculate total expense, profit and closing equity. Also find closing prepaid supplies, equipment and trade payables. Explain why the dividend is absent from expense.
  4. Calculate CFO by both direct receipts/payments and an indirect profit bridge using net receivables. Find closing cash and reconcile the full balance sheet. Explain why adding bad-debt expense again to your net-receivable bridge would be wrong.

Question 2 — Measurement changes and actual settlement · 20 points · Domain D2

The four parts have the relationships stated explicitly below. Use no tax. All assets are available when specified. No fee, impairment or additional obligation exists unless supplied.

  1. A machine begins Year 1 at carrying amount 120 with a previously reasonable estimate of four remaining years and zero residual. New information at the start of Year 1 revises remaining life to three years; use straight-line depreciation prospectively. End-Year 1 recoverable amount is 70. At end-Year 2, after normal depreciation, new qualifying evidence gives recoverable amount 90. Stipulate a cost-model impairment rule and a reversal ceiling equal to the carrying amount that would exist without the impairment, using the revised life. Find both years' depreciation, impairment/reversal and closing carrying amounts. Explain why the reversal is not 55.
  2. A separate premium note begins the year at net amortized-cost liability 104. Its effective annual rate is 5%; a coupon 6 is paid at year-end. Immediately afterward the lender agrees to a full discharge for cash 101, with no remaining claim or fee. Find effective interest, the liability after the coupon and the retirement gain or loss; give the retirement entry. Under the supplied convention, coupon cash is operating and retirement cash financing. Show the indirect operating-cash adjustment needed for this note's net profit effect.
  3. An eligible two-year equipment-use right starts at date 0. The lessee pays 12 immediately and still owes 44 at date 1 and 48.4 at date 2. The supplied annual rate is 10%; no ownership transfer, other initial cost, incentive or residual exists, and the right is consumed evenly. A draft records both liability and right-of-use asset at 92. Determine which opening amount is wrong, and find the correct liability/asset, Year 1 interest, closing liability, depreciation and closing right-of-use asset. Explain why the paid 12 must not remain in the unpaid liability.
  4. As an alternative to keeping the machine in part (a) for Year 2, sell it for cash 74 immediately after its corrected end-Year 1 carrying amount 70 has been established. There are no sale costs or new depreciation. Give the disposal entry, cash-flow classification and gain/loss. Reconcile total expense attributable to the machine during the supplied history—depreciation plus impairment minus gain—to its opening carrying amount less disposal proceeds. Is the gain itself a second cash receipt?

Question 3 — Outside claims, internal profit and taxes · 20 points · Domain D3

Part (a) is a separate share ledger; parts (b)–(c) describe one controlled group; part (d) is a separate tax case. No tax applies to parts (a)–(c). Share counts in part (a) are thousands and share prices are CU, so their product is kCU.

  1. A firm has 120 thousand issued/outstanding shares, capital 120, ordinary share premium 78, treasury-reissue premium 2 and retained earnings 40; equity is 240. It repurchases 20 thousand shares for 3 CU each, then reissues 8 thousand for 2.5 CU each. Apply the taught treasury-share cost method: a reissue below cost uses the available treasury-reissue premium first and retained earnings for the rest. Find outstanding shares, treasury carrying amount, all closing equity components and total equity; give the reissue entry. Is the below-cost shortfall an operating loss?
  2. At the beginning of a year, Parent pays 90 for 60% of Subsidiary and obtains stipulated control of a business. Identifiable fair-value assets are 140 and external liabilities 40. The outside 40% interest has a separately supported full fair value 60. There are no other acquisition adjustments, costs or tax. Compute identifiable net assets, acquisition-date NCI and goodwill. Compare goodwill/NCI under the alternative proportionate-net-assets convention and state the effect of changing that convention on group cash and total group assets.
  3. Continue the full-fair-value NCI case in (b). During the post-acquisition year Parent earns 25 excluding all subsidiary dividend income. Subsidiary earns 50 and pays total dividends 10. Its profit includes a sale to Parent for 30 of goods costing 18; one-third of that batch remains unsold outside at year-end. There are no other group adjustments, goodwill impairment or fair-value depreciation differences. Compute unrealized upstream profit, group profit, profit attributed to NCI and parent owners, and closing NCI. Explain why simply taking 40% of Subsidiary's unadjusted closing book equity would fail.
  4. A separate firm reports pretax book profit 80, including a permanently nondeductible fine 5 and warranty accrual 12 deductible only when later paid. Book depreciation 20 is charged on an asset whose opening book amount and tax base were both 100; tax depreciation is 35. The tax rate is 25%, all resulting deferred tax is recognized, and sufficient future taxable profit is supplied for the warranty deduction. Opening deferred tax and current tax payable are zero; current tax cash paid is 18. Calculate taxable profit, current tax/payable, closing DTA and DTL, deferred tax expense, total tax expense and net income. Reconcile total tax expense minus paid tax without treating deferred tax as cash.

Question 4 — Relevant costs and ratio diagnosis · 20 points · Domain D4

Parts (a)–(b) concern a workshop; parts (c)–(d) are separate ratio cases. All relevant costs are supplied. A product's contribution includes every variable cost of its sale; committed common costs are unchanged.

  1. The workshop has four machine-hours and can make only whole jobs. Job A contributes 11 and needs three hours; job B contributes 7 and needs two hours. Demand caps are two jobs of each type. Find and prove the global best plan. Explain why ranking solely by contribution per hour fails here. Separately, an all-in payment 7 buys two more usable hours, with no other change; determine the best expanded plan and whether the expansion improves profit.
  2. Return to the original four-hour workshop. A separate special job uses two hours, has variable cash cost 4, sells for 16 and requires a separate preparation payment 3. It is all-or-nothing, has no price spillover and does not change normal demand or common commitments. Compare the best feasible regular plan with and without the order. Find its incremental profit and break-even quote, including the opportunity cost once. Explain why its positive own contribution alone would not be enough to make the decision.
  3. A separate entity has cash 12, receivables 18, inventory 60 and PPE 60. Current liabilities are trade payables 15 and current debt 30; long-term debt is 25, leaving common equity 80. It pays 10 of current debt from cash exactly at close, with no current interest or other effect. Calculate current and quick ratios before/after, closing cash and net debt before/after, using quick assets=cash+receivables and net debt=all interest-bearing debt minus cash. Explain why the rising current ratio is not proof of improved payment capacity.
  4. For another single-class company, annual sales 240, EBIT 40, interest 10, tax rate 20% and net income 24 all have the same scope. Average total assets 160, common equity 80, interest-bearing debt 60 and excess cash 20 are supplied. Average operating assets are 140 and operating payables 20; there are no other claims or deferred taxes. Calculate the three DuPont factors and ROE. Reconcile average invested capital from operating and financing sides, then calculate ROIC with NOPAT=EBIT(1−tax rate). Explain why NI is not the numerator for this ROIC.

Question 5 — Funding, comparability and evidence · 20 points · Domain D5

Parts (a)–(b) concern an original single-entity forecast. Opening cash 10 and PPE 90 equal debt 40 plus equity 60. Before interest on the proposed new line, the forecast has net income 18, depreciation 6 and CFO 24; there are no working-capital changes or taxes. Paid capital spending 40 is available only after this period, so it adds no current depreciation. Scheduled old principal payment is 10, and a planned but undeclared and uncommitted dividend is 2. All other interest is already included in 18 and 24. Any new line draw B occurs at the start of the year, remains outstanding all year, charges 10% interest paid at year-end, and provides no tax relief in this no-tax case. The available line permits at most B=30 and requires closing cash of at least 10. All receipts/payments are otherwise fundable at their supplied dates; do not infer additional interim funding facts. Parts (c)–(d) concern a separate two-segment group.

  1. Derive closing cash as a function of B, solve for the draw needed to preserve cash 10, and assess it against the limit 30. At the actual limit, find interest, closing cash and the cash-test shortfall. Explain why ignoring the new draw's own interest gives an incorrect funding requirement.
  2. Management cancels the uncommitted dividend before declaration while leaving every operating and investment assumption intact. Find the minimum line draw, interest, profit and all closing balance-sheet totals under that feasible policy. Reconcile CFO, investing and financing cash to closing cash 10. Why is canceling this proposed distribution different from silently erasing an already payable debt?
  3. A separate group's Segment U has external revenue 180, intersegment revenue 30 and total segment expense 160. Segment V has external revenue 120 and total expense 100, including the same 30 internal charge. All transferred goods/services are sold outside or consumed in external services this year, so no unrealized internal margin remains. Unallocated corporate cost is 12, group interest 6 and tax 10. There are no other eliminations. Find group external revenue, the sum of segment profits, group EBIT, pretax profit and net income. Explain why adding segment revenues or comparing a segment profit margin directly with group net margin would be misleading.
  4. In the group in (c), corporate cost 12 includes recurring cash marketing 5 and a cash reorganization charge 4. Segment expenses include a favorable cash settlement recovery 3 as a reduction of expense. Evidence supports treating the reorganization and recovery as nonrecurring for a particular forecast. Management presents “core EBIT” by adding both 5 and 4 to group EBIT while retaining the recovery. Calculate management's measure and a symmetric recurring scenario. Does either adjustment change historical cash? Separately, receivables rose during the year: state two competing explanations and an evidence request that would help distinguish them without declaring fraud from the increase alone.

Form B — Complete solutions and scoring

Open Form B worked solutions and part-by-part point rubrics

Every part carries 5 points. Award method credit where a later calculation correctly follows a clearly identified carried-forward arithmetic error; do not award a conceptual point for inconsistent scope, invented cash, unearned revenue, double-counted cost or an unsupported legal/intent conclusion. Equivalent correct journal layouts and justified explanations earn the same credit.

Question 1 solutions · D1

  1. Revenue is 60 + 40 + 50 = 150. Customer advances close at 24 + 48 − 60 = 12. Receipt: Dr Cash 48 / Cr Customer advances 48. Performance: Dr Customer advances 60 / Cr Service revenue 60. Total customer receipts are 48 + 40 + 44 = 132. These include cash for unperformed service and settlement of previously earned receivables; revenue includes performance funded by earlier advances and current credit service. Receipt and earning dates differ.

    5-point rubric: earned revenue: 1 point; closing advances: 1 point; advance receipt entry: 1 point; service-performance entry: 1 point; receipts of 132 and timing explanation: 1 point.

  2. Gross receivables close at 30 + 50 − 44 − 4 = 32. The write-off leaves the allowance at 3 − 4 = −1, a temporary debit balance. Required expense is 6 − (−1) = 7, giving closing credit allowance 6 and net receivables 32 − 6 = 26. Write-off: Dr Allowance 4 / Cr Receivables 4. Adjustment: Dr Bad-debt expense 7 / Cr Allowance 7. The write-off uses the loss allowance rather than charging another 4 to expense; the adjusting estimate brings the aggregate allowance to the required amount.

    5-point rubric: gross receivables: 1 point; allowance roll-forward and expense: 1 point; net receivables: 1 point; both entries: 1 point; no-double-expense explanation: 1 point.

  3. Total expense is supplies 20 + other service expense (85 + 8) + bad debts 7 + depreciation 6 = 126. Profit is 150 − 126 = 24; equity closes at 125 + 24 − 5 = 144. Prepaid supplies are 12 + 18 − 20 = 10; equipment 60 − 6 = 54; trade payables 20 − 15 + 8 = 13. The dividend distributes an owner's claim and reduces equity and cash; it is not a cost of earning revenue.

    5-point rubric: expense of 126: 1 point; profit and equity: 1 point; prepaid supplies and equipment: 1 point; payables: 1 point; dividend explanation: 1 point.

  4. Direct CFO is 132 − 18 − 85 − 15 = 14. With net receivables, indirect CFO is 24 + 6 − (26 − 27) − (10 − 12) + (13 − 20) + (12 − 24) = 24 + 6 + 1 + 2 − 7 − 12 = 14. Investing cash is zero and financing cash is −5. Cash closes at 70 + 14 − 5 = 79. Assets 79 + 26 + 10 + 54 = 169; liabilities 13 + 12 plus equity 144 also total 169. The net-receivable movement already incorporates the loss estimate; adding 7 again would overstate CFO. With gross balances one would instead reconcile the allowance/write-off movements consistently.

    5-point rubric: direct CFO: 1 point; indirect signs and reconciliation: 2 points; closing cash and full balance: 1 point; allowance double-counting explanation: 1 point.

Question 2 solutions · D2

  1. Year 1 revised depreciation is 120/3 = 40, leaving 80 before impairment. Impairment 80 − 70 = 10 leaves closing 70. Year 2 depreciation uses the remaining two years: 70/2 = 35, leaving 35 before reversal. Without impairment the machine would be 120 − 40 − 40 = 40. The stipulated ceiling limits the recovered carrying amount to min(90,40)=40, so reversal is 5 and closing carrying amount 40. A 55 reversal would incorrectly lift 35 to 90 despite the consumption-based ceiling. New information changes the prospective estimate; it does not create an earlier error.

    5-point rubric: Year 1 depreciation: 1 point; impairment and closing amount: 1 point; Year 2 depreciation and pre-reversal amount: 1 point; no-impairment ceiling: 1 point; reversal of 5 and explanation: 1 point.

  2. Effective interest is 104×5% = 5.2. After coupon 6 the liability is 104 + 5.2 − 6 = 103.2; the premium amortizes by 0.8. Retirement gain is 103.2 − 101 = 2.2. Entry: Dr Note liability 103.2 / Cr Cash 101 / Cr Retirement gain 2.2. The note's net income effect is −5.2 + 2.2 = −3. For operating cash, subtract the nonoperating gain 2.2 and subtract premium amortization 0.8: −3 − 2.2 − 0.8 = −6, equal to the paid coupon. Financing cash is −101. The gain compares settlement with the recognized liability, not future unaccrued interest.

    5-point rubric: effective interest: 1 point; liability after coupon: 1 point; retirement gain and entry: 1 point; operating-cash bridge including both adjustments: 2 points.

  3. The unpaid liability is 44/1.1 + 48.4/(1.1²) = 40 + 40 = 80. ROU asset is 80 + paid 12 = 92; the draft asset is correct but its liability is 12 too high. Year 1 interest is 80×10% = 8; after payment 44, liability is 44. Straight-line depreciation is 92/2 = 46, leaving ROU 46. Commencement records the asset 92 against liability 80 and cash 12. A paid amount creates no unpaid debt; including it twice overstates liability. The difference between closing asset 46 and liability 44 is not an error.

    5-point rubric: liability present value: 1 point; ROU asset and prepayment distinction: 1 point; interest and closing liability: 1 point; depreciation and closing ROU asset: 1 point; entry or explicit no-double-liability explanation: 1 point.

  4. Gain is 74 − 70 = 4. Net entry: Dr Cash 74 / Cr Machine carrying amount 70 / Cr Disposal gain 4; an equivalent gross-cost/accumulated-allowance entry is acceptable. Investing cash inflow is the full 74, not just the gain. The supplied-history expense is depreciation 40 + impairment 10 − gain 4 = 46, equal to opening carrying amount 120 − proceeds 74. This reconciles only the period starting with the given carrying amount, not unspecified earlier acquisition costs. The gain is an accrual component of the same disposal, not a second cash receipt.

    5-point rubric: disposal gain: 1 point; entry: 1 point; investing cash of 74: 1 point; expense/proceeds reconciliation: 1 point; no-second-cash explanation: 1 point.

Question 3 solutions · D3

  1. Repurchase costs 20×3 =60 and reissue receives 8×2.5 =20. Reissued shares carry cost 8×3 =24; the 4 shortfall uses treasury premium 2 and retained earnings 2. Reissue entry: Dr Cash 20 / Dr Treasury-reissue premium 2 / Dr Retained earnings 2 / Cr Treasury shares 24. Closing issued shares remain 120 thousand; outstanding shares are 120 − 20 + 8 = 108 thousand. Treasury shares carry 36. Equity components are capital 120, ordinary premium 78, treasury premium 0, retained earnings 38 and treasury deduction −36, totaling 200. Cash also falls net 40, reconciling the equity decline 240 to 200. No operating gain or loss is recognized under this cost-method convention.

    5-point rubric: reissue cost and shortfall: 1 point; reissue entry: 1 point; shares and treasury balance: 1 point; equity components and total: 1 point; no-operating-loss explanation: 1 point.

  2. Identifiable net assets are 140 − 40 = 100. Full-value NCI is the supplied 60; goodwill is 90 + 60 − 100 = 50. Under proportionate measurement, NCI=40%×100 = 40, so goodwill 90 + 40 − 100 = 30. Full measurement thus adds 20 to both goodwill/group assets and NCI equity relative to proportionate measurement, with no change in consideration or group cash. The outside stake's full value is evidence supplied separately, not a number established by mechanically scaling a controlling purchase price.

    5-point rubric: identifiable net assets: 1 point; full NCI and goodwill: 1 point; proportionate pair: 1 point; asset/NCI difference and unchanged cash: 1 point; valuation-evidence distinction: 1 point.

  3. The batch margin is 30 − 18 =12; one-third unsold gives unrealized profit 4. Because Subsidiary sold upstream, its adjusted contribution is 50 − 4 =46. Group profit is 25 + 46 = 71; NCI profit is 40%×46 = 18.4; parent profit is 25 + 60%×46 = 52.6. NCI receives dividends 40%×10 =4, so closing NCI is 60 + 18.4 − 4 = 74.4. Subsidiary's unadjusted closing book equity is 100 + 50 − 10 =140; 40% gives 56, which misses both the upstream adjustment and the full-value opening NCI premium. Even 40% of adjusted 136 gives 54.4, not 74.4, because the independently measured opening NCI exceeded proportionate net assets by 20.

    5-point rubric: unrealized margin: 1 point; group profit: 1 point; NCI and parent attribution: 1 point; closing NCI: 1 point; shortcut explanation: 1 point.

  4. Taxable profit is 80 + 5 + 12 − (35 − 20) = 82. Current tax 82×25% = 20.5; current payable 20.5 − 18 = 2.5. Asset book amount 80 and tax base 65 give DTL 15×25% = 3.75. The later-deductible warranty gives DTA 12×25% = 3, fully recognized under the supplied capacity assumption. Deferred expense is 3.75 − 3 = 0.75; total tax expense 21.25 and net income 80 − 21.25 = 58.75. Expense minus cash is 21.25 − 18 = 3.25, comprising current payable increase 2.5 plus deferred expense 0.75. The fine produces no deferred tax because the deduction never arrives.

    5-point rubric: taxable profit, current tax and payable: 1 point; DTL: 1 point; DTA and permanent-fine distinction: 1 point; deferred tax, total tax and net income: 1 point; expense-to-cash reconciliation: 1 point.

Question 4 solutions · D4

  1. At four hours, feasible plans are (0,0),(1,0),(0,1),(0,2), with contributions 0,11,7,14. Thus two B jobs uniquely maximize contribution at 14. A earns 11/3 per hour, above B's 7/2, but one A leaves an unusable hour. Divisible-resource ranking cannot resolve this integer packing problem. At six hours the additional feasible competitors include (1,1) giving 18 and (2,0) giving 22; B demand remains capped at two. The unique expanded optimum is two A, contribution 22. Gross improvement 8 less fee 7 gives net 1, so the expansion is beneficial under the stated assumptions.

    5-point rubric: original optimum: 1 point; exhaustive/global justification: 1 point; ranking failure: 1 point; expanded optimum: 1 point; net benefit and decision: 1 point.

  2. Without the order, regular contribution is 14. Reserving two hours leaves room for one B, contribution 7; one A will not fit. Opportunity cost is 14 − 7 = 7. The special job contributes 16 − 4 =12 before preparation, or 9 after preparation. Incremental profit is 9 − 7 = 2, so accept on these facts. The indifferent quote p solves p − 4 − 3 − 7=0, hence p=14. Equivalently the complete accepted-plan contribution is regular 7 + special 12 − preparation 3 =16, compared with 14. Do not subtract lost regular contribution again after that complete comparison. Own contribution must cover the avoidable preparation and best alternative capacity use.

    5-point rubric: reoptimized regular plan: 1 point; opportunity cost: 1 point; incremental benefit and decision: 1 point; break-even quote: 1 point; complete-scenario/no-double-counting explanation: 1 point.

  3. Initially current assets 90 and current liabilities 45 give current ratio 2; quick ratio (12 + 18)/45 = 2/3. After payment, cash 2, current assets 80 and current liabilities 35 give current ratio 16/7, about 2.286, and quick ratio 20/35 = 4/7, about 0.571. Debt falls 55 to 45, while net debt stays 55 − 12 =45 − 2 = 43. Assets fall 150 to 140 and liabilities 70 to 60, with equity 80 unchanged. The current ratio improves mechanically because both numerator and denominator fall when initial current assets exceed current liabilities. Cash available for near-term bills falls 10 and the quick ratio worsens; payment dates and collectibility still matter.

    5-point rubric: original ratios: 1 point; new ratios: 1 point; cash and net debt: 1 point; coherent balance effects: 1 point; liquidity interpretation: 1 point.

  4. Net margin 24/240 = 10%; total-asset turnover 240/160 = 1.5; equity multiplier 160/80 = 2. Their product is 30%, equal to 24/80. Average invested capital is 140 − 20 = 120 from operations and 60 + 80 − 20 =120 from financing. NOPAT is 40×0.8 = 32; ROIC 32/120 = 4/15, about 26.667%. Net income 24 is after interest and belongs to common owners; NOPAT removes financing allocation for the stated operating-return comparison. Supplied averages and consistent scope make the identities valid.

    5-point rubric: three DuPont factors: 2 points; ROE identity: 1 point; both invested-capital routes: 1 point; ROIC and numerator explanation: 1 point.

Question 5 solutions · D5

  1. Cash before the new line and its interest is 10 + 24 − 40 − 10 − 2 = −18. The draw adds B but its full-year cash interest removes 0.1 B, so C=−18 + 0.9 B. Target C=10 requires B=28/0.9 = 280/9, about 31.111, exceeding the 30 limit. At the cap, interest is 3, cash −18 + 27 = 9, and the cash requirement fails by 1. Ignoring interest suggests B 28, but actual cash would be −18 + 25.2 =7.2. The negative pre-funding number is a requirement calculation, not permission to insert a negative cash asset in the forecast.

    5-point rubric: cash equation: 1 point; required draw: 1 point; line-cap assessment: 1 point; capped interest, cash and shortfall: 1 point; own-interest explanation: 1 point.

  2. Without the dividend, pre-line cash is −16. The draw needed is 26/0.9 = 260/9, about 28.889, within the 30 cap. Interest is 26/9; profit 18 − 26/9 = 136/9, about 15.111. CFO 24 − 26/9 = 190/9; investing cash −40; financing cash 260/9 − 10 = 170/9. Net change is 190/9 − 40 + 170/9 =0, leaving cash 10. PPE 90 + 40 − 6 = 124; assets 134. Old debt closes 30 and new debt 260/9, so total debt 530/9. Equity 60 + 136/9 = 676/9; claims (530 + 676)/9 =134. A proposed, undeclared distribution can be removed under the supplied authorization; deleting an established payable would require an actual settlement, cancellation or other valid event.

    5-point rubric: draw and interest: 1 point; profit and equity: 1 point; CFO/CFI/CFF reconciliation: 1 point; all closing balance totals: 1 point; proposed-distribution distinction: 1 point.

  3. External revenue is 180 + 120 = 300, not 330. Segment U profit is 180 + 30 − 160 =50 and V profit 120 − 100 =20, summing 70. Eliminating internal revenue 30 and the matched expense 30 leaves this profit unchanged because no unrealized margin remains. Group EBIT is 70 − 12 = 58, pretax profit 58 − 6 = 52, and net income 52 − 10 = 42. Segment measures exclude corporate costs, interest and tax; a segment margin using internal revenue also has a different revenue base. Reconcile boundaries and definitions before comparing it with group net margin 42/300 =14%.

    5-point rubric: external revenue: 1 point; segment sum and elimination: 1 point; EBIT: 1 point; pretax and net income: 1 point; comparability explanation: 1 point.

  4. Management reports 58 + 5 + 4 = 67. Retain recurring marketing 5, add back nonrecurring reorganization 4 and remove favorable nonrecurring recovery 3: recurring-scenario EBIT is 58 + 4 − 3 = 59. The difference 8 is the unsupported 5 marketing exclusion plus retained 3 gain. Neither analytical adjustment reverses historical payments/receipts. Genuine late-period service growth or agreed longer terms could raise receivables; deteriorating collection, an unsupported charge or premature recognition could also do so. A useful request matches dated contracts/service acceptance and the receivable cohort to later bank receipts, credits and aging. Accept other concrete evidence tied to those alternatives. An increase alone identifies neither intent nor the appropriate accounting correction.

    5-point rubric: management measure: 1 point; symmetric scenario and reconciliation: 1 point; no historical cash change: 1 point; two competing mechanisms: 1 point; assertion-specific evidence and inference limit: 1 point.
