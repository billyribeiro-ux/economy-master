Investment-committee project — Price, reinvestment and a maturity wall

Task. Advise the committee considering the purchase of Nacre Process Controls. Build an operating forecast, a continuing-business valuation, a matched peer comparison and a dated financing plan. Decide what price and conditions you can defend. This original open-resource project is designed for 12–16 hours, an author planning estimate. It combines cash-flow and claim consistency, terminal economics, required returns, liquidity and payout, comparables, acquisition value allocation and contractual refinancing. Freeze your independent model and initial recommendation before opening the instructor guide or reference output.

Nacre's recent backlog supports strong operating margins, but collections weaken in the downside forecast and a large term payment falls due in year 1. Management proposes an immediate payout while emphasizing the eventual cash generation. The committee must evaluate both continuing value and the route by which the business reaches it. A feasible loan schedule does not establish a good purchase price; a positive DCF does not supply missing cash on a payment date.

Required return and a fully funded terminal model

Use illustrative nominal CU inputs: risk-free return 4%, asset beta 1.2 and market equity premium 5%. Their stipulated model implies an operating required return rU of 10%. This is the opportunity cost for the operating assets; it is not a leverage-weighted use of the loan's promised 6% coupon. With no interest deduction there is no financing tax-shield value to add. MIT's CAPM teaching material supports the method and estimation distinctions, not these invented parameter values.

Each path is a conditional expected operating forecast evaluated using the stipulated required return. The two paths are sensitivity cases, not a complete set of priced states. No physical or risk-neutral probabilities are supplied. The debt's market quote is an independent input. You cannot infer its expected return, price a unique downside default claim or report a uniquely determined probability-weighted acquisition NPV from these data.

At the start of year 4, replace the explicit-period backlog/cost mix with a normalized operating regime. Revenue divided by opening operating capital is 16/13; EBITDA margin is 18%; depreciation is 5% of revenue; tax remains 25%. All capital in place at date 3 earns the resulting return. Closing NWC is 15% of revenue. Thereafter operating capital, revenue and NOPAT grow at a constant nominal g = 2%. End-year net reinvestment equals g times opening capital. Neither PPE nor all NWC is liquidated. Derive year-4 capex from these conditions; do not copy the final explicit-year capex.

For the sensitivity grid use rU = 9%, 10%, 11% crossed with g = 1%, 2%, 3%, holding terminal ROIC and date-3 capital fixed. Changing growth changes required reinvestment as well as the valuation denominator. For reverse valuation, vary terminal ROIC, holding turnover, depreciation ratio, tax, explicit cash, date-3 capital, rU = 10% and g = 2% fixed; derive the corresponding EBITDA margin. These are conditional comparative exercises, not evidence that any rate is empirically correct. Growth and reinvestment and terminal closure provide the scoped methodological background.

Committed financing and payout tests

All terms below are explicit fictional contracts, with no financing fees, unlisted waivers or legal assertions. The term loan pays 6% cash interest on opening principal and repays 150, 50 and 50 at ends 1, 2 and 3. The revolver pays 6% on its opening balance. Its commitment is 120, but at ends 1 and 2, after scheduled term repayment, total remaining term plus revolver debt cannot exceed 1.75 times that year's EBITDA. Closing cash must be at least 30 at every modeled date. Borrow only as needed; excess cash repays the revolver down to zero, after which cash remains an asset.

At end 3, pay all remaining term and revolving principal. An already-signed replacement commitment may then issue an undated fixed-coupon note at par, only for the amount needed to restore cash to 30. Its face cannot exceed 80 or 1.75 times normalized year-4 EBITDA, whichever is smaller; all previous covenant tests must have passed. It has no scheduled principal repayment and pays a fixed 6% cash coupon annually beginning at end 4. This continuing debt must remain visible, including its coupon. No future renewal or further equity contribution is authorized. Test the first continuing year's cash coverage and explain why, under these bounded growth/coupon premises, it suffices for the subsequent modeled tail.

Start by testing sale of the excluded asset, subscription of 70 and zero initial payout. Then test no sale/no subscription, sale only, subscription 70 without sale, and management's initial payout 20 with sale and subscription. Find the minimum date-0 equity with the asset sale and no payout. With subscription 70 and the sale, find the largest initial payout permitted in both forecasts. Finally, hold all downside inputs fixed except an additional year-1 closing NWC balance that is fully released at end 2; find the largest such delay the benchmark plan can fund. A boundary is not a prudent operating buffer. Do not fund an endpoint by assuming an unnamed waiver or by leaving matured revolving debt outstanding.

Exact funding boundaries and their limits

With sale 30, subscription J and initial payout Q, initial cash is 90+J−Q. Downside year 1, before the line, is 1.04(90+J−Q)−11.6−15−150. The effective line ceiling is 49.1. Requiring cash at least 30 therefore gives:

1.04(90+J−Q) − 11.6 − 15 − 150 + 49.1 ≥ 30.
J − Q ≥ 3195/52 ≈ 61.442308.

With Q = 0, minimum equity is 3195/52. Without the asset sale, required equity rises by 30 to 4755/52 ≈ 91.442308, which exceeds the authorized 70. With J = 70, the largest payout is 445/52 ≈ 8.557692. Both boundary policies leave the same initial company cash, so they have identical treasury paths. Neither their different owner outlays nor payouts create value. On the downside boundary the line is exactly 49.1 at date 1; at date 2 it becomes 77.596, and the date-3 replacement note is 42.70176, within its 80 cap. Base also passes later dates. The reference output retains both full boundary paths.

Under the full subscription/no-payout benchmark, unused downside date-1 line room is 49.1−40.2 = 8.9. Hence extra NWC can reach 8.9, total closing NWC 116.9, or 1169/5400 ≈ 21.648148% of sales. The additional balance releases at end 2, so its later cash effect differs from a permanently larger cost. At this boundary, revolver is 49.1 at date 1 and 68.696 at date 2; takeout is 33.26776. All tests still pass. Another 0.01 of year-1 NWC fails by 0.01. Equity 0.01 below its minimum, or payout 0.01 above its maximum, fails by 0.0104 because the lost date-0 cash also loses 4% interest.

These calculations locate contractual edges. They are not sufficient evidence for choosing an edge as policy. Maintaining no initial payout preserves the benchmark's 8.9 of collection room; retaining a contingency reserve or signing additional capacity would require explicit cost and authorization. A seller-price reduction changes buyer cash, not company cash, until a separately authorized subscription transfers it.

Illustrative committee recommendation and evaluation

Decision. I would defer the offered 500 purchase and negotiate a lower price, subject to the specified asset sale, 70 subscription, zero initial payout and signed refinancing commitment. My decision rule requires nonnegative transaction NPV in each supplied forecast and compliance at every modeled payment date. That rule sets a seller-price ceiling of approximately 439.946, after the separate fee of 8. It is a conditional screening rule, not a claim that these two forecasts capture every possible outcome or establish an expected-value optimum. A committee willing to accept a downside loss could choose another rule, but should approve that exposure explicitly.

Control and next action. The committee should approve a price ceiling, contribution cap and payout policy separately. Management compensation tied only to EBITDA or deal completion could favor optimistic margins or an unaffordable payout, so an independent reviewer should sign the capital and covenant reconciliations. Treasury should maintain a dated collection/payment forecast and report changes to effective capacity, not only nominal facility size. A discount from the seller can reduce the buyer's exposure, but it does not automatically authorize extra company equity. Approval should remain conditional on both a defensible price and identified, executable funding.

Grade the memo by the evidence, criterion and mechanics, not agreement with this recommendation. A stronger or weaker risk appetite can be defended.
