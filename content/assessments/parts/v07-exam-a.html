Form A — From transactions to an evidence-based decision

100 points; 180 minutes; closed notes and closed solutions; basic calculator permitted. There are five questions of 20 points, each containing four parts of 5 points. Budget about 36 minutes per question. Show entries, equations, signs and assumptions. Exact fractions or sensibly rounded values with unrounded working earn equal credit. No code, browsing or recall of paragraph numbers is required.

Every entity and transaction below is fictional and original. Monetary amounts are kCU, thousands of currency units, except Question 4(a–c), which uses CU. X1 and X2 are illustrative annual periods. Each named company or explicitly separate case has its own accounts. Do not combine them. Unless a question says otherwise, there is no tax, foreign exchange, impairment, unlisted transaction or owner flow. The supplied recognition and cash-flow conventions govern the calculation; these simplified cases do not establish compliance with any real reporting framework. An annual closing model does not by itself establish cash availability on every intervening date.

Question 1 — A merchant's cutoff, closure and cycle (20 points; D1)

At the opening of its 360-day X1, Cedar Supply has cash 40, gross receivables 60, inventory 90 and net equipment 120. It owes trade payables 50 and a long-term, interest-free loan 100. Ordinary contributed capital is 100 and retained earnings 60. There is no receivable allowance, and all receivables are collectible. During X1, Cedar earns cash sales of 60 and delivered credit sales of 300; it collects 280 against receivables. Credit inventory purchases are 260, supplier payments 240, cash-paid wages 50 and equipment depreciation 12. The cost of the goods actually delivered is 240. There is no capex, loan repayment, dividend or other transaction.

In addition to those facts, the unissued draft erroneously records an invoice of 50 as revenue and a receivable, and transfers its goods' cost of 30 from inventory to cost of sales. Those goods are still Cedar's inventory at year-end. Their contract transfers control only on delivery in X2; there is no performance, unconditional claim or customer payment in X1. The erroneous invoice and cost release are not included in the correct delivered sales and cost figures above.

  1. Give the two correcting journal entries. State their combined effects on draft profit, total assets, total liabilities, equity and cash. Explain why sending the invoice does not supply the missing recognition fact.
  2. Using corrected transactions, produce the income statement and all closing balance-sheet balances. Close retained earnings and verify assets equal liabilities plus equity.
  3. Reconstruct operating cash flow both from receipts/payments and from net income. State investing and financing cash flows and reconcile closing cash. Explain how the cash-flow result can survive correction of the erroneous profit.
  4. Use arithmetic means of opening and corrected closing balances. Calculate DSO with credit sales, DIO with cost of sales, DPO with credit inventory purchases, and the cash-conversion-cycle proxy DSO + DIO − DPO. Give one reason that this proxy does not identify the peak funding need or individual payment dates.

Question 2 — Asset consumption and dated obligations (20 points; D2)

These four situations are independent. All stated payments occur when specified. Use straight-line depreciation with fractional years proportional to months. Cash purchases/disposals of owned equipment are investing; consumed training and service payments are operating. In part (d), cash lease interest is operating and the payment's liability-principal portion is financing under the stipulated presentation.

  1. A new machine becomes ready for use on 1 July X1. Its invoice is 96, transport 4 and installation 8, all necessary to place it in its usable condition and all paid in X1. Staff training consumed in X1 costs 6; routine service consumed in X1 costs 3. The case requires those latter two costs to be expensed. Residual value is 8 and useful life five years from readiness. Calculate initial asset cost, X1 depreciation, closing net asset, total X1 expense from these facts and operating/investing cash outflows.
  2. A separate tool opens X1 at cost 90 and accumulated depreciation 60. Its unchanged annual depreciation is 12. It is sold on 30 June, after six months' depreciation, for cash 22 with no selling fee. Give current depreciation, pre-sale carrying amount, disposal gain/loss and the disposal entry. Explain the tool's total X1 profit effect, indirect operating-cash adjustments and investing cash.
  3. On 1 January X1, a borrower receives 100 for an eligible amortized-cost zero-coupon note. Its only payment is 121 on 31 December X2; the effective annual rate is 10%. There is no fee or modification. Calculate interest and carrying amount for both years, including the final settlement. Give the X1 interest entry and the maturity entry. Explain why absence of a cash coupon does not imply absence of interest expense.
  4. An equipment-use right begins on 1 January X1 and expires after two years, with no ownership transfer, purchase option or residual value. Payments of 60.5 are due at the end of each year; the appropriate annual discount rate is 10%. There is no prepayment, incentive, initial cost, restoration obligation or impairment. Apply the supplied separate interest and straight-line ROU-depreciation model. Find initial liability/ROU asset, first-year interest, closing liability/ROU asset, total first-year expense and the operating/financing cash split. Identify the noncash initial recognition.

Question 3 — Owners, a controlled group and a future deduction (20 points; D3)

Part (a) is an ordinary-equity issuer; parts (b–c) describe the same controlled group; part (d) is a separate taxpayer. There are no preference shares, transaction costs or complex instruments.

  1. Vale has 100 thousand ordinary shares with CU 1 par value, share capital 100, share premium 200 and retained earnings 150. It issues 20 thousand new shares at CU 8 each for cash; an existing holder keeps all of their 25 thousand shares and buys none. Subsequent X1 net income is 30 and paid dividends are 12. Give the issue entry, closing retained earnings and total equity, and the holder's ownership percentage before/after. Explain whether the issuance itself creates revenue or profit.
  2. At the opening of X1, P pays 225 cash for 75% of S and obtains control. S's identifiable fair-value assets are 400 and liabilities 160; book values equal those amounts. The acquisition uses proportionate-net-asset measurement of noncontrolling interests (NCI), with no deferred tax or other acquisition adjustment. Immediately after payment, P's separate assets are cash 80, equipment 260 and its investment in S 225; its liabilities are 165 and equity 400. S's assets consist of cash 40, inventory 160 and equipment 200. Calculate acquisition-date NCI, goodwill and the consolidated asset balances, liabilities and equity. Explain the investment elimination.
  3. During X1, P sells inventory costing 48 to S for 60, and 40% of those particular goods remain unsold outside the group at year-end. The invoice is fully settled. P's separate X1 income is 80, including its full transfer profit; S's is 40. There are no other intragroup trades, distributions, acquisition-value adjustments, tax effects or impairment. Calculate unrealized profit in closing inventory, consolidated income and its parent/NCI attribution. Give the two consolidation entries for the sale and remaining profit. Explain whose income bears the downstream adjustment.
  4. A separate service firm earns and collects 170 and incurs/pays ordinary expense 70 in X1. Completed work also creates a supported warranty provision and expense of 20, with no warranty payment yet. Fictional enacted tax law allows that 20 deduction only on future payment; all other book/tax timing agrees. Both current and applicable future tax rates are 25%, and sufficient usable future taxable profit supports full DTA recognition. Opening tax balances are zero; all current tax is paid in X1. Compute book/taxable profit, current tax, the warranty liability's tax base and DTA, total tax expense/net income, and direct/indirect CFO. Does recognizing the DTA itself provide cash to settle claims?

Question 5 — Fund a forecast, align measurements, investigate claims (20 points; D5)

Parts (a–b) concern Maple Works’ next 360-day year; (c) is a separate inventory comparison; (d) is a separate evidence case. Maple Works opens with cash 20, receivables 30, inventory 40 and net PPE 100; payables are 25, interest-bearing debt 75 and common equity 90. Forecast credit sales are 240, cost of sales 144, cash operating expense 36, depreciation 10 and interest 6. Closing receivables/inventory/payables are planned at 48/50/30. Cash capex is 30, year-end principal repayment 15 and planned year-end dividends 12. No impairment or other movement occurs.

For Maple Works, book and taxable profit agree and current tax at 25% is fully paid within the year; no deferred tax arises. Interest and tax are operating cash, capex investing, and debt/ordinary equity/dividends financing. The 6 interest is fixed by the opening debt contract. New borrowing is funded only at the closing date and first incurs interest after that date, with no fee. Required closing unrestricted cash is 10. All capex enters PPE; the supplied 10 is total annual depreciation, including any depreciation on those additions. A new draw is an actual funding transaction, never revenue.

  1. Initially assume the required closing-date draw is available. Compute forecast net income and CFO, the minimum new draw that reaches cash 10, closing debt/equity and the full closing accounting-equation total. Show a direct or indirect cash bridge and the cash financing equation.
  2. Only collection timing deteriorates: closing receivables become 70, with no loss or revenue change. The binding facility permits at most 35 of new closing-date borrowing. Management can cancel any of the planned 12 dividend before declaration/payment, but cannot defer capex, principal or other payments. Find required borrowing if dividends stay 12, and the cash-buffer shortfall after drawing 35 and cancelling all dividends. Find the minimum new common equity received at the close that cures the remaining shortfall, and reconcile the repaired closing balance sheet. State the limitation of this annual feasibility conclusion.
  3. An analyst has a verified same-entity, same-period FIFO-versus-LIFO inventory bridge. LIFO inventory is 45 at opening and 65 at closing; FIFO inventory minus LIFO inventory is 12 at opening and 8 at closing. LIFO profit is 40 and CFO is 30. There is no tax, write-down, currency effect, acquisition or other measurement difference; cash transactions are identical. Find comparable FIFO closing inventory, profit and CFO, explaining the reserve's stock/flow distinction and the indirect-cash cancellation. Before asserting that either published treatment complies with the issuer's actual rules, name one precise framework/version/adoption fact and document you would verify.
  4. A separate firm's reconciled cash bridge is NI 40 + depreciation 10 − receivable increase 35 − inventory increase 8 + payable increase 5 = CFO 12. Its defined accounting EBITDA is 70 and includes a shutdown expense of 12, a favorable settlement recovery of 10 and recurring warranty expense of 5. Evidence supports treating only the shutdown and recovery as nonrecurring for the scenario. Management adds back 12 and 5, retaining the recovery. Calculate management's measure and a symmetric recurring EBITDA scenario. Give one benign and one adverse explanation for the receivable/cash gap, an evidence request capable of distinguishing them, and a defensible statement about cash history and intent.

Form A — Full solutions and point rubrics

Every part is worth 5 points; the twenty rubrics total 100. Award a stated point for the specified method or interpretation even when a prior arithmetic slip propagates consistently. Do not award a point for a conclusion that contradicts its own entries or assumptions. Accept equivalent balanced journal presentations, correctly labeled cash conventions where the question allows them, and other specific evidence requests that test the same assertion. No examiner may infer proof of fraud or real-world compliance from these fictional totals.

Question 1 solutions — Cutoff, statements and operating cycle (20 points)

  1. Reverse the unsupported sale: Dr Revenue 50; Cr Receivables 50. Restore the owned goods: Dr Inventory 30; Cr Cost of sales 30. Profit decreases by 50 − 30 = 20; assets decrease by 50 − 30 = 20; closing equity decreases by 20. Liabilities and cash are unchanged. Under the supplied delivery condition, invoicing has neither transferred control nor created the stipulated unconditional claim. The documents' billing date alone cannot replace those facts.

    Scoring: revenue entry 1; inventory/cost entry 1; profit/assets/equity effects 1; unchanged liabilities/cash 1; recognition explanation 1.

  2. Revenue is 60 + 300 = 360. Subtract cost of sales 240, wages 50 and depreciation 12 to obtain NI 58. Gross profit is 120 and profit before depreciation 70. Receivables close at 60 + 300 − 280 = 80; inventory at 90 + 260 − 240 = 110; payables at 50 + 260 − 240 = 70; net equipment at 120 − 12 = 108. Cash is 40 + 60 + 280 − 240 − 50 = 90. Retained earnings close at 60 + 58 = 118; contributed capital stays 100. Thus assets are 90 + 80 + 110 + 108 = 388, liabilities are 70 + 100 = 170 and equity is 218: 388 = 170 + 218. The loan receipt was prior-period funding, not X1 revenue.

    Scoring: income statement/NI 1; receivables/inventory/payables rollforwards 1; cash/equipment 1; retained earnings/equity 1; complete closed totals 1.

  3. Direct CFO is 60 cash sales + 280 collections − 240 suppliers − 50 wages = 50. The corrected indirect bridge is 58 + 12 depreciation − (80 − 60) receivables − (110 − 90) inventory + (70 − 50) payables = 50. CFI = CFF = 0; cash closes at 40 + 50 = 90. The erroneous draft would have NI 78, receivables 130 and inventory 80; its mechanically reconciled bridge is 78 + 12 − 70 − (−10) + 20 = 50. Correcting profit by −20, receivables by −50 and inventory by +30 changes this bridge by −20 + 50 − 30 = 0. Closure alone would therefore not have authenticated the sale.

    Scoring: direct CFO 1; indirect signs/amounts 1; zero CFI/CFF 1; cash reconciliation 1; correction cancellation/recognition limit 1.

  4. Average receivables are 70, inventory 100 and payables 60. DSO = 360×70/300 = 84 days; DIO = 360×100/240 = 150 days; DPO = 360×60/260 = 1080/13 ≈ 83.077 days. CCC is 84 + 150 − 1080/13 = 1962/13 ≈ 150.923 days. Using cash collections, total sales or supplier payments as silent substitutes changes the matching convention. The endpoint means conceal the within-year path and may mix changing sales cohorts, inventory ages and settlement dates. A dated receipts/payments calendar is needed to find a peak cash deficit; this proxy is not that calendar.

    Scoring: DSO 1; DIO 1; matched DPO 1; CCC 1; specific limitation 1.

The disposal entry is Dr Cash 22, Dr Accumulated depreciation 66, Dr Disposal loss 2; Cr Equipment cost 90. The tool reduces X1 profit by 6 + 2 = 8. Indirect CFO adds back the noncash depreciation 6 and disposal loss 2, leaving zero operating cash from these asset expense entries. Investing cash is +22. The entire sale receipt belongs to investing here, not only the loss or a gain component.

    Scoring: depreciation/carrying amount 1; loss 1; balanced derecognition entry 1; profit/indirect adjustments 1; investing receipt 1.

  3. Initial PV is 121/1.1² = 100. X1 interest is 100×10% = 10 and closing liability 110; record Dr Interest expense 10; Cr Note liability 10. X2 interest is 110×10% = 11; liability immediately before settlement is 121. After accruing that 11, record Dr Note liability 121; Cr Cash 121, leaving zero.     Scoring: X1 interest/carrying amount 1; X2 interest/carrying amount 1; X1 entry 1; maturity entry/zero close 1; coupon/expense and lifetime explanation 1.

  4. Initial liability is 60.5/1.1 + 60.5/1.1² = 55 + 50 = 105. The initial entry is Dr ROU asset 105 / Cr Lease liability 105, with no commencement cash flow. First-year interest is 10.5; payment principal is 60.5 − 10.5 = 50; closing liability is 105 + 10.5 − 60.5 = 55. ROU depreciation is 105/2 = 52.5, leaving net ROU asset 52.5. Total expense is 10.5 + 52.5 = 63, while cash paid is 60.5. The cash split is CFO −10.5 and CFF −50. Question 3 solutions — Equity, group and tax (20 points)

  1. The issue receives 20 thousand×CU 8 = 160 kCU: Dr Cash 160; Cr Share capital 20; Cr Share premium 140. Capital becomes 120 and premium 340. Retained earnings are 150 + 30 − 12 = 168; total equity is 120 + 340 + 168 = 628, also 450 + 160 + 30 − 12. Shares outstanding rise from 100 to 120 thousand. The holder moves from 25/100 = 25% to 25/120 = 5/24 ≈ 20.833%, without selling a share. The primary issue is an owner contribution and creates no revenue or profit; a lower voting fraction alone does not quantify a wealth loss.

    Scoring: cash/par/premium entry 1; retained earnings 1; total-equity bridge 1; before/after ownership 1; funding versus profit distinction 1.

  2. Identifiable net assets are 400 − 160 = 240. NCI is 25%×240 = 60; goodwill is 225 − 75%×240 = 45. Consolidated cash is 80 + 40 = 120; inventory 160; equipment 260 + 200 = 460; goodwill 45. Total assets are 785, equivalently 565 P assets + 400 S assets − 225 investment + 45 goodwill. Liabilities are 165 + 160 = 325; parent equity is 400 and NCI 60, so 785 = 325 + 400 + 60. Eliminate P's investment and S's acquisition-date equity to avoid counting the same controlled net resources twice; retain 100% of S's underlying assets/liabilities, with the outside claim separately shown.

    Scoring: net assets/NCI 1; goodwill 1; consolidated asset components 1; liabilities/equity closure 1; control and investment-elimination explanation 1.

  3. Total transfer profit is 60 − 48 = 12; 40% remains unrealized outside the group: 4.8. S carries the unsold portion at 24, but the group cost is 19.2. Eliminate the full internal sale with Dr Revenue 60; Cr Cost of sales 60, then eliminate the unsold profit with Dr Cost of sales 4.8; Cr Inventory 4.8. Group income is 80 + 40 − 4.8 = 115.2.     Scoring: unrealized profit/inventory basis 1; consolidated income 1; NCI/parent attribution 1; both consolidation entries 1; downstream explanation 1.

  4. Book PBT is 170 − 70 − 20 = 80; taxable profit is 100, because no warranty deduction is yet permitted. Current tax is 25. Liability tax base is 20 carrying amount − 20 future deduction = 0; the deductible temporary difference 20 supports a DTA of 5. Deferred tax expense is −5, so total tax expense is 25 − 5 = 20 and NI is 60. Direct CFO is 170 − 70 − 25 = 75. Indirectly, 60 + 20 unpaid provision − 5 noncash deferred-tax benefit = 75; tax payable remains zero. Recognizing the DTA produces no cash receipt. Relative to opening balances, the added cash 75 and DTA 5 equal the provision 20 plus retained profit 60, another check on the signs.

    Scoring: book/taxable profit and current tax 1; tax base/DTA 1; total tax/NI 1; both CFO paths 1; no cash receipt and recognition-condition interpretation 1.

Question 4 solutions — Relevant cost and ratio diagnosis (20 points)

  1. The fitted variable rate is (3500 − 2600)/(700 − 400) = 3 CU per hour. Fixed support is 2600 − 3×400 = 1400 CU, also 3500 − 3×700. Thus C(h) = 1400 + 3h. Two observations fit a line but do not establish causal cost behavior: changing prices, product mix, maintenance, quality or a capacity step could change the relationship. Parts (b–c) rely on the additional stipulated engineering/contract evidence and actual capacity, not on extrapolating two observations without justification.

    Scoring: difference calculation/rate 2; intercept and check 2; concrete identification/range limitation 1.

  2. A variable cost is 13 + 3×1 = 16 and contribution 30 − 16 = 14. B variable cost is 22 + 3×2 = 28 and contribution 22. Total fixed cost is 1400 + 600 = 2000. A (2,1) bundle contributes 2×14 + 22 = 50 and uses 4 hours. The first nonloss plan is 2000/50 = 40 whole bundles: 80 A, 40 B, 160 hours and zero profit. At 39 bundles profit is −50. Target profit 600 requires (2000 + 600)/50 = 52 bundles, using 208 hours, so it is infeasible. Capacity permits at most 50 bundles, yielding maximum fixed-mix profit 500; favorable demand does not create another eight hours.

    Scoring: unit costs/contributions 1; total fixed and bundle contribution 1; first whole nonloss plan 1; hours/capacity check 1; target infeasibility with numerical bound 1.

  3. Regular sales use 80 + 2×40 = 160 hours, leaving 40 idle. The order needs 60, so it must displace 20 regular hours. A earns 14/hour and B earns 22/2 = 11/hour. Forgo 10 regular B at opportunity cost 220. The order adds 30(40 − 28) − 30 = 330 before displacement, and 110 after it. Accept under the stated facts. The resulting plan is 80 regular A, 30 regular B and 30 special B, exactly 200 hours. Its revenue 5100 less variable cost 2960, fixed cost 2000 and delivery 30 gives profit 110, versus baseline zero. Break-even quote is 28 + (30 + 220)/30 = 109/3 ≈ CU 36.333 per special unit. Removing any A instead would sacrifice more contribution per hour; all ten forgone B are available, so the bound is feasible. Extra future customer harm or payment risk would require revising the decision inputs.

    Scoring: idle/required/displaced capacity 1; optimal lost contribution 1; incremental profit 1; feasible acceptance with conditions 1; quote threshold 1.

  4. Opening current assets are 100 and current liabilities 50, so the current ratio is 2; closing current assets are 160 and current liabilities 80, also 2. Quick ratios are (10 + 40)/50 = 1 and (5 + 65)/80 = 7/8 = 0.875. Interest coverage is 30/5 = 6. The unchanged total ratio conceals less cash and greater dependence on selling inventory and collecting receivables. Neither that ratio nor accrual EBIT/interest schedules the cash needed for bills. Obtain, for example, a dated receivable aging/subsequent-receipts file matched to the current liability maturity calendar, or inventory aging and realizable-sale evidence. A credible answer distinguishes a warning from proof of inability to pay.

    Scoring: both current ratios 1; both quick ratios 1; interest coverage 1; composition/timing diagnosis 1; specific useful record and bounded conclusion 1.

Question 5 solutions — Forecast, comparability and evidence (20 points)

  1. EBIT = 240 − 144 − 36 − 10 = 50; PBT = 50 − 6 = 44; tax = 11; NI = 33. Indirect CFO is 33 + 10 − (48 − 30) − (50 − 40) + (30 − 25) = 20. Directly, collections are 240 − 18 = 222; purchases 144 + 10 = 154; supplier cash 154 − 5 = 149; CFO = 222 − 149 − 36 − 6 − 11 = 20. The closing cash equation is 20 opening + 20 CFO − 30 capex − 15 principal − 12 dividends + B = −17 + B. Thus B = 27 reaches cash 10. Closing debt is 75 − 15 + 27 = 87, equity 90 + 33 − 12 = 111, and PPE 100 + 30 − 10 = 120. Assets are 10 + 48 + 50 + 120 = 228; liabilities plus equity are 30 payables + 87 debt + 111 equity = 228. Investing cash is −30 and financing cash 27 − 15 − 12 = 0. The new draw does not change this year's stated interest or profit.

    Scoring: NI with interest/tax 1; CFO bridge 1; cash equation/minimum draw 1; closing debt/equity 1; complete asset/PPE and equation closure 1.

  2. The extra 22 receivables reduces CFO from 20 to −2 without changing NI 33. With the dividend, closing cash is 20 − 2 − 30 − 15 − 12 + B = −39 + B, requiring B = 49. Capping the draw at 35 would leave cash −4; cancelling all 12 dividends raises cash to 8, still 2 below the required buffer. With all other allowed choices exhausted, the minimum new equity is 2. Repaired debt is 75 − 15 + 35 = 95; equity is 90 + 33 + 2 = 125. Closing assets are 10 cash + 70 receivables + 50 inventory + 120 PPE = 250, equal to 30 payables + 95 debt + 125 equity. CFO −2 + CFI −30 + CFF (35 − 15 + 2) = −10 reconciles cash 20→10. These are conditional closing-date transactions; commitments, their actual receipt and every earlier due date require a dated funding calendar. A year-end balance does not establish uninterrupted solvency.

    Scoring: revised CFO/uncapped draw 1; capped-draw/dividend repair and remaining gap 1; minimum equity 1; repaired debt/equity/assets closure 1; annual-versus-dated funding limitation 1.

  3. The closing FIFO inventory is 65 + 8 = 73 and opening FIFO inventory 45 + 12 = 57. The reserve change is 8 − 12 = −4. FIFO cost of sales therefore exceeds LIFO cost by 4, and comparable FIFO profit is 40 − 4 = 36. The ending reserve 8 adjusts a closing stock, not this year's income. FIFO inventory rises 73 − 57 = 16, versus the LIFO rise 20. The indirect CFO difference is −4 profit − (16 − 20) inventory adjustment = 0; CFO remains 30. A higher FIFO ending asset can coexist with lower FIFO current profit when the reserve shrinks. For a compliance claim, inspect the issuer's reporting-basis note and audited statement of compliance to establish the actual jurisdiction/framework and period; then match the relevant standard's effective version and any documented early adoption/local endorsement. Merely labeling a provider column “IFRS” or seeing a current web title does not supply that evidence. This calculation is an analytical bridge, not permission for either method under every framework.

    Scoring: FIFO stock 1; reserve change/profit 1; indirect cancellation/CFO 1; stock-versus-flow interpretation 1; precise dated framework fact and supporting document 1.

  4. Management's number is 70 + 12 + 5 = 87. The stipulated symmetric recurring scenario is 70 + 12 − 10 = 72, retaining recurring warranty expense. The difference 15 comprises the unsupported warranty exclusion 5 and retained one-off gain 10. Those analytical adjustments do not reverse any historical receipt or payment; actual CFO remains 12. A benign receivable explanation is a recent batch of valid credit sales whose agreed payment dates fall after year-end. An adverse alternative is unsupported premature sales, disputed claims or a deteriorating debtor. Request the population of closing invoices matched to contract delivery/acceptance terms, due dates, later bank receipts and credit notes; inspect specific unmatched items and subsequent credits rather than only management's aggregate explanation. Receipt evidence can support collection without alone proving original cutoff or intent. The cash gap and an aggressive alternative measure justify investigation, but neither proves fraud. Equivalent specific competing explanations and evidence plans receive credit.

    Scoring: management measure 1; symmetric scenario/difference 1; coherent benign and adverse alternatives 1; evidence that distinguishes the asserted explanations 1; unchanged cash and bounded intent conclusion 1.
