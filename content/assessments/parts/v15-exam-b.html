Form B — Diagnose, reconstruct and defend

This reassessment has five20-point domains, each with four5-point subparts:100points total. Suggested time is300minutes; this is an uncalibrated instructional design, not an empirically equated professional examination. Work closed-book with a basic calculator, then use the solutions for remediation. Show equations, feasibility and the counterparty behind each payment. All data and institutions are synthetic; no actual policy or population parameter is being estimated.

Advancement requires85overall and at least15in every domain, followed by a new transfer problem and teach-back. An unresolved error in identification, units, funding or integrity requires remediation regardless of the total. Each solution gives an additive rubric; award credit for a correct equivalent argument. The central task is to repair attractive but false claims without silently changing the stated model.

1. A tax-funded training proposal — public finance and labor

A competitive goods market has demand Q=10−p and supply Q=s−2, where p is the consumer price and s the producer price. Quantities are nonnegative; consider the positive-quantity branch. A per-unit tax creates p−s=t. Consumers have100initial numeraire units, producers own their firms, and real production cost is2Q+Q²/2. A proposal sets (demand intercept,supply intercept,t)=(10,2,2). Revenue is available before training. Training suppliers charge their actual resource cost, three per student, and earn no profit.

There are two potential students L,H, each with one initial liquid good and no borrowing. Their future output and competitive wage without training are4and6; training raises output by2and4respectively. One unit of training is indivisible, its private price and real cost are three, and it requires no additional labor time. All consumption values are quasilinear and dates are undiscounted. A free in-kind place is accepted whenever it raises the student's wage; it cannot be resold. The wage primitives are (baseline L,baseline H,increment L,increment H,cost,liquidity)=(4,6,2,4,3,1). The policy team proposes universal free training financed by the goods tax and calls all revenue an extra social benefit.

  1. Reconstruct the untaxed and taxed market, tax incidence, receipts and total goods-market surplus including government revenue. Diagnose the claim that revenue itself is a resource gain.
  2. For each student distinguish privately worthwhile self-financed training, financially feasible training, and acceptance of a free place. State what zero observed self-financed enrollment does and does not identify.
  3. Compare universal free training with a selective place for H and rebate of unused tax receipts to goods consumers, holding the goods tax fixed. Reconcile the public budget and each program's combined real surplus change relative to no tax/no training. Is the selective policy a strict aggregate improvement?
  4. Under selective training, the team treats the trained/untrained wage gap as the causal training return. Compute that gap, the actual treated-person effect and the two-person average effect. Construct the decomposition identifying the selection error and state the assumption needed for the trained person's before/after comparison.

2. Market power and cross-border accounting — markets and trade

Two firms simultaneously post prices in[0,12]for a homogeneous good. Market demand is12−p at the lowest price; the lower-priced firm supplies all demand and equal prices split demand equally. Both have constant marginal real cost three, unlimited capacity and no fixed cost. Firms maximize profit. A consultant calls equal prices seven an equilibrium because each firm earns positive profit. Use (demand intercept,cost,claimed price)=(12,3,7).

A separate contracted production network has domestic final good H and a foreign component F. One H requires one-half F plus domestic primary inputs costing two; one F requires one-quarter H plus foreign primary inputs costing one. Final orders are seven units of H. Gross quantities satisfy xH=7+xF/4 and xF=xH/2. Competitive unit costs determine prices, both countries own their primary factors, and all primary income buys final H. Imported H used to make F is not final consumption. There are no tariffs or transport losses. The primitives are (final H,H per F,F per H,domestic primary cost,foreign primary cost)=(7,1/4,1/2,2,1).

  1. Refute the claimed price-seven equilibrium with an explicit profitable deviation, including its exact demand and profit. Explain why comparing only the original firm's profit with zero is insufficient.
  2. Find and prove the complete pure-strategy pricing equilibrium, including deviations below cost, asymmetric prices and equal prices above cost. Give total quantity and profits.
  3. Solve the network quantities and prices. Reconstruct domestic and foreign primary incomes, total final expenditure, gross sales and intermediate sales. Correct a national-income calculation that adds gross sales and calls the result final output value.
  4. Raise foreign primary unit cost from one to two, holding the stipulated real orders and technical coefficients fixed. Reconstruct prices, primary incomes, each country's final H consumption and gross cross-border payments. Explain why the nominal bill increase alone is not an identified welfare loss.

3. An apparent development breakthrough — coordination and history

Two villages simultaneously decide whether to invest four goods from an initial endowment of four. If a village invests, its gross output is eight when the other invests and two otherwise. A village that does not invest retains its four goods and has zero incremental payoff. Thus investing gives incremental payoffs four or minus two. A historical credit program offers loans to these already funded villages, but changes neither returns nor feasible actions. A different policy credibly insures any investing village for three goods if the other fails to invest, with a public reserve of three available before decisions. If both or neither invest, no insurance claim is due. The primitives are (initial endowment,cost,coordinated output,solo output,insurance)=(4,4,8,2,3).

A historical panel records outcomes for a treated group T and comparison group C at dates−1,0,1: (T path,C path)=((8,10,16),(6,8,10)). Treatment begins only at date1. Separately, in the two-village insurance experiment, let each village independently receive coverage with probability one-half. If at least one village is insured, use the equilibrium implied by the game; if neither is insured, the maintained selection is the no-investment equilibrium. Outcomes are each village's incremental consumption payoff, excluding the public reserve that remains unused in equilibrium.

  1. Derive every pure equilibrium and the interior mixed equilibrium without insurance. Explain why merely extending credit cannot select the high-investment outcome in this model, and whether observed noninvestment establishes a borrowing constraint.
  2. Prove what credible insurance does to equilibrium when both villages are covered and when only one is covered. Reconcile the largest possible claim with the reserve, and distinguish actual equilibrium spending from the resources that must be available.
  3. Compute the historical difference-in-differences and prior-period trend difference. Construct two complete treated-group untreated counterfactuals consistent with all observed means: one with a date1effect four and one with effect zero. Explain what assumption separates them.
  4. Under independent individual coverage, compute the own-coverage mean contrast and compare it with assigning coverage to both villages versus neither. Explain the spillover and identify the experiment appropriate to the latter policy contrast.

Diagnosis and reconstruction

1a. A payment is not a free resource

Without tax,10−p=p−2gives p=s=6,Q=4. With tax two, p=s+2and10−p=s−2give s=5,p=7,Q=3. Consumers pay one more and producers receive one less: statutory remittance does not determine economic incidence. Revenue is6. Consumer and producer surplus are each Q²/2, so taxed total including receipts is9/2+9/2+6=15versus16without tax. Equivalently real surplus is∫₀Q[(10−z)−(2+z)]dz=8Q−Q². The vector is (untaxed price,untaxed Q,taxed p,taxed s,taxed Q,revenue,untaxed surplus,taxed surplus)=(6,4,7,5,3,6,16,15). Tax receipts transfer money from the market participants to government; adding them restores the part omitted from private surplus, but cannot create additional goods. Rubric: untaxed market1; taxed market/incidence1; revenue1; surplus/resource calculation1; transfer diagnosis1.

1b. Returns and access are separate margins

Net real gains from training are2−3=−1for L and4−3=1for H. Lwould decline an affordable self-financed place, whereas Hwould want one. But both have only one liquid good, less than the cost three, and cannot borrow: neither can self-finance. With free in-kind places both accept because wage increments2and4are positive. Thus (L net return,H net return,L free increment,H free increment)=(-1,1,2,4). Zero enrollment is consistent with different constraints and different returns. It does not identify that every person has a negative return, and a positive average return alone would not establish affordable access. Rubric: both net returns1; private ranking1; liquidity constraint1; free-place responses1; identification distinction1.

1c. The targeted repair breaks even in aggregate

Universal training spends all six receipts on two places, creates future output gains six and consumes six current goods. Its incremental training surplus is zero, so combined with the goods-market loss one its total change is−1. Selective training spends three, rebates three and creates output gain four. Its incremental real training surplus is one, offsetting the same market loss one, for combined change zero. The accounting is (universal spending,universal training surplus,universal combined change,selective spending,rebate,selective training surplus,selective combined change)=(6,0,-1,3,3,1,0). Government balances in each policy. In the selective policy consumer surplus falls3.5but the rebate restores3, leaving consumers down0.5; producers lose3.5and H gains4, totaling zero. Initial consumer resources cover the21goods bill and all program receipts are already collected. The selective policy improves on the universal proposal but is not a strict aggregate gain over the stated baseline, nor a Pareto improvement. Rubric: universal budget1; universal real surplus1; selective budget1; combined comparison1; incidence/no strict improvement1.

1d. A selected wage gap

Selective training gives Hfuture wage10while Lremains at4. The observed gap is6, but H's effect is10−6=4and the average effect over the two types is(2+4)/2=3. A trained-person before/after comparison recovers four here because the stipulated counterfactual wage is unchanged at six; in data, absence of other time effects would require justification, and random assignment or a credible comparison would be preferable. Rubric: observed gap1; treated effect1; average effect1; decomposition1; temporal-counterfactual qualification1.

2a. Profitable is not a best response

2b. The full pure-strategy price equilibrium

Both prices equal3is an equilibrium: each earns zero, raising one's price loses all demand and still earns zero, and lowering price sells at a loss. No equilibrium can have a lowest price below3, because a firm selling positive quantity below cost can instead post12and earn zero. Suppose the lowest price exceeds3. If prices tie below12, a sufficiently small undercut captures almost twice the previous quantity at almost the same margin and raises profit; if both equal12, a price just below12earns positive profit instead of zero. If prices differ, the high-priced firm earns zero and can choose a price between3and the lower rival price to earn positive profit. Finally, if one price is3and the other exceeds3, the low-price firm can raise its price slightly while staying below its rival and earn positive profit. These cases exhaust the domain. The unique price vector is(3,3), total quantity9and zero profits: (p₁,p₂,Q,profit₁,profit₂)=(3,3,9,0,0). Rubric: candidate1; all candidate deviations1; exclusion below cost1; equal/asymmetric above-cost exclusion1; mixed cost/asymmetric case and uniqueness1.

3a. The missing coordination margin

If the other invests, investing earns4rather than0; if it does not, investing earns−2rather than0. The pure equilibria are therefore both invest and neither invest. Against investment probability r, own investment earns4r−2(1−r)=6r−2, so indifference requires r=1/3. The unique fully interior mixed equilibrium has both investing independently with probability1/3. Thus (mixed probability,high-equilibrium total gain,low-equilibrium total gain)=(1/3,8,0). Since each already owns the four needed goods, additional credit does not enlarge the feasible action set or change these payoff comparisons. Noninvestment can be a coordination equilibrium without a borrowing constraint. This does not claim that credit constraints are absent in actual villages. Rubric: best responses1; both pure equilibria1; mixed derivation1; slack-credit diagnosis1; observational qualification1.

Remediation and source scope

Map errors to the mechanism, not only the domain score. For receipts versus resources revisit public finance and policy feasibility; for training selection use labor. For pricing and gross-output accounts use market organization and trade. For coordination and historical counterfactuals use development and history. For collective choice and elicitation use institutions and behavior. For the final domain use environment, health, space and the synthesis chapter. Then solve a fresh version that changes a binding constraint and explain why the original recommendation changes or survives.
