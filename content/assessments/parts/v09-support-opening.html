Volume 9 assessment — investments and asset pricing

Turn a return claim into a priced, funded and testable investment decision.

This package assesses all twelve chapters. It combines a prerequisite diagnostic, two distinct written examinations, an integrated portfolio project, oral transfers and a delayed assessment. The cases and data are original and synthetic. Their finite models make assumptions and errors inspectable; their numerical results are not forecasts, recommendations or evidence of investment skill in a real market. Complete the diagnostic before the volume, the examinations and project after the chapters, and the retention assessment after a delay.

Prerequisite diagnostic

Allow 40 minutes, without notes, using a basic calculator. Each item is worth five points, for 40 overall. Readiness requires at least 34/40 and at least four points on each of items 2, 3, 6 and 7. These items test earlier probability, algebra, valuation, funding and data skills, rather than assuming the new investment theory has already been learned. Record the reason for every error before opening the answers.

  1. A contribution is not a gain. An account starts with 200 CU. Its existing assets gain 10% in year 1. Immediately afterward the owner contributes another 50 CU. All assets then lose 10% in year 2. There are no fees or other flows. Find ending assets and cumulative investment profit in CU. Explain why ending assets divided by the original 200, minus one, does not measure the investment gain on all money supplied.
  2. Probability and risk units. A risky investment returns 20% with probability 2/3 and −10% with probability 1/3. A safe investment returns 5% in both states. Find the risky mean and variance, then the mean and variance of a portfolio that starts with 40% risky and 60% safe. Use decimal returns, not percentages, when reporting variance.
  3. Match rights before prices. A security costs 1.70 CU and pays (3, 1) CU in two terminal states. A bond costs 0.90 and pays (1, 1). Trading is frictionless; signed positions and borrowing are allowed. Solve for a portfolio paying (2, 0) and calculate its cost. State the implication for another freely traded claim with exactly those same dated payments and rights in an arbitrage-free market.
  4. A signal's denominator. In a specified population, an event occurs in 10% of cases. A positive signal appears in 80% of event cases and 20% of non-event cases. Calculate the probability of the event given a positive signal. Explain why the answer is not 80% and state one reason this posterior might fail to transfer to another population.
  5. A constrained maximum. Maximize the dimensionless objective f(w) = 0.06w − 0.08w² over 0 ≤ w ≤ 0.25. Give the unconstrained stationary point, the constrained maximizer and its objective value. Explain why a positive derivative at the upper endpoint is consistent with optimality.
  6. Funding has a calendar. Cash of 18 CU is available on Friday. A binding Friday payment is 12 CU, after which at least 8 CU must remain in cash. A separate 7-CU receipt arrives Monday; it cannot be pledged or accelerated. Borrowing is unavailable. Does the proposed payment satisfy the reserve rule? Quantify any Friday gap and explain whether Monday's receipt cures that earlier failure.
  7. Keep the original cohort. Three funds are all investable at the initial date. Each receives one CU. Their one-period total returns are 20%, 10% and −100%; the last fund liquidates with zero recovery. Find the equal-initial-investment cohort return and the average return reported after deleting the failed fund. Explain which population the latter describes and what to do with an unverified missing return in a different dataset.
  8. Uncertainty and selection. For a prespecified estimator, a valid normal approximation gives an estimated premium of 4 percentage points with standard error 3 percentage points. Use estimate ± 1.96 × standard error for an approximate 95% interval. Does inclusion of zero establish a zero premium? Would this unadjusted single-estimator calculation automatically justify a claim about the best of 100 rules selected using these same observations?
Diagnostic solutions and five-point scoring
  1. Year-1 assets are 200 × 1.10 = 220; the contribution makes 270; ending assets are 270 × 0.90 = 243 CU. Total supplied capital is 250, so cumulative investment profit is 243 − 250 = −7 CU. The raw ratio 243/200 − 1 = 21.5% includes an external contribution in its numerator without recognizing it as supplied capital. It is not a return measure for this funded history. Credit: intermediate timing 1; ending assets 1; supplied capital and profit 2; denominator explanation 1. Repair cash dating with capital budgeting; the new return conventions follow in Chapter 1.
  2. The risky mean is (2/3)(0.20) + (1/3)(−0.10) = 0.10. Variance is (2/3)(0.10)² + (1/3)(−0.20)² = 0.02 in decimal-return-squared units. Portfolio state returns are 0.11 and −0.01, mean 0.07 and variance 0.4² × 0.02 = 0.0032. The safe return has zero variance and covariance here. Credit: risky mean 1; risky variance 1; portfolio mean 1; portfolio variance 1; units or state reconstruction 1. Review expectation and covariance.
  3. For security holding a and bond holding b, solve 3a + b = 2 and a + b = 0. Thus a = 1, b = −1; the cost is 1.70 − 0.90 = 0.80 CU. Equal dated rights must have that same price under the stated trading assumptions. Otherwise buy the cheaper payoff and sell the dearer one, with matching terminal obligations. Credit: equations 1; positions 1; both payoff checks 1; cost 1; equality with assumptions 1. Review linear systems and state-contingent valuation.
  4. The event-positive mass is 0.08 and the non-event-positive mass is 0.18. Therefore P(event | positive) = 0.08/0.26 = 4/13 ≈ 30.77%. The supplied 80% conditions on the event, not the signal. A changed event prevalence, measurement procedure or conditional signal law would require a new calculation. Credit: both joint masses 2; denominator and posterior 2; reversed-conditioning/transport explanation 1. Review Bayes' rule.
  5. The derivative is 0.06 − 0.16w, so the unconstrained stationary point is 0.375. The strictly negative second derivative, −0.16, certifies concavity. The function increases throughout the feasible interval; its maximum is at w = 0.25, with f = 0.015 − 0.005 = 0.010. Its endpoint derivative is 0.02: the improving direction would require violating the upper bound. Credit: derivative/stationary point 2; concavity and constrained choice 1; value 1; feasible-direction explanation 1. Review constrained optimization.
  6. Friday closing cash would be 18 − 12 = 6 CU, two below the required eight. The payment itself can settle, but the complete reserve-constrained plan fails. Monday closing cash could later become 13; that does not supply two CU on Friday. A valid plan needs an authorized, available Friday remedy rather than a backdated receipt. Credit: Friday cash 1; reserve gap 1; settlement versus reserve distinction 1; date argument 1; feasible remedy requirement 1. Review payout and liquidity.
  7. The final cohort wealth is 1.20 + 1.10 + 0 = 2.30 CU from three initially invested, a return of 2.30/3 − 1 = −7/30 ≈ −23.333%. Deleting the failure produces (20% + 10%)/2 = 15%, a survivors-only statistic, not the performance of the original opportunity set. An unverified missing observation needs a provenance investigation and an explicit missing-data rule; neither zero recovery nor survival can be invented. Credit: cohort wealth/return 2; survivors' mean 1; population distinction 1; missing-data treatment 1. Review provenance and sampling and estimation.
  8. The interval is [4 − 5.88, 4 + 5.88] = [−1.88, 9.88] percentage points. Under the stated approximation it does not reject a zero premium at the corresponding two-sided 5% level; it neither establishes exact zero nor rules out economically meaningful effects. Searching 100 rules using the same data changes the claim and the selection distribution. Selection-aware inference or genuinely unused evaluation data is needed, with its own stated assumptions. Credit: interval 2; nonrejection versus proof 2; selection qualification 1. Review confidence intervals and tests and model validation.

Examination administration, coverage and advancement

Each examination allows 240 minutes and totals 100 points: five questions, each with four subparts worth five points. Use a nonprogrammable calculator and blank paper; no notes, internet or code. Form A is the initial examination. Form B is a distinct reassessment after documented remediation. The forms change economic mechanisms, information and feasible choices; they have not been statistically equated through student trials. Work the full form before opening its solutions. All numerical information needed is in its prompts. Preserve sufficient intermediate precision; accept economically equivalent rounding that does not reverse a boundary or feasibility conclusion.

Pass a written form with at least 85/100 and at least 15/20 in every essential domain, then pass the project and oral transfer. Passing a second form requires the same standard. These criterion thresholds are the library's design choices, not a university's rules or a certification of professional competence. A correct total does not excuse a critical error: financing a due payment with unavailable cash, treating a nonpositive pricing kernel as an arbitrage-free certificate where strict positivity is required, confusing a selected historical fit with demonstrated future skill, using future information in a past decision, or changing a benchmark retrospectively to conceal a loss requires repair and fresh transfer.

Scoring protocol. The solution for each five-point subpart allocates method, calculation and interpretation credit explicitly. Award follow-through credit when a prior arithmetic error is clearly identified and the later reasoning correctly uses the carried value; do not award feasibility or economic-conclusion credit for an impossible position. Penalize the same numerical slip once within a causal calculation, while separately scoring an unrecognized critical implication. For defensible open answers, accept different conclusions when their assumptions, funding and evidence satisfy the specified criteria. Record domain scores, critical errors and a short explanation of the learner's weakest transfer.

Remediation sequence. Locate the failed chapter route in the table, reconstruct one worked example without its answer, solve a new instance with a changed constraint or information set, and explain why the previous method failed. Repeat an examination only after that record is complete. Keep project computation open-resource and reproducible; examination timing is not a substitute for reviewing code, data rights or decision governance.
