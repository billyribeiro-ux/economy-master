Oral transfer and teach-back

Use the following five transfers after the written work and project. Allow about ten minutes per domain, with paper and a basic calculator. The examiner may change one stated input after the initial answer; the learner must identify what changes and what remains invariant. Each transfer is worth four points: one for the quantitative or formal core, one for a complete cash or information argument, one for its assumptions/limits, and one for a coherent response to the changed input. Pass at 17/20 with at least three points in every transfer and no unresolved critical error. This protocol supports human assessment; the library does not claim that a learner has already performed it.

  1. Rate matching is local. A liability and a hedge both have value 100 CU and first derivative −200 CU per unit parallel yield shift. Their second derivatives are respectively 500 and 300. Starting from zero surplus, estimate hedge-minus-liability surplus after a 0.01 parallel increase through second order. Does initial value/duration matching immunize every shock? Transfer: explain what must be added before drawing a conclusion about a nonparallel curve move.
  2. Cash arrives on dates. A desk has five CU immediately available. It must post three as initial margin and then pay a four-CU variation loss before receiving a six-CU separate settlement tomorrow. Borrowing and collateral release are unavailable today. Reconcile today's gap and explain why tomorrow's receipt does not remove it. Transfer: a confirmed two-CU facility becomes drawable before variation is due, but has an interest charge; state which conclusion changes and which valuation/cost questions remain.
  3. A nonlinear function needs the Itô term. Under a specified pricing law, dS=0.02Sdt+0.30SdW with no dividend. Derive d(S²) and the drift of e−0.02tS². For a claim paying S₁² and S₀=10, calculate its discounted expectation using the GBM second moment. Transfer: explain why zero discounted stock drift does not make every nonlinear discounted stock function a martingale.
  4. Inversion is not a joint price test. Matched zero-rate European calls with S=100 and strikes 90, 100, 110 cost 14, 9 and 5. Test the normalized butterfly. Change only the middle quote to 10 and repeat. Both middle quotes lie inside their individual inversion bounds. Explain why that fact does not decide the butterfly question, and why a passing finite screen still does not identify a dynamic volatility law.
  5. Fit does not identify the requested use. A price family is P(t)=100−t+c·t(t−1), per 100 CU future payment. Calibration at years 0 and 1 is exact for every c. Under an explicitly imposed −0.25≤c≤0.25, find the year-2 range. A policy permits a range no wider than 0.25 CU for point valuation. Give a decision, a responsible remediation and a closure test. Transfer: explain what changes if use is restricted to year 1.
Oral answer standards
  1. The value and first-order terms cancel. The second-order surplus is ½(300−500)(0.01)²=−0.01 CU. Equal value and duration therefore do not imply protection from every parallel shock; curvature and higher terms matter. Nonparallel shifts require the actual curve coordinates, key-rate sensitivities or a stated model of curve moves, and possibly cross-curvature. The examiner can reverse the shock: this quadratic term keeps its sign. A correct response distinguishes a local approximation from a global guarantee and identifies the missing exposure rather than asserting that more duration precision would solve it.
  2. After posting initial margin only two CU remain; paying four creates a two-CU funding gap. The six-CU future settlement may improve later wealth but cannot settle today's obligation under the stated rights. A usable earlier two-CU facility closes this immediate gap, subject to its actual draw and repayment terms. Its interest, any commitment fees, collateral rights and future repayment then belong in the funded ledger; access to financing does not erase economic cost or counterparty risk. The answer must keep initial collateral, variation settlement and unrelated receipts distinct.
  3. Itô gives d(S²)=(2·0.02+0.30²)S²dt+2(0.30)S²dW=0.13S²dt+0.60S²dW. Discounting leaves drift 0.11e−0.02tS²dt. The discounted expectation is 100e0.11≈111.627807046. This uses the stipulated Q and finite GBM moments; it is not the physical forecast under an unspecified law. Discounted stock is a martingale because its linear function has zero second derivative. Curvature creates the additional term for S². A general growth/uniqueness theorem has not been proved merely by naming that expectation.
  4. The normalized butterfly costs ½(14+5)−9=0.5 CU, which passes this nonnegative-cost test. At middle quote 10 it costs −0.5 CU, although all three individual inversion bounds still pass. Buying the two half-sized wing calls and selling the middle call produces a nonnegative terminal triangle and an initial receipt in the second case under executable matched rights. An individual inverse faithfully reproduces even a jointly incoherent set of prices. Finite passing quotes establish neither every missing strike/maturity condition nor a unique process; the required response names the claim each test actually addresses.
  5. P(2)=98+2c, giving [97.5,98.5] and width 1 CU. The imposed restriction supplies a scenario range, not information from the two calibration observations. The point-valuation request fails its 0.25 range criterion. Assign an owner to obtain relevant evidence or justify a narrower intended use, an independent challenger to review it, and a reproducible closure test tied to the requested target and version. Year-1 use has the common value 99 and zero range; that specific scope gate passes, while other numerical, contract and governance gates still require evidence. A committee cannot turn an unsupported parameter into an identified fact.

Delayed retention assessment

Return after 14–28 days without rereading the solutions. Allow 100 minutes with a basic calculator and the numerical inputs in these prompts. Five questions cover the five essential domains; each is worth 20 points. Pass at 85/100 with at least 15 in each and no unresolved critical error. These are new short transfers, not a substitute for either full examination. Explain a result in words after calculating it.

  1. D1 — A curve and its perturbation. A certain one-year 100-CU payment costs 97. A two-year bond paying five CU at year 1 and 105 at year 2 costs 99.35. (a, 5 points) Bootstrap the two discount factors and the effective forward rate from year 1 to 2. (b, 5) Price a certain 150-CU year-2 liability. With its continuously compounded year-2 zero rate z₂ independently shifted up by 0.002, calculate its first-order price change and exact change. (c, 5) Explain which other curve coordinates are held fixed, and why matching only sensitivity to a parallel move would not generally neutralize arbitrary key-rate shifts. (d, 5) Does this curve by itself identify the physical expectation of the future one-year short rate? Give the missing assumption or evidence.
  2. D2 — Carry and a stated credit contract. A stock costs 48. It pays a known two-CU dividend at half a year; the half-year and one-year discount factors are 0.99 and 0.94. (a, 5) Find the one-year forward delivery price and the current value of a long forward with delivery price 49, under frictionless matching rights. (b, 5) Separately, a one-year note pays 100 if it survives and 35 if it defaults, with stipulated pricing default probability 0.08 and payment always at year 1. Price the note and the discounted loss relative to a certain 100. (c, 5) A synthetic protection contract pays 65 at year 1 on that default. Its premium is 100s at year 1 only if survival occurs, with no accrual or other payments. Derive the zero-upfront annual rate s. (d, 5) Explain why the rate is neither an automatically observed physical hazard nor transferable to a contract with different accrual or settlement rules.
  3. D3 — State matching and a measure change. (a, 5) A one-period stock costs 50 and ends at 40 or 70; the bank factor is 1.05. Replicate a strike-52 call, giving shares, terminal bank cash, initial cost and pricing up probability. (b, 5) In a separate continuous model dS=0.08Sdt+0.20SdW under P with proportional yield 0.01 and bank rate 0.03, derive d log S under P and the required stock drift under Q. (c, 5) For the constant-density construction in Chapter 8, find λ so B=W+λt is Brownian under the new law; verify E exp(−λWt−λ²t/2)=1 and give the log-stock drift under Q. (d, 5) State why normalization of a density alone does not prove every discounted gain is a true martingale or establish all admissibility conditions.
  4. D4 — A mark approximation and a sampling unit. (a, 5) A desk is short 200 option units, each with supplied delta 0.45, gamma 0.02, vega 30 per unit volatility and annual calendar theta −5. Find the stock holding that offsets delta. For a spot rise of two CU, a volatility rise of 0.01 and elapsed time 1/250 year, compute the abbreviated Greek mark change per option and the combined option-plus-fixed-stock change. Treat coefficients as the stated local worksheet; do not claim an exact price or funded profit. (b, 5) Name the omitted exposure/error terms and explain what is needed to obtain financed trading profit. (c, 5) An ideal IID raw simulation has 400 observations and sample SD 6 CU. A separate antithetic run uses 200 independent pair means, each from two payoff evaluations, with sample SD 3 CU across pair means. Find both estimated standard errors and compare cost-adjusted sample variances of the means. (d, 5) Give the approximate 95% half-widths using 1.96 and explain what these intervals do not measure.
  5. D5 — A price interval and a failed use. Dates are 0 and T. A stock costs 50 and ends at (20,50,80); a certain terminal CU costs 0.95. All three states are physically possible, and unrestricted stock/cash trading is allowed only at 0 and T. A call pays (0,0,30). (a, 5) Characterize all strictly positive pricing probability vectors and derive the call's price interval. (b, 5) Give sub- and superhedges, and show why the limiting endpoints are excluded. (c, 5) A separate model comparison has candidate-minus-benchmark unit error +0.02 CU on 600 long units. The unit tolerance is 0.03 but the signed book limit is eight CU. Give the pass/fail results and effect of reducing quantity to 400. (d, 5) A size exception is still valid, but the requested version differs from the reviewed one. Write a scoped decision distinguishing the nonunique call price, the comparison error and the version gate.
Delayed retention solutions and scoring

  1. (a) D₁=0.97; 5D₁+105D₂=99.35 gives D₂=(99.35−4.85)/105=0.90. The one-year effective forward rate is D₁/D₂−1=7/90≈7.777778%. Award 1 point for D₁, 2 for the bootstrap equation/result and 2 for the correctly scaled forward.

    (b) Liability PV is 135 CU. With D₂=e−2z₂, derivative is −2(150D₂)=−270, so the first-order change is −0.54 CU. Exact change is 135(e−0.004−1)≈−0.538921439 CU. Award 1 for PV, 2 for sensitivity and 2 for exact change.

    (c) This scenario holds the one-year discount coordinate and the cash contract fixed while shifting z₂. Distinct maturity exposures need a vector of sensitivities; one scalar parallel constraint cannot generally set every component to zero. Award 2 for held-fixed coordinates, 2 for the dimension/exposure argument and 1 for a concrete nonparallel interpretation. (d) No: observed discount prices are pricing restrictions. Expectations of future short rates under P also involve risk premiums and a physical dynamics specification or evidence. An expectations hypothesis would be an additional assumption, not a fact supplied by two prices. Award 2 for the conclusion, 2 for P/Q or premium reasoning and 1 for naming the missing evidence.

  2. (a) Prepaid terminal stock costs 48−2(0.99)=46.02; forward delivery is 46.02/0.94=2301/47≈48.957446809. The existing long forward is worth 46.02−49(0.94)=−0.04 CU. Award 2 for the dividend strip, 1 for delivery and 2 for signed value.

  3. (a) Payoffs are (0,18); Δ=18/30=0.6, terminal bank cash −24, and initial bank value −24/1.05=−160/7. Call cost is 30−160/7=50/7≈7.142857143. Pricing up probability is (1.05·50−40)/30=5/12. Award 2 for payoff matching, 1 for bank dating, 1 for cost and 1 for the probability.

    (b) Itô gives d log S=0.06dt+0.20dW. Under the pricing law the ex-dividend drift must be r−q=0.02 in the stated model. Award 3 for the quadratic-variation correction and 2 for total-gain pricing. (c) λ=(0.08+0.01−0.03)/0.20=0.3. The normal moment identity E exp(aWt)=exp(a²t/2) makes the density expectation one. Substituting dW=dB−0.3dt leaves stock drift 0.02 and log drift 0.02−0.02=0. A local martingale need not be a true martingale under arbitrary coefficients or strategies. Apply the square-integrable/admissible class actually justified rather than extending the result to every proposed model.   4. (a) The short options have delta −90 shares, so hold 90 shares. The mark approximation per option is 0.45(2)+½(0.02)(2²)+30(0.01)−5/250=1.22 CU. Short-option change is −244; fixed-stock change is +180; combined change is −64 CU. Award 1 for shares, 2 for the four terms and 2 for signs and combined value.

    (b) This abbreviated expression omits mixed/time/volatility curvature terms and higher remainders; its held-fixed coefficients need a valid local domain.     (c) Raw SE is 6/√400=0.30; pair SE is 3/√200≈0.212132034. The corresponding estimated mean variances are 0.09 and 0.045, at the same 400 payoff evaluations.   5. (a) The forward is 50/0.95=1000/19. Let p₁=a. The stock restriction gives p₃−p₁=5/57, so p=(a,52/57−2a,a+5/57), with 0<a<26/57. The call costs 0.95·30(a+5/57)=5/2+(57/2)a, giving (2.5,15.5) CU. Award 2 for the calibrated vector, 1 for strict domain and 2 for the price interval.

A symbol has only its stated local definition. In particular, q often denotes dividend yield in continuous pricing and a branch pricing probability in a finite tree; β can be a diffusion coefficient, a statistical coefficient or a numerical weight. Rewriting the local definitions is part of a correct derivation. Do not infer a common meaning from the same letter.

From conditional prices to an accountable valuation system

The volume begins with a dated cash claim. Curves assign present values; derivatives of those values expose local risks. Credit and funding change both the payments and the available trades. Finite replication gives a transparent pricing argument, while stochastic calculus explains which continuous gains a hedge can match. A pricing measure and PDE acquire financial meaning only through that cash, information and admissibility structure.

Greeks and numerical methods make the structure usable, with their own coordinates and errors. Discrete trading, uncertain volatility, additional drivers and contractual monitoring expose the limits of a single formula. In an incomplete market several positive pricing laws can agree on traded assets and disagree on another claim. Governance then asks which calculations have actually been verified, which values are identified or assumption-dependent, and what use the evidence can justify.

The diagnostic, written forms, project and oral transfers demand these connections rather than isolated formula recall. Their scoring thresholds, synthetic cases and review procedures are original instructional choices. They do not accredit the learner, guarantee exam or employment outcomes, or replace external review of professional judgment. Further research requires the broader convergence, semimartingale, calibration and empirical theories explicitly identified in the chapter extensions.

Volume 11 places these tools inside banks and financial institutions: balance sheets, liquidity, counterparties, regulation and international exposures make the timing and model-use decisions consequential.
