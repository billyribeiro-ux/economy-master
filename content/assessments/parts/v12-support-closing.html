Oral defense and fresh transfer

After the written form and project, allow about 30 minutes for five explanations. Each carries four points. The suggested pass is 17/20 with at least three points on each item and no unresolved critical error. This is an authored criterion, not a calibrated professional examination. Before showing the illustrations, have a reviewer ask one unannounced follow-up per item. Change an assumption or input and require a new conclusion, rather than accepting a recitation of the supplied answer. Record the exact follow-up and its assessment. If studying alone, record the explanation before opening the illustration; seek an informed reader for feedback on the research defense.

  1. An instrument's name is not a restriction. A centered population obeys Y=βD+γZ+u, with E[Z²]=1, E[Zu]=0, E[ZD]=2 and E[ZY]=4. Explain what the usual ratio assumes about γ. If only −1≤γ≤1 is known, derive the identified set for β and give two compatible complete data-generating constructions with different β.
  2. A comparison can already be treated. Two equally sized cohorts E and L have observed means at dates 0,1,2 of (0,5,6) and (0,0,2), respectively. E starts treatment at 1 and L at 2. In the stipulated teaching world, every cohort's untreated mean is zero at every date and there is no anticipation. Compute the date-1-to-2 difference-in-differences using E as L's comparison. Compare it with L's true date-2 effect. Explain how an additional never-treated cohort with means (0,0,0) changes the relevant comparison.
  3. A stable relation need not have stable levels. Let X₀=0, Xₜ=Xₜ₋₁+Uₜ, and Yₜ=2Xₜ+Vₜ, where all Uₜ and Vₜ are mutually independent fair signs. Give a stationary linear relation, compute Var(Xₜ), and explain why applying a stationary-level regression theorem without checking its assumptions is unjustified. Does finding this relation establish the causal effect of intervening on X?
  4. A sufficient rate condition is not a verdict. For an orthogonal cross-fitted score, suppose both nuisance L² errors are Oₚ(n−1/5). The available remainder bound is a constant times their product, with uniform overlap and bounded outcomes. Evaluate its root-n order. Does this prove that the estimator cannot be asymptotically normal? State one concrete rate improvement that makes this product remainder negligible, and identify other conditions still needed.
  5. Reproduce the claim as well as the number. A report says “the randomized invitation increased treatment receipt's effect by four.” Two scripts both return four and have recorded hashes. One computes the difference in outcomes by invitation, the other by actual receipt; no output labels or assignment log were retained. Explain why agreement does not resolve the report. Specify the minimum next evidence you would request and the wording you could currently defend.
Oral illustrations and four-point rubrics
  1. Multiplying by Z and taking expectations gives 4=2β+γ. The usual ratio 4/2=2 assumes the exclusion γ=0 in addition to the stated moment restriction. The sensitivity set is β=2−γ/2, hence [3/2,5/2]. For complete witnesses let Z be a fair sign, D=2Z, Y=4Z and u=0. Both (β,γ)=(3/2,1) and (5/2,−1), and every intervening pair, generate exactly those same observed variables. The set is sharp under the allowed model and is not a sampling confidence interval. Credit: moment equation/exclusion 1; set 1; complete witnesses 1; identification-versus-uncertainty distinction 1. Follow-up: a narrower admissible direct effect should narrow the set through the same equation.
  2. The late cohort's change is two; the already-treated cohort's change is one. The comparison therefore returns 1, although L's date-2 treatment effect is 2. The omitted one is E's treatment-effect growth from five to six. With the never-treated cohort, L's change relative to its zero change is two, justified by the explicitly stipulated untreated trends and no anticipation. The arithmetic does not establish those conditions in an observed study. Credit: comparison 1; true effect 1; contamination mechanism 1; valid alternative and its assumption 1. Follow-up: change E's date-2 effect to five and explain why this particular contaminated comparison then happens to recover two.
  3. Yₜ−2Xₜ=Vₜ is iid with mean zero and variance one, while Var(Xₜ)=t. The levels lack a time-invariant finite variance, so a covariance-stationary-level theorem cannot be invoked. Cointegration concerns a stable stochastic relation among nonstationary series; its estimation and inference require a suitable nonstationary theory. The construction supplies no independent policy-intervention contract: an intervention might change the relation itself or other structural channels. Credit: relation 1; level variance 1; theorem scope 1; causal boundary 1. Follow-up: replacing 2 by a known alternative coefficient in the law changes the stationary relation, not the need to check level behavior.
  4. The available product bound is Oₚ(n−2/5), whose root-n order is Oₚ(n^(1/10)). This bound does not guarantee an oₚ(1) root-n remainder. It does not prove that the actual remainder grows or that normality fails: the bound may be loose or the bias terms may cancel. Improving one nuisance to Oₚ(n−1/2) while retaining Oₚ(n−1/5) for the other gives root-n product Oₚ(n−1/5)=oₚ(1). One still needs an appropriate independent sampling unit, fixed or otherwise controlled folds, held-out evaluation, nuisance L² convergence, common overlap, integrability and a nondegenerate oracle variance with valid estimation. Credit: product and scaling 1; sufficient-condition logic 1; valid improvement 1; remaining conditions 1.
  5. Invitation and receipt index different comparisons. The randomized invitation contrast can target an intention-to-treat effect under the actual assignment contract; the receipt contrast can retain selection. Their numerical equality may be accidental. Request the unit and assignment mechanism/log, versioned inputs and transforms, the two exact estimators and denominators, noncompliance information, outcome/missingness definitions, and the assumptions connecting receipt to outcomes. To turn a randomized invitation into a receipt-effect claim requires further conditions such as relevance, exclusion and the relevant compliance restriction, with the corresponding target stated. Presently one can say only that two retained programs returned four on unspecified analysis inputs; neither a reproducible estimate with verified inputs nor the quoted causal interpretation has been established. Credit: target distinction 1; concrete evidence 1; receipt-identification restrictions 1; defensible wording 1.

Delayed retention assessment

After two to four weeks without rereading these solutions, attempt the five new problems. Allow approximately 120 minutes with a basic calculator and no notes; this timing is uncalibrated. Each problem carries ten points. The proposed threshold is 43/50 overall, at least eight on each item, and correction of every critical error. These compact transfers do not replace the full written form or project. If a score falls short, reconstruct the relevant chapter argument and then solve a fresh problem with altered inputs.

  1. Two moments, one parameter. A correctly specified scalar model has population moment vector a−Bβ, with a=(2,6)′ and B=(1,3)′. Assume the stated iid regularity gives a centered root-n sample-moment normal limit at the true parameter, with covariance Ω=[(2,1);(1,4)]. For identity-weight GMM find β, the row K mapping the moment fluctuation into the parameter fluctuation, and its limiting variance KΩK′. Find the efficient row and variance. Prove that this efficient row minimizes variance among all rows L satisfying LB=1. State why the calculation alone does not establish validity of proposed empirical moments.
  2. Random assignment with missing outcomes. An iid population has potential binary outcomes Y(1),Y(0), potential response indicators R(1),R(0), and a fair assignment Z independent of their whole joint law. Only R=R(Z) and the responding outcome are observed. In both arms the response probability is 3/4; respondent outcome means are 2/3 in arm 1 and 1/3 in arm 0. No other restriction connects response and outcome. Derive the sharp population ATE bounds and the complete-case contrast. Construct full potential-variable laws attaining the endpoints. Then impose the additional information E[Y(1)|R(1)=0]∈[1/5,2/5] and E[Y(0)|R(0)=0]∈[3/5,4/5]; derive the new sharp bounds. Distinguish these bounds from a confidence interval.
  3. Negative serial correlation. For all integer t let εₜ be iid N(0,9), and define Yₜ=μ+εₜ−εₜ₋₁/3. Derive the lag-zero and lag-one autocovariances, all later autocovariances and the long-run variance. Find the exact variance and distribution of the five-observation sample mean, and compare with an independence calculation. Give an exact central 95% interval for μ using the standard-normal 0.975 quantile z₀.₉₇₅. In a separate forecasting exercise with known μ=0 and observed innovation state ε₅=3, find the conditional mean and variance of Y₆ given the innovations through date 5.
  4. Scaling a penalty and updating a prior. For a fixed centered design, X′X/n=I₂ and X′y/n=(2,−1/2)′. Minimize ‖y−Xb‖²/(2n)+(3/4)(|b₁|+|b₂|), and verify the active and inactive KKT conditions. Replace the first design column by 2X₁, retaining the same unweighted coefficient penalty. Find the new first coefficient and its contribution expressed as a coefficient on the original X₁. What penalty weight restores the old fitted contribution? Separately, θ is zero or one with prior P(θ=1)=1/4. A binary observation has P(Y=1|θ=0)=1/4 and P(Y=1|θ=1)=3/4. After observing Y=1, compute the posterior probability of θ=1 and state its evidential scope.
  5. Selection and transport are different operations. Under a global null, two prespecified p-values are independent U(0,1), and a report releases only their minimum M. Derive P(M≤u), a valid continuous selection-adjusted p-value as a function of M, and its value when M=0.04. Would that value reject at 0.05? Separately, a source population has covariate-stratum weights (3/4,1/4) and known stratum treatment effects (−2,4). Under conditional effect transport, a target has weights (1/3,2/3). Calculate both average effects. If only the target stratum-1 weight w∈[2/5,4/5] is known, derive the sharp target-effect set. State what must be retained to audit each calculation.

  2. Let a and b denote the two missing-outcome means. Then μ₁=(3/4)(2/3)+(1/4)a=1/2+a/4 and μ₀=(3/4)(1/3)+(1/4)b=1/4+b/4. The ATE is 1/4+(a−b)/4, with sharp set [0,1/2]. Synthesis and the next stage

An econometric argument begins with a question about a population or intervention. Identification determines what the observable law can settle under explicit restrictions. Estimation computes an answer from a finite sample; inference describes uncertainty under a specified repetition or assignment law. Design, dependence, support, missingness and analysis choices affect these stages differently. The most useful conclusion can be an estimate, a bound, a sensitivity relation or a precise statement of what the available evidence cannot distinguish.

The graduate theory volume asks which preferences, technologies, information and equilibrium restrictions support an economic mechanism. Graduate macroeconomics and macro-finance connect such mechanisms to dynamic aggregate evidence. Use the distinction between observable fit and invariant primitives throughout those volumes. The later graduate qualifying sequence integrates econometrics, microeconomic theory and macro-finance; it remains a separate assessment whose availability is shown in the assessment standard and curriculum.

A finished project should let another reader reconstruct both the outputs and the limits of the claim. The external-paper transfer asks the learner to apply that discipline to real research with real access constraints. This library supplies instruction, original problems and evaluation protocols. Original contribution, research judgment and successful supervision require the learner's work and appropriate independent feedback.
