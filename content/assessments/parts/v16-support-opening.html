Volume 16 assessment — specialization and original research

Turn a question into an auditable argument, and distinguish a rehearsed research skill from an original contribution accepted by qualified readers.

This package contains a prerequisite diagnostic, two distinct written forms, an open-resource research rehearsal, oral transfer and delayed retention. Every supplied study, bank record and numerical model is an original synthetic teaching case. A score measures performance on this material. Actual scholarship also requires a learner-generated contribution, warranted access to evidence, transparent limitations and substantive external evaluation. Keep the first attempt, feedback, corrections and version history.

Prerequisite diagnostic

Before studying this volume, allow a proposed 75 minutes, using paper and a basic calculator without notes or code. Eight tasks carry 5 points each. Readiness requires 34/40 and correction of every critical identification, unit, feasibility or integrity error. The tasks diagnose earlier quantitative and empirical reasoning; definitions needed here are supplied. Use the routes in the answers for remediation and explain a changed example without the key before returning. Timing and thresholds are instructional choices, not empirically calibrated standards.

  1. Choose a growth estimand. Two banks have opening loan stocks (50,80), and closing stocks (55,72), in the same million-currency units. Compute each growth rate, the equal-bank mean, and aggregate stock growth. Show the weighting identity. Does any result measure new loan originations?
  2. Supply the missing counterfactual. An exposed group has mean outcome (before,after) (20,18); a comparison group has (30,33). Define D as exposed change minus comparison change. Compute D. Give two complete counterfactual mean stories consistent with these observations: one satisfying parallel untreated changes and one with zero intervention effect. Assume no anticipation and an unaffected comparison group. Are equal initial levels required?
  3. Condition correctly. The probabilities (high state, positive given high, positive given low) are (1/4,3/4,1/4). Compute the probability of a positive signal and the posterior high-state probability after it. Explain what changes if only selected positive records enter the dataset.
  4. Prove a model optimum. A quasilinear consumer has gross benefit B(q)=9q−q²/2, and a competitive producer has cost C(q)=q+q²/2, q≥0. Assume sufficient numeraire and no externalities. Derive equilibrium quantity and price, prove global surplus maximization, and calculate the surplus lost if quantity is restricted to 3.
  5. Interpret an interval. In a stipulated normal location model with known standard error, (estimate, standard error, rounded two-sided critical value) are (0.4,0.5,1.96). Using that critical value, compute the approximate 95% interval. Does failure to reject a zero effect prove zero? Is a positive effect of 1 excluded?
  6. Bound incomplete measurement. Inputs (observed share, observed mean, lower outcome bound, upper outcome bound) are (3/4,4,0,8) for a fixed population. Outcomes are continuous and every outcome, observed or not, lies within those bounds. Give the sharp bounds for the full population mean and show endpoint and interior attainability. Why is reporting 4 alone insufficient?
  7. Distinguish rerunning from correctness. A script reports stock growth as (end−start)/end. With (start,end)=(100,120), its saved output exactly matches a fresh run. The intended definition is change divided by opening stock. Compute both numbers and write a correction instruction that preserves the ability to reconstruct the original release.
  8. Make a feasible evidence claim. An analyst proposes: “Our selected six banks prove that funding access raises national lending.” Only before/after bank stock totals are supplied; no assignment or counterfactual evidence exists. The analyst has (capacity, source/data checks, analysis, writing, review) hours (120,40,30,30,30). Calculate feasibility, write a defensible one-sentence claim, and propose an exact budget repair that preserves source checks and review.
Complete answers and additive scoring

  1. Individual growth rates are (55−50)/50=1/10 and (72−80)/80=−1/10. Their equal-bank mean is zero, whereas total stock falls from 130 to 127. (1/10,-1/10,0,-3/130) lists these four results. Aggregate growth uses opening-stock weights: (50/130)(1/10)+(80/130)(−1/10)=−3/130. Equal-bank growth answers a different question. Stocks combine new lending, repayments, defaults, sales and other adjustments; they do not identify gross originations. Specify the population, weighting and stock definition before using either result.

    Rubric (5 points). Individual rates 1; equal mean 1; aggregate 1; weighting identity 1; stock/origination distinction 1. Review data construction and stock and flow accounts.

  2. D=(18−20)−(33−30)=−5. Let m be the exposed group’s after-period mean without intervention. Its effect is τ=18−m; differential untreated change is b=(m−20)−3=m−23, hence D=τ+b. Parallel changes set m=23, giving (τ,b)=(−5,0). Zero effect sets m=18, giving (0,−5). Thus (-5,23,-5,0,18,0,-5) records D and the two triples (m,τ,b). Both reproduce all four observed means. The initial level gap does not contradict parallel changes, and neither observed change alone establishes the restriction on m. These are mean stories; they do not impose equal individual effects.

    Rubric (5 points). Observed D 1; identity 1; first world 1; second world 1; assumption versus levels 1. Review difference-in-differences.

  3. The joint high-and-positive probability is 3/16; the low-and-positive probability is also 3/16. The signal probability and posterior are therefore (3/8,1/2). The posterior follows from exhaustive states and the stated likelihoods for the signal actually observed. Selection into a record can alter the relevant conditional probabilities. If retention differs by state even after conditioning on the signal, the supplied signal likelihoods are insufficient for the posterior among retained records; one needs retention probabilities as well.

    Rubric (5 points). Two joint probabilities 2; denominator 1; posterior 1; selection condition 1. Review conditional probability.

  4. Marginal benefit is 9−q and marginal cost is 1+q. Clearing yields q=4 and p=5. Total surplus is S(q)=8q−q²=16−(q−4)², so its global maximum on q≥0 is 16, uniquely at 4. At q=3 it is 15, a loss of 1. The checked results are (4,5,16,15,1). This calculation is conditional on the supplied preferences, technology, competition and absence of other effects. The price transfers numeraire between the two agents and is not a separate social resource loss.

    Rubric (5 points). Marginal equations 1; equilibrium 1; global proof 1; restricted surplus/loss 1; maintained conditions and transfer interpretation 1. Review competitive equilibrium and concavity.

  5. The margin is 1.96×0.5=0.98, giving (-0.58,1.38). Zero is inside, so the corresponding two-sided test does not reject it. One is also inside and is not excluded by this interval. Failure to reject is compatible with a range of substantively different effects; it is not proof of zero. In repeated draws under the stipulated normal model, intervals made this way have approximately 95% coverage because the quantile was rounded. This is not a 95% posterior probability that the fixed parameter lies inside the realized interval, nor evidence that the normal model holds in an application.

    Rubric (5 points). Margin 1; endpoints 1; zero-test interpretation 1; effect of 1 interpretation 1; sampling/conditional coverage 1. Review estimation and intervals.

  6. Writing μmiss for the missing mean gives μ=(3/4)4+(1/4)μmiss=3+μmiss/4. The sharp bounds are (3,5). Set every missing outcome to 0 or 8 to reach the endpoints. For any desired μ in [3,5], setting each missing outcome to 4(μ−3) realizes it and remains within [0,8]. The observations do not determine μmiss; reporting the observed mean as the population mean adds a missingness or equal-mean restriction. The interval concerns the finite population, not sampling uncertainty.

    Rubric (5 points). Population identity 1; endpoints 2; sharpness including interior 1; selection/uncertainty distinction 1. Review selection and missing data.

  7. The recorded code produces 20/120=1/6; the intended growth is 20/100=1/5. Thus (1/6,1/5). A suitable instruction is: “Retain the original input, code and output as version 0. Make a separately identified corrected version using opening stock as denominator, add a hand calculation and zero-opening-stock rejection, rerun all dependent tables and replace each affected claim. Record the changed definition and the numerical discrepancy.” Matching saved output establishes a narrow computational agreement, not validity of the formula, source data or causal claim.

    Rubric (5 points). Faithful output 1; intended output 1; version preservation 1; dependent correction/test 1; reproduction/correctness distinction 1. Review reproducibility.

  8. The schedule totals 130 hours and exceeds capacity by 10: (130,10,40,20,30,30) gives total, shortfall and one repaired allocation. Narrow the analysis to a descriptive six-bank stock reconciliation requiring 20 hours, retain 40 for source/data checks, 30 for writing and 30 for review, and confirm that this smaller analysis is feasible before committing. A defensible claim is: “The supplied records describe changes in outstanding lending for six selected banks; they do not identify a funding-access effect or a national response.” The repair changes scope explicitly; it does not pretend the original causal project became valid by shortening its schedule. Access failure or unusable records triggers reconsideration rather than fabrication.

    Rubric (5 points). Total/shortfall 1; exact feasible allocation 1; explicit reduced scope 1; descriptive claim 1; causal/population limits 1. Review policy synthesis and empirical project design.

Administration and advancement

Form A constructs a research argument from supplied notes and records. Form B audits and repairs a different argument. Each has twenty 5-point tasks across five 20-point domains, totaling 100. For each form allow a proposed 240 minutes, closed book, with paper and a basic nonprogrammable calculator; no code or internet. Required constants and case materials are supplied. Answer prompts before opening their solutions. The forms are substantively different but have not been statistically equated or timed with learners.

For written-form advancement require at least 85/100 overall, at least 15/20 in every domain, repair of all critical errors and a successful unseen transfer explanation. Critical errors include changing the population or units without disclosure, treating an unidentified association as causal, releasing data without warranted authority, hiding a consequential correction, or inventing source inspection or external approval. Additive rubrics award each stated point once; equivalent valid derivations earn credit. After targeted remediation and a study interval, administer the other form. Do not count reading a solution as an independent successful attempt.

The project is open resource and separately scored. The complete teaching dossier permits actual reconstruction, correction, model checks, writing and defense practice. Record tools and assistance, rerun the downloadable standard-library Python materials, and compare an independently constructed calculation with the instructor output. The project’s five-domain score cannot certify novelty or real research competence. The continuation requirements demand an actual question, full source reading, authorized data access where relevant, learner-generated paper and qualified independent evaluation. The separate research portfolio capstone is a distinct production item; its status is displayed on the assessment index.
