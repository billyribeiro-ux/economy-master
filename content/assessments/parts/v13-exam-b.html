2. Two production stages and the ownership of intermediate receipts — 20 points

There are raw input R, intermediate input M and final consumption F. A and B consume only (R,F)≥0 with utility R·F. Initial raw endowments are (4,1); neither owns M or F. Firm 1 uses z≥0 raw and supplies at most √z intermediate. Firm 2 uses m≥0 intermediate and supplies at most 2√m final. Free disposal is allowed. Firms maximize profit at common nonnegative, nonzero prices; after establishing positivity normalize the raw price to one and denote the other two prices by p,r.

A initially owns all of firm 1 and B all of firm 2. A share pays net profit, never gross receipts. This deterministic market explicitly permits enforceable intra-cycle trade credit: inputs are delivered against invoices in production order; a clearinghouse nets final commodity invoices and declared profit entitlements at zero interest. All claims and deliveries are enforced. There is no outside cash grant or default. This authority is a primitive and does not establish cash-in-advance feasibility. Any raw transfer must be authorized by the actual owner before production.

The policy target is A:(3,3/2), B:(1,1/2), with original firm ownership for the transfer in 2c. For that part alone evaluate W=(3/4)log(R_A F_A)+(1/4)log(R_B F_B), with weights (3/4,1/4). Extend W to −∞ at a zero product; this is the stipulated planner criterion, while individual preferences remain R_i F_i.

  1. Derive both firms’ global choices and profits, households’ demands, and every competitive equilibrium. Check all three commodity markets and exclude zero-price cases rather than invoking an unchecked general existence theorem. (7 points)
  2. Exchange the households’ ownership of the two firms while keeping raw endowments unchanged. Find all resulting prices, profits, incomes and consumption. Explain exactly why prices do not move in this example. (3 points)
  3. With original ownership restored, decentralize the target by a balanced pre-production transfer of raw input. Give a global certificate for the stated planner objective. Explain why the positive target wealth and a cheaper bundle upgrade its support inequalities to full household optimality. (6 points)
  4. Reconcile the two firms’ baseline input invoices, output receipts and distributions through the settlement chain. Give the household settlement accounts. Audit the proposal to replace net profits by both firms’ gross output receipts in household income. (4 points)

3. Pessimistic entry beliefs can be consistent without surviving a stronger restriction — 20 points

An entrepreneur privately knows type G or B, with fixed positive prior probabilities (1/3,2/3). Enter costs disutility (1,3) for G,B, whether or not backed. Out gives both players zero. An investor may Back or Decline. Backed entry transfers 2 to the entrepreneur and gives the investor nontransferable benefit (5,0) for G,B. Thus backed payoffs are (1,3) for G and (−1,−2) for B; unbacked entry gives (−c_type,0). The investor owns two cash units before any choice. Costs/benefits are utility, not additional cash; no other transfers or hidden actions occur.

First entry and backing are simultaneous, with the investor observing neither type nor entry. Back then authorizes payment only if entry occurs. Next change only timing: the investor observes Enter before deciding, with no action after Out. Types are remembered and the game has perfect recall. Permit independent mixing or behavioral randomization as appropriate.

Apply this additional belief restriction only after sequential equilibrium: at an off-path entry node a type is dominated for entry when its greatest entry payoff over all receiver mixtures is strictly below its equilibrium payoff. If that set is nonempty and proper, assign it posterior probability zero. This is a supplied finite restriction, not a silent part of Bayes consistency or a claim about all refinements.

  1. Let g,b be type-contingent entry probabilities and q the investor’s Back probability in the simultaneous protocol. Derive the entire Bayes–Nash set, including all boundary mixing. Give actual cash and utility accounts at the trading equilibrium. (5 points)
  2. In the sequential protocol, let mu be the investor’s posterior probability of G after entry and q its backing probability there. Find the complete sequentially rational, Bayes-on-path assessment set before checking consistency. Include all off-path mixing boundaries. (6 points)
  3. Show that every assessment just listed is consistent. Give explicit completely mixed approximating behavior for each posterior boundary and explain why consistency does not require each approximating behavior to be an equilibrium. (5 points)
  4. Apply the additional belief restriction as stated. Which sequential equilibria survive? Explain why this is an extra refinement rather than an implication of Bayes consistency, and audit a claim that more credible beliefs alone prove an unconditional welfare gain. (4 points)

5. A unique network equilibrium is not a welfare certificate — 20 points

Three agents form the complete undirected graph and independently choose verified x_i∈[0,1]. Baseline utilities, in one declared transferable-utility scale, are u_i=a_i x_i−x_i²/2+(1/4)x_iΣ_{j≠i}x_j, with a=(1/4,1/2,1/4) and interaction weight 1/4. Costs are disutility, not cash payments. Real welfare is the sum of these baseline utilities. Independent mixed strategies are permitted.

A sponsor may commit nonnegative per-unit rates t_i and pay t_i x_i after effort verification. It owns cash K, cannot borrow and must cover every feasible effort profile, so Σt_i≤K. It retains unused cash and receives no extra service benefit; its utility change equals minus actual payments. The target is (1,1,1). Only this linear committed class is available.

A numerical report returns candidate (3/5,3/4,3/5). Separately, it claims the exact unassisted Nash point has a global objective gap at most 1/100 for an approximate welfare function uniformly within 1/100 of true welfare on the cube. A local first-order condition is not the stated global-gap premise.

  1. Derive global best responses, prove existence and uniqueness of unassisted equilibrium on the whole cube, and solve it. Explain whether independent mixed equilibria add further outcomes. A finite grid is not an existence or uniqueness proof. (5 points)
  2. Prove the unique global welfare optimum without relying on a local first-order condition. Compare it with equilibrium and exhibit an actual profitable individual deviation from the planner allocation. (5 points)
  3. Find the minimum sponsor budget in the declared policy class that uniquely implements full effort over all independent equilibria. Prove necessity, sufficiency, attainment and every-state financing. Give the target agent/sponsor accounts and the limit of a Pareto claim. (5 points)
  4. For the reported numerical candidate compute the true best-response residual, its certified distance to Nash and its actual distance. Derive the uniform-objective/global-solver bound and audit the separate claim about the exact unassisted Nash point. State the smallest valid replacement for each inference. (5 points)

  1. Part 1a

    For row t and column s, the slack U_t+lambda_t p_t·(x_s−x_t)−U_s is the matrix ((0,1/2,9/8),(1/4,0,9/16),(1/4,9/4,0)); hence every inequality holds with strictly positive multipliers. Set A(z)=min_t{U_t+lambda_t p_t·(z−x_t)}. At z=x_s, every plane is at least U_s and plane s equals it. On budget s, plane s is at most U_s, so A(z)<=A(x_s). A finite minimum of affine functions is concave and continuous. If one coordinate increases strictly and none decreases, every plane increases by at least the positive minimum of its slope in that direction, so A strictly increases. The alternative first-budget bundle costs one; its three plane values are (1,5/4,17/16), so it also attains A=1.   2. Part 1b

    Write a=p_y/(2p_x). With budget exhaustion, put s=sqrt(x) in [0,sqrt(m/p_x)]; the objective is s+m/p_y−(p_x/p_y)s^2, a strictly concave quadratic. Thus s*=min(a,sqrt(m/p_x)), x*=s*^2 and y*=(m−p_x s*^2)/p_y. Equivalently the wealth threshold is M=p_y^2/(4p_x): for m<=M, demand is (m/p_x,0) and v(p,m)=sqrt(m/p_x); for m>=M, demand is (p_y^2/(4p_x^2),m/p_y−p_y/(4p_x)) and v=m/p_y+p_y/(4p_x). The formulas coincide at M; m=0 gives the sole affordable origin. To attain target ubar, a cost minimizer has y=max(0,ubar−s). A choice s>ubar is dominated in cost by s=ubar, so minimize p_x s^2+p_y(ubar−s) on [0,ubar]. The unique solution s_h=min(ubar,a) gives h=(s_h^2,ubar−s_h) and e=p_x s_h^2+p_y(ubar−s_h). Therefore e=p_x ubar^2 below ubar=a and e=p_y ubar−p_y^2/(4p_x) above it. Both expressions and their first target derivatives agree at a; ubar=0 gives h=0 and e=0. Substitution reproduces every observed bundle.

    These global quadratic arguments establish the boundary, without applying an interior first-order condition to an axis point.

    Scoring: 3 for complete Marshallian and value; 3 for complete expenditure and Hicksian; 1 for boundary and observation checks.

Because the conditional Z law is the same under both actions, summing these utility means with either action’s success probabilities preserves both expected utility payments. Squaring a conditional mean weakly lowers the conditional mean square, strictly here in both fibers, so H wage cost falls from 35/6 to 17/4, saving 19/12. Net H utility is 4/3 and L utility 7/6, leaving strict incentive gap 1/6 and satisfied participation. These comparisons cover mixtures of the two actions by linearity.

    Common wage bounds and the sixteen-unit escrow remain satisfied.

    Scoring: 1 for all action conditioning proof; 2 for replacement cost and incentive accounts.

  16. Part 4d

    Each changed row sums to one, success probabilities remain (2/3,1/3), and the Z=1 probability remains 1/2 under each action. But conditional Z laws differ by action. Original net H utility is 7/6 and L utility 11/12, so H is strictly preferred by 1/4. Under the H conditional law the two mean utility payments are (17/8,3/4), giving replacement wages (289/64,289/64,9/16,9/16). H utility stays 7/6, but L utility becomes 29/24; the incentive gap is -1/24, so the worker strictly chooses L. Expected cost under the hypothetical target H falls from 14/3 to 307/96, but that is not an implementable H cost because H-IC fails. The valid preservation theorem requires the conditional law within each retained statistic fiber to be identical for every feasible action, equivalently every action likelihood ratio relative to a positive reference law must be constant on each fiber.

    Then conditioning utility, not raw wages, preserves all action values; common wage restrictions must also allow the replacement. Identical separate marginals alone do not supply that condition.

    Scoring: 1 for marginals and original IC; 2 for exact conditioned values and failure; 2 for cost interpretation and precise repair.

  17. Part 5a

    Completing the own-action square gives B_i(x)=clip_[0,1](a_i+(1/4)sum_{j!=i}x_j), the unique global reply. Clipping is one-Lipschitz and each row has two weights one quarter, so B is a one-half contraction in the sup norm and maps the closed cube into itself. Successive iteration increments shrink geometrically; their summable tail makes every iteration Cauchy, with a limit in the cube. Continuity gives a fixed point, and the contraction inequality gives uniqueness. The interior equations imply (5/4)x_i=a_i+S/4 and S=2, yielding x*=(3/5,4/5,3/5); all coordinates lie in the declared cube and directly satisfy the clipped map. Utilities are (9/50,8/25,9/50), with real welfare 17/25. Against any independent distribution of opponents, expected payoff remains a strictly concave quadratic in own action with a unique pure maximizing reply determined by their means.

    A best-response distribution must therefore be degenerate, so no additional independent mixed equilibrium exists. This does not rule out correlated recommendations or a different graph/action domain.

    Scoring: 3 for global response and complete contraction proof; 2 for exact equilibrium and mixed scope.

  18. Part 5b

    Let d_i=1−x_i. Expanding the exact polynomial gives W(1,1,1)−W(x)=a·d+(1/4)sum_{i<j}(d_i−d_j)^2. Every term is nonnegative on the cube, and because all a_i>0 the difference vanishes only when d=0. Thus the unique global optimum is full effort and W=1, versus equilibrium welfare 17/25, a gap of 8/25. Baseline full-effort utilities are (1/4,1/2,1/4), each higher than its equilibrium utility. Nevertheless agent 1 can reduce effort from one to 3/4 when the others stay at one, raising utility from 1/4 to 9/32, a gain 1/32. The same holds symmetrically for agent 3. Mutual preference for the planner outcome does not make it a Nash equilibrium; the polynomial certificate concerns the social objective, not unilateral incentives.

    Scoring: 3 for global polynomial certificate and uniqueness; 2 for exact welfare and profitable deviation.

  19. Part 5c

    At the target the unclipped own optimum is a_i+t_i+1/2. Full effort is a best reply exactly when t_1>=1/4, t_2>=0 and t_3>=1/4. Hence any implementation requires sum t_i>=1/2. Rates (1/4,0,1/4) attain that bound; they make full effort a fixed point. The slope and clipping Lipschitz constants are unchanged, so the same contraction proves it the unique pure equilibrium, and strict own concavity again excludes additional independent mixed equilibria. Equality at the cap does not create a second best reply: the own quadratic is still strictly concave. This differs from the unattained strict threshold in a binary adoption game. Maximum possible payout is one half, exactly prefunded by K=1/2, including every off-path action profile. At the target each agent’s subsidized utility is one half, sponsor utility change is -1/2 and consolidated welfare is one.

    The sponsor loses relative to no payment, so this is not a Pareto gain when the sponsor is included. Optimality is only over the announced nonnegative linear rate class, not every conceivable tax or mechanism.

    Scoring: 2 for necessary bound and attained rates; 2 for all equilibrium sufficiency and cash; 1 for consolidation and policy scope.

  20. Part 5d

    The candidate’s response vector is (47/80,4/5,47/80), so its sup-norm residual is 1/20. If e=||xhat−x*||_infinity and r=||xhat−B(xhat)||_infinity, the triangle and contraction inequalities give e<=r+e/2, hence e<=2r=1/10; the actual distance here is 1/20. This is distance to the unique Nash fixed point, not a welfare guarantee. For any true maximizer x^W, uniform error epsilon and a genuine global approximate-objective gap eta imply W(x^W)−W(xhat)<=epsilon+[sup W-hat−W-hat(xhat)]+epsilon<=2epsilon+eta by inserting W-hat at both points. The asserted values would limit welfare regret to 3/100. But the exact Nash point has regret 8/25, so the two asserted approximation premises cannot both hold for that return. Its best-response residual is exactly zero, supplying a direct counterexample to a welfare inference from that residual alone.

    A valid report either uses the contraction certificate for its stated equilibrium target, or establishes the true global objective-gap premises for its welfare target; it does not switch targets after seeing a small numerical residual.

    Scoring: 2 for residual and distance certificate; 2 for global objective proof and exact contradiction; 1 for correct target and minimal repair.
