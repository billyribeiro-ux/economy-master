The economic and evidence contract

Two households share a nonrival service of quantity g≥0 per period: each receives the same total g. Household i's benefit in currency per period is Bi(g)=aig−g²/2. The quadratic is a maintained toy preference, including decreasing and eventually negative marginal benefit; it is not a fitted demand curve. Provision costs cg, c>0. There are no external parties, uncertainty, strategic reports, administration costs or income effects in this model. Quasilinear utility is benefit minus the household's payment, and total welfare subtracts the real provision cost once. Transfers between households cancel in that total. An institution would need to test all these exclusions before using the model.

In private provision households simultaneously choose contributions qi≥0, receive g=q1+q2 and pay cqi. In the proposed collective scheme a coordinator chooses a common g and charges personalized per-unit prices ti. It may also require a disclosed lump-sum transfer T from household1 to household2. These are different institutions, not two names for the same choice. The public procurement budget limits cg; private contribution equilibria are a separate benchmark. Proposed collective prices must be nonnegative, expenditure must fit the stated budget, and any compensating transfer must have assumed authority and leave both households weakly above the selected private benchmark. These feasibility requirements are stipulated policy rules, not universal welfare axioms.

Base primitives (a1,a2,c,budget,T) are (6,4,4,12,2); the transfer is assumed permitted only for this synthetic case. The delayed task uses (7,5,4,16,3). Sensitivities separately set the base budget to10; use values10/2 with cost4,budget16,T0; use equal values5/5 with cost4,budget12,T0; or remove transfer authority from the base. The inputs distinguish these assumptions from response evidence and from normative scoring criteria.

Task1 — Full lesson and economic key

Objective and opening explanation. “By the end, distinguish an individual's contribution incentive from a planner's sum of benefits, then test whether the resulting financing leaves each household willing to participate. Imagine two households using the same lighted path. One extra unit of lighting benefits both. A contributor pays for the whole extra unit but counts only its own marginal benefit. A planner counts both benefits. Counting both can justify more service, but someone must pay: a larger pie does not specify its division.” The analogy illustrates nonrival receipt only; it does not establish actual preferences, nonexcludability or financing authority.

Minutes25–35, complete the household payment/utility table together; ask which household loses before transfer. Minutes35–42, derive the transfer interval and compare the budget. The teacher's board text is the three objectives Ui=ai(qi+qj)−(qi+qj)²/2−cqi, W(g)=(a1+a2−c)g−g², and Uicollective=Bi(g)−tig plus its stated transfer. Students must identify each payment's recipient.

Global private-provision proof. Holding qj fixed, the derivative in own contribution is ai−qi−qj−c, with second derivative−1. Completing the square, or strict concavity with the boundary, gives the unique best response max{0,ai−c−qj}. If a1>a2 and a1>c, both contributions cannot be positive at equilibrium: their first-order conditions would require a1=a2. Only household2 positive is also impossible, since then total would be a2−c and household1 would want a positive contribution. Therefore q1=a1−c,q2=0. Each is indeed a best response. Reverse the labels if household2 has the higher value. If max ai≤c, both best responses imply the unique zero profile. If a1=a2=a>c, every nonnegative split summing to a−c is an equilibrium, and the best-response conditions exclude other totals. If a≤c, zero remains unique. This proves the entire equilibrium correspondence, not only a stationary candidate.

Efficient scale and implementation proof. Let S=a1+a2. Completing the square in W gives the unique g*=max{0,(S−c)/2}. If S≤c, every positive quantity has smaller welfare than0; otherwise the positive square's vertex is feasible. At g*>0 choose ti=ai−g*. Each household choosing a common quantity against its own fixed per-unit price uniquely demands g*, since its objective has derivative ai−g−ti and curvature−1. Prices sum to S−2g*=c, so payments cover cg* exactly. When g*=0, prices ai induce zero and no money is collected or spent. Some prices can be negative: that violates this project's proposed nonnegative-price scheme even though the preference model and efficient quantity are valid. Agreement at fixed prices is not incentive compatibility of truthful reports, a political consent result or a mechanism for discovering unknown values.
