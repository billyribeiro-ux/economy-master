Oral transfer and remediation

After the written exam and project, allow 20 minutes for these four explanations and follow-ups. Record your answers before revealing the benchmarks. Each prompt is five points: two for the correct mechanism/calculation, one for dates and claim boundaries, one for assumptions or evidence, and one for a clear conditional decision. Aim for at least 17/20 with no unresolved material valuation or funding error. A reviewer should change one input again and ask you to reconstruct the result; memorized language is insufficient.

  1. Growth needs a balance sheet. At date 0 a stable business has operating capital 200, next-year EBIT 32, a stipulated operating tax rate of 25%, perpetual capital growth of 3% and an applicable operating discount rate of 9%. Returns on operating capital stay constant. Derive next-year reinvestment, free operating cash and value. Then change the tax rate permanently to 20%, holding pre-tax operations, the capital-growth policy and the stipulated discount rate fixed. Recompute the return and value; explain which quantities should change together.
  2. Value does not arrange a loan. Closing cash before decisions is 24; committed investment needs seven immediately, a financing fee needs one, and minimum postclosing cash is ten. A proposed dividend is six. A new collection delay requires four more cash at closing; no extra financing is committed. Explain the funding problem and the largest revised dividend. Identify what would be needed to defend retaining the original dividend.
  3. A premium uses surplus. A buyer's standalone common equity is 440 and the target's is 180. An all-cash acquisition generates net surplus 30 after all integration costs, transaction fees and financing effects. Fair financing is already available. The buyer proposes to pay target shareholders 220. Compute both owners' gains, the combined gain and the highest payment leaving the buyer no worse off. Explain why an expected increase in earnings per share would not change those value identities.
  4. A conditional value is not an observed price. Your project uses forecast state cash flows and pricing weights derived from a stated traded-clone benchmark. The committee now says that the clone does not in fact match the project's risks. Explain what remains arithmetically valid, what pricing conclusion no longer follows and what evidence or revised analysis you would request. Include how an unfunded downside or missing creditor consent affects a recommendation.
Oral benchmarks and follow-up logic
  1. NOPAT is 32(1 − 0.25) = 24 and ROIC is 24/200 = 12%. Capital grows by six, so next-year FCFF is 18 and date-0 value is 18/(0.09 − 0.03) = 300. Under the changed stipulated tax, NOPAT becomes 25.6 and ROIC 12.8%; the same capital-growth policy still requires six, giving FCFF 19.6 and value 980/3 ≈ 326.667. Reinvestment fraction changes from 25% to 6/25.6 = 23.4375%; holding that fraction fixed would instead change capital growth. These are supplied steady-state assumptions: tax could affect risk, prices or investment in a real setting, but those channels were explicitly held fixed here. If the discount rate also changes, use the revised rate and check that it remains above growth before applying this perpetuity.
  2. Before the delay, cash after investment and the fee is 16, so six can be paid while preserving ten. The delay reduces that cash to 12: retaining the six dividend leaves six, four below the reserve. The largest feasible dividend is two. Neither positive NPV nor a later expected collection creates an authorized closing credit line. Retaining the dividend would need a specifically committed and priced four-unit funding source or an effective modification of the payment/reserve obligations; incorporate fees, rights and later repayment before concluding that the repair is worthwhile.
  3. Target gain is 220 − 180 = 40. Buyer gain is 30 − 40 = −10, so its residual equity is 430. Combined owner wealth becomes 430 + 220 = 650 versus 620 initially. Maximum buyer-participation payment is 180 + 30 = 210. Since surplus was already net of all costs, subtracting a fee again would be an error. An EPS change can reflect financing, share count or differing earnings multiples; it neither creates an extra cash benefit nor repairs the buyer's ten-unit value loss.
  4. The conditional cash-flow arithmetic and any complete sources-and-uses identity can remain correct. The claim that the clone uniquely prices the project's nonreplicable risk cannot. Obtain evidence about state-by-state payoff matching, timing, rights and funding, or replace the clone with a justified pricing/decision framework and disclose sensitivity or incomplete-market limits. Physical probabilities alone do not restore the missing market-price argument. Preserve mechanically calculated shortfalls as diagnostics; do not price a covenant-breached continuation as executable without specified waivers or rescue terms. A restructuring with positive total surplus still needs class participation and the required signatures. Score a specific coherent alternative analysis equally; there is no requirement to recommend investment.

For a failed domain, use the concept index below, reconstruct one worked example without looking at its answer, then solve a fresh problem changing timing, claim rights or a binding constraint. Keep an error log with the original statement, the violated assumption, the corrected equation and evidence of transfer. Reattempt the other form only after this repair. For a numerical disagreement, reconcile the full cash/claim ledger before changing the discount rate to make the answer fit. For a source disagreement, check the precise method, contractual setting and date to which the cited claim applies.

Delayed retention assessment

After two to four weeks, allow 75 minutes without notes, using a basic calculator. Six ten-point items give 60 points; aim for at least 51/60 and at least eight points on every item. Repair every critical timing, funding, claim-allocation or source error. All cases are independent, amounts are kCU unless shares or percentages are stated, and all tax/contract/pricing assumptions are supplied teaching rules. Revisit failed mechanisms with a changed case one week later.

  1. A two-year project buys equipment for 40 at date 0, depreciates it by 20 each year and has no salvage. It requires six of working capital at date 0, fully recovered at the end of Year 2. Incremental annual EBITDA is 36; the operating tax rate is 25%, with all deductions usable in the consolidated company. There are no later capital expenditures. Using the project also consumes an existing exclusive right that could instead be sold now for four after tax; it has no value after the project. A study paid for last month cost three and is unrecoverable. The supplied discount rate for these project cash flows is 20%. Derive the dated incremental cash flows and NPV; state the treatment of the study and right. If a mutually exclusive feasible alternative has NPV five and no other differences matter, which do you select?
  2. At the valuation date, next year's stable NOPAT is 24. Thereafter it grows perpetually at 3%, with return on incremental invested capital of 12%; the cash-flow model requires reinvestment fraction g/ROIC. The matching operating discount rate is 9%. Find next-year FCFF and operating value. Add separately valued cash 12 and nonoperating assets eight, then subtract debt market value 80 and preferred-claim value ten. There are ten thousand common shares. Find common equity and price per share. Recompute if sustainable ROIC is only 6%, with the same initial NOPAT, growth and other inputs; explain the value difference.
  3. A firm's funded operating assets have unlevered value 160, excluding cash and financing effects. A safe existing loan of 60 is worth par, pays 5% annual interest at the ends of Years 1 and 2 and repays principal at Year 2. Interest deductions are fully usable at 25%; their cash benefits are certain and discounted at 5%. Opening cash is 24. Before the stated operating assets are funded, the firm must pay seven of committed investment and one financing fee immediately; these two outlays are excluded from the supplied 160. There are no other immediate payments and minimum remaining cash is ten. Calculate the shield value, the largest immediate dividend, and old-owner wealth just before versus just after that dividend. Assume no personal taxes, payout costs or information effects, and show the cash/equity bridge without counting the outlays twice.
  4. A freely exercisable investment right permits paying 80 at date 1 for an asset then worth 130 in the high state or 50 in the low state. These date-1 asset values include all later asset payoffs. Today, prices of claims paying one only in the high or low state are 3/5 and 3/10 respectively; the clone and funding assumptions required for using these prices hold. The right is already owned and has no exercise fee or other effect. Find its value, the value of a contract forcing investment in both states and the value of discretion. Derive the one-period safe discount factor. A manager instead uses physical high probability 0.2 and that safe factor to value the option; calculate the shortcut and explain why it differs.
  5. A buyer has common equity value 360 and 18 thousand shares. A target's common equity is worth 120. A stock-only merger creates net surplus 40 after every cost and financing effect; no separate claims remain to adjust. The buyer issues six thousand equal-rights common shares to target owners. Find the postmerger price, both groups' gains and the largest issuance consistent with buyer participation. Separately, a venture investor contributes 500 at a negotiated pre-money headline value of 2,000 and receives a 1× nonparticipating preferred claim, convertible into 20% of common equity. There are no other claims, dividends, option pools or preferences. Find investor/common-founder cash at exits with distributable equity cash of 1,000 and 3,000. Explain why the headline ownership fraction alone is insufficient at the low exit.
  6. Immediate liquidation yields net collateral of 50 and an unencumbered cash pool of 40. Secured debt face is 70; its deficiency shares that pool pro rata with unsecured bonds of face 60. A consensual continuation instead needs ten of new cash immediately, all spent on restart, and yields certain distributable cash of 110 at date 1. New money receives a senior 10.5 then; the proposed old-claim waterfall pays old secured debt next, up to face 70, with remaining cash to old bonds. The applicable certain-payment discount rate is 5%; every old class must agree voluntarily, with immediate liquidation as its outside option. Compare liquidation recoveries with continuation PVs. Does positive aggregate surplus suffice? Exhibit a changed allocation of the same date-1 cash that satisfies both old classes, without reducing new-money repayment.
Retention solutions and ten-point analytic scoring
  1. EBIT = 36 − 20 = 16; tax = four, NOPAT = 12 and annual operating cash before working-capital recovery = 12 + 20 = 32. Date 0 uses 40 + 6 + 4 = 50; Year 1 pays 32 and Year 2 pays 32 + 6 = 38. NPV = −50 + 32/1.20 + 38/(1.20)² = 55/18 ≈ 3.056. The sunk three is common to both current decisions and excluded; the four is an incremental foregone after-tax sale receipt. The exclusive feasible alternative with NPV five is preferred under the stated objective, despite this project's positive NPV. Credit: EBIT/tax and cash two; full dated investment/recovery and opportunity cost three; PV/NPV three; sunk cost and mutually exclusive choice two.
  2. Reinvestment fraction is 0.03/0.12 = 1/4, so reinvestment is six and FCFF 18. Operating value is 18/(0.09 − 0.03) = 300. Common equity = 300 + 12 + 8 − 80 − 10 = 230 kCU; price = 23 CU. At ROIC 6%, half of NOPAT must be reinvested: FCFF is 12, operating value 200, common equity 130 and price 13. The 100 reduction comes from the greater capital required to sustain the same growth, not from changing the given first-year profit. Both cases satisfy discount rate above growth; neither justifies a perpetuity without its stable operating assumptions. Credit: first reinvestment/FCFF two; operating value two; claim/share bridge two; revised value two; economic explanation and assumptions two.
  3. Annual interest is three and each tax shield is 0.75. Shield PV = 0.75/1.05 + 0.75/(1.05)² = 205/147 ≈ 1.395. After investment and the fee, cash is 16; the maximum dividend is six. Before payout, old equity is 160 + 205/147 + 16 − 60 = 17257/147 ≈ 117.395. After payout it is 160 + 205/147 + 10 − 60 = 16375/147 ≈ 111.395; adding the six cash received leaves the same 117.395 wealth. Equivalently start with cash 24 and deduct the seven and one once. The loan's face equals value only because safe par debt is stipulated; the certain shield rate follows the supplied usable deductions. Credit: shield amounts/PV three; funded dividend two; before/after equity and wealth equality three; claim/timing assumptions and no double deduction two.
  4. The discretionary payoff is (50,0), worth (3/5)50 = 30. Forced investment pays (50,−30), worth 30 − nine = 21, so discretion adds nine. A certain unit costs 3/5 + 3/10 = 9/10; the safe gross return is 10/9. The physical-probability shortcut gives (9/10)[0.2×50 + 0.8×0] = nine, not 30. Normalized pricing weights are 2/3 and 1/3; physical frequencies need not equal them. The state prices already include discounting, and the exercise cost is paid inside each date-1 net payoff. Credit: feasible payoff and value three; forced/discretion comparison two; safe factor one; shortcut two; pricing/timing explanation two.
  5. Combined common value is 360 + 120 + 40 = 520. With 24 thousand shares, price is 520/24 = 65/3 CU. Old buyer owners receive 18×65/3 = 390, a gain of 30; target owners receive 130, a gain of ten. For x thousand new shares, 18×520/(18+x) ≥ 360 implies x ≤ eight. The venture's negotiated conversion fraction is 500/2500 = 20%, but its preference is a contractual alternative. At exit 1,000, the investor chooses 500 rather than conversion value 200; founders receive 500. At exit 3,000, the investor converts for 600 and founders receive 2,400. The preference protects a claim before the remainder is divided; a post-money headline is not a guarantee of proportional cash at every exit. Credit: merger price and both gains four; participation ceiling two; both venture allocations three; contractual distinction one.
  6. Secured deficiency is 20; the general pool faces eligible claims 20 + 60 = 80, so recovery rate is one half. Secured liquidation recovery is 50 + ten = 60; bonds receive 30. Continuation pays new money 10.5, leaving 99.5 for old creditors: proposed secured 70 and bonds 29.5. Their PVs are 200/3 ≈ 66.667 and 590/21 ≈ 28.095. Old total PV is 1990/21 ≈ 94.762, exceeding 90 by 100/21 ≈ 4.762, yet bonds lose 40/21 ≈ 1.905 and can refuse. A repair pays secured creditors 68 and bonds 31.5: PVs are 1360/21 ≈ 64.762 and 30. Both meet their outside options; 10.5 + 68 + 31.5 = 110. More generally the secured final allocation can lie from 63 to 68, with bonds receiving 99.5 minus it. This establishes value-based participation, not actual signatures or an unrestricted legal power to rewrite claims. Credit: liquidation allocation three; continuation and total PV two; losing-class diagnosis two; valid financed repair two; consent limitation one.

Notation index

Corporate-finance notation requires dates, currency, claim boundaries and a declared model
SymbolMeaning and consistency rule
PV, NPV; I0, CFtPresent value, incremental net present value, initial investment and dated cash flow. Compare mutually exclusive feasible choices on a common basis; sunk costs and foregone opportunities are different.
IRR, MOICA cash-flow discount-rate root and invested-money multiple. Specify dated investor cash flows and possible root ambiguity; neither alone adjusts for risk, scale or funding timing.
EBIT, EBITDA, NOPATOperating earnings before interest and tax; before depreciation/amortization as well; operating profit after the stated operating tax. EBITDA omits investment, working capital and cash taxes.
FCFF, FCFE; ΔNWC, Capex, DepCash flow before financing and to common equity; change in operating working capital, capital expenditure and depreciation. Define what the working-capital balance excludes, match debt flows and do not deduct interest twice.
rU, rE, rD, rf; βRequired operating-asset, equity, debt and safe returns; exposure to a specified market factor. Levered equity beta is not automatically a project's asset beta; yields on promised debt cash need not equal expected returns.
WACC; APV; τWeighted average cost of capital; adjusted present value; supplied tax rate. Use compatible market weights, leverage policy, usable deductions and risk assumptions. Separate financing effects under APV without charging them again in operating cash.
IC, ROIC; g, b; TVTInvested capital, return on matched capital, growth, reinvestment fraction and terminal value at date T. In the specified stable model, g = b×ROIC and TV uses the first cash flow after T; constant perpetuity discounting requires r > g.
Vop, EV; E, D, COperating value or a precisely defined enterprise-value measure; common equity, debt and cash. Identify nonoperating assets, leases, preferred claims and minority interests before applying any bridge.
N, x; P; SOld shares, new shares, common price and net transaction surplus. Postissue value and rights determine allocation; the old quotation does not automatically price a materially changed company.
ps, qs, πsPhysical probabilities, normalized market pricing weights and date-0 prices of state-specific dated units. State prices include discounting; physical probabilities require a separate risk/decision criterion.
Recovery, deficiency, priorityActual claim payment, unpaid secured claim eligible for the general pool under the specified contract, and payment order. Use net collateral and avoid counting the deficiency as a second full claim.
DPI, RVPI, TVPI; carryDistributions, residual value and total value relative to stated paid-in capital; performance allocation under a particular fund waterfall. Investor-net calls, fees, accrued carry, dates and valuation scope matter.

Volume synthesis and next steps

Corporate finance combines an operating decision with a set of claims and a financing calendar. Begin with the feasible operating counterfactuals and incremental cash flows. Value those flows with assumptions that match their risks, currencies and dates. Add the financing effects once, then distribute the resulting value according to actual rights. Finally check whether the cash and consents needed to implement the plan arrive when required. A correct model can support rejection, delay, renegotiation or a conditional approval as well as immediate investment.

Four independent questions help expose an incomplete analysis: What creates total surplus? Who receives it? Who supplies the cash? What observation or contract change reverses the decision? A high return percentage, rising EPS, a favorable comparable or a positive expected terminal balance answers only part of that sequence. The investment-committee project requires the whole argument and a record another analyst can reproduce.
