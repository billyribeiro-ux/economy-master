Cumulative examination — Form A

100 points; 240 minutes; closed book; nonprogrammable calculator permitted. Answer all five questions. Each question is an essential domain worth 20 points; each of its four parts is worth 5 points. Show derivations, dated cash and the conditions behind your conclusions. Exact fractions or adequately precise intermediate calculations are acceptable. The progression standard is at least 85/100 and at least 15/20 in every domain, followed by the separate transfer and teach-back requirements and resolution of any critical error.

All records, opportunities and contracts are original synthetic cases. Questions are independent; separate subcases within a question are identified explicitly. CU means currency units. A decimal return of 0.01 means 1%; a difference of 0.01 is one percentage point. Cash flows occur at the stated dates. There are no omitted taxes, fees, distributions or financing rights. Use the supplied benchmark and original target rather than replacing them after observing results. A model implication is not evidence that its inputs describe an actual market.

Question 4 — Liability hedges, dynamic objectives and a private call (D4, 20 points)

Parts (a–b), a liability-allocation case. At date 0 there is no investment decision or cash flow. A public opportunity state s is revealed at date 1, before investment. The states H and L have equal probabilities. In either state liquid wealth at date 1 is 100 CU. An investor then places x CU in a risky asset and 100 − x in a safe asset; 0 ≤ x ≤ 100, no borrowing or shorting, no costs, and safe gross return one to date 2. The risky simple return is μs + 0.20U, with μH = 0.04 and μL = −0.02. Independently of s, U is ±1 with probability one half, revealed only at date 2. A date-2 liability is 30 + 10U CU. Surplus S is terminal investment wealth minus that liability.

For part (a), each date-1 decision explicitly maximizes E[S|s] − (0.10/2)Var(S|s), with the coefficient in inverse CU. For part (b), evaluate the resulting contingent policy from date 0 using the different criterion E[S] − (0.10/2)Var(S) over all four joint states. The manager claims that averaging the two optimized conditional scores equals this date-0 score and proves optimality for that initial criterion. You must audit that claim rather than assume it.

Parts (c–d), a separate limited private contract. A prospective investor may accept one indivisible, nontransferable commitment. At annual date 0 it pays 40 CU. At date 1 it must pay a further call of 20 before receiving a distribution of 8 later that day. At date 2 it receives a final distribution of 61.60; the claim then ends with zero residual value. All these amounts are stipulated enforceable net investor cash flows after all fund fees. There is no tax, secondary sale, early redemption, borrowing or ability to pledge the later distribution. A separate safe investment earns 5% per year, including on cash reserves; no interest accrues between the date-1 call and its later same-day distribution. There are no other income or spending flows. This is a limited contractual opportunity, not a competitive-equilibrium pricing assertion.

  1. Express surplus in each opportunity state, derive its conditional mean and variance, and solve both constrained date-1 allocation problems. Separate the speculative and liability-hedge contributions to the unconstrained holding and verify feasibility.
  2. For the policy from part (a), calculate the two conditional surplus means, average conditional variance, variance of conditional means and total variance. Find its date-0 score and the average of the two optimized conditional scores. Evaluate the manager's equality and optimality claims using the law of total variance; you are not asked to solve the full date-0 commitment problem.
  3. For the separate private commitment, give the annual net investor cash sequence and solve for its economically admissible IRR. Compare its cash flows with the 5% benchmark by computing terminal-date excess wealth and date-0 NPV. Explain why a positive comparison does not by itself establish that this investor can fund the contract.
  4. An investor has only 50 CU liquid at date 0 and no other resources. Test the contract at every relevant date up to the first possible failure, retaining the gross call's timing. Find the minimum date-0 wealth that can fund this indivisible contract by keeping all money left after the initial 40 in the safe reserve. State the admissible decision with only 50 and explain why netting the later distribution against the call would be invalid.

Question 5 — Actual execution, hard limits and mandate authority (D5, 20 points)

An account initially owns 200 shares of E and 1,000 CU cash. There is no debt, cash earns zero interest, and no distributions occur during execution. At decision time 0, E's midpoint is 10. The approved original instruction is to sell 80 shares. The fixed paper benchmark sells all 80 at that decision price, incurs no implementation costs and thereafter holds its resulting shares and cash; do not reset it to actual fills.

The order arrives at time 1 when the quoted bid/ask are 9.80/10.00. Exactly 50 shares sell at 9.80, with a fee of 0.02 per executed share. The other 30 remain unfilled through the time-2 evaluation, when midpoint is 9.60. There are no other orders or fees. Use the arrival midpoint for spreads; use the decision price and original 80-share target for whole-target implementation shortfall. For its decomposition, assign decision-to-arrival delay to all 80 shares.

At time 2, prospective risk is measured over one new common period. E's simple-return variance is 0.01; safe cash is deterministic. Both actual and fixed benchmark holdings face the same E return. The risk budget limits the variance of the CU difference between their next-period wealths to 400 CU², and accessible current cash must be at least 1,600 CU. The previous implementation loss is retained as a fixed wealth difference, not discarded by renormalizing both accounts to one.

For part (c), the committee explicitly authorizes a temporary exception to full target completion until its next review. The desk may sell an integer n from 0 through 30 additional shares to restore the two hard limits. The available execution price for every such share is 9.50 with the same 0.02 fee; that depth is guaranteed for this stipulated repair window. The valuation midpoint remains 9.60 throughout the repair. No new money, purchases or borrowing are allowed. Choose the smallest n that meets both hard limits. In part (d), the committee withdraws that temporary completion exception and requires the entire original remaining target to be sold at those same repair terms.

  1. For the original 50-share fill, calculate the signed effective spread, realized spread at time 2 and midpoint component, all in CU per share. Calculate whole-target delay, execution, opportunity and fee components and total shortfall. Explain why a negative realized spread is compatible with a positive whole-target shortfall.
  2. Reconcile actual and paper cash, shares and wealth at time 2. Derive the variance of their next-period CU wealth difference from their holdings and the supplied return variance. Identify each hard-limit breach and explain why the fixed prior loss affects the mean difference but not this prospective variance.
  3. Derive the cash and active-risk constraints as functions of n. Find the smallest permitted integer repair, prove that a smaller integer fails, and report resulting cash, shares, wealth and active variance. State how many shares of the original instruction remain unfilled under this temporary exception.
  4. After the completion exception is withdrawn, compute the full remaining-target sale's cash, shares, wealth, total shortfall versus the unchanged paper benchmark and prospective active variance. Give a concise execution/governance report that distinguishes this outcome from the temporary repair and identifies which authority may change the target or benchmark prospectively.

Form A solutions

Each mapped answer is worth 5 points. Award method credit when a carried earlier arithmetic error is used consistently; do not penalize that same error again. A new timing, sign, unit, financing, information or mandate error is a new error. Correct numbers obtained by violating stated rights do not earn the corresponding reasoning credit.   1. After the contribution, wealth is 110 + 90 = 200. Gross ending wealth is 220 and net ending wealth is 218. The first return is 10%, and the second net return is 218/200 − 1 = 9%. Cumulative net TWR is 1.10×1.09 − 1 = 19.9%. Net CU gain is 218 − 100 − 90 = 28. The ratio 28/100 includes gain earned on the later 90 while using only the original capital denominator; it does not geometrically link the two portfolio returns. The fee belongs in the second-period investment return.

    Credit: Flow and fee timing/final wealth (1); two subperiod returns (1); TWR (1); CU gain (1); denominator explanation (1).

  2. The means are μA = 0.12 and μB = 0.06. Centered A values are (0.20,0.20,−0.20,−0.20); centered B values are (0.20,0,0,−0.20). Probability-weighted products give

    Σ = [[0.04,0.02],[0.02,0.02]].

    At equal weights, variance is (1/4)0.04 + (1/4)0.02 + 2(1/2)(1/2)0.02 = 0.025. The probabilities describe the whole population, so divide the four equally weighted state contributions by four. The n − 1 correction concerns an estimator from observations when the mean has been estimated; using it here would inflate the specified population moments. The determinant is 0.0004 > 0, consistent with strict positive definiteness.

    Credit: means (1); covariance matrix from centered states (2); equal-weight variance (1); population/sample distinction (1).

  3. Expected return is 0.06 + 0.06w. Expanding the variance gives 0.04w² + 0.04w(1 − w) + 0.02(1 − w)² = 0.02 + 0.02w². Hence J = 0.02 + 0.06w − 0.04w², J′ = 0.06 − 0.08w, and J″ = −0.08. The unrestricted maximum is w = 0.75. On [0,0.60] the derivative is positive, so the unique constrained maximum is w = 0.60. Its mean is 0.096, variance 0.0272, and score 0.0416. Strict concavity plus the one-sided derivative proves global optimality.

    Credit: mean/variance objective (1); derivative and unrestricted point (1); constraint and global certificate (1); mean/variance at optimum (1); score (1).

  4. For w > 0, the score increases with A's mean, so the worst permitted mean is 0.10; at w = 0 that mean is irrelevant. Worst-case J becomes 0.02 + 0.04w − 0.04w². Its derivative is 0.04 − 0.08w and its second derivative is −0.08, giving feasible global maximum w = 0.50. Worst-case mean is 0.08, variance 0.025, and score 0.03. Question 2 solutions

  1. Write e = μ − rf1. The FOC is e − γhΣxh = 0, with unique optimum xh = Σ−1e/γh because −γhΣ is negative definite. Wealth-weighted clearing gives wM = [(1/2)/2 + (1/2)/6]Σ−1e = (1/3)Σ−1e. Therefore e = 3ΣwM, so each expected excess return equals three times its covariance with the market. The market premium is 3×0.04 = 0.12, and total expected market return is 0.14. Risky demand is proportional to 1/γ, so aggregation averages wealth-weighted risk tolerance and then inverts it; the arithmetic average γ = 4 is inappropriate.

    Credit: FOC and sufficiency (1); wealth-weighted clearing (1); coefficient 3 (1); market premium/return (1); risk-tolerance explanation (1).

  2. Model beta is 0.06/0.04 = 1.5; the required mean is 0.02 + 1.5(0.12) = 0.20.
