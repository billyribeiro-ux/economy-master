Volume 15 assessment — economic fields, institutions, history and policy

Defend a mechanism, establish what the evidence supports, and show how the proposed decision can be implemented.

This package contains an eight-part prerequisite diagnostic, two distinct written examinations, a reproducible policy laboratory, oral transfer and delayed retention. All numerical economies, signals and input data are original synthetic teaching cases. They are conditional models, not current empirical forecasts or legal recommendations. Preserve original attempts and corrections. Reading solutions is preparation; independent work and human evaluation are needed to judge a learner’s competence.

Prerequisite diagnostic

Before the volume, allow75minutes with paper and a basic calculator, without notes or code. The proposed duration is uncalibrated. Eight tasks carry5points each: readiness requires34/40, at least4on questions1,4,7, and correction of every feasibility, identification or transfer-accounting error. The poverty and inequality definitions needed here are supplied in the prompts.

  1. Trace a tax wedge. A competitive market has inverse demand12−q and inverse supply2+q. A per-unit tax of two is imposed. Derive quantity, consumer and producer prices, receipts, both surpluses and deadweight loss relative to no tax. Assume quasilinear demand, a consumer endowment50 and cost q²/2+2q.
  2. Respect a binding constraint. Maximize6x−x² on0≤x≤2. Prove the global optimum, compute its value and the upper-bound multiplier, and explain why setting the derivative to zero fails.
  3. Condition on a signal. A high state has prior probability1/3. A positive signal has probability3/4 in that state and1/4 otherwise. Find its unconditional probability and the posterior high-state probability after it. State the required likelihood assumption.
  4. Separate an IV ratio from exclusion. A randomized offer changes treatment probability by2/5 and mean outcome by2. Under constant treatment effects and exclusion, identify the effect. Then allow a direct offer effect1/2 on everyone. Recompute the treatment effect and identify the failed assumption.
  5. Measure poverty explicitly. For three equally weighted incomes(1,3,6), poverty line4, define Pα as the average of[(4−y)/4]^α over incomes below4 and zero otherwise; for α=0 count poor people. Compute P0,P1,P2 and explain why they need not rank transfers alike.
  6. Bound missing outcomes. Eighty percent of an original assigned population is observed with mean outcome5. Every outcome, including missing ones, lies in the closed interval[0,10]. Bound the original population mean sharply. Explain why the observed mean alone is not the answer.
  7. Cancel transfers before claiming gains. A project generates gross benefit10 and uses real resources6. Separately, a transfer3 is paid to its beneficiary and funded by tax3 on another person. Calculate aggregate surplus with costless tax collection; then introduce collection resources equal to one fifth of the amount transferred, funded by an additional levy on the taxpayer. Assume that extra levy has no further collection cost. Give the beneficiary and taxpayer gains in each case.
  8. Keep personal inequality separate from units. For equal-weight incomes(1,3,5), calculate Gini G=Σi<j|yi−yj|/[nΣyi]. Recalculate after adding two to every income and after instead doubling every original income. Explain the different effects.
Complete answers and additive scoring

  1. Clearing requires12−q=(2+q)+2, so (4,8,6,8) gives quantity, buyer price, seller price and receipts. Consumer surplus is q²/2=8, producer surplus is also8; total including receipts is24. Without tax q=5 and total surplus25, so (8,8,24,1) lists the surpluses,total and loss. Consumer expenditure32 is feasible, producer receipts24 pay real cost16, and government receives8. The wedge changes quantity; transfers do not themselves destroy eight goods.

    Rubric (5 points). Clearing1; prices1; receipts/accounts1; surpluses1; loss/counterfactual1. Study route: v05-c04.

  2. The derivative6−2x is positive throughout[0,2], so the unique optimum is the upper endpoint. Strict concavity also verifies globality. (2,8,2) gives choice,value and upper multiplier. The unconstrained root3 is infeasible. For a nearby bound B<3, V(B)=6B−B² and V′(2)=2: the multiplier values a marginal relaxation of the actual bound.

    Rubric (5 points). Domain/global argument2; choice/value1; multiplier1; infeasible stationary point1. Study route: v02-c08.

  3. Joint high-and-positive probability is1/4; low-and-positive is1/6. Thus (5/12,3/5) are signal probability and posterior. These are stipulated likelihoods for the observed signal under the two exhaustive states. If selection changes who generates or preserves signals, the likelihood for the observed record may differ; Bayes arithmetic does not validate it.

    Rubric (5 points). Joint probabilities2; denominator1; posterior1; likelihood/selection scope1. Study route: v03-c05.

  4. With a nonzero first stage and exclusion, reduced form equals treatment effect times2/5, giving5. With direct effect1/2, the treatment component is3/2, so the effect is15/4. Hence (5,15/4). Random assignment can identify the offer effect while exclusion fails. Without the constant-effect or appropriate monotonicity interpretation, one cannot rename the ratio an unrestricted population average treatment effect.

    Rubric (5 points). Moment equation1; first ratio1; direct-effect subtraction1; corrected ratio1; exclusion/scope1. Study route: v12-c03.

  5. Normalized shortfalls are3/4,1/4,0. The three statistics are (2/3,1/3,5/24). The headcount changes only when a person reaches the line; the gap responds linearly below it; squared gaps place larger marginal weight on deeper shortfalls. The line, units and population weights must remain consistent across a comparison.

    Rubric (5 points). Shortfalls1; headcount1; gap1; square1; transfer distinction1. Study route: v05-c04.

  6. The full mean is(4/5)5+(1/5)μmissing. Therefore its sharp interval is (4,6). Assign every missing outcome0 or10 to attain the endpoints; constant intermediate missing outcomes fill the interval when outcomes can vary continuously over[0,10]. The observed mean identifies the original mean only under an additional restriction on missingness or the missing mean.

    Rubric (5 points). Weighted identity1; endpoints2; attainability1; selection assumption1. Study route: v12-c06.

  7. Without collection costs the beneficiary gets10−6+3=7 and the taxpayer loses3: aggregate4. With collection resources3/5, the beneficiary still gains7 while the taxpayer pays18/5, giving (7,-3,4,7,-18/5,17/5). The added levy funds a real cost; charging a tax is not by itself a cost equal to receipts. Sufficient initial cash for all payments and an enforceable transfer are maintained, rather than inferred from positive aggregate gains.

    Rubric (5 points). Initial incidence1; transfer cancellation1; collection resources1; revised incidence1; total/funding1. Study route: v06-c07.

  8. The three unordered gaps sum to8 and nΣy=27, so G=8/27. Adding two leaves gaps8 but raises the denominator to45. Doubling multiplies both numerator and denominator bytwo. Thus (8/27,8/45,8/27). A uniform absolute gain compresses relative inequality in this positive-income example; merely changing currency units does not.

    Rubric (5 points). Pair gaps1; original1; addition1; scaling1; interpretation1. Study route: v03-c02.

Administration and advancement

Form A asks you to construct economies and global solutions. Form B asks you to diagnose claims and reconstruct decisions in materially different environments. Each form has twenty five-point parts across five twenty-point domains, totaling100. Allow300minutes, using paper and a nonprogrammable calculator without notes, solutions or code unless a question expressly supplies a tool. Timing and equivalence have not been empirically calibrated.

Essential domains for both forms
DomainChaptersRequired reasoning
Public and labor economics1–2Incentives, labor responses, incidence and public finance
Markets and trade3,5Firm conduct, entry, international payments and welfare
Development and history4,7Poverty, institutions, archival evidence and causal restrictions
Politics and behavior6,8Collective rules, commitment, preferences and credible measurement
Environment, social services, space and policy9–12External costs, service incentives, location and implementable decisions

Advance after85/100overall and at least15/20in each domain, with every critical error repaired. Critical errors include an unfunded policy, failure to check a binding constraint or off-path incentive, treating a transfer as new resources, calling an unidentified effect causal, or hiding evidence contrary to the conclusion. Equivalent valid methods earn credit. Do not award the same reasoning point twice under an additive rubric. After remediation and a study interval, use the other form, complete a changed transfer task and explain the reasoning aloud.

The project is open resource and separately assesses reproducibility and decision argument. Run the five supplied reader files offline using standard-library Python; retain input provenance, all code, outputs and unsuccessful diagnostics. A good fit to synthetic data cannot establish a policy’s real effect. A human evaluator should inspect the independent implementation, literature and institutional reasoning. Internal score thresholds are instructional choices, not external credentials.
