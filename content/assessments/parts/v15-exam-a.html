Form A — construct a feasible policy economy

This original 100-point examination has twenty five-point parts in five domains. Allow 300 minutes as an uncalibrated design allowance. Work independently with paper, a basic calculator and the chapter notation; no outside datasets are needed. All economies are synthetic. Show global arguments, feasible choices and payer accounts. A stationary point or a balanced treasury alone is insufficient. Carry exact fractions where possible. Each part's additive rubric appears in the disclosed solutions.

Advancement requires at least85 overall and15 in each domain, correction of any critical error in feasibility, identification or double counting, and a later changed-primitive solution and teach-back. These thresholds are instructional criteria, not measured equivalence to an institutional examination. The chapter links identify the methods being assessed; the problems combine them in new configurations.

Domain 1 — public finance changes the return to working and learning

Use public economics and labor economics. A unit continuum of identical price-taking workers supplies n≥0, produces wn goods and has utility c−n²/2+5J, with J∈{0,1} a common project. Each ignores its effect on aggregate tax receipts. Firms have constant marginal product w and zero profit. A proportional earnings tax t∈[0,1] finances the project's real cost3 when J=1. No funds may be wasted; in Parts1–2 receipts must exactly equal3. A lump-sum rebate, when specified later, is independent of an individual's action. Primitive tuple (w,project cost,project benefit,training cost,trained wage)=(4,3,5,2,5). Future and current goods have equal present value; safe storage/loans return one. Sufficient other endowments make consumption nonnegative.

  1. Construct the worker's globally optimal labor supply for every tax rate. Find every exactly financing tax rate for J=1 and its labor supply. Establish that no financing root is missing.
  2. Choose globally among no project and the two exactly financing project policies using aggregate utility with equal weights. Reconcile output, effort disutility, real project cost and taxes. Compare with an enforceable lump-sum project bill3, and explain which implementability restriction makes the difference.
  3. Hold the lower financing tax rate fixed. Training before work costs2 real goods per person and raises w from4 to5; each worker can initially finance it. Any receipts above3 are rebated equally after work. Derive the private training surplus ignoring the individual's negligible rebate effect, the additional public receipts, and the total resource/disutility gain if everyone trains. Prove the trained labor choice globally and reconcile private and fiscal margins.
  4. Instead each worker has only1/2 available before training and can borrow at most1. A separate public reserve holds1/2 per worker, funded by taxpayers included in welfare. Loan repayments are enforceable from later earnings; lenders have enough goods and a zero-profit storage outside option. Construct the smallest grant that implements the profitable training choice. Give the training bill, repayment and reserve accounts, private gain and consolidated gain. Explain why a promise of later tax revenue alone would not fund the initial gap.

Domain 2 — a border separates two successive monopolists

Use industrial organization and trade. Home consumers have quasilinear gross benefit B(q)=10q−q²/2, q∈[0,10], and ample numeraire. A domestic retailer needs one imported component per final unit and has no other cost. A foreign manufacturer has constant real marginal cost2, no fixed cost, and first sets a uniform wholesale price v≥2. The retailer then chooses q; Home collects a specific component tariff z≥0 and rebates receipts lump sum. Foreign firms and households have ample resources; no borrowing constraint binds. Baseline ownership of the manufacturer is entirely foreign. The input tuple is (demand intercept,component cost)=(10,2). A zero-margin retailer accepts positive trade when indifferent to shutdown. Tariff policy is chosen before wholesale pricing; no other trade exists.

  1. Construct the subgame equilibrium under a uniform wholesale contract for all z≥0. Derive quantities, consumer and wholesale prices, both profits and tariff receipts on the positive-trade branch. Give the no-trade branch and all relevant boundary qualifications.
  2. Choose the globally optimal Home tariff under foreign ownership. Include consumer surplus, domestic retailer profit and revenue exactly once. Compare its Home and world surplus with free trade, showing why the objectives differ. World surplus counts the manufacturer's real input cost and every ownership group.
  3. Now require z=0 but permit a two-part contract: nonnegative wholesale price v and a nonnegative fixed franchise fee F. The manufacturer makes a take-it-or-leave-it offer; the retailer's outside profit is zero and it accepts at equality. Construct the manufacturer's globally optimal contract and quantity, with a proof over all feasible contracts. Reconcile Home consumer surplus, retailer profit, foreign profit and world surplus. Do not assume the fixed fee is a real production cost.
  4. Return to uniform wholesale pricing. Suppose Home households own fraction α∈[0,1] of the manufacturer's profit; ownership does not change its profit-maximizing choices and the shares are inherited, with no current acquisition payment. Derive the entire globally optimal tariff correspondence as α varies, including the boundary at which free trade first becomes optimal. Explain the ownership mechanism.

Tuple (entrepreneurs,wealth each,bank wealth,treasury wealth,machine cost,grid cost,grid output,no-grid output,pledge share)=(2,2,8,5,6,4,9,5,1/2).

  1. Determine machine adoption without a grid and with a freely provided grid. Then suppose the treasury demands a tax2 from each entrepreneur at date0 to cover the grid. Determine whether the intended adoption is feasible. Distinguish physical profitability, credit feasibility and the treasury's accounting.
  2. Move each tax2 to date1, payable only if the machine is installed. The treasury can spend its own4 on the grid and hold the remaining1 as a bank-payment reserve. Find the minimum public bank payment per entrepreneur needed to make a loan4 feasible.   3. Compare Part10 with universal storage and no grid. Compute each group's consumption change and the total resource gain.   4. A separate historical archive has100 people in each of two districts. Domain 4 — a majority must fund an incentive-compatible implementation

Use political economy and behavioral economics. Three voters A,B,C have linear utility over dated goods. At date0 each values all date1/2 net consumption by βᵢ, with βA=1/4, βB=1/2 and βC=1. At date1 each values date2 consumption by the same βᵢ. There is no additional discounting. A and B can each undertake a task costing3 goods at date1 and producing8 goods for that person at date2; A’s task additionally produces4 goods delivered to B at date2. These are real additional outputs, not a payment from A’s eight. C cannot undertake a task. Everyone has at least5 goods at each date before policy, so the stated bills and sanctions are payable. Voters understand their later choices.

A program can target the publicly known type A. It audits A's date1 action with probability q. An audit is accurate; if A failed it collects fine f∈[0,2], while if A succeeded it pays bonus b∈[0,2]. No audit means no payment. Date1 cost/bonus/fine all enter date1 consumption. The treasury commits to enforcement and payment; type B has no contract. Administration consumes6q real goods at date0. Expected bonus qb is funded in advance by a uniform tax (6q+qb)/3 on each voter, with an exact continuum of independent copies of each three-person community so the audit frequencies equal q. Fine receipts in a deviation are additional treasury consumption and never an action-dependent rebate to the fined person. For the equilibrium account nobody pays a fine. Treasury storage returns one. A chooses the task at an incentive tie; voters support a proposal at a utility tie, and a strict majority implements it. No program is the status quo. Inputs (betaA,betaB,cost,own benefit,spillover to B,maxfine,maxbonus,admin slope)=(1/4,1/2,3,8,4,2,2,6).

  1. Determine both agents' date1 task choices without a program. Derive A's exact incentive constraint under (q,f,b). Explain how A can prefer a credible commitment at date0 despite declining the unaided task later.
  2. For q=1/2,f=2,b=0, construct behavior, the initial tax, each voter's date0 utility change and the majority result. Compute the undiscounted equal-weight resource/disutility gain relative to the no-program status quo, including B's task already performed without policy. Explain why this criterion differs from adding the voters' date0 utilities.
  3. Among all contracts that induce A, choose one maximizing undiscounted aggregate resources, breaking ties by minimum initial tax. Derive a global lower bound on feasible q from the payment caps, construct the optimal fine/bonus/audit contract, and check its funding and majority support. Compare with Part14.
  4. Suppose an experiment reports only that A declines without a contract but complies under Part14. Construct a time-consistent alternative type with benefit8 and a different real task cost that produces both observations. Propose an additional voluntary date0 commitment choice that distinguishes that alternative from the stipulated present-biased type under clearly stated assumptions. Distinguish identifying a policy response from identifying a welfare criterion.

Domain 5 — pollution exposure, treatment and the housing response

Use environmental economics, health and social investment, spatial equilibrium and policy implementation. Six identical mobile workers each need one housing service. A worker in A produces12 goods; one in B produces10. If n live in A, 6−n live in B. Competitive housing-service costs are Cᵢ(N)=N²/2, with price-taking site owners receiving rents rᵢ=N. Workers do not own sites. A exposes workers to pollution; a verified health-protection program of size k∈[0,n] reduces aggregate harmful exposure to E=n−k, at real cost k²/2. Baseline aggregate health damage is E²/2, borne equally by all six workers in consumption-equivalent units. Each infinitesimal worker takes aggregate damage and lump-sum rebates as given. All agents have ample other numeraire.

A government may charge an A-residence levy τ per worker, purchase k under a verifiable fixed-quantity contract and rebate excess receipts equally. A provider can deliver any h≥0 at real cost h²/2, receives k²/2 only on delivery h≥k and zero otherwise, and at a zero-profit tie chooses k. This explicit tie convention supplies implementation at cost. Site rents, levies, rebates and provider receipts are transfers, not extra resource benefits. Inputs (workers,wA,wB,housing slope,health-cost slope,damage slope)=(6,12,10,1,1,1). No program means τ=k=0.

  1. Construct the no-program migration equilibrium. Then maximize aggregate output minus housing cost, health-protection cost and health damage over 0≤k≤n≤6. Prove the optimum globally using an explicit positive quadratic loss or strict concavity with feasible first-order conditions. Report the resource gain over no program.
  2. Construct a residence levy and health purchase implementing Part17. Show worker location indifference, provider global choice, receipts, health bill and per-worker rebate. Reconcile aggregate worker welfare and owner surplus with the planner value; include the equally shared damage and do not count rents twice.
  3. Health administration can deliver at most k=1. Construct the globally optimal constrained allocation and an implementing levy with a balanced fiscal account. Removing the capacity limit would cost2/5 additional real goods, financed from receipts. Decide whether to expand, comparing complete values rather than the unconstrained first-order condition alone.
  4. For the fixed constrained policy from Part19, allow actual damage to be θE²/2 with θ∈[1/2,3/2]; no-program and policy location choices stay as stipulated because individuals ignore aggregate damage. Add1 real good of implementation/monitoring cost, funded from the same levy. Derive the sharp net-gain interval against no program and the remaining rebate. Give a prespecified two-look monitoring rule with family false-alarm probability at most1/20 when each look supplies a valid marginal p-value; explain its assumptions and identify a real health/distribution outcome to track beyond program counts.
Form A — full solutions and additive scoring

Form A solutions

1. Two financing branches

Ignoring constants, utility is (1−t)wn−n²/2= [(1−t)w]²/2−[n−(1−t)w]²/2. Thus n=(1−t)w is the unique global maximum, including n=0 at t=1. Revenue is tw²(1−t). At w=4, exact funding requires16t(1−t)=3, or(t−1/4)(t−3/4)=0. Both roots lie in[0,1]; the quadratic has no others. The result is (tax low,labor low,tax high,labor high)=(1/4,3,3/4,1). Revenue rises and then falls; exact budget balance does not pick an efficient branch.

2 points for the completed-square labor proof including the endpoint; 2 for both financing policies; 1 for exhaustiveness and interpretation.

2. Compare feasible policies globally

Without the project, a zero tax gives n=4 and W=16−8=8. With J=1, consolidated welfare is W=4n−n²/2−3+5. At n=3 it is19/2; at n=1 it is11/2. Therefore (no project,low-tax project,high-tax project,lump-sum project)=(8,19/2,11/2,10). Comparing every feasible candidate selects the low tax, gaining3/2 over no project. Tax receipts are spent on the real project; do not subtract the tax from household consumption and again subtract its spending after already consolidating.

A nondistorting bill3 leaves the marginal labor reward4, so n=4 and W=8−3+5=10. The extra1/2 equals the labor distortion at n=3. Its superiority is conditional on the enforceable lump-sum instrument being available. The original policy class allowed only proportional taxation; an infeasible instrument cannot be silently substituted into that choice problem.

2 points for all welfare values and the global ranking; 1 for the consolidated account; 2 for the lump-sum allocation and instrument qualification.

3. Training creates a fiscal margin

At t=1/4, trained after-tax marginal earnings are15/4. Completing the same square gives unique n=15/4, with private labor value225/32 instead of9/2=144/32. The gross private gain is81/32; subtracting the real training bill2 yields17/32, so every unconstrained worker trains even before any additional rebate. Gross output is75/4; receipts75/16 exceed the old3 by27/16.

The trained resource/disutility surplus before project terms is75/4−(15/4)²/2−2=311/32, compared with old12−9/2=15/2=240/32. The gain is71/32, exactly17/32+27/16. Hence (trained labor,private gain,extra receipts,consolidated gain)=(15/4,17/32,27/16,71/32). Rebate payments distribute the public margin; they do not create it a second time. The private return omits receipts induced by the worker's productivity, illustrating an external fiscal margin within this particular tax system. It is not a measured return to schooling.

1 point for global trained labor; 2 for the private training decision; 2 for receipts and the exact consolidated reconciliation.

4. Finance at the date of the bill

Available private funds are1/2+1=3/2, short of2 by1/2. Any smaller grant leaves training infeasible; the reserve's full1/2 suffices. At date0 own wealth1/2, bank loan1 and grant1/2 pay the real training bill2. The bank receives1 from later earnings, exactly its storage return. The reserve is depleted by1/2; later incremental payroll receipts are27/16, leaving the public sector's combined present-value gain19/16 relative to its initial stored reserve and old receipts.

Private out-of-pocket cost in present value is3/2, so its gain is81/32−3/2=33/32, excluding the rebate. Consolidation gives33/32+19/16=71/32, unchanged from Part3: (minimum grant,private gain,public net gain,total gain)=(1/2,33/32,19/16,71/32). Counting the later rebate moves the public gain to recipients but leaves this sum intact. Initial wealth and the loan repayment are already incorporated in the out-of-pocket cost. Future receipts can support initial borrowing only if an enforceable financing arrangement and actual lender resources exist; the prompt instead supplies an explicit initial reserve.

1 point for the minimal grant; 2 for dated bills, repayment and reserve; 2 for both gains and the financing distinction.

5. Solve both stages and shutdown

The retailer maximizes (10−v−z)q−q² on[0,10]. Its global choice is q=max{(10−v−z)/2,0}; the upper bound never binds when v,z≥0. If0≤z<8, let A=8−z>0 and x=v−2≥0. Manufacturer profit is x(A−x)/2 on[0,A], zero at or above the shutdown threshold. Completing the square gives A²/8−(x−A/2)²/2. Therefore v=6−z/2, q=(8−z)/4, consumer price p=8+z/4, retailer profit q², manufacturer profit2q² and receipts zq.

At z≥8, any v≥2 yields q=0 and profit zero for both firms; wholesale price is nonunique and a transaction price for absent retail trade is not observed. At z=8 the positive-branch formulas limit to v=2,q=0, but this is only one supporting wholesale choice. At free trade (v,q,p,retailer profit,manufacturer profit,revenue)=(6,2,8,4,8,0). Home demand expenditure equals wholesale receipts plus tariff and retailer profit, and foreign receipts pay real cost2q plus manufacturer profit.

2 points for both global stage optimizations; 2 for the positive-trade accounts; 1 for the full no-trade and boundary qualification.

6. National incentives need not maximize world resources

For positive trade, Home welfare is q²/2+q²+zq. Substitute z=8−4q, with0<q≤2, obtaining H(q)=8q−5q²/2=32/5−(5/2)(q−8/5)². Its global maximum is q=8/5,z=8/5; no-trade policies give zero and cannot improve it. The associated prices are v=26/5,p=42/5. Home welfare rises from6 to32/5, a gain2/5.

World welfare is B(q)−2q=8q−q²/2, since wholesale prices, tariff, franchise rents and retail revenue are transfers. It is14 at free trade and288/25 under the optimal Home tariff, falling62/25. At the latter allocation consumer surplus is32/25, retailer profit64/25, tariff revenue64/25 and foreign profit128/25; these sum288/25. Thus (tariff,q,Home welfare,Home gain,world welfare,world loss)=(8/5,8/5,32/5,2/5,288/25,62/25). The manufacturer's lower price transfers some surplus to Home while reduced trade destroys world surplus. A national gain is not a world Pareto improvement.

2 points for the global tariff certificate; 1 for national incidence; 2 for complete world consolidation and the differing objectives.

7. Contract design removes one markup

At z=0 the retailer's operating profit is q² with q=max{(10−v)/2,0}. Participation allows F≤q². The manufacturer chooses the maximal fee, leaving zero retailer profit. Its combined receipts net of real costs then equal total channel profit (10−q−2)q=8q−q²=16−(q−4)². Since v≥0 allows0≤q≤5, q=4 is feasible and uniquely maximizes this bound. It is attained by v=2,F=16; thus the bound is global over all contracts, including shutdown. The fixed fee is affordable by assumption, and the acceptance tie is explicitly settled.

Consumers pay24; the retailer remits wholesale cost8 and fee16. Manufacturer receipts24 less production cost8 give16. Home consumer surplus is8, retailer profit zero and world surplus24: (v,F,q,price,Home welfare,foreign profit,world welfare)=(2,16,4,6,8,16,24). The wholesale marginal price now equals real cost, while the fixed fee extracts the downstream rent without distorting its marginal choice. This removes double marginalization; it does not remove the remaining integrated monopoly markup or attain the competitive q=8.

2 points for the feasible contract and global channel-profit bound; 1 for participation/timing; 2 for payment and welfare accounts including the remaining markup.

8. Ownership changes the national objective

Add α times manufacturer profit2q² to the old Home objective. Then Hα(q)=8q−(5/2−2α)q², q∈[0,2]. Its second derivative−5+4α is strictly negative throughoutα∈[0,1]. The unconstrained vertex is8/(5−4α). It lies at or below2 iffα≤1/4. Therefore for0≤α<1/4, q*=8/(5−4α) and z*=8−32/(5−4α); for1/4≤α≤1, q*=2,z*=0. Atα=1/4 the vertex is exactly the boundary and strict concavity still makes the quantity unique. Every no-trade policy yields zero, so none adds another optimum.

Selected checks are (tariff at alpha0,at alpha1/8,at alpha1/4,at alpha1)=(8/5,8/9,0,0). Home internalizes more of the foreign profit reduction as ownership rises. This affects the government's objective, not firm conduct. The result relies on inherited shares, complete inclusion of their profit income and no new acquisition or enforcement cost; it is not a claim that nominal corporate nationality alone determines incidence.

2 points for the ownership-adjusted objective and global curvature argument; 2 for both regimes and threshold; 1 for the ownership mechanism and accounting scope.

9. Profitable equipment can remain unavailable

Without the grid a machine converts6 into5, losing one real good. It also cannot raise the required loan4: pledgeable output is5/2, below4. Both physical return and financing reject adoption. With a freely supplied grid, output9 exceeds cost6, and pledgeable output9/2 covers loan4. Each uses own2 plus borrowed4, repays4, consumes5, and gains3 relative to storing2. Banks lend all8 and recover8.

A date0 tax2 exhausts each entrepreneur's wealth. The loan required becomes6, but pledgeable output is only9/2. The financing gap is3/2 per person, despite the treasury collecting the grid's4. The checks are (no-grid loan capacity,free-grid loan capacity,required loan before tax,required after early tax,post-tax gap)=(5/2,9/2,4,6,3/2). The grid still makes the technique physically productive. Tax timing has removed the private down payment, so the announced grid-plus-adoption package is not feasible merely because its public budget balances.

2 points for both baseline adoption comparisons; 2 for the early-tax financing failure; 1 for separating physical, credit and treasury objects.

10. An actually funded public bank payment

After the senior tax, a machine leaves9−2=7, of which7/2 can be enforced for the bank. The loan needed is still4, so the smallest public payment is1/2 per entrepreneur. Any smaller payment fails the lender's unit-return participation condition. Date0 treasury spends4 on the grid and stores1; entrepreneurs contribute4 in total and banks8, financing machinery12. All initial goods17 are assigned:16 to real investments and1 to storage.

Date1 output18 and stored reserve1 provide19 goods. Each entrepreneur pays tax2 and private debt7/2, retaining7/2. Treasury receives4 tax, uses its stored1 to pay the two bank supplements and retains4. Banks receive7 private repayment plus1 public payment, recovering8. Thus (public payment each,entrepreneur consumption each,bank total,treasury consumption,total)=(1/2,7/2,8,4,19). The bank supplement is paid in the stipulated state. Calling it a guarantee does not justify omitting it as though the borrower would repay an unenforceable extra amount.

11. A gain with a losing fiscal owner

Without adoption or a grid, storing every initial good yields entrepreneurs2 each, banks8 and treasury5, totaling17. Under Part10 the entrepreneurs jointly gain3, banks gain zero and treasury loses1; total gain2. Directly, output18 less grid4 and machinery12 gives2 net additional goods. Hence (entrepreneur total gain,bank gain,treasury gain,total gain,weight threshold)=(3,0,-1,2,3). Weighted change is3−ω. The policy is strictly preferred for0≤ω<3, tied at3 and disfavored forω>3. Equal-weight efficiency does not imply unanimous gain.

The model stipulates output, feasible technology, enforcement, timing and all counterparties. An actual historical claim needs evidence that the grid altered those objects, preceded investment, and was not simply placed where growth or credit was already improving. Later adoption is compatible with the mechanism but does not by itself distinguish it from selection, contemporaneous reforms or changes in records. Neither the ethical weight nor the causal counterfactual is learned from a balanced account.

2 points for group/resource reconciliation; 1 for the full weighted ranking; 2 for chronology, selection and the model-to-history limit.

12. Bound the archive before interpreting a contrast

Treated mean lies between(40·12+60·0)/100=24/5 and(40·12+60·20)/100=84/5. Comparison mean lies between4 and14. With no joint restriction, the difference lies in[24/5−14,84/5−4]=[−46/5,64/5]. Thus (treated low,treated high,comparison low,comparison high,difference low,difference high)=(24/5,84/5,4,14,-46/5,64/5). For the lower endpoint give every missing treated person zero and every missing comparison person20; reverse these assignments for the upper endpoint. Continuous outcomes and mixtures of these completions fill the interval, so the bounds are sharp under the stated restrictions.

Even a full-population contrast is not a causal effect without a defensible untreated comparison: for example a justified assignment rule or comparable untreated trends, no anticipation and a declared treatment/interference definition. Date grid announcement, construction, actual connection, credit approval and machine purchase using contemporaneous records. A credit mechanism predicts changed financeability at connection; loans or machinery installed well before a credible announcement would weaken that timing explanation. Prefer actual loan terms and refused applications to only successful factory histories. These are restrictions on counterfactuals and missingness, not sampling confidence levels.

2 points for all mean/contrast bounds; 1 for sharp completions; 2 for the causal/chronology and discriminating-evidence distinction.

13. The action date matters

Without a contract A's date1 gain is−3+(1/4)8=−1, so A declines. B's is−3+(1/2)8=1, so B performs. Under the contract A's action gain is−3+qb+βA8, while inaction gives−qf. Thus the exact condition for action, using the declared tie rule, is q(f+b)≥1. Expected sanctions and bonuses enter at date1; multiplying them by βA again in this comparison would misdate them.

At date0, a costless enforceable commitment to A's task yields βA(8−3)=5/4 compared with zero. Both its effort bill and output are then future goods. At date1 the effort bill becomes immediate but the output remains discounted. Thus the same sophisticated person can want the earlier commitment and later decline the unaided action. This does not select a social welfare criterion. The checks are (A unaided gain,B unaided gain,required expected incentive,A date0 commitment value)=(-1,1,1,5/4).

1 point for baseline choices; 2 for the exact incentive inequality and timing; 2 for the date0 comparison and welfare distinction.

16. A response does not uniquely identify a preference

A time-consistent selfish type with β=1, own benefit8 and real task cost9 has unaided gain−1. Under Part14 it faces expected nonperformance fine1; performing gives−1 and not performing also gives−1, so the stated action tie reproduces compliance. The observed action pair therefore fits both models. The extra output delivered to B is external to either selfish type's private choice. The alternative's realized resource cost differs; one cannot use the original cost3 welfare calculation after replacing its preference/technology interpretation without measuring that cost.

Offer a voluntary, costless, binding date0 commitment to the task alone, with no tax, bonus or benefit to refusing, before the task date.
