Form B — Corporate finance reassessment

100 points; 240 minutes; closed book; nonprogrammable calculator permitted. Answer all five questions, allowing about 48 minutes each. Each question has exactly four parts worth 5 points each. Show dated cash, claim boundaries and reasoning; a correct unexplained number may not earn all available credit. Fractions and reasonably rounded decimals are acceptable. Keep unrounded intermediate values.

All cases and figures are original fictional teaching inputs. Money is kCU, share counts are thousands, and share prices and EPS are CU per share. Dates 0, 1 and 2 are one year apart. No current market observation or real legal/tax rule is asserted. Rights, tax use, financing availability and pricing models are exactly those stipulated. Different questions and expressly separate subcases are independent. There are no omitted claims, fees, cash flows or distributions. A quoted value or accounting profit is not automatically cash available to settle a payment.

The five question totals are domains D1–D5, each out of 20. The library's advancement rule is at least 85/100 overall and 15/20 in every domain, followed by the separate transfer and teach-back task. These are curriculum design thresholds, not externally calibrated examination standards. Remediate a critical claim, timing, funding or integrity error even if the aggregate score passes.

Question 1 — Bramble's capital policies and reverse valuation · 20 points · D1

Part (a) investment alternatives. Bramble owns an idle asset it can sell immediately for net cash 8 if it undertakes neither project. Either project consumes that asset. A study cost of 3 was irreversibly paid before date 0. Small requires additional cash 32 at date 0; Large requires 72. They are mutually exclusive, both have enough funding available, and neither has date-1 cash. Small receives 60 at date 2; Large receives 112. These receipts include every after-tax operating, working-capital and terminal consequence, with no remaining asset or claim. The appropriate common annual discount rate is 10%.

Part (b) separate annual business. Revenue is 220, cash operating costs are 136, depreciation 24, capex 30 and the increase in operating working capital 12. Cash interest is 10. Taxes are 25% of positive EBIT less interest and settle currently; unlevered tax is 25% of EBIT. At year-end new borrowing is 5 and principal repayment 18. There is no other cash flow or reserve change.

Parts (c)–(d) separate continuing operation. A proposed date-1 operating continuation value is 800. First continuing NOPAT, generated during year 2, is fixed at 60. From year 2 onward, new productive capital earns a maintained return R = 15%, the compatible operating discount rate is r = 10%, and growth g must satisfy 0 ≤ g < 10%. Old earnings are maintained; growth requires net reinvestment fraction g/R. Replacement spending is already allowed for. These stable assumptions continue indefinitely. The quote includes only cash after date 1.

  1. Find each project's relevant date-0 cost, NPV and annual IRR. Choose between them and rejection. Explain the treatment of the asset-sale opportunity and study cost, and why a ranking by IRR alone would give a different answer.
  2. Calculate FCFF and FCFE for the separate annual business, deriving FCFE once from net income and once from FCFF. Reconcile cash to shareholders plus net cash to lenders with the actual total cash available, including the tax effect.
  3. Infer the growth rate required by the 800 continuation quote. Calculate year-2 net reinvestment and FCFF, verify the valuation and check the admissible growth restrictions. Do not add year-2 cash separately to a continuation value that already includes it.
  4. Change only the maintained return R to 10%, retaining next NOPAT 60, required return 10% and quote 800. Can any admissible growth rate justify that quote? Derive the result, explain its investment economics and identify one other valuation input whose evidence would need reconsideration.

Form B solutions

Scoring rule. Each answer below has an explicit allocation totaling 5 points. Accept mathematically equivalent methods and exact fractions or rounding consistent with shown work. Carry a candidate's earlier arithmetic forward without penalizing it again when the later method and interpretation are correct. An inherited error does not earn credit for a new conceptual mistake, such as counting debt proceeds without the claim, ignoring a funding condition or paying mutually exclusive claims twice. Do not award unexplained conclusions credit allocated to reasoning. Open recommendations are graded on the stated constraints and evidence, not on preferred wording.
