Integrative project — a funded local opportunity program under disputed effects

Build and defend a finite policy laboratory connecting public finance, labor opportunities, housing and service capacity, environmental costs, distribution and causal identification. Every person, observation, technology and institutional rule below is synthetic. The exercise supplies a completely specified decision problem; it does not estimate an actual city's treatment effects or recommend a real program. Use Chapters1–12 to question the assumptions as well as solve the model.

Work individually first, then conduct an adversarial review. Suggested effort is10–14hours with open resources and a standard-library Python interpreter. Timing, the100-point rubric and the85-point advancement threshold are authored, uncalibrated study choices. Require at least15/20in every domain, repair any critical accounting or identification error, and complete an oral transfer before advancement. A high score is not institutional certification.

Population, choices and real resources

There are four atomless household groups, each of mass one: P1,P2,R1,R2, in that fixed priority order. Think of four continua of small households, not four strategic players. Their baseline real output accrues as income, with no omitted employer loss: incomes are(20,20,30,30). Every household owns its baseline output and consumes numeraire. It can apply for an optional service-linked work opportunity. If admitted, its additional real production is θ+vᵢ, credited entirely to its income; travel consumes one real good. Offsets v=(3,2,1,0) are assumed known, not estimated. These additional outputs do not reduce anyone else's production. There is no labor disutility, commuting congestion or external employer profit beyond the costs stated here.

Program participants need one capacity unit each. The authority chooses capacity k∈{0,1,2,3}, fee f∈{0,1,2}, subsidy s∈{0,1}, and abatement a∈{0,1}. For k=0only(f,s,a)=(0,0,0)is allowed; this is the outside policy. For positive k all combinations enter the candidate menu. One program capacity unit costs two real goods regardless of utilization; administration costs one per admitted participant. Abatement costs one real good if installed, and reduces each participant's emissions from one to one quarter. Idle capacity is not resold in this one-period comparison.

Participants pay f and receive s. Every infinitesimal household takes aggregate environmental harm and the common fiscal refund as fixed when deciding to apply. It applies iff θ+vᵢ−1−f+s≥0, accepting exact indifference by convention. Applicants are admitted in listed-group priority until k units fill; if fewer apply, capacity remains unused. All members of a group have identical primitives; integral capacities and unit group masses make whole-group allocations possible. A denied household cannot purchase another group's slot or change its priority. An admitted household cannot lose money relative to not applying at the same aggregate prices and transfers. Nevertheless, a nonparticipant can be worse off under the policy than under no program because it pays taxes and suffers pollution. Participation is not a Pareto test.

The groups' shares of total environmental damage are h=(1/2,1/4,1/8,1/8), summing to one. If m households participate, emissions E=m(1−3a/4) cause a real consumption loss dE, allocated hᵢdE. This is a stipulated linear loss in goods, not a health estimate or a monetary value assigned to an observed life. It occurs after program output is available. Weighted evaluation assigns weightsω=(2,2,1,1)to changes in group consumption; the separate unweighted resource calculation gives every good the same weight.

The compact primitive record is (income;offset;damage share;weight)=((20,20,30,30),(3,2,1,0),(1/2,1/4,1/8,1/8),(2,2,1,1)). Technology and policy bounds are (capacity cost,administration,travel,abatement cost,emissions,reduction,reserve limit)=(2,1,1,1,1,3/4,10). There is no endogenous housing rent, migration or electoral contest in this laboratory. Priority assignment and construction cost replace those mechanisms explicitly; the urban and political chapters explain why such omissions matter for transport.

Timing, treasury and feasibility

First the authority commits to a policy before learning θor d. It raises a reserve R=k(3+s)+a through an equal levy ℓ=R/4. It may collect at most10in total. This deliberately conservative reserve covers capacity, abatement, administration and subsidies if every slot fills; anticipated user fees do not relax the initial borrowing prohibition. Fees and subsidies are settled at admission before new output is received. Initial cash after the levy must be nonnegative and sufficient for a nonnegative net user charge max(f−s,0). The supplied incomes satisfy these liquidity restrictions for every policy within the reserve limit.

Second the state is revealed to households; their productivity response and applications occur, then capacity priority determines admission. The authority incurs real public cost C=2k+m+a and cash expenses C+sm, collects fm, and refunds the entire unused balance R+fm−C−sm equally across all four groups. Let b=(R+fm−C−sm)/4. The reserve formula ensures b≥0 because the residual is(1+s)(k−m)+fm. There is no retained treasury profit or unrecorded deficit. Output, travel and environmental loss are then realized. Final consumption must be nonnegative in every decision state.

With zᵢ∈{0,1}indicating admission, final consumption is cᵢ=yᵢ+zᵢ(θ+vᵢ−1−f+s)−ℓ+b−hᵢdE. The benchmark tests reserve, cash, capacity, treasury balance and final consumption separately. A syntactically valid but unfunded policy returns an infeasibility record; it is never treated as a zero-welfare policy. Uniform feasibility means the policy is feasible in every listed state and is required before expected or robust optimization. This uses one common comparison set for state frontiers and regrets.

For example k=3,s=1,a=1requires R=13even at a high fee. It is inadmissible under the reserve limit10; a fee receipt later in the period cannot repair prohibited initial borrowing. There are37candidate policies and31uniformly feasible ones. The six exclusions are precisely k=3,s=1with either abatement choice and any fee. Their reserves are12or13. The smallest final consumption over all feasible policy/state/group combinations is29/2.

Synthetic moments, heldout fit and decision states

A separate synthetic observational cohort supplies untreated and treated conditional means. At covariate x=0the means are8and12; at the heldout x=2they are10and14. A candidate conditional model is μ₀(x)=8+x and μ₁(x)=8+x+θ+B, where B is selection into treatment. Calibration uses only x=0; the x=2cell is held out until the prediction rule is fixed. Thus calibration identifies θ+B=4. The heldout means check extrapolation of the baseline relationship, but do not separately identifyθandB. These are supplied population moments with no sampling uncertainty; creating fictitious standard errors would add information the input does not contain.

Impose the sensitivity restriction B∈[1,3]. Then the sharp causal interval is θ∈[1,3]: every θin that interval is paired with B=4−θand matches all four means. No value outside it can both fit the treated difference and obey the bound. The choice to transport the sameθto the program groups and add their known offsets is a structural assumption. The observational cohort does not establish it.

For the finite decision exercise only, retain θ∈{1,2,3}and d∈{2,4}, independently. Assign effect weights(5/8,1/4,1/8)and damage weights(1/2,1/2). These subjective forecast weights and the finite support are supplied decision assumptions, not probabilities identified by the means. Robust optimization ignores their magnitudes but retains their support. It is robust over these six states only; claiming robustness over everyθ∈[1,3]would require checking application thresholds between the grid points. The state order is(T1D2,T1D4,T2D2,T2D4,T3D2,T3D4), with weights (5/16,5/16,1/8,1/8,1/16,1/16).

Three models(θ,B)=(1,3),(2,2),(3,1)fit training and heldout treated means exactly. A competing slope-two baseline8+2xalso fits training but predicts heldout untreated12and treated16, both too high by2. That failure rejects this particular extrapolation; passing the heldout check does not validate exclusion or remove selection. The four cell means are observed within the supplied synthetic evidence. Baseline linear extrapolation, constant selection acrossx, transport ofθto the program, productivity offsets, atomless behavior, priority and technology are model assumptions. The bound on selection and finiteθ/dsupport are sensitivity restrictions; forecast weights are additional decision inputs. Welfare weights and the choice of expected welfare versus regret express evaluative judgments. Forecast probabilities could in principle be informed by evidence, but these inputs do not estimate them. A perfect moment fit cannot determine who deserves a welfare weight of two or make a funding constraint disappear.

2. Choice, rationing and funding

Compute each net participation gain gᵢ=θ+vᵢ−1−f+s. Mark applications where gᵢ≥0. Visit the four groups in fixed order, admitting an applicant iff the current admitted count is belowk. This admits exactlymin(k,number of applicants)groups, all with nonnegative gain. If an alternative admitted set first differs at indexi, it either excludes an eligible priority group while admitting a later group or leaves a slot idle despite an applicant. Both violate the rule. Thus the set is unique, including equality because the rule explicitly accepts a zero gain. Whenk=0no one is admitted even though several would like to join.

For(3,2,0,0),θ=1gives gains(1,0,−1,−2); P1and P2apply and both enter, leaving one slot unused. Atθ=2the gains are(2,1,0,−1); R1also enters. These are genuine behavioral differences, not a welfare formula evaluated at a fixed participant count. Gross reserve is3(3)+0=9in both states. Atθ=1, real expenses are6+2=8, fees4, and total refund5; atθ=2expenses9, fees6, refund6. Hence (participants low,participants middle,reserve,refund low,refund middle)=(2,3,9,5,6).

There is one outside action plus3·3·2·2=36positive-capacity actions. The reserve is k(3+s)+a. Only k3s1violates10: reserves12or13for all three fees, so six fail and31remain. 5. Regret protects against a different error

Define rₚₛ=Mₛ−Wₚₛ≥0and Rₚ=maxₛrₚₛ. The unique minimizer is(2,0,1,1), with regrets (5/4,3,0,0,0,0)and worst regret3. The expected choice(1,0,1,1)has regrets(3/8,5/4,9/8,1/4,25/8,9/4), so its worst regret25/8exceeds3. No program has worst regret27/4. Thus (outside,expected choice,robust choice)=(27/4,25/8,3). The robust program has expected weighted change−1/8under the supplied probabilities. A regret criterion can select a negative-expectation action when that action avoids sufficiently large state-specific opportunities forgone.

Globality is elementary but conditional: the Cartesian policy constructor generates every allowed positive-capacity tuple exactly once plus the outside tuple. The funding and all-state feasibility filters implement the stated constraints. Every survivor's unique allocation and exact accounts are evaluated in every state. Taking finite maxima and then a minimum therefore examines the entire admissible domain. Report every equal minimizer before applying lexicographic selection; this instance has one. Exact fractions avoid numerical near-ties. No claim extends to continuous capacities, different fees, adaptive state-contingent policy or lotteries.

A lottery chosen before the state changes feasible payoffs to convex combinations, and worst regret becomes a piecewise-linear function of lottery probabilities. It can improve the deterministic value; the deterministic optimum does not solve that enlarged problem. Likewiseθvalues between the supplied grid points can cross participation thresholds and need separate analysis. Neither a finite robust result nor a heldout fit authorizes quietly enlarging its scope.

6. Exact forecast switches

For each policy, average the two damage-state payoffs atθ1to get Aₚ, and subtract that average from the correspondingθ3average to get Bₚ. Then forecast value is Aₚ+Bₚq. A policy is optimal exactly where(Aₚ−Aⱼ)+(Bₚ−Bⱼ)q≥0for every feasible competitorjand0≤q≤1. A positive slope difference yields a lower bound onq, a negative one an upper bound, and a zero slope requires a nonnegative intercept difference. Intersecting all these closed half-intervals gives the full support set, including singleton ties. This is a necessary-and-sufficient global certificate.

Only three policies have nonempty support intervals: no program has0on[0,13/64];(1,0,1,1)has−13/16+4qon[13/64,21/64]; and(2,0,1,1)has−17/8+8qon[21/64,1]. Neighboring policies tie at each shared endpoint; elsewhere the maximizer is unique. The breakpoint record is (first switch,second switch,baseline mean-equivalent q)=(13/64,21/64,1/4). All other28uniformly feasible policies have empty support intervals.

The baseline effect distribution has mean3/2, equal to the two-endpoint distribution atq1/4. This lies strictly inside the one-slot interval and reproduces its optimal choice. This agreement follows from the computed upper envelope and these candidates' affine effect payoffs, not from a blanket mean-sufficiency theorem. Other policies have participation thresholds and nonlinear payoff responses; the forecasts differ in their probability onθ2, and a regret calculation uses support rather than a mean. Preserve those distinctions when transporting this sensitivity result.

7. Reproduction and a conditional recommendation

The reference API is run(data): it returns strict JSON-compatible values without writing, printing or mutating data. Integers and rational strings preserve exact fractions. The reader CLI quietly writes its neighboring output, or accepts explicit input and output paths. It rejects direct, resolved, symlink and existing-hardlink aliases of input, code, README or manifest before writing. The manifest binds the other four downloadable payloads; it is not a signature or a guarantee of scientific correctness. Compare its hashes before running and use the output's complete rows to reconstruct a policy independently.

A complete audit demonstrates the actual isolated execution, verifies every protected byte after an attempted alias write, and shows an unfunded policy's reserve-limit diagnostic. Check total mass, positive normalized state weights, shares and nonnegative consumption; do not renormalize malformed inputs silently. The reference uses exact rational arithmetic, so exact fields have zero tolerance. If a learner uses floating point, require account and payoff residuals at most10⁻⁹and separate tie logic from arbitrary rounding; the exact benchmark resolves this instance's ties. Successful code execution alone is insufficient: a checker that imports the same payoff function as its sole oracle could repeat the same error.

An acceptable memo might select the one-slot policy under the stated expected weighted objective, while disclosing its expected resource loss and losses for nonpriority groups. A different memo may select no program under unweighted resources or two slots under deterministic minimax regret. Full credit requires matching the recommendation to the declared objective and correctly reporting all comparisons; it does not require a preferred political viewpoint. The two-slot policy needs reserve9, which is within10but would fail a tighter reserve cap8unless redesigned.

Possible omitted mechanisms include capacity rents and migration induced by a scaled program, or administrator discretion that changes access and leakage. The first needs credible supply/migration response evidence and a larger equilibrium model; the second needs verified eligibility, expenditure tracing and incentive analysis. A prespecified revision could suspend expansion if a randomized offer study, with spillover measurement, places the causal gain below the range making the selected program preferable after actual resource costs, or if verified financing cannot meet the initial reserve. A trial would need its own budget and assignment design; no free pilot is embedded here. Observational fit, a favorable weighted calculation and affordability within this toy treasury do not establish actual legal authority, administrative capacity or democratic legitimacy.
