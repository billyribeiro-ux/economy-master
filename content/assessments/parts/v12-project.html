Harbor Skills Outreach — replicate the numbers, audit the causal claim

Capability. Produce a reproducible empirical-style research package whose estimands, assignment law, regressions, uncertainty and conclusions agree. This open-resource project follows all twelve Volume 12 chapters. Allow roughly 12–18 hours for the synthetic core, an uncalibrated planning estimate; actual external-paper work depends on access and scope. Submit an independent implementation and initial identification memo before consulting the reference output or instructor guide.

Harbor Skills is a fictional invitation programme. Its draft research brief reports a positive treatment-receipt slope and concludes that training raises every worker's readiness. Your commission is to reproduce the brief, distinguish what random invitations identify from what receipt regressions describe, and explain how the conclusion changes when access weakens or invitations have a direct effect. All people, data and potential outcomes below are original synthetic teaching material. Reproducing them is a real computational exercise, but it is not replication of an actual empirical paper. A separate actual-paper audit is mandatory.

A fixed population and its actual assignment law

There are six centres j = 1,…,6, with two workers r = 1,2 each. Measure baseline readiness B before encouragement. Choose exactly three centres uniformly from the twenty possible sets. Both workers in a chosen centre receive Z = 1; other centres receive Z = 0. Course receipt D occurs next and follow-up readiness Y is measured afterwards. Set W = Y−B. These are ordered measurement occasions, not years. Readiness and change are measured in score points; Z and D are binary. The actual encouraged centres are {1,3,5}.

The population is fixed: there is no random sampling or superpopulation law. Each centre has assignment probability 1/2, but the six indicators are dependent because exactly three equal one. Twelve measured outcomes do not provide twelve independently randomized units. There are no spillovers between workers, within or across centres, in this stipulated model. Each outcome depends on that worker's receipt and own centre's invitation. Those restrictions are assumptions, not consequences of randomization.

The brief to reproduce and challenge

The fictional draft reports a change contrast of 11/3, first stage 5/6, sample Wald ratio 22/5 and receipt-OLS slope 118/35. Its primary statistic was specified as the change contrast before follow-up measurement. Post-only and baseline-adjusted specifications are disclosed alternatives. It makes four claims: (i) random invitations make course receipt random; (ii) twelve workers justify individual iid uncertainty; (iii) the Wald ratio is finite-sample unbiased and a sharp adjusted-outcome inversion is a generic confidence interval for a heterogeneous complier effect; and (iv) the invitation's randomization proves exclusion. Reproducing the numbers must not silently endorse these claims.

Define the finite encouragement effect θZ = (1/12)Σ[W(D(1),1)−W(D(0),0)], first stage π = (1/12)Σ[D(1)−D(0)], and baseline complier receipt effect θC = (1/6)ΣCτ. Distinguish these from the observed ratio TW/TD, the descriptive OLS coefficient of W on 1,D, and the coefficient of centre W on 1,Z,centred centre B. A change outcome does not itself establish observational parallel trends; this project's assignment law identifies θZ.

Complete instructor guide, derivations and extension answers

1. Reproduce the table and alternative specifications — P2

The centre baseline means are (41,45,49,53,57,61), change means (1,1,11/2,−1,9/2,0) and receipt means (1,0,1,0,1/2,0). Encouraged mean change is 11/3 and unencouraged mean is 0, so TW = 11/3. The baseline contrast is -4, hence post-only ITT is -1/3. This negative post contrast and positive change contrast are not a contradiction: they use different outcomes with an observed baseline imbalance. Both remain design-unbiased for the same encouragement effect because B is fixed before assignment.

The receipt contrast is 5/6, so the sample Wald ratio is 22/5. Among the five recipients, total change is 19; among seven nonrecipients it is 3. OLS on an intercept and binary D is their mean difference, 19/5−3/7 = 118/35. That coefficient is descriptive. Always-takers and compliers select into receipt differently, and fixed untreated changes differ among workers; random Z does not make D independent of potential outcomes.

For centre ANCOVA, put b = B̄−51 = (−10,−6,−2,2,6,10) and X = [1,Z,b]. The exact normal equations are

[[6,3,0],[3,3,−6],[0,−6,280]] β = (11,11,−2)′,
β = (−5/32, 191/48, 5/64)′.

The determinant is positive because Z is not affine in the six distinct baselines; equivalently its residual sum of squares below is positive. For an independent FWL derivation, residualize Z on 1,b: z̃ = Z−1/2+(3/140)b. Then z̃′z̃ = 48/35 and z̃′W̄ = 191/35. Their ratio is the ANCOVA invitation coefficient 191/48. Substituting in the remaining equations gives the intercept and baseline slope. All twenty assignment designs have positive residual rank, which the exact finite computation verifies; no pseudoinverse or “randomization guarantees rank” assertion is needed.

The reproduction log records each transformation and discrepancy. A coefficient copied from a supplied output without a calculation is not replication. The routine for these results needs only observed B,Y,D,Z and group keys. The oracle appears only in the subsequent design audit.

2. Identify assignment effects, then examine estimators — P1 and P2

Write Wzjr = Wjr(Djr(z),z). Exclusion implies W¹−W⁰ = τ[D(1)−D(0)]. Monotonicity and binary receipt make the bracket 1 for C and 0 for A/N. Summing gives π = 6/12 = 1/2, θZ = (2+4+6+2+4+6)/12 = 2 and θC = 4. Thus θZ = πθC. Positive π permits division. Without exclusion, assignment effects can include a direct invitation effect; without monotonicity, positive and negative receipt switches can enter with opposite signs.

At the centre level W̄⁰ = (−2,1,9/2,−1,9/2,0) and W̄¹ = (1,4,11/2,−1,9/2,5). Their differences are (3,3,1,0,0,5), averaging 2. With three treated centres, TW = (1/3)ΣZjW̄¹j−(1/3)Σ(1−Zj)W̄⁰j. Since E[Zj] = 1/2, E[TW] = (1/6)Σ(W̄¹−W̄⁰) = θZ. The identical argument applies to receipt and to post-only outcomes, whose individual encouragement differences equal change differences.

For finite expectations, regenerate D,W,Y under each of the twenty equally likely assignments using the fixed oracle. This is a distribution under the heterogeneous alternative, not a null permutation. Summing each estimated statistic and dividing by twenty gives:

3. Derive dependence-appropriate uncertainty and exact tests — P3 and P4

For j ≠ k, E[ZjZk] = (3/6)(2/5) = 1/5. Hence Var(Zj) = 1/4 and Cov(Zj,Zk) = −1/20. Let aj = W̄¹j+W̄⁰j. TW differs from (1/3)ΣZjaj only by a constant. Expanding with these covariances gives

Var(TW) = (1/9)[(1/4)Σa² − (1/20)Σj≠kajak] = (1/30)Σ(aj−ā)² = S²a/6 = S²1/3 + S²0/3 − S²effect/6.

Every S² uses denominator 5 across the six centres. The final equality follows from S²sum+S²difference = 2S²1+2S²0. Here S²0 = 23/3, S²1 = 20/3 and S²effect = 4; variance is 37/9. Directly summing (TW−2)²/20 over assignments yields the same value.

For any fixed six numbers x, the sample variance of a uniform three-element subset is unbiased for the population sample variance. To see this, use s² = [1/(3·2)]Σj<k in sample(xj−xk)². Each pair is selected with probability 1/5, and Σj<k(xj−xk)² = 6Σ(x−x̄)². Taking expectation gives (1/30)·6Σ(x−x̄)² = S². Apply this to each arm's fixed potential outcomes. Thus E[V̂N] = S²1/3+S²0/3 = 43/9, exceeding true variance by 2/3.

In the actual sample, treated centre variance is 67/12 and control centre variance is 1, giving V̂N = 79/36. Treating workers as iid instead gives 68/45. Neither expression creates an exact normal interval. More subtly, 79/36 < 37/9: the Neyman estimate is conservative in expectation, not pointwise for every realized assignment. The finite-randomization source distinguishes this design perspective from superpopulation inference.

For the sharp outcome null Wzjr identical for all assignments, keep the actual observed W fixed. For every three-centre set compute its contrast. Four of twenty absolute contrasts are at least |11/3|, giving 1/5. For ANCOVA keep actual W and B fixed but refit 1,Z,b for every assignment: six coefficients have absolute value at least |191/48|, giving 3/10. The two statistics were declared as separate alternatives, not chosen by their resulting p-values. A selection rule would itself have to be replayed for a selection-aware exact test.

Why is this exact? Under the sharp null the full outcome vector is fixed, so the actual assignment is uniform among the twenty reference entries. Sort the absolute statistics from largest to smallest, retaining tied blocks. The inclusive upper-tail rank of an entry equals the cumulative size of its block. The set of entries with p ≤ α contains at most 20α entries, so its probability is at most α. This is a finite counting proof; it does not use a Gaussian approximation, independent worker errors or a probability that the null is true.

4. Invert the adjusted-outcome sharp null — P5 and P6

Under Hτ, Rjr = Wjr(z)−τDjr(z) is invariant across assignments. Its observed value therefore supplies the full fixed residual vector required by the rank argument, even if unobserved receipt decisions are unknown. Under exclusion, Hτ requires the common effect τ among the compliers; A/N can have other receipt effects because their receipt never changes. An average-effect restriction alone does not make the missing residuals known.

aS = contrast of fixed observed W under S; bS = contrast of fixed observed D under S.
p(τ) = (1/20)ΣS1{|aS−bSτ| ≥ |a0−b0τ|}, with (a0,b0) = (11/3,5/6).

Each comparison changes only where [(aS−a0)−(bS−b0)τ][(aS+a0)−(bS+b0)τ] = 0. For a nonzero linear coefficient record its exact rational root. Constant nonzero factors have no root. If a comparison is identically tied, meaning (a,b) = (a₀,b₀) or (−a₀,−b₀), count its tie for every τ but skip it when collecting roots. There are at most forty candidate roots from the remaining comparisons. On each resulting open interval both signs are fixed, so one rational interior point determines the count on the entire interval. Test every boundary separately and both unbounded tails. Merge adjacent accepted cells only when their shared boundary is included. This proves completeness of the algorithm on R.

The baseline distinct roots are 0, 1, 2, 11/4, 3, 13/4, 11/3, 15/4, 13/3, 9/2, 11/2, 6, 13/2, 20/3, 7, 22/3, 9, 11. Exact evaluation of all roots and intervening cells gives p ≤ 1/5 outside [1,9] and p > 1/5 throughout [1,9]. For example p(0) = 1/5, p(1) = 3/10, p(4) = 9/10, p(9) = 3/10 and p(10) = 1/5. Therefore rejection at p ≤ 1/5 leaves [1,9], including both endpoints. The output retains the complete root list and explicit endpoint flags; a finite plotting grid cannot prove the same conclusion.

If a true scalar τ satisfies Hτ, the finite rank proof gives Pr(τ retained) ≥ 1−α. This is coverage for the stated sharp model. Here the six complier effects are (2,4,6,2,4,6), so no common scalar satisfies Hτ. The fact that [1,9] contains their mean 4 establishes no generic LATE coverage. As a separate validation population, set all six complier effects to 4 while preserving types, untreated changes and A/N effects. H4 then holds. Enumeration gives true-null rejection probability 1/5 at α = 1/5, consistent with the bound; this population is not the observed trial.

Every assignment S has a distinct equally likely complement with the opposite residual contrast. Their absolute values tie, so p ≥ 2/20 for every τ, including values with zero actual contrast. At α = 1/20 no τ is rejected: the inversion is all of R, and true-null rejection probability is zero. This coarse resolution is an honest limitation of six centres. It cannot be fixed by declaring twelve independent assignments.

5. Access loss changes both the target and the evidence — extension 1

The new world removes the centre 2 complier with receipt effect 6. The sum of complier effects falls from 24 to 18 and the count from 6 to 5. Hence θZ = 3/2, π = 5/12 and θC = 18/5. This is a new potential population and assignment, not a correction of the original records after seeing results.

Under {1,2,4}, encouraged centre changes are (1,1,−1), with mean 1/3; unencouraged changes are (9/2,9/2,0), with mean 3. The observed difference is -8/3. Each arm has mean receipt 1/3, so the first stage is 0. The ratio is undefined. Return a null value with undefined_zero_first_stage; zero, an infinite coefficient or a narrow normal interval would be false precision.

Regenerating all twenty assignments in this changed world yields three zero first stages. An unconditional expectation of the ratio is not defined on this assignment space. Averaging only the other seventeen observations would condition on a data-dependent event and answer another question; the supplied output therefore reports a null expectation with a support-failure reason.

The sharp adjusted-outcome test itself needs no division by the first stage. Its actual line is a0−b0τ = −8/3. Its distinct crossing roots are −9, −7, −5, 1, 7, 9, 11. Evaluate all cells as before: p > 1/5 for τ ≤ 1 or τ ≥ 7 and p ≤ 1/5 on (1,7). At the boundaries p(1) = 3/10 and p(7) = 2/5; p(4) = 1/5. The complete inversion is (−∞,1] ∪ [7,∞). Unboundedness and disconnection are valid properties of the test, not serialization defects. Its heterogeneous-effect confidence limitation remains unchanged. At α = 1/20 the complement argument again gives all of R.

6. Prove what exclusion cannot be learned from these observables — P7

For a complier, D(z) = z. Substituting into the changed law gives Wδ(D(z),z) = v+(τ−δ)z+δz = v+τz. Thus for every worker, both possible own-centre assignments and every one of the twenty allowed joint assignments, B,D,W,Y are exactly as before. A/N laws are unchanged by construction. The complete observed-data law, not merely one fitted moment, is identical for every δ ∈ [−1,1].

Holding z fixed and intervening on receipt gives Wδ(1,z)−Wδ(0,z) = τ−δ. Averaging among compliers yields θC,δ = 4−δ, so the allowed set is [3,5]. The lower endpoint uses δ = 1, the upper uses δ = −1, and each intermediate target c uses δ = 4−c. That admissible construction attains every point while preserving the entire observable law, proving sharpness. Bounds outside this declared common complier-specific direct-effect class have not been proved.

The invitation ITT remains 2 and the first stage 1/2. Their ratio remains 4, but now equals the receipt effect 4−δ plus the direct invitation contribution δ among receipt-switchers. It cannot isolate training alone. Random assignment protects the invitation comparison from selection; it does not prevent the invitation from changing motivation, information or other outcome determinants. Those channels are substantive identification questions.

7. A defensible referee report and external transfer

An adequate synthetic referee report opens with a conditional conclusion: “The fixed experiment identifies the average effect of encouragement under the stipulated assignment and no-interference contract. The observed change contrast is 11/3, while its finite assignment expectation is 2. Under additional exclusion and monotone receipt, the ratio of finite assignment effects identifies the complier receipt effect 4; the observed ratio 22/5 is neither that exact target nor an unbiased estimator in this small design.” It then documents the receipt-OLS, assignment-unit, sharp-null and exclusion errors, their numerical consequences and the revisions required. It does not infer no effect from p = 1/5 or superiority from one smaller p-value. The ASA interpretation principles support that caution.

The external dossier has no fabricated numerical answer key. Full credit requires evidence appropriate to the selected paper: exact claim/primary location; coherent estimand and counterfactual; assumptions distinguished from diagnostics; actual dependence and selection rules; a reproducible input-to-exhibit map; and a conclusion limited to those facts. In the execution branch, matching one table is a reported computational result, while identification and transport remain separate. In the limitation branch, a specific documented obstacle, truthful “not executed” status and executable-in-principle protocol earn audit credit, never an invented empirical achievement.

For example, a feasible identification extension to an actual invitation study could specify a justified direct-effect sensitivity class, state the additional evidence needed to bound it, and show how the receipt conclusion varies. A feasible inference extension could replay that study's actual clustered or stratified assignment with its prespecified statistic, but only if the design and needed records are available. These are illustrative proposals, not claims that a selected external paper has that design or that its results have been run. The learner must adapt them to inspected evidence rather than copy the Harbor Skills assumptions.

For grading, a correct exact alternative implementation, an independently proved bound or an evidence-supported different research recommendation can earn full credit. A protocol without actual execution must retain that status even with an excellent score. The completed textbook exercise does not itself certify the learner's research, teaching or institutional competence.

Retention answer

The sharp inversion is all of R because its two-sided p-value is at least 1/10. The isolated receipt effect is 4−1/2 = 7/2, while every observed score, receipt and assignment effect is unchanged. The ratio of assignment effects is still 4 and no longer isolates receipt alone. A failed permitted access attempt supports a documented limitation and a proposed protocol; it supplies no executed empirical result. The teach-back must name the assignment-law expectations and distinguish common sharp effects from heterogeneous averages.
