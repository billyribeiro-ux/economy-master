Integrative project — From a corrected ledger to a fundable operating plan

Task. Advise Rowan Industrial Supply's board using a fully reconciled historical account, an evidence-based correction, and alternative operating and financing forecasts. Deliver the numbers and a financial-quality decision memo. This is an original open-resource project designed for 8–12 hours; the time estimate is an author planning choice. Use the tools taught in all twelve accounting chapters, with particular attention to tax and earnings quality, diagnostic definitions, forecasting and evidence and inference. Freeze your own solution before opening the instructor guide or preserved output.

Management's unissued draft appears profitable and shows strong operating cash. Its asset register contains a routine-maintenance payment recorded as investment. The tax worksheet already treated that payment correctly. After correcting the records, the board must choose whether a proposed 2026 plan can be financed, which distributions or investments can change, and what an outside equity contribution would imply. A balance-sheet identity cannot decide those questions on its own.

Every record is fictional and author-created; there is no real company or market dataset. Rowan is one standalone distributor with only ordinary shares. Money is kCU; quantities and share counts are in thousands; unit prices, unit costs and the subscription price are in CU. Thus 80 thousand units at 10 CU produce 800 kCU of sales. The opening date is 31 December 2024, the historical year ends 31 December 2025, and both alternative forecasts end 31 December 2026. Each flow period has 365 days. Forecast outcomes are conditional assumptions, not observed 2026 data or predictions with assigned probabilities.

All sales and goods purchases pass through trade receivables/payables. There are no returns, credit losses, inventory write-downs, acquisitions, disposals, foreign exchange, leases, subsidiaries, noncontrolling interests, preferred shares, interest income or unlisted transactions. Ordinary operating expense and routine maintenance are paid within the year. COGS is inventory cost; equipment depreciation is a separate operating expense. Cash is unrestricted. Interest and cash tax are operating, qualifying equipment purchases investing, and principal, dividends and shares financing under this project's supplied presentation.

Genuine 2026 purchases begin service on 1 January 2027, with the same life and residual convention. No forecast 2026 depreciation attaches to those final-date purchases. The fictional tax rate is 25%; genuine book and tax depreciation agree. All tax effects are in profit and taxable profits remain nonnegative in the assigned cases.

Diagnostic definitions and required decision test

Use current assets = cash + AR + inventory, and current liabilities = AP + current tax payable + current term principal + revolver. Quick assets are cash + AR. Interest-bearing debt excludes AP and tax balances. Current tax payable and the draft operating DTL are deducted in this project's operating-capital definition:

IC = AR + inventory + PPE − AP − current tax payable − operating DTL
= term debt + revolver + common equity − cash.
NOPAT = EBIT × 0.75; ROIC = NOPAT / mean opening-and-closing IC.

This explicitly extends Chapter 9's capital reconciliation to the tax balances present here. It does not make a tax payable permanent or costless funding. ROE, asset turnover and the equity multiplier also use matching endpoint averages. Calculate DSO as 365×average AR/credit sales if used in the memo. Final-date capex and new equity make these averages rough exposure measures; they are neither marginal project returns nor a subscription investor's market return. Do not substitute the draft close for the corrected forecast opening.

For the sensitivity, hold every adverse operating input fixed except its closing-AR percentage. Use zero dividend, capex 50, subscription 30, revolver at most 75 and cash at least 25. Derive the maximum permitted AR amount and percentage and explain what an extra 1 kCU of uncollected receivables does. This is a conditional funding boundary, not a probability of collection or an industry credit standard. Your memo must identify how a dated collection/payment calendar could still reject a plan that passes this annual test.

The historical correction lowers reported performance and operating cash without changing liquidity stocks or total cash. That is a classification/measurement correction. The adverse forecast is a different operating scenario: lower volume, higher unit cost, slower collections and more inventory. It is not another historical adjustment. No universal ratio threshold turns either into a fraud finding. The supplied line and cash minimum, however, are explicit constraints that must be enforced.

Collection boundary. Under adverse operations, zero dividend and capex 50, subscription 30 leaves required revolver 70.5, so only 4.5 more borrowing is available. Every extra 1 of closing AR reduces collections and CFO by 1, with sales, expenses, tax and profit held fixed. The largest supportable AR is therefore 168 + 4.5 = 172.5, or 172.5/840 = 23/112 ≈ 20.536% of sales. CFO becomes −8; 28 − 8 − 50 − 20 + 30 + 45 = 25. At AR 173.5, cash using all allowed funds is 24, so the remaining gap is 1. This boundary does not prove that collections will occur by the relevant payment date.

Illustrative memo reasoning. Correct and retain the historical record before presenting operating quality. Maintenance is a continuing resource cost under H-REPAIR and the forecast expressly requires another 20; adding it back merely to restore draft EBIT 110 would misdescribe this supplied continuing-cost comparison. The base funds its annual endpoints, but relies on supplier credit and does not establish the daily cash path. The original adverse plan fails the stated facility. The minimum repair can fund annual endpoints under the given engineering and subscription premises, but fully uses the line and leaves cash exactly at the required minimum, with no headroom above either constraint. Using subscription 30 rather than 25.5 preserves 4.5 of line capacity at the cost of issuing 10 thousand rather than 8.5 thousand shares; the new investor's fraction becomes 1/11. That is a transparent tradeoff, not an automatically optimal answer.

Four well-targeted requests might be: the maintenance work orders and approvals to test recognition and recurrence; an invoice-level aging/collection calendar to distinguish later genuine sales from slow or doubtful payment and test the AR boundary; inventory counts, aging and purchase commitments to distinguish precautionary stock from obsolescence; and signed line/subscription settlement terms matched to mandatory payment dates to establish usable funding. An asset engineering/capacity review tests the capex deferral. A reconciled tax schedule checks that the 4.5 payable is paid once. These records answer different assertions; none alone establishes intent.

Acceptable alternatives. A learner may prefer more equity within the supplied 30, decline or delay the plan, or recommend obtaining evidence before commitment. Full decision credit requires the correct mathematical constraints and a clear account of shareholder dilution, future investment consequences and missing daily liquidity. A proposal beyond the given menu can be a conditional recommendation, but cannot be labeled feasible until its new funding, operating and timing assumptions are specified and all statements recomputed. Do not grade agreement with an instructor's preferred risk tolerance.

Defense and remediation. Ask the learner to explain why correcting maintenance reduces CFO but not cash, why the 4.5 tax payable is not a new 2026 expense, and why a 75 line permits only 45 of new borrowing. Then change one closing-AR amount and require the funding consequence before opening the code. A material failure should trigger reconstruction of the relevant journal or cash identity and a new transfer case. A successful program and an articulate memo are both necessary; neither substitutes for the other.
