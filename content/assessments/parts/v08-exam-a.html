Cumulative examination — Form A

100 points; 240 minutes; closed book; nonprogrammable calculator permitted. Answer all five questions. Each question is one essential domain, worth 20 points; each of its four parts is worth 5 points. Show dated cash flows, claim boundaries and reasoning. Give exact fractions or enough intermediate precision to support amounts rounded to two decimals. The progression standard is at least 85/100 and at least 15/20 in every domain, followed by the separate transfer and teach-back requirements. These are this library's assessment rules.

All businesses, prices, contracts and data below are original fictional cases, independent of the chapter businesses and of one another unless explicitly connected. Amounts are thousands of currency units (kCU); share counts are thousands, so kCU divided by thousand shares gives CU per share. Dates are relative dates, not observations from a real market. Cash flows occur at the stated dates; there are no omitted taxes, fees, claims or side effects. Use each case's own risk and financing assumptions. “Value” means the date-0 value unless another date is named.

A completed feasibility study cost 6 last year and cannot be recovered. Measure opening operating capital for this decision as equipment plus operating working capital plus the land's foregone net sale value. Subsequent operating capital increases by capex less depreciation plus the increase in operating working capital.

Question 2 — Risk transfer, financing incentives and payout capacity (D2, 20 points)

The project will maintain debt/value of 40% and has debt beta 0.1. There are no taxes, distress costs or other financing effects; the CAPM applies to all claims. The risk-free rate is 2% and the market equity risk premium is 6% annually. There are no nonoperating assets.

Part (b), a separate incentive case. At a zero interest rate, market pricing weights for the low and high states are 0.6 and 0.4. A firm owes old debt with final face 90 and limited liability. After that borrowing is fixed, shareholders choose either a prudent policy with final asset cash 110 in both states, or a gamble with final asset cash 30 in the low state and 180 in the high state. There are no intermediate flows. Then consider a separate prospective issue of the same face-90 debt by an initially unlevered owner: competitive lenders price the policy that will actually be chosen. An enforceable covenant can require the prudent policy, for a date-0 resource fee of 8 paid by the owner from outside the firm. Without it, shareholders retain the later policy choice. Compare the owner's debt-issue proceeds plus the retained equity value, less that outside fee; do not treat the two issues as simultaneous financing.

Control improvements have present value 12 and combination benefits have present value 50. Those two values include all future operating taxes and future investment, but exclude the following separate date-0 payments: required additional investment 18, integration 9 and advisory fee 5. All 32 must be paid at closing from the two firms' combined excess cash; there is no new debt, outside equity financing or other cost. The benefit values include the operating consequences of that additional investment, so do not capitalize its cost a second time. Standalone values are unaffected by rejecting the deal. The proposed consideration is eight thousand newly issued Hallowell ordinary shares, shared equally per Vesper ordinary share. All ordinary shares then have identical rights and markets price the stated net benefits.

Parts (c–d), a separate primary venture round. Marden Sensors initially has nine thousand founder ordinary shares and one thousand employee ordinary shares. There are no taxes or time discounting in this one-date distribution exercise.

  2. Find the post-deal price per ordinary share, each old shareholder group's wealth gain, and the maximum new share count that leaves old Hallowell owners no worse off. Explain why multiplying the offered eight thousand shares by Hallowell's old price does not fix actual consideration.
  3. Find the venture round's issue price, new preferred share count, reserve share count and final fully diluted ownership fractions for founders and the investor. Reconcile all four groups' fractions and explain the effect of creating the reserve after pricing.

For venture exit pots X = 10 and X = 20, calculate the investor's optimal election, its payment and the payment to founder, existing employee and award holders together, excluding the investor even if it converts. Question 5 — Recovery, class participation and a funded decision (D5, 20 points)

Kintyre Packaging must choose immediate liquidation or a one-year continuation. The following priorities are a complete, stipulated contract for this case, not a description of any jurisdiction's insolvency law. There are no omitted taxes, fees, senior claims or asset recoveries. Current cash is 4. Existing claims are secured debt with face 70, bonds with face 30, trade claims with face 10 and residual ordinary equity. Existing face amounts are frozen without further interest. Every old creditor class must consent to continuation; a class can veto a proposal whose present-value recovery is below its liquidation recovery. There is no unilateral power to alter this rule.

Liquidation at date 0. Collateral sells for 55 with a sale cost of 5 paid from that collateral. Any secured deficiency joins the bonds and trade claims as equal-ranking claims on the general pool. Unencumbered assets sell for 40, with a sale cost of 4 paid from those assets. The current cash of 4 also enters the general pool. After all creditor claims, any surplus belongs to ordinary equity.

Baseline continuation. A date-0 restart and restructuring payment of 24 includes every immediate investment and operating requirement. It is funded by current cash 4 and a committed new loan of 20; there are no interim cash requirements. Final date-1 cash is a certain 117.6, after all operating costs, taxes, investments, working-capital releases and asset disposals, but before debt and shareholder payments. There is no later continuation value. The new loan is senior to all old claims and must receive 21 at date 1; the appropriate annual discount rate for every certain receipt is 5%. After the new loan, the proposed contract pays old secured creditors 70, then divides available cash between bond and trade claims in proportion to their faces 30:10, up to those faces. Any residual goes to old ordinary equity.

  1. Calculate the secured deficiency, general-pool recovery fraction, and each old group's liquidation recovery. Reconcile all recoveries to net liquidation resources.
  2. Verify baseline closing funding and the new lender's fair value. Find total present value available to old claimants and its gain over liquidation. Compute each old creditor class's proposed present-value recovery and identify which classes can veto even though aggregate value rises.
  3. The parties may agree to transfer Z of final date-1 cash from the old secured payment to the bond/trade pool, preserving the 3:1 division within that pool and leaving the new lender unchanged. Derive the full interval of Z that makes every old creditor class weakly prefer continuation. Evaluate all three classes' present-value gains at Z = 2.1 and reconcile the gains to total surplus.
  4. A required stress scenario raises the immediate restart payment to 27 and reduces final certain cash to 110.25; every other resource and commitment initially remains unchanged. Find the closing funding gap. Then, only as a conditional comparison, suppose another 3 of new money can be supplied fairly at 5% with the same priority and no extra fee. Find value left for old claimants and test whether any redistribution can make all of them at least as well off as liquidation. Give a brief board recommendation covering baseline consent and stress feasibility; do not assume the extra financing has already been committed.

Question 1 solutions — Incremental investment and sustainable continuation

  1. New cash paid is 160 + 20 = 180. Choosing the project also forgoes the land's net sale receipt of 20, so the incremental economic outlay and opening operating capital are 200. The date-0 decision cash flow is therefore −200 relative to selling the land and not undertaking the project. The prior study's 6 is sunk: it occurs in neither future alternative and does not enter incremental NPV. Including the land's opportunity cost does not imply a second current purchase of the already-owned asset. Land remains inside the operating capital/continuation perimeter.

    Scoring: new cash 180, 1; foregone sale/opportunity cost 20, 1; economic outlay and capital 200, 1; exclusion of sunk study with reason, 1; distinction between incremental alternative and new cash payment, 1.

  2. Year 1 operating income is 140 − 80 − 20 = 40, tax 10 and after-tax operating profit 30. Year 2 operating income is 160 − 96 − 20 = 44, tax 11 and after-tax operating profit 33. In each year, net reinvestment is 35 − 20 + 5 = 20. FCFF is therefore 10 and 13. Equivalently, 30 + 20 − 35 − 5 = 10 and 33 + 20 − 35 − 5 = 13. Capital closes at 220 and then 240. Depreciation reduces taxable operating income but is not another cash payment; the net-investment bridge makes the capital consumed and replaced explicit.

    Scoring: both operating-income/tax/profit calculations, 1; net reinvestment, 1; year-1 FCFF, 1; year-2 FCFF, 1; both capital closes, 1.

  3. Year 3 opens with capital 240. After-tax operating profit is 0.12×240 = 28.8; reinvestment is 0.03×240 = 7.2, and FCFF is 21.6. Closing capital is 247.2, so year-4 profit and reinvestment both grow by 3%, as does FCFF. Date-2 continuation value is 21.6/(0.09 − 0.03) = 360. It starts with the payment at date 3 and excludes date-2 FCFF of 13. Thus NPV = −200 + 10/1.09 + (13 + 360)/1.09² = 1,462,800/11,881 ≈ 123.12. Operating present value before the economic outlay is approximately 323.12. The stable condition 0.03 < 0.09 holds, and the terminal numerator is after the reinvestment needed to maintain growth.

    Scoring: year-3 profit 28.8, 1; reinvestment 7.2 and FCFF 21.6, 1; continuation 360 with admissible spread, 1; correct date-2/date-3 boundary and discounting, 1; NPV 123.12, 1.

  4. With stable return 9%, year-3 profit is 0.09×240 = 21.6, reinvestment remains 7.2 and FCFF is 14.4. Continuation value is 14.4/0.06 = 240. NPV becomes −200 + 10/1.09 + (13 + 240)/1.09² = 262,800/11,881 ≈ 22.12. For inherited capital K and this stipulated constant-return capital policy, Fnext = (R − g)K and TV = (R − g)K/(r − g). If R = r and g < r, TV = K: growth requires investment that earns exactly its opportunity cost. This cancellation concerns the specified stable policy with fixed inherited K, not an assertion that growth never matters. Year-2 FCFF 13 comes from the explicit profit/reinvestment pair 33 and 20; year 3 deliberately switches return and reinvestment policy. Using 13×1.03 would preserve an obsolete cash-conversion ratio and violate that transition.

    Scoring: revised profit/reinvestment/FCFF, 1; continuation 240, 1; revised NPV 22.12, 1; growth cancellation with fixed K and admissible g, 1; explicit-to-stable numerator explanation, 1.
