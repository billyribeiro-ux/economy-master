Form A — Targets, information and valid inference

This original closed-book examination has five 20-point domains and sixteen scored parts, for 100 points. Allow 300 minutes: approximately 55 minutes per case and 25 minutes to read and recheck. This is an uncalibrated design allowance, not measured examination timing or equivalence to another form. Use blank paper and a nonprogrammable calculator; no notes, internet or code. Exact fractions, log(10) and symbolic square roots are acceptable. No external probability table or numerical integration is needed. Every numerical input is supplied below.

State the target, independent information unit, relevant assumptions and scope of each probability statement. Cases concern separate synthetic populations unless explicitly connected. The full teaching laws let you evaluate methods; they do not mean a hypothetical analyst observes latent variables or has empirically verified those laws. Show derivations. A correct number attached to the wrong target or sampling law is not a complete answer.

Advancement uses author-created thresholds: at least 85/100 overall, at least 15/20 in every domain, and no unresolved critical identification, inference or integrity error, followed by an unfamiliar transfer and teach-back. Examples requiring remediation are treating dependent rows as independent units, claiming an unidentified counterfactual is identified, attaching the wrong selection law to a p-value, or claiming access to unreleased information. These requirements imply no institutional recognition or guaranteed outcome.

Question 1. A differenced count panel — 20 points

One scientific unit contributes two dates. Draw Z, U, E₁ and E₂ independently, each equal to −1 or +1 with probability 1/2, and repeat complete units iid. Define

α=10+2Z,   X₁=0,   X₂=Z+U,
Y₁=α+E₁,   Y₂=α+βX₂+U+E₂.

Y is an event count and X a centered input index. The teaching truth for calculations is β=2, giving sixteen equiprobable atoms and positive counts. The analyst observes (Z,X₁,X₂,Y₁,Y₂), but not α,U,E₁,E₂ or the teaching β. The laws above are supplied for this examination, not established from a fitted regression.

Compare through-origin first-difference OLS, the ratio ΣZΔY/ΣZΔX, and the alternative ratio ΣY₁ΔY/ΣY₁ΔX. In asymptotic definitions assign an arbitrary fixed value on a zero-denominator event; its probability tends to zero when the population denominator is nonzero. An actual zero denominator must be reported as a computation failure. Separately, use each unit's Y₂ exactly once in an intercept-only Poisson working log likelihood with parameter γ∈ℝ and mean exp(γ). The true finite Y₂ law is not Poisson.

  1. (a) Show that differencing removes α. Derive the probability limit of difference OLS and distinguish its target from β.
  2. (b) Derive the Z-IV target and a root-N normal limit. State the independent information unit, score variance and standard-error scale. Do not assert exact finite-sample normality.
  3. (c) Derive the population target and sample maximizer of the marginal Poisson working likelihood, proving global optimality. Derive its robust root-N variance, compare inverse working information, and interpret the fitted parameter.
  4. (d) An analyst replaces Z by the earlier outcome Y₁ because it precedes Y₂. Compute relevance, its error cross moment and its ratio target. Diagnose the timing argument.

  1. Subtracting the two dates gives ΔX=Z+U and ΔY=βΔX+v, where v=U+E₂−E₁. The correlated unit effect cancels exactly. Independence and zero means imply E[ΔX²]=2 and E[ΔXv]=1. Removing a time-constant level does not remove U from the transformed input and error. The projection slope is not β=2.

  2. E[ZΔX]=E[Z²+ZU]=1 while E[Zv]=0; E[ZΔY]=β. The population ratio is therefore β. On a nonzero sample denominator,

    √N(β̂−β)=[N−1ΣZᵢΔXᵢ]−1N−1/2ΣZᵢvᵢ.

    The independent unit is the complete two-date record. Its bounded score Zv has variance E[Z²v²]=E[v²]=3, because U,E₁,E₂ are independent centered signs. The moments (E[ZΔX],E[ZΔY],Var(Zv)) are (1,2,3). The denominator converges to one and the iid unit CLT plus Slutsky gives √N(β̂−β)⇒N(0,3). The arbitrary extension on zero-denominator events is asymptotically harmless since their probability tends to zero; an observed zero denominator is still a computation failure.
