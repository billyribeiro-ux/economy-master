Integrative project: correcting a market with two distortions

Task: advise a hypothetical market authority choosing between no intervention, a uniform output tax and equal firm output standards. Derive the market response, reconcile the welfare ledger and explain what evidence would be needed before applying the result anywhere real. This is an open-resource project drawing on Volume 5 and the reproducibility methods of Volume 4. Allow approximately 8–12 hours. Freeze your own report before consulting the instructor guide or benchmark output.

The authority's proposed rule is “tax each unit by its marginal environmental damage.” That rule has a precise competitive justification. Here the firms may instead restrict output strategically. A policy can correct one distortion while worsening another. Your recommendation must therefore follow from the feasible instrument's actual equilibrium and net welfare, including administration, rather than from the name of the instrument.

One-period model, timing and feasible instruments

Two firms produce a homogeneous good. Output qi is measured in units per operating period and Q = q₁+q₂. Inverse demand is P(Q) = A−bQ, and buyers' gross benefit is B(Q) = AQ−bQ²/2. Firm i has real cost Ci(qi) = ciqi+kiqi²/2 and physical capacity 0 ≤ qi ≤ ui. Damage borne by outsiders is D(Q) = hQ²/2. All scenarios satisfy b(u₁+u₂) ≤ A, so the stated linear price is nonnegative throughout the entire feasible output box. Do not extrapolate to negative prices or add an unstated demand truncation.

A, ci, P and the unit tax are USD per output unit. The slopes b, ki and h have the corresponding USD per squared output-unit scale in this fixed-period normalization. Benefit, cost, damage, profit, revenue and administration are USD per period. There is one emission unit per output unit, with no separate abatement technology. The output tax therefore prices this particular emission mapping; it would not reward an unmodeled change in emission intensity.

Preferences are quasilinear with sufficient numeraire wealth and no binding financing constraint. Unweighted total surplus includes buyers, firms, government and affected outsiders. Dollar damage is a money equivalent of harm, not necessarily a cash expenditure. Firms have no fixed operating cost, entry decision or repeated-game arrangement. The two participants remain fixed even when one chooses zero output. All primitives within a deterministic scenario are common knowledge.

The regulator commits to one policy before firms choose output. Under competition, each firm takes the market price as given and chooses output, with demand clearing the market. Treating two production technologies as price takers is a comparison benchmark, not a claim that two real suppliers behave competitively. Under Cournot, firms choose quantities simultaneously and each anticipates its own effect on P. Consumers then buy Q at the clearing price. Output and compliance are perfectly observed and enforced at the stated real administrative expense.

  5. Evaluate evidence and recommend conditionally. State which primitives and conduct assumptions would need empirical support; propose an identification/data-collection plan rather than estimating nonexistent data. Compare welfare, funding and distribution under your explicit criterion. Name an observation that would change the recommendation. Global calculation route: for each firm consider lower, interior and upper output states. Conditional on those states, the two marginal equations are linear; under a scalar tax or standard the quantities are affine in that policy parameter. Restrict each branch to the parameter interval where its output bounds and marginal inequalities hold. Substituting into W gives a concave quadratic or constant on that interval. Compare its endpoints and any interior stationary point, then compare the separate no-policy option. This finite procedure is the benchmark's optimization method. The chart grid is solely for presentation.

For each fixed rival output, Cournot profit has second derivative −(2b+ki) < 0. Its clipped best response has slope magnitude at most b/(2b+ki) < 1, giving a contraction in the maximum norm for the two-response map and therefore a unique fixed point on the output box. For a fixed tax and standard, the competitive allocation uniquely maximizes strictly concave B−ΣC−tQ over the effective output box, including any standard; the efficient target uniquely maximizes strictly concave B−ΣC−D over the physical output box. These facts justify identifying the active-set candidate that satisfies every marginal inequality. They do not say that a competitive allocation is the Nash outcome of the Cournot game.

Chart: plot baseline Cournot net welfare under a deployed tax against t from 0 to 40, using the 41 supplied chart coordinates. Label the axes “tax, USD per output unit” and “net welfare, USD per period.” Mark t = 16 and t = 32, and show no-policy welfare 1200 as a separate horizontal reference. The deployed-tax curve starts at 1180 because its administration operates even at t = 0. State that the exact optimum comes from the quadratic and boundaries, not the plotted grid. Supply a readable chart or a legible hand drawing with the values table; a second axis is unnecessary.

A fixed policy before damage is known

For this additional exercise use the baseline private demand, costs, capacities and administration. The damage state is low (h = 1/4), baseline (h = 1) or high (h = 4), with stipulated probabilities 1/4, 1/2 and 1/4. The authority chooses one policy before learning the state, cannot revise it afterward, and maximizes expected unweighted net welfare. Firms' private profit does not depend on h. Thus a fixed policy produces the same outputs in each state. Prove that expected damage is E[h]Q²/2 and solve the fixed-policy Cournot problem globally using E[h]. Report state outcomes at that same policy and reconcile the probability-weighted welfare with the expected objective. Also report expected welfare from retaining the original tax 16, the original standard 16 and no intervention. Do not substitute a different state-contingent optimum after the state is revealed. The probabilities express supplied uncertainty, not estimated frequencies or confidence levels.

Minimum empirical identification plan

Your plan should define a product/geographic market, decision period, output and emission units, affected population, intervention timing and source availability. Collect buyer prices and transactions, firm-level outputs and cost shifters, capacities, entry/exit, emission intensity, exposure/harm measures and actual enforcement/administration expenses. Preserve source vintage, units, missingness, transformations and a discrepancy log. Quantity and price in an equilibrium do not by themselves trace out demand or identify marginal cost: both can respond to common shocks. Explain what independent demand or cost variation and exclusion assumptions would identify each relationship, and how conduct would be distinguished from technology.

A possible policy-effect design assigns a clearly specified tax or standard at the market level across comparable markets, with a fixed outcome window and a stated interference model. Where assignment is unavailable, give a defensible comparison design and its assumptions rather than invoking regression alone. A before/after price change can reflect demand shifts, input costs, entry or anticipation. An output effect does not automatically identify exposure reduction, damage valuation or welfare. Controlling for post-policy output while claiming the total policy effect on harm can remove a policy pathway; the estimand and adjustment choices must agree. These data are absent from the synthetic package, so the project makes no causal estimate.

Complete instructor guide and benchmark targets

Baseline derivations and ledger

Competitive interior choice sets P−t = 20+qi. Symmetry and P = 100−2q give q = (80−t)/3, subject to the output bounds. Cournot's derivative is 80−t−qj−3qi, giving q = (80−t)/4. No policy therefore gives q = 80/3 under competition and q = 20 under Cournot. The physical upper bound 40 is slack. Both conditions are global given the stated concavity; the Cournot formula is a best response to quantities, whereas the competitive condition treats P as given.

Equal allocation minimizes private cost for a given Q: ΣC = 20Q+Q²/4. Thus gross welfare is 80Q−5Q²/4 = 1280−(5/4)(Q−32)². The unique efficient target is Q* = 32 and q* = 16. Marginal damage is hQ* = 32. Competition implements it with t = 32. Cournot implements it with t = 16: its private marginal condition is P−bqi−MCi−t = 0, while the social interior condition is P−MCi−hQ = 0, so the required tax is ti = hQ*−bqi*. With symmetric firms this is one common t. It need not be nonnegative or common across heterogeneous firms.

For baseline Cournot, Q(t) = 40−t/2 throughout 0 ≤ t ≤ 40. Deployed-tax net welfare is 1180+10t−5t²/16, maximized at t = 16 with value 1260. Endpoints t = 0 and 40 give 1180 and 1080. The mechanical marginal-damage-at-target tax 32 gives Q = 24, gross welfare 1200 and net welfare 1180. The separate no-policy outcome has welfare 1200, so the best feasible tax improves it by 60. In competition the corresponding efficient tax 32 gives net welfare 1260, compared with no-policy welfare 6400/9.

An equal standard gives q = min{s,q(0)} in the symmetric baseline. At s = 16, a Cournot firm facing qj = 16 would choose 64/3 without the standard, so each cap binds. In competition P = 68 exceeds each firm's MC = 36 at q = 16, also making the cap bind. For s below the unregulated output, net welfare is 160s−5s²−35, maximized at s = 16. Larger nonbinding standards retain unregulated output while still consuming 35. The optimal standard gives 1245, below the tax's 1260 solely because its stipulated administration costs 15 more. No permit price or permit revenue has been assumed.

Low damage: gross welfare is 80Q−7Q²/8, maximized at Q* = 320/7. This exceeds unregulated Cournot Q = 40. Its implementing tax would be −80/7, an unavailable subsidy. Every permitted tax weakly reduces output, as does a binding standard; any deployed regime also costs resources. No intervention therefore wins. Competition starts above Q*, and tax 80/7 reaches it, leaving net welfare 12800/7−20. The unachieved Cournot gross efficiency gain is 12800/7−1800 = 200/7; it is not a feasible-policy gain.

High damage: gross welfare is 80Q−11Q²/4, with Q* = 160/11. Cournot would need tax 560/11, above 40; competition would need 640/11, also above 40. Their best permitted taxes are therefore the upper endpoint 40, with net welfare 480 and 1420/9 respectively. Standard s = 80/11 is feasible, both firms want to produce more than that bound, and net welfare is 6400/11−35 = 6015/11. It beats both tax and no-policy outcomes. This is a ranking under the specified instrument bounds and costs, not a universal advantage of quantity regulation.

Tight capacity: the feasible efficient allocation is (12,12), not the unconstrained (16,16). Both no-policy regimes already produce it. The Cournot marginal profit at qi = qj = 12 is 32−t, so the capacities remain binding up to t = 32; further taxes reduce output below the efficient feasible quantity. The competitive margin there is 44−t, positive throughout [0,40]. A standard at least 12 changes neither output, although deploying it still costs 35. No policy avoids unnecessary administration. In the fixed-policy stress test, standard 16 is therefore nonbinding but costly.

Costly administration: the physical targets and private incentives are unchanged. Cournot's maximum gross gain is 80, below tax administration 100, so no policy wins. Competition's gross gain from 6400/9 to 1280 is much larger; tax 32 still yields 1180 and beats its no-policy benchmark. Do not reuse the same “intervention is worthwhile” conclusion across conduct assumptions.

Asymmetric cost: the efficient interior equations imply q₁−q₂ = 24 and Q* = 136/5, giving (q₁*,q₂*) = (128/5,8/5) and gross welfare 5344/5. Competition allocates efficiently at each price; its implementing common tax is hQ* = 136/5. Cournot instead has q₁ = 23−t/4 and q₂ = 11−t/4 for all 0 ≤ t ≤ 40, so q₁−q₂ remains 12. A uniform tax cannot implement the efficient vector. Along that restricted allocation path, gross welfare is 68Q−5Q²/4+108, maximized at Q = 136/5 and t = 68/5. The resulting quantities (98/5,38/5) have the efficient total but excessive output at the costly firm. Gross welfare is 5164/5, exactly 36 below the efficient value; net welfare is 5064/5.

For completeness, the asymmetric Cournot standard has three branches: q₁ = q₂ = s on [0,14]; q₁ = s, q₂ = (56−s)/3 on [14,23]; and (23,11) on [23,40]. Gross welfare on the first branch is 136s−5s², whose stationary point is 68/5. On the middle branch it is 1568/3+(128/3)s−s², maximized at s = 64/3 with gross value 8800/9. On the last branch gross welfare is 975. Comparing stationary points and shared endpoints gives deployed-standard net maximum 8800/9−35 = 8485/9, below no-policy 975 and the optimized tax. Matching the efficient vector would require discriminatory Cournot taxes (8/5,128/5), which are outside the menu. This diagnostic is not an available recommendation.

Inactive firm: firm 2 with c₂ = 100 cannot obtain a positive marginal return at zero output when firm 1 produces. Set q₂ = 0 and solve the remaining firm's problem. Competition without tax reaches physical capacity q₁ = 40; Cournot produces q₁ = 80/3. Efficient welfare is 80q₁−3q₁²/2, also maximized at 80/3. Thus no Cournot intervention is beneficial after administration. Competition reaches that target with tax 80/3. The zero output of firm 2 is an optimal boundary for a fixed participant, not a prediction about entry or long-run exit.

Uncertainty, checks and interpretation targets

The stipulated mean damage slope is E[h] = (1/4)(1/4)+(1/2)1+(1/4)4 = 25/16. Because h affects neither private choices nor administration, expected Cournot gross welfare is 80Q−49Q²/32. Its efficient total is 1280/49; feasible tax t = 80−2Q = 1360/49 implements it. Each firm produces 640/49, and expected net welfare is 51200/49−20 = 50220/49. This tax lies within [0,40]. The equal standard 640/49 implements the same output but costs 15 more, giving 49485/49. No-policy expected welfare is 750. Fixed original tax 16 gives expected welfare 972; fixed original standard 16 gives 957. These are comparisons of policies chosen before the state, not averages of retrospectively reoptimized policies.

The preserved JSON contains all seven deterministic scenario ledgers, both conduct regimes, every policy candidate and its valid affine region, fixed-baseline-policy stress results, state ledgers under the chosen ex ante policy and chart values. Every resource ledger must reconcile exactly. Appropriate independent checks include direct clipped best responses, competitive supply at the computed price, planner marginal inequalities, active-set interval coverage, no-policy comparison and substitution into the original welfare function. A finite grid is useful as an error diagnostic but cannot replace the endpoint/stationary-point proof.

A strong memo recommends tax 16 only conditionally on baseline Cournot, the feasible menu, enforcement, the stated resource costs and the chosen welfare criterion. It explains why low damage or large administration favors no intervention, why high damage favors the standard here, and why asymmetry creates an allocation problem even when the total is correct. It identifies who bears price changes and harm without equating a surplus increase with unanimous approval. Its evidence plan specifies credible variation, timing, measurement and exclusions; its limitations distinguish sensitivity, estimation uncertainty, causal identification and institutional feasibility. Those explanations, together with a reproducible record, are the evaluation targets for open-ended portions.
