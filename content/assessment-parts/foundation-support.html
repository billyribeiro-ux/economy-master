Foundation examination and data practical

Connect a financial decision to a mathematical argument, an uncertainty model and a reproducible empirical calculation.

This assessment follows all four foundation volumes. Its purpose is to test whether you can choose and combine methods when the question no longer names the chapter. Two written forms, a practical analysis and oral transfer provide different kinds of evidence. The problems and datasets are original. Completing them does not establish expertise in the later economics, finance or research curriculum.

Administration and advancement

First finish the chapters and assessment packages for economic and financial reasoning, mathematics, probability and statistical decisions, and data and computation. Their exercises, projects and delayed checks supply the wider coverage on which this cumulative assessment relies. A single examination samples capabilities; it cannot examine every theorem and boundary case in 48 chapters.

Take one written form in 180 minutes, with closed notes and solutions, a basic nonprogrammable calculator, and paper for derivations. No internet, code execution or assistance is needed for the written problems. Code or pseudocode may be written where requested. State the target and units, show the argument and check feasibility or assumptions. Give enough intermediate reasoning that a reviewer can distinguish a method error from a minor arithmetic slip.

Each form has four essential domains worth 25 points each, with five 5-point parts per domain. The written gate is 85/100 overall and at least 19/25 in every domain. The domain threshold rounds 75% of 25 upward to a whole point. Follow-through credit can recognize a correct subsequent method using an earlier wrong number; it cannot award points for a false interpretation or a missing condition. Equivalent valid methods earn credit. The supplied scoring is criterion based, not a percentile ranking.

Complete the open-resource data practical separately. A planning allowance of 3–5 hours covers analysis, checks and a concise report; it is not a measured completion-time norm. Its separate gate is 85/100 and 19/25 in each of the same four domains. The benchmark is a reference for checking your completed analysis. Running it unchanged and copying its outputs is not evidence that you can construct or defend the workflow.

Then attempt the four oral transfer tasks without notes. Allow 5–8 minutes each, with a short preparation sheet. The oral gate is 34/40 overall and at least 8/10 per task; 8 is the whole-point threshold above 75% of 10. An examiner should request one explanation in ordinary language and probe an assumption or changed input. Independent grading is preferable. If studying alone, record your reasoning before opening the answer and keep a discrepancy log; label that evidence as self-assessment.

A critical error in units, budget reconciliation, information availability, probability conditioning, causal identification or integrity requires a demonstrated repair even if a total passes. Record the mistaken inference, its consequence, the corrected argument and a fresh changed-input answer. Genuine disagreements over an open decision are resolved through its stated loss, assumptions and evidence, not conformity to a preferred opinion.

Reserve the other written form for reassessment after targeted remediation, preferably after a 7–14 day interval. If you already studied its solutions, it is practice rather than an unseen test; ask a reviewer to change inputs and verify the new solution before using it as transfer evidence. Keep the practical as a separate requirement. Do not average a failed essential domain away using a strong unrelated score. The time allowances, thresholds and suggested intervals are this library's design choices; they are not university requirements or empirically calibrated guarantees.

The solution panels are intentionally accessible for self-study and expand when printing. For supervised closed-note work, provide only the question sections and supply the solutions after collection. This HTML file is instructional material, not a secure examination system.

Oral transfer: explain and adapt

Each task is worth 10 points with its allocation stated below. Use the given synthetic quantities exactly. The short answers are benchmarks; a valid equivalent derivation receives the same credit. In addition to obtaining a number, explain what would make it answer a different question.

  1. Opportunity cost and real units. You own an idle machine with historical book cost 120 USD. You can sell it today for 70 USD. Alternatively, retaining it and spending 20 USD today yields a certain 120 USD in one year, then no resale value. There are no taxes, other costs or intermediate payments. The effective nominal annual discount rate is 10%, and the price level will rise by 20%. Relative to selling now, compute the investment's incremental present value and explain which machine value belongs in it (4 points). Reproduce the same answer in today's purchasing-power units using the exact real rate (3 points). If the 20 USD improvement payment can instead be made in one year with everything else unchanged, calculate the increase in present value (3 points).
  2. A boundary and a value derivative. For real b, maximize f(x;b) = bx − x²/2 on 0 ≤ x ≤ 2. Derive the optimizer for every b and give a global uniqueness argument (4 points). Find the value function V(b), its first derivative and its behavior at b = 0 and b = 2 (3 points). At b = 3, reduce the upper bound from 2 to 1. Calculate the exact value loss and compare it with the first-order loss predicted by the original marginal value of capacity (3 points). Benefit units and choice units are abstract and fixed.
  3. A sampling law and a repeated search. A finite population consists of four labeled units with values (0, 0, 4, 4). Select two distinct units uniformly without replacement and average their values. List the sample-mean distribution, expectation and variance (5 points). Compare that variance with two independent draws with replacement from the same population and explain the difference (2 points). Under this fixed population, an alarm occurs when the mean is 4. What is its probability for one sample, and for at least one alarm in two independent samples when the population is reset before the second sample? Explain why reporting the selected alarm as if only one sample had been examined misstates the search procedure (3 points).
  4. Composition, timing and claims. A complete synthetic operating register reports March outcomes: group A has 9 failures among 90 cases and group B has 3 among 10. April has 1 failure among 10 A cases and 27 among 90 B cases. Calculate each month's overall failure rate and the change in percentage points (3 points). Standardize April to March's group proportions, then calculate both months under equal group weights; explain the disagreement with the raw change (3 points). April outcomes were released on 15 May. Can their realized failures be features for a forecast made on 1 May? Does the raw month-to-month change identify the effect of a new operating policy? State what is missing from each claim (4 points).
Oral transfer solutions and scoring

  1. The relevant investment at date 0 is 70 + 20 = 90 USD: retaining the machine forgoes its current sale proceeds. The historical 120 USD is sunk in this comparison. NPV = −90 + 120/1.1 = 210/11 ≈ 19.090909 USD, so retaining and improving exceeds selling under the supplied alternatives and discount rate. These are incremental values, not a restatement of historical accounting profit. 4 points: opportunity-cost explanation 2, correctly timed present value 2.

    The exact real gross rate is 1.1/1.2 = 11/12, hence rreal = −1/12. Next year's 120 nominal USD is 100 USD in today's purchasing power. Thus −90 + 100/(11/12) = 210/11 again. A negative real rate is consistent with the higher stated inflation; subtracting nominal inflation from the nominal rate is only an approximation. 3 points: gross-rate conversion 1, deflated cash flow 1, equality 1.

    Deferring the cost changes its present value from 20 to 20/1.1. The gain is 20/11 ≈ 1.818182 USD, and the new NPV is 230/11. Equivalently, discount the net year-one receipt 100 and subtract the forgone sale 70. 3 points: changed timing 1, gain 1, new-value reconciliation 1.

  2. Complete the square: f(x;b) = b²/2 − (x − b)²/2. The unique closest point to b in [0,2] is x*(b) = 0 for b ≤ 0, b for 0 < b < 2, and 2 for b ≥ 2. This constructs a feasible global optimum; strict concavity or strict squared-distance comparison establishes uniqueness, including the endpoints. Setting f′ = 0 alone would omit the binding boundaries. 4 points: all regimes 2, global existence/uniqueness argument 2.

    V(b) is 0 when b ≤ 0, b²/2 when 0 ≤ b ≤ 2, and 2b − 2 when b ≥ 2. Therefore V′ is 0, b and 2 respectively, with matching one-sided derivatives at 0 and 2. V is continuously differentiable; its second derivative does not exist at the transitions because the adjacent second derivatives are 0 and 1. The optimizer has kinks, which do not imply a kink in V itself. 3 points: value regimes 1, derivative 1, boundary distinction 1.

    At b = 3 the old optimum is 2 with value 4; the new optimum is 1 with value 5/2. The exact loss is 3/2. For capacity K near 2 with K < 3, Vcapacity(K) = 3K − K²/2 and its derivative at 2 is 1. A one-unit reduction therefore predicts a first-order loss of 1, understating the exact loss by 1/2. The derivative describes a local change, not an exact price for a finite change across a curved value function. 3 points: exact values/loss 1, marginal approximation 1, explanation of departure 1.

  3. The six unordered labeled pairs are equiprobable. One pair contains both zeros, four pairs contain one zero and one four, and one pair contains both fours. Thus the mean has values 0, 2, 4 with probabilities 1/6, 4/6, 1/6. Its expectation is 2 and variance is 4/6 + 4/6 = 4/3. Counting value patterns as if the three patterns were equally likely would be wrong. 5 points: labeled-pair counting 2, probabilities 1, expectation 1, variance 1.

    A single uniform draw has variance 4; two independent draws with replacement have mean variance 4/2 = 2. Without replacement the draws have covariance −4/3, so averaging gives (4 + 4 − 8/3)/4 = 4/3. The finite-population dependence reduces variance. 2 points: with-replacement result 1, dependence explanation 1.

    One alarm has probability 1/6. After a reset and an independent second sample, at least one has probability 1 − (5/6)² = 11/36 ≈ 0.305556. The selected event comes from a two-sample search; the relevant event is a union, not one prespecified alarm. This calculation is a property of the supplied finite sampling design, not the probability that an unspecified scientific hypothesis is true. 3 points: one-sample probability 1, union probability 1, interpretation 1.

  4. March is (9 + 3)/(90 + 10) = 12%; April is (1 + 27)/(10 + 90) = 28%, a rise of 16 percentage points. Both months have group rates 10% for A and 30% for B. 3 points: each overall rate 1, percentage-point change 1.

    March weights are 0.9 and 0.1, so standardized April is 0.9(0.1) + 0.1(0.3) = 12%, with zero standardized change. Equal weights give 20% in both months. The observed overall change comes from more weight on the higher-rate group, while the conditional rates stay fixed. Both raw and standardized summaries are correct for their respective populations; a chosen target determines which comparison answers the question. 3 points: fixed-March result 1, equal-weight result 1, population interpretation 1.

    On 1 May the realized April failures have not been released and cannot be permitted observed features. A forecast of April failures constructed using only then-available information would be a different variable and must be evaluated as such. The overall descriptive change also supplies no treatment definition, counterfactual or assignment justification identifying a policy effect. An observed census does not remove this identification problem. 4 points: release-time conclusion and distinction 2, causal-target/design gap 2.

Coverage, remediation and evidence record

Return to the indicated instruction, reconstruct a worked example, solve a changed-input problem without its answer, and explain the repaired argument. From foundations to economic mechanisms

You should now be able to trace a question from alternatives and units to a model, from the model to a derivation, and from data to a defensible calculation. You should also recognize when the last step does not identify the intended economic claim. A reliable foundation combines these abilities: correct arithmetic applied to the wrong population or counterfactual does not repair the question.
