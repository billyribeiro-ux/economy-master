Basic data-analysis practical — A repair desk's records and equipment choice

Format: open-resource individual analysis, 100 points in four essential domains of 25 points. Each domain has five parts worth 5 points. Allow roughly 3–5 hours for planning; this is an author estimate, not measured completion-time evidence. Use the accepted chapters, supplied files and standard-library Python. Advancement requires at least 85/100 and 19/25 in every domain, correction of critical errors, and the separate oral transfer. These are library design thresholds, not university rules or certification.

Every record and probability below is synthetic. The fictional repair desk has no actual customers, bank records or measured investment returns. April 2025 dates are invented scenario timestamps, preserved to test information availability. The case was authored on 7 October 2026 for the 6 October 2026 edition boundary. Your task is to make correct conditional calculations and explain their limits, not to present the result as evidence about a real firm.

Submit a short decision memo, a technical appendix answering the twenty parts, original inputs with their manifest, your executable analysis and preserved output, a discrepancy/test log and one independent calculation for each numerical domain. The equipment proposal is a separate, unexecuted decision at time 0 after the ledger closes. Its cash price is 500 USD. A purchase must leave immediate cash of at least 2,000 USD, with no new financing. The two later cash flows occur exactly one and two years after purchase. Use the stipulated effective annual nominal discount rate of 25%; all future amounts are nominal USD on the same price basis. This rate is an illustrative opportunity-rate assumption, not an APR, market quotation or empirical risk adjustment.

One state persists through both years. In the high state, probability 4/5, gross savings are 450 USD each year. In the low state, probability 1/5, gross savings are 250 USD each year. Using the space sacrifices 50 USD of rent at each year end. This is an incremental opportunity cost despite having no invoice in the historical ledger. No other cost, tax, salvage or terminal benefit occurs. Deferring produces zero incremental payoff in both states. Preferences are risk neutral; maximize expected discounted incremental USD subject to the cash reserve, and defer at an exact tie. These probabilities and savings are supplied assumptions; do not estimate them from the operating regression.

For a second representation of the decision, define statewise regret loss as the larger of buy's NPV and zero, minus the chosen action's NPV. These reported regrets compare both payoff alternatives before imposing cash feasibility; an infeasible purchase cannot be selected, whatever its unconstrained expected loss. Both the baseline and the 520-USD transfer below are feasible. Regret is measured in time-0 USD, distinct from a squared cost-forecast error in USD². Enumerate the two states before simulating. The optionality in this problem is choosing buy or defer before the state is revealed, not switching after seeing it.

Scoring, methods and critical errors

Each numbered part is worth 5 points. The instructor guide gives specific numerical targets and splits the points between calculation and interpretation. Equivalent correct derivations receive full credit. D1 is economic/accounting discipline; D2 mathematical derivation and exact checks; D3 probability and justified decisions; D4 auditable data/timing and reporting. A complete file submission is required for execution credit. Reserve conclusions about real institutions, statistical calibration and causality unless their required evidence is supplied.

Critical errors requiring repair include missing-as-zero imputation, a future revision used at the origin, counting a loan as revenue, mixing cents and dollars or USD and USD², changing simulation seeds to obtain the preferred action, treating model probabilities as estimated facts, or claiming that the OLS slope identifies causal cost. Repair the failure and explain a changed-case check before advancement even if the total score exceeds 85.

Accepted method routes: opportunity cost, stocks and balance sheets, present value, linear systems, moments, preferences and decisions, provenance, Monte Carlo, projection, identification, prediction timing and numerical audits. This practical reuses taught methods; no new external empirical source is being asserted.
