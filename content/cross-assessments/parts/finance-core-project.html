Integrated practical: Morrow's production order and Cedar's client promises

This open-resource project asks one question: may a reserve institution finance a productive order while meeting its own dated promises? A positive project NPV, a profitable accounting period and a positive expected portfolio surplus are three different pieces of evidence. You must connect them through actual ownership and cash. The supplied implementation is an instructor benchmark; submit your own equations and code before consulting it.

Suggested work: 10–14 hours plus a 20-minute defense, not a calibrated completion-time norm. Prerequisites are the accepted finance core in Volumes 7–11. This practical samples reconciled accounts, claim valuation, mandate-constrained allocation, credit protection, settlement and governance. It does not examine every detail of all 60 chapters, estimate empirical probabilities or certify professional practice. Every institution, CU amount, legal right, price and probability below is an original teaching assumption.

1. One contract, three terminal cutoffs

Morrow Components has a legacy operating business and owns an exclusive new production opportunity. A separate project company, or SPV, will undertake it. Cedar Reserve may buy part of the SPV's note. Cedar has already received 57.6 CU of client prefunding and 6.4 CU of sponsor equity: its opening cash is 64, not an unfinanced promise of future support. It owes clients 20 at 09:00 and 44 at 12:00 on terminal day T. All three cutoffs below are on that same day, not three years. The discount from entry t0 to any T cash is 0.9; intraday carry is zero. The difference between noon and 16:00 still matters because a deadline is a contractual constraint.

Parent and SPV property are separate. A reserve floor is a decision test, not a claimant inserted ahead of clients. All customer receipts and the equipment purchase are stipulated funded external payments at 12:00. Their payers' full balance sheets, the legacy parent's later debt service and the wider economy are outside the conservation claim.

3. What Cedar can buy, and who funds each payment

A separately endowed underwriter buys whatever fraction of the note Cedar does not buy. It has 50 cash. A new equity subscriber has 20 cash. The sponsor has 10 cash before paying 6.4; the client payer has 60 before paying 57.6. The bill issuer has 100 cash before receiving Cedar's bill subscription; its redemption is funded from these resources. An equipment seller and inventory supplier receive the original investment cash. No provider's unspent money is available to Cedar without a new contract.

An existing dealer owns one whole unit of each protection claim below before t0. Cedar buys a fraction from that dealer, not a newly written fraction of an underfunded promise. The dealer keeps every unpurchased fraction, including the whole unselected claim. Each unit's aggregate claim is fixed. Purchase prices go to the dealer; they do not top up either protection pool. The pools were separately endowed before t0 and cannot be pledged twice or used to pay the SPV's operating costs.

Cedar's note purchase incurs an execution fee of 0.1 per whole note in addition to its price. It chooses note fraction x from {1/2, 3/4, 1}. For each x it chooses exactly one of five protection actions: none; credit-exposed h=x/2 or h=x; escrow h=x/2 or h=x. Thus the complete menu has 15 policies. It cannot mix the two protection claims. Invest all remaining cash B in bills at 0.9 per terminal unit. There are no other fees or trades.

The mandate is to hold at least half the production note, pay both client deadlines in every supplied state, and retain terminal net capital of at least 2 in every state. Net capital is remaining cash minus any surviving client arrears, after actual late cures. Among qualifying policies maximize P-expected terminal net capital. Exact ties favor lower x, then lower h, then the alphabetically earlier claim label. The parent cash gate must also pass for the integrated approval. The minimum production allocation and the 2 capital floor are board choices, not regulatory ratios or universal investment advice.

Baseline and before-trade comparisons select a prospective funded entry policy. After a revenue, priority or delivery shock is discovered after entry, actual holdings remain the baseline half-note/quarter-escrow position. Comparing the other 14 policies then asks what would have survived had it been chosen at the original entry tickets. Such a historical-entry counterfactual supplies no unwind price, refund, rebalance cash or authority to switch now. The output labels this distinction and tests actual post-trade approval against the unchanged holdings. A change only to physical probabilities is a new prospective assessment, with the same market prices.

Report risk relative to the terminal net cash-only benchmark K₀=64/(9/10)−64, even though cash-only violates the production mandate. Define loss L=K₀−K. Report its physical mean, left 90% quantile and worst 10% average including only the needed fraction of a probability atom. Negative loss means outperformance of this stated benchmark. Risk reports do not replace either payment deadline.

5. Instructor benchmark: reconstruct before judging

D1 — Two cash routes and the parent's gate

Income is 100−60−20−10=10. Direct operating cash is 90−55−20=15. Closing receivables are 20+100−90=30; inventory 30+50−60=20; payables 20+50−55=15. Indirect operating cash is 10+10−(30−20)−(20−30)+(15−20)=15. The subtraction of a negative inventory change adds 10; the reduction in payables uses 5.

Closing assets are cash 25, receivables 30, inventory 20 and equipment 50, totaling 125. Payables 15, old debt 40 and equity 70 also total 125. Paying the remaining 15 trade payables leaves cash 10, exactly the retained operating floor. That payment is not a second expense. The new SPV does not supply this cash. The conclusion is this narrow operating gate, not a valuation or solvency opinion on the entire legacy parent.

Inspect sale contracts and delivery/acceptance evidence for recognition; match customer remittances and bank receipts to individual receivables; match supplier invoices, inventory receipts and dated payment instructions. A fictitious sale debits receivables and credits income in balance while overstating both assets and earnings. An early supplier payment can be arithmetically correct but violate an assumed cash date. Independent external evidence and the stated recognition convention therefore matter beyond debit/credit equality.

D2 — One investment, several claims

Operating income is (−20,10,40). Final unlevered cash is sales−30+10=(30,60,90): the inventory was paid at t0 and cannot be subtracted a second time from terminal cash. Equivalently add depreciation 20 and the 20 inventory expense already paid at t0 (subtract the inventory change of −20), then collect the equipment sale 10. This inventory adjustment is not an extra inventory-sale receipt. Project value is (1/5)30+(3/10)60+(2/5)90=60, so incremental NPV is 10.

The note pays (30,50,50), price 41. Equity pays (0,10,40), price 19. Their state payoffs and prices add to the project's; subtracting face 50 from enterprise value 60 gives the wrong equity value of 10. Debt proceeds 41 leave 9 of new cash equity to raise. The outside subscriber receives 9/19 of residual equity; the parent keeps 10/19, worth 10. An exclusive opportunity can explain that retained value while contributing no spendable CU to the SPV. The pre-existing parent opportunity owner bears its own opportunity cost; it is not an unexplained windfall created by the financing journal.

Initial book assets are 50, debt carrying value 41 and contributed book equity 9. Accreting the 9 discount produces pre-resolution income (−29,1,31) and book equity (−20,10,40). In the down state, the estate pays the note 30 and leaves a nominal unpaid claim of 20; the contractual shareholder payout is zero. A negative pre-resolution book residual is neither a negative shareholder distribution nor permission to delete the creditor. The modeled estate has no further recovery, but the nominal claim survives. The normal/up debt payoff is 50, and all residual equity cash goes to the two owners in their fractions.

At the chosen x=1/2, Cedar and the underwriter each pay 41/2 for the note; the equity subscriber pays 9. These cash sources total 50, paid as 30 to the equipment seller and 20 to the inventory supplier. The underwriter retains 59/2 cash; the subscriber retains 11. At T, Cedar/underwriter each receive (15,25,25); the outside equity holder receives (0,90/19,360/19); the parent receives (0,100/19,400/19). Each state's receipts add exactly to (30,60,90). In the down state each note holder separately retains a nominal unpaid claim of 10. The parent's legacy reserve of 10 is never in this waterfall.

D5 — A conditional institutional decision

Under every supplied baseline fact, approve the SPV's funded issue and Cedar's x=1/2,h=1/4 escrow strategy. Morrow's retained cash is exactly 10; Cedar pays every deadline and retains more than 2 in every state. State the narrow scope: the legacy debt's later service, states outside the table and the external counterparties' true financial condition have not been established. Do not approve a larger allocation solely because its expected capital is higher; the unhedged whole-note score 94/9 accompanies a real default.

The controller owns recognition and bank-statement reconciliation. The financing officer verifies the entire 50 cash subscriptions, issue price and equity register before spending. Treasury verifies bill redemption and actual receipt identifiers, custody segregation, the noon escrow release condition and the exclusive allocation of each pool. Risk independently challenges the down revenue, pricing/physical distinction, cap and omitted catastrophe state. An authorized committee approves the finite mandate and any bridge as a new contract; an operator cannot create authority by editing the model. Stop execution if entry funds, delivery priority or payment instructions are unverified. Report a deadline failure even after its cure, together with the amount, cause, claim identifier and corrective owner.

Require two-person confirmation of custody and payment evidence, reconcile dealer and Cedar unit ownership to one, and test the model against an independent payoff equation and transaction ledger. Preserve input hashes, code version, changed assumptions and rejected hypotheses. Reward correct escalation and accurate failure records, not a quiet terminal balance. These controls are reasoned design proposals, not estimated prevention probabilities. A counterparty attestation, a pricing quote, and a successful unit test answer different questions; none alone proves timely cash delivery.

The instructor should accept an explicitly funded and authorized alternative only as a changed contract, then grade its complete revised cash and rights rather than silently enlarging the baseline menu. If the student's independent code disagrees with the benchmark, require a minimal reproducible contract and reconciled recipients; do not award points merely for matching a saved JSON file.

Transfer 5 — A prior right changes available collateral

The senior holder receives 5 from the 20 pool first, leaving 15. In the down state the actual old partial holder receives 15/4 rather than 5; the dealer receives 45/4. Their nominal unpaid hedge rights are 5/4 and 15/4. Down capital falls to 65/36; other-state capital stays 145/18, giving mean 1115/144. Clients are paid but the capital floor fails. Full half-note escrow is the best historical-entry counterfactual: down capital 13/3, other capital 41/6 and mean 161/24. Full three-quarter protection also qualifies but has a lower mean; full whole-note protection has down capital 14/9 and fails. No unwind or new hedge purchase is provided, so this comparison is not an executable post-discovery switch.

Under the counterfactual half-unit escrow holding the pool would pay senior holder 5, Cedar 15/2 and dealer 15/2 in the down state, with aggregate unpaid hedge rights of 5 split as 5/2 each. In the other states, either holding pays the senior holder 5 and returns 15 to the writer, with no triggered hedge claim. The separate credit pool is unchanged and never fills the escrow gap. If known before a new trade, the delivered escrow cash would have state-price value 3; the stipulated discovery after payment instead leaves a historical purchase at 4 and a loss to its holders. The runtime deliberately rejects combining its revenue-only before-trade contract with this priority discovery.

Transfer 6 — An open incomplete-price interval

The note pays (10,30,50,50), equity (0,0,10,40). Let the first state price be t. Solving the bill, note and equity price equations gives π(t)=(t, 1/5−2t, 3/10+4t/3, 2/5−t/3). All components are strictly positive exactly when 0<t<1/10. Conversely every such vector prices the three securities correctly, so this is the full family. The claim price is 20[π₁(t)+π₂(t)]=4−20t, hence the open interval (2,4). At t=1/20 the vector is (1/20,1/10,11/30,23/60) and the claim costs 3. No physical probability is implied.

The subhedge 25 bills minus one-half note costs 2 and pays (20,10,0,0), no more than the claim. The superhedge 50 bills minus one note costs 4 and pays (40,20,0,0), no less than the claim. At a claim price of 2, buy the claim and sell the subhedge for zero entry cash; the resulting payoff is (0,10,0,0). At price 4, buy the superhedge and sell the claim for zero cash; the payoff is (20,0,0,0). Both are nonnegative with a strictly positive payoff in a possible state. Thus either endpoint contradicts the reference market's no-arbitrage assumptions with these unrestricted comparison positions. Positive interior prices are attainable by the displayed pricing family. The original three-state replication pays too much in the new catastrophe state, so it is no longer an exact hedge. This is a constructive finite proof, not a general continuous-time completeness claim or an executable short position authorized for Cedar.

7. Defense, reassessment and scope

Teach-back. In two minutes explain why 10, 19, 41, 50 and 60 can all be correct in the same project, and why a positive final reserve can coexist with a client breach. A complete explanation identifies respectively incremental opportunity value, residual-equity value, note proceeds, initial cash investment or note face (two different uses), and project value; it connects each to its dated cash or claim. It then uses the late-protection ledger, rather than a slogan, to show the missed deadline. The listener should be able to identify who pays each number.

Delayed retention. After 7–14 days, hide the table and reconstruct the selected down capital from its four t0 purchases and two client payments. Correct reconstruction is 64−20.5−0.05−1−0.1=42.35 invested in bills; 42.35/0.9−20+15+5−44=55/18. Then explain why deleting the fee, pooling the two writers' estates or moving a 16:00 receipt to noon changes the contract. Repeat the failed domain with a new timing or ownership transfer and an updated correction log. This protocol measures the submitted performance, not a guaranteed long-term competence result.

For remediation revisit statement reconciliation and debt book versus market value; claim-consistent free cash flow; finite-state pricing and investment mandates; incomplete prices and contingent rights; and loss atoms, dated funding and controlled decisions. This small case omits tax, stochastic continuous-time hedging, estimation error, legal adjudication, endogenous prices and whole-economy equilibrium. These omissions delimit the inference; they do not become zero risks in a real institution.

8. Reproducibility and source boundaries

Download original inputs, benchmark Python, baseline results, standalone instructions and the four-file manifest. Keep the four payloads together. The default command python3 /absolute/path/finance-core-analysis.py is quiet and writes the neighboring output file. An explicit --input INPUT --output OUTPUT works from another directory; python3 -O has the same behavior. Importing the file and calling run(data) is quiet, does not mutate its argument and returns strict JSON-safe data.

The model supports exactly three states and 15 policies, collections in [80,90], a down-sales loss in [0,10], strictly positive physical weights summing exactly to one, the two stated escrow cutoffs and a discovered senior claim in [0,5]. Other structural terms are deliberately fixed. Before-trade information reprices revenue only and cannot be combined with a discovered priority or delay. Each decimal's reduced rational denominator must be at most 1012; this is a denominator bound, not a count of decimal places. Exact rational arithmetic determines decisions. JSON rejects duplicate keys, nonfinite values, booleans as numbers and undeclared fields. Direct, resolved-path, symlink and hardlink aliases to inputs or protected payloads are refused before any output write. The separate checker reconstructs economic equations and uses focused counterfactuals; a manifest proves byte identity, not financial truth or actual settlement.
