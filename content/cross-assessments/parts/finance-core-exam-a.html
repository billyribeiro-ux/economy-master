Use a nonprogrammable calculator and blank paper; no notes, code, internet or solutions. Exact fractions or correctly labeled approximations earn credit. All amounts are in fictional currency units, CU. Dates, taxes, accounting policies, trading rights, prices and counterparty resources are stipulated teaching assumptions; no actual company, market observation or national legal rule is asserted.

The cases follow an operating company, a prospective investor and a treasury intermediary, but each restates its own complete information at a separate decision date. Case 3 — An investable objective and two return measures

The prospective investor also manages a separately endowed account with 100 CU. In this independent one-period market, a traded growth asset costs ten and pays terminal liquidation cash 18 in U or six in D, with no intermediate dividend. The physical probabilities are one half each. Safe cash has gross return one. No borrowing, shorting, fee, external flow or other asset is permitted in this prospective decision. Invest fraction w∈[0,1] in the growth asset and the remainder in safe cash. The mandate maximizes E[ln(W1/100)], where W1 is terminal wealth. The liquidation payments and safe cash are stipulated funded at that terminal date.

For a separate historical performance question about this manager's account, take two equal one-year periods. It begins with 100; immediately before an external contribution at the end of year 1, its value is 120. The owner then contributes 80 of separately funded cash, so year 2 begins with 200. Ending year-2 wealth is 180, with no other flows or fees. This realized account history is not an assertion that the prospective two-state opportunity repeats through time.

  1. Prove the funded global choice. Write terminal wealth in both physical states, derive the log-utility objective's first and second derivatives, and prove the unique global optimum on [0,1]. Report the actual CU holdings, number of growth-asset units, two terminal wealths and physical expected wealth. State the positivity condition making the logarithms and proof valid.
  2. Price and probability have different jobs. Derive the two strictly positive Arrow prices implied by the growth asset and safe cash. Use them to value the optimal terminal portfolio, and compare that price with physical expected wealth. Explain what changes, and what need not change, if the physical forecast changes while the traded prices and payoffs remain fixed.
  3. Audit the performance claim. Calculate each historical subperiod return, cumulative two-year time-weighted return and annual money-weighted internal rate of return. State and solve the dated cash-flow equation for the latter, restricting the annual gross factor to be positive. Explain why the two returns differ and why neither the 80% increase in account value nor a single favorable time-weighted result establishes investment skill.

Case 4 — A binary operating bonus and a constrained hedge

A separate Juniper contract buys from a hedge trust a European binary claim paying 30 CU at date 2 only if the terminal stock is 144; otherwise it pays zero. Juniper has 20 CU reserved at date 0 to pay the benchmark premium. The claim cannot be exercised early. In the benchmark, stock S0=100 and at each of two equal trading periods it multiplies by u=6/5 or d=4/5. Thus date-1 stock prices are 120 and 80; date-2 prices are 144, 96 and 64. There is no dividend. The borrowing and lending gross factor for each period is 11/10. Fractional positions and all required stock trades are allowed without transaction costs.

The trust receives the claim's replication premium and uses it entirely to establish its hedge. The bank position B is a dated CU asset if positive and an owed CU balance if negative. Stock-sale cash arrives before the corresponding bank and binary-claim settlement. All held stock secures the borrowing and cannot be separately pledged or spent. Controlled sales release title against lender repayment. The secured lender begins with 60 CU of separately available cash. Undrawn lender reserves are held at zero return; accrued loan interest increases its claim but does not arrive as cash before repayment. Stock counterparties have sufficient titled inventory (at least two shares available for these trades) and separately available cash of 200 at the relevant purchase cutoffs. Those funded-market assumptions are part of this finite model, not an audit of an entire economy.

At date 0 and after the date-1 rebalance, debt may not exceed (1−h) times the market value of held shares. The original haircut is h=1/4. The lending agreement permits the advance and controlled stock purchase to settle together against the completed collateral position. There is no separate pre-rebalance margin cutoff, no cash margin account and no further intermediate call before date 2. At date 2, controlled liquidation proceeds pay the secured loan, then the binary claim; any residual belongs to the trust's equity holder.

  1. Derive the finite hedge. Work backward from all terminal states to obtain the claim's value and stock/bank holdings at each date-1 node, then the date-0 holdings and premium. State the local pricing up probability implied by the traded stock and bank account. Verify the terminal claim in every path; no physical probability is supplied or needed.
  2. Show every rebalance is financed. At the up node, calculate the extra shares bought, their cash cost, the bank debt before and after rebalancing, and the lender's actual remaining cash after both advances. At the down node, show how stock sale repays the grown debt. Derive the largest haircut consistent with the original debt/share positions at the monitored dates and verify that h=1/4 is admissible.
  3. Change collateral access at the up node. After reaching S1=120, the permitted haircut becomes 1/3. Keep the binary obligation and required terminal hedge stock holding unchanged. A separate investor has five CU available at that date and may inject actual equity cash, receiving the entire remaining terminal residual after the loan and binary claim; no old shareholder has a duplicate right to that residual. Find the minimum injection, the resulting loan, and the investor's terminal receipts in both remaining states. Check the cash budget and the date-1 value of that new claim. Explain what is established if the investor delivers the cash, and what fails if it merely indicates interest in subscribing.

Case 5 — Gross network payments are not new outside resources

At a separate stress cutoff, two distressed counterparties A and B enter an enforceable simultaneous proportional clearing batch. Incoming batch receipts may finance outgoing batch payments. This is explicitly not sequential gross settlement with an unmodeled intraday credit line. A owes total face 12: one quarter to B and three quarters to an outside treasury bank T. B owes total face 16: one half to A and one half to other outside creditors. All those claims are due in the batch, and there is no fee or interest. External cash already available to A and B is two and three respectively. No other asset can provide batch cash. Every unpaid nominal claim remains recorded after a partial payment.

pA = min(12, 2 + pB/2),   pB = min(16, 3 + pA/4).

T owns all nine of A's outside face claim. Before this stress its stated balance sheet is cash two, that claim carried at nine, and a long asset carried at 20, funded by deposits of 20 and equity 11. In the joint stress, the long asset is marked at 18 and cannot be sold or borrowed against before the deadline. Carry the A claim at its actual clearing receipt under the chosen policy, receive that cash before T's payment, and recognize the impairment once. No recovery of the residual nominal A claim is included in the assessed horizon. T must pay eight of its deposit principal immediately after the batch; the other 12 is due later. When cash is insufficient, pay all available cash and retain the unpaid deposit. There is no automatic insurance, state facility or debt cancellation.

An independent donor has six CU of its own usable cash and actual authority to make irrevocable grants buying no claim or ownership. One permitted route gives x≥0 to A and y≥0 to B before the batch, subject to x+y≤6; replace their external cash by 2+x and 3+y. An alternative route gives cash directly to T before its deposit deadline. Compare the routes under the narrow mandate of paying T's eight on time with the smallest donor outlay. No broader welfare ordering is stipulated.

  1. Clear and reconcile the original batch. Solve the displayed equations, verify the active cap regimes, and prove uniqueness using the maximum absolute coordinate difference between two possible fixed points. Compute each outside group's receipts and reconcile them to the original external cash. Retain the unpaid nominal claims; do not count internal network payments as additional external wealth.
  2. Trace the bank's loss and deadline. Compute T's marked equity before payment, its actual available cash, deposit payment, missed amount and remaining deposit liability. Reconcile the post-payment balance sheet and surviving nominal A claim. Explain why adding A and B's aggregate unpaid faces to T's six-unit claim impairment would not produce a valid further loss for T.
  3. Prove and govern a funded response. Solve the grant-funded clearing system for every x+y≤6 and prove that its caps remain slack throughout that domain. Derive the global minimum total upstream grant that permits T to pay eight, including the allocation, both batch payments and outside receipts at equality. Compare the minimum direct grant to T and identify who receives the difference between the routes. Give a conditional decision under the stated narrow mandate, naming actual funding/delivery evidence, responsibility and one fact or broader objective that would require a new decision.
