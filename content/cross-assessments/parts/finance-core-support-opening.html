Cumulative assessment · Volumes 7–11

Finance core: accounts, value, investment and institutions

Defend a financial decision from the transaction that creates a claim to the date on which someone must fund it. Reconcile reported performance, economic value, portfolio risk and actual settlement before recommending an action.

This package follows the accepted chapter and assessment work in accounting, corporate finance, investments, fixed income and derivatives and banking and institutional risk. Its cases require connections across those subjects. It samples essential capabilities; it does not retest every result in all sixty chapters or establish a learner’s professional authorization.

Administration and evidence of mastery

Complete the diagnostic before either written form. Attempt Form A first and reserve Form B for reassessment after correcting the mechanisms behind any errors. Each form contains five 20-point domains, for 100 points. Work without notes, code, internet access or solutions; a nonprogrammable calculator and blank paper are allowed. Suggested time is 300 minutes per form. That is an author’s planning estimate, not a measured completion time or evidence that the forms are statistically equated. A grader may arrange accommodations and record the actual administration conditions.

All case quantities, probabilities, prices, rights and funding commitments are original teaching assumptions unless explicitly identified otherwise. Do not import a country’s tax treatment, accounting rule, deposit guarantee, insolvency power or market convention into an explicitly stipulated contract. Identify the date, currency, owner, availability and priority of each resource. If a required action needs a new source of money or permission, name it and recompute the affected claims.

The practical is open resource. Produce an independent analysis and implementation before examining its supplied reference output or instructor guide. A working benchmark is a comparison tool; executing it does not demonstrate that you can reconstruct the accounts, justify the valuation or detect a model’s unsuitable use. Retain your original submission, corrections, input provenance and explanation of changed conclusions.

  • Written advancement: at least 85/100 overall and 15/20 in each domain, followed by satisfactory unfamiliar transfer and teach-back. Use the same criterion for reassessment, with a fresh attempt rather than editing the first response while reading the answer.
  • Practical advancement: at least 85/100, at least 15/20 in each of its five essential domains, reproducible evidence, and a defensible response to the assigned transfer. A plausible recommendation alone is insufficient.
  • Critical errors: invented cash or authority; deleting a surviving claim; treating a financing inflow as operating profit; inconsistent cash flow and discount rate; substituting physical probabilities for pricing weights without justification; spending collateral twice; funding an earlier payment with an unbridged later receipt; presenting a fitted model as independently validated; or altering evidence to conceal a failure. Correct each such error and pass a new transfer before advancement, regardless of total score.
  • Grading: carry a transparent arithmetic error through consistent later work without repeatedly deducting for it. Deduct separately for a new conceptual error. Accept an alternative recommendation when its original-contract calculations are correct and its changed assumptions, funding, rights and trade-offs are explicit. Do not award points merely for reproducing the instructor’s policy preference.

These thresholds are the library’s criterion-based design. They are neither admissions requirements nor a credential. Research originality, investment skill, observed teaching and real institutional judgment require evidence and independent human evaluation beyond completing these synthetic assessments.

Five essential domains and their connections

DomainRequired reasoningA connection that must survive the calculation
D1 · Accounts and operating evidenceReconcile transactions, accrual income, operating cash and estimates; identify the claim behind an entry.An allowance changes carrying value without supplying cash; a forecast must reconcile its financing requirement.
D2 · Value and financingChoose incremental cash flows, consistent discounting and the correct enterprise-to-claim bridge.Debt face, debt proceeds, ownership dilution, book equity and economic value have different meanings.
D3 · Investment and evidenceSeparate physical risk from claim pricing, solve a feasible allocation problem and assess implementation or performance evidence.A return target or fitted premium does not remove covariance uncertainty, costs, liabilities or trading constraints.

The domain scores support diagnosis; they do not imply that a question belongs to only one volume. A funded acquisition, for example, may require all five domains. The examinations identify the outcomes they sample, and the practical combines them in one documented decision.

Prerequisite diagnostic · 40 points

Suggested time: 60 minutes, under the written-form resource rules. Each item is worth five points. Attempt all eight before opening the solutions. Advance to the written form at 34/40 or higher, with at least four points each on items 1, 2, 6 and 7, and no unresolved critical error. Otherwise use the remediation routes below and take a changed version of the failed mechanism.

  1. Profit and actual cash. A fictional company records revenue 100 CU and collects 80 CU. Gross receivables begin at 15 and end at 35. Cash operating expenses are 55 and depreciation is 10. There are no other expenses, accruals, taxes or interest. Cash begins at 12; the company pays capital expenditure 20, receives new borrowing 8 and pays a dividend 5. Compute income, operating cash flow and closing cash. Show why neither the borrowing nor capital expenditure belongs in this period’s stated income calculation.
  2. The claim being valued. EBIT is 40 CU, the stipulated unlevered cash tax rate is 25%, depreciation is 8, capital expenditure is 15 and the increase in operating net working capital is 5. Financing interest of 4 is reported separately. Compute FCFF. An independently justified operating enterprise value is supplied as 180; debt market value is 50 and unrestricted surplus cash is 10. Compute equity value and explain why one period’s FCFF does not, by itself, establish the supplied enterprise value.
  3. A funded total return. A share costs 80 CU, pays a dividend of 4 during the holding period and ends at 88. For this exercise the dividend is held as cash without interest; there are no fees or intermediate contributions. A separate portfolio begins with 100: 60 invested in this stock return and 40 in a bond with total return −5%. Compute the stock’s total return, each holding’s final wealth, and the portfolio’s total return. Do not add the dividend a second time.
  4. Two kinds of weights. In a complete one-date, two-state teaching market, Arrow claims paying one CU in states U and D cost 0.4 and 0.5 CU respectively. Physical probabilities are 0.7 and 0.3. Price a claim paying 20 in U and zero in D, compute its physical expected payoff, and derive the gross return on a traded claim paying one in both states. Explain why the two claim quantities differ.
  5. Dates on a curve. A bond pays 5 CU at date 1 and 105 at date 2. The matching zero-coupon discount factors are 0.96 and 0.90, with no credit loss or transaction costs. Compute its full price and the net forward rate for the one-period interval from date 1 to date 2. State the investment comparison that establishes that forward rate.
  6. Replication, including its debt. A stock costs 50 CU and will be worth either 65 or 40 at the next date. It pays no dividend. The bank account has gross return 1.05; borrowing and lending at this rate, fractional shares and the required short positions are permitted without frictions. A European call has strike 50. Derive the stock holding, initial bank position and call price. Verify the terminal portfolio in both states and state the risk-neutral up probability implied by these traded assets.
  7. A valuable firm can miss a payment. A firm has assets 200 CU, liabilities 170 and equity 30. Its assets include usable cash 4 and a receivable of 12 that will be paid only after an obligation of 9 is due. No other resource arrives at that first cutoff. An outside lender has five CU of separate available cash and offers to deliver five before the obligation; repayment 5.5 is due after the receivable is collected. Compute the initial payment gap and the two cash balances if the bridge is executed and all stated payments occur. Explain the effect of collecting the receivable on income.
  8. The first cash flow in a terminal value. A complete perpetual contract pays FCFF of 12 CU at date 1, then grows that payment by 2% each year forever. The appropriate constant annual discount rate is 8%. There are no separate terminal assets or additional cash flows. Compute the continuation value immediately after the date-1 payment and the total value at date 0. Explain the convergence condition and where the date-1 cash enters.
Diagnostic solutions and five-point rubrics
  1. Income 35; operating cash 25; closing cash 20 CU. Income is 100−55−10=35. Operating cash is receipts less paid expenses, 80−55=25; equivalently 35+10−(35−15)=25. Capital expenditure is an asset investment under the stipulated accounts; depreciation, rather than the whole purchase, is the current expense. Borrowing creates an equal cash asset and financing liability, not revenue. Closing cash is 12+25−20+8−5=20. Receivables reconcile as 15+100−80=35. Credit five: income one; operating cash and receivable bridge two; closing cash one; correct investment/financing classification one.
  2. FCFF 18; equity value 140 CU. Unlevered after-tax operating income is 40(1−0.25)=30. FCFF=30+8−15−5=18. Financing interest is excluded from this cash flow available before distributions to capital providers; subtracting it here while using an enterprise discounting approach would change the object being valued. Equity=180+10−50=140 because surplus cash is outside the supplied operating value and debt is another claim on the combined resources. A value needs the relevant future cash-flow path, risk and discounting assumptions; a single observed 18 does not supply them. Credit five: FCFF computation two; interest treatment one; enterprise/equity bridge one; limitation of a single period one.
  3. Stock return 15%; closing wealth 69 and 38; portfolio return 7%. The stock delivers 88+4=92 on an initial 80, so its total return is 12/80=0.15. The stock allocation ends at 60(1.15)=69, already including its proportionate dividend; the bond ends at 40(0.95)=38. Total final wealth is 107 and the funded return is (107−100)/100=0.07. Equivalently 0.6(0.15)+0.4(−0.05)=0.07. This is a one-period total return under the specified dividend convention, not a time-weighted attribution for an account with outside flows. Credit five: stock return one; two terminal holdings two; portfolio return one; correct dividend/contribution treatment one.
  4. Price 8; physical expected payoff 14 CU; risk-free gross return 10/9. Replicating the claim requires twenty U Arrow claims, costing 20(0.4)=8. Its physical mean is 0.7(20)+0.3(0)=14. One claim in each state costs 0.4+0.5=0.9 and pays one surely, giving gross return 1/0.9=10/9. State prices combine discounting and the price of state-contingent delivery; physical weights describe the assumed frequencies. Multiplying the physical mean by an arbitrary discount factor is not a substitute for the specified market’s replicating price. Credit five: price one; physical mean one; sure claim and gross return two; interpretation one.
  5. Price 99.3 CU; net forward rate 1/15, approximately 6.6667%. Value each date separately: 5(0.96)+105(0.90)=4.8+94.5=99.3. The no-arbitrage gross forward factor is D(0,1)/D(0,2)=0.96/0.90=16/15; subtract one to get 1/15. Buying one date-1 CU now costs 0.96; agreeing to reinvest that CU at the forward rate must deliver the same date-2 amount that 0.96 buys directly in the date-2 zero, namely 0.96/0.90. A yield to maturity is not supplied or needed. Credit five: dated price two; forward factor and net rate two; matched-date investment argument one.
  6. Hold 3/5 of a share and a bank position −160/7 CU; call price 50/7 CU. The payoff difference is 15−0 across a stock difference 65−40=25, so Δ=3/5. In the down state the stock delivers 24 and the bank must deliver −24; its initial position is −24/1.05=−160/7. Initial portfolio value is (3/5)50−160/7=50/7. At maturity it delivers 39−24=15 in U and 24−24=0 in D. The implied up weight satisfies 50(1.05)=65q+40(1−q), giving q=1/2; discounting its expected call payoff gives the same 50/7. No physical probability is needed. Credit five: stock position one; initial bank amount one; price one; both state cash checks one; implied pricing weight one.
  7. Early gap five; funded cash zero at the first cutoff and 6.5 later. Positive equity 30 does not change 9−4=5 unavailable at the deadline. The lender’s actual five gives 4+5−9=0 after paying the obligation. Later, collection 12 and bridge repayment 5.5 leave 6.5. The lender supplies cash from its stated endowment and receives principal plus 0.5 interest; the bridge is a new claim with a later maturity. Collection exchanges the receivable for cash and is not another twelve of revenue; the 0.5 bridge interest is a financing expense under the stated transaction. Credit five: deadline gap one; dated balances two; new funded liability/interest one; collection classification one.
  8. Continuation at date 1 is 204; value at date 0 is 200 CU. The first payment belonging to the continuation is date-2 cash 12(1.02)=12.24. Immediately after receiving date-1 cash, the remaining stream is worth 12.24/(0.08−0.02)=204. Hence date-0 value is (12+204)/1.08=200, also 12/(0.08−0.02). The discounted geometric ratio 1.02/1.08 is below one, so the infinite sum converges. The date-1 payment is included once outside its after-payment continuation; including it again inside that continuation would double count. The contract fixes growth and discounting forever, a strong assumption to challenge in a real valuation. Credit five: correct continuation numerator/date one; continuation value one; present value one; convergence condition one; correct first-payment accounting one.
