Graduate microeconomics: administration and prerequisite routes

Each form has 100 points: five essential domains, four five-point parts per domain. Allow 300 minutes per form as an uncalibrated design choice. Work individually, closed notes except a basic calculator; exact derivations and equivalent valid proofs earn full credit. Later computational reproduction can audit arithmetic but does not replace the written proof. The advancement rule is 85 overall and at least 15 in each domain, plus correction of critical incentive, funding, theorem-domain or identification errors and a changed-primitive oral defense. These thresholds do not claim institutional equivalence or human certification.

Form A constructs and certifies; Form B diagnoses and reconstructs different economies. The field sampling is explicit, not an examination of every result in the literature. Prerequisite routes: preferences, duality, risk and information, equilibrium, welfare, static games, dynamic games, private information, contracts, mechanisms, matching, and theory critique. Scored cross-volume transfer appears in A5 through local causal effects and in B5 through likelihood and identification. All numerical populations and observations are synthetic; no current law or empirical calibration is asserted.

Form A — Construct and certify

1. A smooth consumer with a costly price change — 20 points

A consumer has goods x,y≥0, positive prices p,q and wealth m≥0. Utility is U=xy/(x+y) when x+y>0 and U(0,0)=0. This is a maintained cardinal index only when explicitly used under risk; the present problem is deterministic. Parameters are (p,q,m,pnew,qnew)=(1,4,18,4,4). No outside transfers are implicit. Hicksian demand minimizes expenditure subject to U≥u, for u≥0. Define compensating variation CV=e(pnew,qnew,uold)−m and equivalent variation EV=m−e(p,q,unew).

  1. Prove global Marshallian demand and indirect utility for every p,q>0,m≥0. Treat boundary wealth, and justify global rather than only stationary optimality. (5 points)
  2. Derive expenditure and Hicksian demands for every nonnegative target. Prove the expenditure minimum is attained and give the old allocation and utility. (5 points)
  3. Calculate the compensated price-derivative matrix at the old prices and old utility. Prove symmetry, negative semidefiniteness and the price-null direction for general positive prices. (5 points)
  4. Calculate the new Marshallian allocation, CV and EV. Explain why these two exact money metrics differ and why adding uncompensated consumer spending changes is not a welfare calculation. (5 points)

2. Owned production, pollution and an actual tax rebate — 20 points

Two households each own ten units of a numeraire good and half of one firm. A firm converts X² numeraire units into X≥0 units of another good, whose consumer price is p; numeraire price is one. Household i has utility θᵢlog xᵢ+yᵢ−X²/2 and yᵢ≥0, with (w1,w2,theta1,theta2)=(10,10,2,1). Households take aggregate pollution X and prices as fixed when choosing; the firm ignores damage. They receive net profits, not gross revenue. A government may tax each output unit by t≥0 and rebate all tX equally, as privately fixed lump sums. All choices, technologies and emissions are contemporaneous.

  1. Construct the unique untaxed positive-price competitive equilibrium. Derive all private optima, output, household purchases, profits and numeraire allocations; close both resource markets. (5 points)
  2. Maximize the sum of the two stated cardinal utilities over feasible allocations. Prove the global planner solution, including optimal division of the produced good, and explain the failure of the undistorted welfare theorem. (5 points)
  3. Choose a unit tax supporting that planner allocation. Calculate the firm’s net price/profit, tax receipts, equal rebates and both households’ numeraire consumption. Verify every private optimality condition and goods account. (5 points)
  4. Compute each household’s utility change from the untaxed outcome under this exact rebate and ownership rule. Is it a Pareto gain? Explain why the conclusion depends on the funded incidence rule rather than efficiency alone. (5 points)

3. A price agreement that must survive every history — 20 points

Two identical firms repeatedly set simultaneous prices in [0,4]. Each date one buyer purchases exactly one unit at the lower price if it is at most four; equal prices split the sale equally. Cost is zero. Flow profits are the only firm payoff and discounts are δ∈[0,1); use unnormalized discounted sums. Prices and sales are public. In the proposed grim strategy both post four until a detected deviation, then both post zero forever. In a variant, after any compliant cooperative date an independent public false alarm arrives with probability ε and also triggers permanent zero pricing. A unilateral off-rule price is detected with certainty. The numerical variant is (delta,epsilon)=(3/4,1/5). Buyer values are fixed at four, no collection or enforcement cost is hidden.

  1. Find every pure Nash equilibrium of the one-date price game. Give a complete argument over the continuous price interval, including equal and unequal prices. (5 points)
  2. For ε=0 derive the exact δ set supporting the stated grim profile as a subgame-perfect equilibrium. Handle undercutting’s unattained supremum and prove sufficiency against arbitrary multi-date deviations. (5 points)
  3. Derive the cooperative value and exact incentive threshold with false alarms. Evaluate the numerical variant and check punishment histories. Do not reinterpret the random alarm as private information. (5 points)
  4. For a known finite number of repetitions, establish the pure-strategy subgame-perfect price outcome by backward induction. Compare buyer surplus and total surplus under perpetual high-price cooperation and zero pricing, and state why an observed price alone cannot establish the repeated-game mechanism. (5 points)

4. Efficient trade and the person paying its incentive transfers — 20 points

One seller owns an indivisible object with private opportunity cost c; one buyer has private value v. They are independent uniform on [0,1], quasilinear, with money sufficient for any stated payment. Nontrade utilities are normalized to zero; trade yields buyer v−payment and seller receipt−c. A direct deterministic mechanism trades iff v>c, and does not trade at equality. No-trade reports make no payments to either party. Require dominant-strategy truthfulness and ex-post participation. An outside sponsor may cover deficits but values only its own retained cash. The domain is (value lower,value upper,cost lower,cost upper)=(0,1,0,1). A separate balanced alternative is a single posted price p∈[0,1], with trade when c≤p≤v and acceptance at equality.

  1. Derive the efficient allocation and its expected total gains from trade. Prove why efficiency applies state by state and specify the equality case. (5 points)
  2. Derive all trade payments compatible with the stated efficient allocation, no-trade payment normalization and DSIC. Prove both necessity and sufficiency using all lower and higher reports, including thresholds. (5 points)
  3. Show why this normalized efficient mechanism cannot balance its budget ex post. Calculate the expected subsidy and the smallest reserve that covers every type report, distinguishing an attained requirement from a supremum. (5 points)
  4. Among single balanced posted prices find the global maximum of expected gains from trade. Calculate the exact loss relative to efficiency and explain what this establishes about the restricted institution, not all Bayesian mechanisms. (5 points)

5. A training encouragement that raises wages and lowers real surplus — 20 points

A unit population has three types with masses, earnings gains Δ=Y(1)−Y(0), and real training costs C given by ((1/4,1/2,1/4),(2,4,-2),(1,5,1)). Y(d) is individual real net output accruing as income, with no omitted employer loss or other production cost; C is the additional real resource cost of training. Everyone has Y(0)=10, knows their own type, is risk neutral and can finance training; d∈{0,1}. Training gives net private gain Δ−C+s, where s is a subsidy paid only if training occurs; at a tie train. An independent randomized encouragement Z sets s=0 or s=2. No spillovers, hidden outcomes, general-equilibrium wage response, attrition or direct subsidy effect on measured earnings is present. An outside taxpayer finances every subsidy from its own numeraire wealth. The proposal calls the randomized Wald wage effect the welfare gain from universal training.

  1. Derive training choices under both assignments, compliance types, the first stage and the earnings intention-to-treat effect. State why monotonicity holds for these choices. (5 points)
  2. Derive the Wald ratio from the potential-outcome/compliance decomposition, and compare it with the population average gross earnings effect of universal training. Which population does the ratio describe? (5 points)
  3. For moving the entire population from s=0 to s=2, compute subsidy spending, trainees’ aggregate private utility change, the outside payer’s change and consolidated resource welfare. Reconcile the positive wage response with the welfare sign. (5 points)
  4. Give a research and transport protocol that could evaluate a real version of the proposal. Include preassignment funding constraints, a treatment-specific outcome, uncertainty/dependence, one exclusion failure and a prespecified stopping or revision rule. Distinguish facts supplied in this synthetic model from evidence not yet collected. (5 points)
Form A: all twenty solutions and additive rubrics

1. A smooth consumer with a costly price change: solutions

A1a

On a positive budget U is positive at an interior affordable bundle, whereas either axis has utility zero. Exhaustion follows from strict monotonicity in each good in the interior. For x,y>0, ∇²U=−2/(x+y)³ times the matrix ((y²,−xy),(−xy,x²)), whose quadratic form is −2(yh₁−xh₂)²/(x+y)³≤0. Along a positive-price budget line the zero-curvature radial direction cannot be tangent; hence the restricted objective is strictly concave. The FOCs give y/x=√(p/q). Combining with px+qy=m gives x=m/[√p(√p+√q)], y=m/[√q(√p+√q)], v=m/(√p+√q)². The feasible budget is compact, so a maximum exists; the feasible interior stationary point and concavity make it the unique global choice. At m=0 the sole bundle is zero and v=0. These arguments also exclude unexamined axis optima for m>0.

Scoring: 2 for budget/domain and interior argument; 2 for global concavity, demands and indirect value; 1 for zero-wealth endpoint.

A1b

For u>0 an expenditure minimizer must have both goods positive and bind U=u; reducing both goods radially would otherwise lower cost. Equivalently 1/x+1/y=1/u. The candidate hₓ=u(1+√(q/p)), hᵧ=u(1+√(p/q)) meets that equality and costs e=u(√p+√q)². Its global minimality follows from the Marshallian formula just proved: any bundle costing less than e has maximum attainable utility less than u, a contradiction. This also establishes attainment and uniqueness. At u=0 positive prices make the unique cheapest bundle zero. At the old prices and wealth, (x,y,u)=(6,3,2). The dual expression is derived from the full feasible choice problem, not inferred from a local envelope identity.

Scoring: 2 for binding target and Hicks construction; 2 for attainment/global minimum and old values; 1 for zero target.

A1c

Differentiate h: Hxx=−u√q/(2p^(3/2)), Hxy=Hyx=u/(2√(pq)), Hyy=−u√p/(2q^(3/2)). At the old point, ((-2,1/2),(1/2,-1/8)). Symmetry is explicit. For any direction z, zᵀHz=−[u/(2√(pq))](√(q/p)z₁−√(p/q)z₂)²≤0. Multiplying the matrix by (p,q) gives zero in each row, the degree-zero Hicksian response to rescaling all prices. At u=0 the whole matrix is zero. These are compensated restrictions for the stated smooth positive-price domain; an arbitrary fitted demand system need not satisfy them.

Scoring: 2 for general derivatives and numeric matrix; 2 for square-form negativity and null direction; 1 for compensated-domain scope.

A1d

At the new prices the demand is (x,y,u)=(9/4,9/4,9/8). Old utility costs 16·2=32 at the new prices, so (CV,EV)=(14,63/8), because old prices require 9·(9/8)=81/8 to attain the new utility. CV uses the old reference indifference curve at new prices; EV uses the new curve at old prices. Income effects therefore make the two monetary numbers differ even though they agree on the direction of harm. Actual expenditure remains eighteen before and after; that equality plainly does not imply unchanged welfare. Either money metric is conditional on the maintained preference and price counterfactual, not an estimate from an expenditure total alone.

Scoring: 2 for new allocation and both money metrics; 2 for reference-utility interpretation; 1 for expenditure versus welfare.

2. Owned production, pollution and an actual tax rebate: solutions

A2a

At any p>0, the firm maximizes pX−X², a strictly concave coercive objective, so X=p/2 and profit p²/4. Each household’s income is 10+profit/2>θᵢ, so its unique optimum has xᵢ=θᵢ/p and yᵢ=income−θᵢ: strict concavity in x and the budget give sufficiency. Clearing gives 3/p=p/2, hence p=√6, X=√6/2, (profit,y1,y2)=(3/2,35/4,39/4). The purchases are (2/√6,1/√6). Their sum is X; y₁+y₂=37/2=20−X². Price zero gives no finite maximizer of household log demand, and the positive root is unique. Damage is −3/4 for each, omitted from private incentives but not from the subsequent welfare calculation.

Scoring: 2 for private global choices and clearing price; 2 for profits and two goods accounts; 1 for zero-price exclusion/uniqueness.

A2b

For any X>0, maximizing 2log x₁+log x₂ subject to x₁+x₂=X gives x₁=2X/3,x₂=X/3, uniquely by strict concavity. Numeraire feasibility binds at y₁+y₂=20−X²; its division does not change this equal-weight objective while consumptions stay nonnegative. Reduced welfare is 20−2X²+3log X+2log(2/3)+log(1/3). Its derivative 3/X−4X vanishes at X*=√3/2, and its second derivative is −3/X²−4<0. Welfare tends to minus infinity at zero; the candidate is feasible, so it is the unique optimal positive output and division of x. 3/4 is X*². Pollution is an uncompensated external effect. Competitive private budgets omit its marginal social cost; the hypotheses of the no-externality welfare argument fail.

Scoring: 2 for optimal division and reduced welfare; 2 for global output and shutdown boundary; 1 for externality theorem limitation.

A2c

At the target, household demand requires p*=3/X*=2√3. The firm needs net price p*−t=2X*=√3, so t=√3, equal to aggregate marginal damage 2X*. Firm profit is (p*−t)X*−X*²=3/4. (revenue,rebate1,rebate2,y1,y2)=(3/2,3/4,3/4,73/8,81/8). Each income is 10+3/8+3/4, and subtracting expenditure θᵢ gives those y values. Thus x₁=1/√3,x₂=1/(2√3), and y₁+y₂=77/4=20−3/4. The tax is paid from consumer receipts, then returned; it is not destroyed or counted as extra production. Firm strict concavity and household strict concavity certify their choices at these privately fixed tax/rebate terms.

Scoring: 2 for supporting gross/net price and tax; 2 for profit, rebates and household accounts; 1 for private sufficiency/resources.

A2d

Each numeraire allocation rises by 3/8, and each pollution loss falls by 3/8. Each x purchase is multiplied by 1/√2. Therefore gains are (3/4−log2,3/4−(log2)/2), approximately (0.056852819440,0.403426409720). Both are strictly positive, so this funded implementation is a Pareto improvement among the two owners/consumers; government has zero net transfers and uses no goods. Efficiency alone would not guarantee this: redirecting all receipts or changing ownership could leave one household worse off. The calculation relies on observable emissions, costless collection, known preferences and the stated lump-sum rebates, not a general claim about environmental tax incidence.

Scoring: 2 for both exact utility differences; 2 for Pareto classification with payer included; 1 for funding and ownership scope.

3. A price agreement that must survive every history: solutions

A3a

If both prices are the same positive p, posting p−h with 0<h<p/2 wins the whole sale and exceeds p/2, so the pair is not Nash. If prices differ and the lower price is positive, the high-price firm can undercut it slightly and earn a positive amount rather than zero. If the lower price is zero and the higher is positive, the low-price firm can raise its price to a strictly intermediate positive number and retain the whole sale. Thus only (0,0) remains. At that pair, a higher price loses the sale and a zero price earns zero; negative prices are infeasible. Both firms are best responding, proving the unique pure equilibrium. No characterization of all mixed equilibria is needed for this question or for the stated pure punishment.

Scoring: 2 for equal-price deviations; 2 for unequal-price and zero cases; 1 for verification and pure-strategy scope.

A3b

Compliance gives V=2/(1−δ). Any strict undercut p<4 yields p now and zero continuation; prices above four are excluded. Undercutting has supremum four, which is not attained because price four is compliance. All deviations are unprofitable exactly when V≥4, or 1/2≤δ<1. If δ<1/2, V<4 and an actual p strictly between V and four is profitable. At equality every strict undercut gives less than V; the limiting supremum does not break the equilibrium. Punishment is the stage Nash pair after every history. For arbitrary deviations truncate them after N dates and revert to the proposed strategy. Replacing the last deviation by compliance weakly raises payoff by the one-date comparisons, then work backward. The remaining tail difference is at most 4δᴺ/(1−δ), which vanishes. This supplies the all-history sufficiency argument rather than only a local incentive calculation.

Scoring: 2 for exact threshold including unattained supremum; 2 for all-history truncation and punishment; 1 for threshold necessity witness.

A3c

The public compliant continuation remains cooperative with probability 1−ε, so V=2+δ(1−ε)V, or V=2/[1−δ(1−ε)]. A deviator still earns less than four immediately and receives zero subsequently. Thus the exact condition is δ(1−ε)≥1/2; if it fails, an attainable undercut strictly between V and four is profitable. At the supplied values, (continuation factor,value)=(3/5,5), so compliance beats every unilateral undercut. Alarm histories prescribe the same credible zero-price Nash continuation. Discounted payoffs remain bounded, so the preceding finite-deviation truncation also proves sufficiency under public random alarms after integrating over the common signal law. False alarms lower the value of maintaining the agreement; they do not change the payoff of the prescribed punishment.

Scoring: 2 for continuation value and general threshold; 2 for numeric value and credible punishment; 1 for public-signal/all-history scope.

A3d

In the last date every subgame must use the unique pure stage Nash outcome (0,0) when restricted to pure stage continuations. Within the requested pure-strategy class, the last-date continuation payoff is zero for either firm at every history, so the penultimate date has the same unique pure best-response outcome; induction yields zero prices throughout. Under high-price cooperation buyer surplus is zero and firms jointly receive four; under zero pricing the buyer receives four and firms zero. Total real surplus is four in either case because quantity, value and production cost are fixed. The model illustrates redistribution, not a quantity-based deadweight loss. Similar prices can arise from different costs, demand or conduct; evidence on these primitives, monitoring and relevant deviations is needed to identify this mechanism.

Scoring: 2 for pure finite-horizon induction; 2 for buyer/firm and real surplus comparison; 1 for mechanism identification limits.

4. Efficient trade and the person paying its incentive transfers: solutions

A4a

Trading changes the two participants’ total real utility by v−c. The efficient rule therefore trades for v>c, declines for v<c, and is indifferent at equality; the question selects nontrade there. Expected gains are ∫₀¹∫c¹(v−c)dv dc=1/6. Equivalently the maximum net gain is max{v−c,0} in every realized state; integrating this pointwise upper bound proves global expected efficiency for the stipulated allocation objective. Transfers cancel between buyer and seller only if any outside sponsor is also accounted for; transfer feasibility will be a separate constraint.

Scoring: 2 for pointwise efficient allocation and tie; 2 for exact expected gains integral; 1 for transfer versus resources.

A4b

Fix a seller report c<1. Every winning buyer report must have the same payment: otherwise a type that wins at the higher payment can keep the allocation while reporting the lower-payment winner. Call it P. Losing types v≤c obtain zero, so DSIC for them requires v−P≤0; take v↑c to obtain P≥c. Winning types v>c can lose for zero, so v−P≥0; take v↓c to obtain P≤c. Hence P=c. Symmetrically, fixing v>0 forces the common seller receipt on all cost reports below v to be R=v: otherwise winners choose the larger receipt, and the winning/losing IC limits pin the threshold. Endpoint profiles without trade need no trade payment. With these payments, the buyer chooses whether to obtain surplus v−c and the seller whether to obtain v−c; truthful reports select the nonnegative option, with zero at ties. This verifies DSIC and ex-post IR globally over all reports, not only derivatives.

Scoring: 2 for constant winner transfers and threshold necessity; 2 for all-report DSIC sufficiency and IR; 1 for endpoint/no-trade normalization.

A4c

At every traded profile the seller receives v but the buyer pays c. The sponsor must contribute v−c>0. Thus the allocation plus normalization and incentive restrictions cannot also have no deficit at every profile. Expected subsidy is the same integral as gains, 1/6. A reserve of 1 covers every report, and is necessary because (v,c)=(1,0) requires one: here the maximum is actually attained, even though that single point has probability zero under the continuous law. Both parties then obtain v−c, whose sum double counts the social gain unless the sponsor’s loss is subtracted. Consolidation restores total v−c. This direct threshold proof is narrower than a general impossibility theorem with Bayesian incentives or different no-trade transfers.

Scoring: 2 for statewise deficit and expected subsidy; 2 for attained reserve and sponsor consolidation; 1 for restricted impossibility scope.

A4d

At price p, trade occurs on 0≤c≤p≤v≤1. Its expected gains are ∫₀ᵖ∫p¹(v−c)dv dc=p(1−p)/2. The second derivative is −1, so the unique global maximum on the compact price interval is p=1/2; endpoints give zero. The maximum is (gains,first-best loss)=(1/8,1/24). Each trade charges and pays the same p, so no outside subsidy is required and truthful accept/reject choices are individually optimal. The loss consists of beneficial pairs on the same side of the posted price that cannot trade through this institution. This proves optimality only within the announced single-price class; it does not establish the best revenue-balanced Bayesian direct mechanism or erase distributional differences between the buyer and seller.

Scoring: 2 for expected posted-price gains integral; 2 for global optimum and exact efficiency loss; 1 for institutional scope and balance.

5. A training encouragement that raises wages and lowers real surplus: solutions

A5a

With zero subsidy, Δ−C is (1,−1,−3), so only the first type trains. With subsidy two the gains become (3,1,−1), so the first two types train. They are respectively always takers, compliers and never takers; no defiers exist because adding a nonnegative conditional subsidy cannot reverse a strict training preference. (takeup0,takeup1,first stage,earnings ITT)=(1/4,3/4,1/2,2). Mean earnings are 21/2 and 25/2. This is an encouragement effect on realized choice, not an assignment that forces everyone to train. The maintained financing premise rules out a separate liquidity constraint that could alter this choice table.

Scoring: 2 for all private choices and compliance types; 2 for first stage and earnings ITT; 1 for monotonicity/funding premise.

A5b

Random assignment makes observed mean earnings differences equal E[Y(D(1))−Y(D(0))]. Under exclusion, always and never takers’ two terms cancel; monotonicity leaves only compliers, giving ITT=P(C)E[Y(1)−Y(0)|C]. The first stage is P(C), positive here. Dividing gives (Wald,universal gross earnings ATE)=(4,2), because the ATE is (1/4)2+(1/2)4+(1/4)(−2)=2. The ratio identifies the wage effect for the half of the population induced by this offer under these assumptions. It neither averages over never takers nor subtracts training’s real cost, and a different encouragement can induce a different group. These are the precise V12 local-effect conditions used in a microeconomic choice problem.

Scoring: 2 for complier decomposition and assumptions; 2 for Wald and universal ATE; 1 for estimand population and cost distinction.

A5c

Total subsidy spending is two times the trained mass 3/4, hence 3/2. Always takers receive an additional two each, adding 1/2 in aggregate; compliers gain one each after their cost and subsidy, adding another 1/2. Thus (trainee gain,payer change,net resource gain)=(1,-3/2,-1/2). Equivalently only compliers change real training: each produces four extra earnings goods at cost five, so mass one half generates −1/2 net goods. Wage ITT is +2, but additional training costs total 5/2. Subsidy payments cancel upon consolidation; paying existing trainees is a transfer windfall, not extra production. Universal training would average gross gain two and cost three, giving net −1, also different from the policy-induced change. All welfare signs here use the stipulated linear utility and real-cost interpretation.

Scoring: 2 for subsidy and individual gains; 2 for payer and consolidated resource calculation; 1 for wage-versus-welfare interpretation.

A5d

Before assignment measure eligibility, baseline liquidity and the costs the offer actually changes; preserve the randomization unit and a funded budget for expected and feasible realized take-up. Record training completion, later earnings over a prespecified horizon, and real resource/time costs separately from subsidies. Analyze assignment first, then a local-effect ratio only if exclusion, monotonicity and a nonzero first stage survive; use uncertainty matching the design, including clustering or interference if training is delivered in groups. A direct employer wage subsidy or information effect of the offer on nontrainees would violate the stated earnings exclusion. Do not extrapolate to never takers or a universal mandate without additional effect and cost information. One prospective revision rule is to withhold expansion if the lower confidence bound on the funded net-benefit criterion fails a specified standard, or if measured capacity costs exceed the prefunded limit. The numbers here are synthetic known primitives; no actual data access, randomized field implementation or causal result has been established.

Scoring: 1 for assignment, liquidity and funding; 1 for separate earnings/cost outcomes; 1 for valid inference and uncertainty; 1 for exclusion and transport threat; 1 for prospective revision rule.

Form B — Diagnose and reconstruct

1. A valid expenditure function with disconnected compensated choices — 20 points

A consumer has U(x,y)=max{x,2y} on the nonnegative orthant, positive prices and wealth. A report claims that any continuous monotone utility has convex preferences, a unique Hicksian demand, and a differentiable expenditure function. The diagnostic price, wealth and target are (p,q,m,u)=(1,2,6,6). For a policy comparison raise p to3/2, leaving q=2 and wealth6. This problem concerns complete choice correspondences, not a selected point renamed as a unique optimizer.

  1. Find the entire Marshallian choice correspondence for arbitrary p,q>0,m>0 and at the diagnostic budget. Prove global optimality and explain why an interior budget mixture can be strictly worse than either endpoint. (5 points)
  2. Derive the expenditure function and full Hicksian correspondence for u>0. Prove global expenditure minimization despite the nonconvex upper contour and calculate the correspondence at the diagnostic target. (5 points)
  3. Diagnose the differentiability claim at p=1,q=2. Derive the directional derivative of expenditure and a supergradient that is not an actual Hicksian bundle. Explain the relevant convexity distinction. (5 points)
  4. Compute compensating variation for the stated price increase and all new choices. Does zero CV imply no reallocation or identify a unique pre-reform demand? State the minimal repairs to the report’s claims. (5 points)

2. A fixed cost that a marginal price cannot pay — 20 points

A representative household owns one indivisible firm and ten numeraire goods. Its utility is 3log(1+x)+y with x,y≥0. The firm may shut down at zero cost, or produce any X>0 using 1+X² numeraire units. There is one actual plant, no lotteries or fractional activation. Output price is p≥0 and the numeraire price one. Net profit is distributed to the owner. A report sets price equal to marginal cost, deletes the fixed cost from profit, and declares a competitive equilibrium. Primitives are (endowment,taste,fixed cost,quadratic coefficient)=(10,3,1,1). An authority can instead make a lump-sum payment only when the plant operates, financed by an equal household lump-sum tax; it cannot create goods.

  1. Derive household demand and the complete firm supply correspondence at every price. Prove there is no deterministic competitive equilibrium with the original unsubsidized plant. (5 points)
  2. Find and globally verify the efficient deterministic allocation, including shutdown as a feasible alternative. Explain why first-order conditions alone would miss the relevant comparison. (5 points)
  3. Construct an operating subsidy and consumer price that support the efficient positive allocation. Give a precise activation tie rule and reconcile household income, firm net profit and real resources. (5 points)
  4. At output one-half compare actual plant cost with the cost predicted by averaging shutdown and production of one. Diagnose a proposed convexification-based equilibrium proof and state what added institution or aggregation assumption would be required. (5 points)

3. The joint information law matters even when mean output fits — 20 points

Two firms choose quantities qᵢ∈[0,4] simultaneously after privately observing own constant marginal cost cᵢ∈{0,2}. Inverse demand is P=max{8−q₁−q₂,0}; profit is qᵢ(P−cᵢ), with production financed from sufficient owner wealth. Initially costs are independent, each state equally likely. A report uses the complete-information formula q(c)=(8−c)/3 and argues that matching mean total output proves Bayesian equilibrium. The common primitives are (intercept,low cost,high cost,low probability,quantity cap)=(8,0,2,1/2,4). In a second economy the costs are perfectly correlated: both zero or both two with probability one-half, and firms know that joint law.

  1. Derive the unique Bayesian equilibrium in the independent-cost economy, proving global private best responses and uniqueness without assuming equal type strategies from the outset. (5 points)
  2. Test the reported strategy against each type’s actual conditional best response. Compute the equilibrium mean total quantity and expected profit per firm, and explain how the wrong strategy can match that mean. (5 points)
  3. Solve the perfectly correlated economy and compare mean total quantity, total-quantity variance and expected firm profit with the independent case. Date the information that lets own cost reveal the rival’s cost. (5 points)
  4. Give a concrete evidence design capable of distinguishing the two information structures. State why matching marginal cost frequencies or a mean quantity is insufficient, and distinguish identifying a joint law from assuming players knew it. (5 points)

4. An efficient assignment does not identify its payoff division — 20 points

Workers1,2 and firms A,B can form at most one pair each. A pair (i,j) produces transferable surplus Sᵢⱼ, split into worker utility uᵢ and firm utility vⱼ; unmatched utility is zero. No outside subsidy exists. Pair surplus is net of all real costs and can fund the transfers. Matrix rows are workers and columns firms: ((8,6),(7,1)). A feasible stable payoff vector is nonnegative, has equality uᵢ+vⱼ=Sᵢⱼ on matched pairs, and permits no pair to give both members a strict improvement. A report selects the efficient assignment and calls the wage vector unique. A technology perturbation reduces S₂A from7to5, leaving everything else unchanged.

  1. Find the efficient assignment, including comparison with partial matching. Prove the pairwise payoff inequalities necessary for stability and show why they rule out all blocking coalitions in this transferable one-to-one model. (5 points)
  2. Derive the full stable payoff polytope supporting the efficient assignment. Give two different interior or boundary payoff divisions, reconcile total surplus, and diagnose the uniqueness claim. (5 points)
  3. After the technology perturbation determine the efficient assignment and its complete supporting payoff inequalities. Check whether the original vector (u₁,u₂,vA,vB)=(3,1,6,3) remains feasible and stable; construct a funded replacement. (5 points)
  4. Explain which conclusions rely on transferable utility, verifiable pair surplus and outside options. Give one explicit data comparison and one institutional deviation that a research claim about observed wages would need to address. (5 points)

5. The same insurance choice can hide different risks and tastes — 20 points

An individual has wealth10 and exogenous loss6 with probability p∈(0,1), no hidden effort, no loading requirement and no borrowing constraint. A contract pays benefit b∈[0,6] after loss and charges premium h before the state. A risk-neutral individual has utility c; a CARA individual has utility −exp(−ac), a>0. Accept insurance at indifference. A report observes an exact reservation premium of3 for full benefit6 and declares that p=1/2 and preferences must be risk neutral. Compare that world N with world R having (wealth,loss,full reservation,partial benefit,offer premium,R probability)=(10,6,3,3,5/3,1/3) and a=log2/3. For a prospective extra risk sample there are90 independent untreated observations of the same population,30 losses; treat these as synthetic counts, not real data. Contracts and losses are fully enforced; realized insurer solvency requires capital even when expected profit is positive.

  1. Derive the complete CARA (a,p) family with full-insurance reservation premium3, and its risk-neutral limit. Verify that worlds N and R are observationally equivalent for the original observation. (5 points)
  2. Derive the CARA reservation premium for partial benefit3 along that family. Compare acceptance of the stated offer in N and R and calculate its expected insurer profit in any world that actually accepts. Does one binary response point-identify the continuous family? (5 points)
  3. Derive the global binomial maximum-likelihood loss probability from the reserved risk sample and the implied CARA a if the full-premium restriction is treated as exact. State the domain restriction and why finite sampling uncertainty prevents an automatic exact structural conclusion. (5 points)
  4. Prove that an exact partial reservation premium strictly identifies a within the positive-CARA family once the full reservation is fixed. Propose a prospective choice/risk validation design and identify a concrete failure that would invalidate transport of the inferred risk law to the insured population. (5 points)
Form B: all twenty solutions and additive rubrics

1. A valid expenditure function with disconnected compensated choices: solutions

B1a

For every affordable bundle, x≤m/p and 2y≤2m/q, so U≤max{m/p,2m/q}. Each upper bound is attained by the corresponding axis endpoint. If p<q/2, only (m/p,0) attains the larger bound; if p>q/2, only (0,m/q) does; if p=q/2, precisely the two endpoints attain it. Any bundle with both goods positive spends on the other good and falls strictly below the common bound in each component. Thus at the diagnostic budget the choice set is ((6,0),(0,3)). Its midpoint (3,3/2) has utility three, whereas each endpoint has utility six. Continuity and monotonicity are satisfied, but convex preferences are not: the upper contour is a union of two halfspaces, not their intersection.

Scoring: 2 for complete three-branch demand set; 2 for global bound and strict midpoint loss; 1 for convexity versus continuity.

B1b

U≥u means x≥u or y≥u/2. On the first feasible branch the cheapest bundle is (u,0), cost pu; on the second it is (0,u/2), cost qu/2. Therefore e=u min{p,q/2}. Pick only the cheaper endpoint when prices are unequal in that comparison and both endpoints at equality. Positive prices exclude any extra positive good from a cheapest bundle. At the supplied prices/target, (expenditure) = 6 and the two Hicksian bundles are exactly the two Marshallian endpoints above. At u=0 the unique cheapest bundle would be zero. The expenditure function remains concave and degree one in prices: it is the pointwise infimum of feasible linear expenditure functions, regardless of whether the feasible upper contour is convex.

Scoring: 2 for two expenditure branches and full Hicks set; 2 for global minimum and attainment; 1 for concavity of price value versus preferences.

B1c

For a direction d that preserves positive prices locally, e((1,2)+hd,6)=6[1+h min{d₁,d₂/2}] for h>0 sufficiently small. Its directional derivative is 6 min{d₁,d₂/2}, which is not linear in d; no ordinary gradient exists. Both minimizing bundles are supergradients of this concave price function, and so is every convex combination. In particular (3,3/2) supports e from above, since min{p,q/2}≤(p+q/2)/2. But that vector has utility three and cannot be a Hicksian choice for target six. A superdifferential can convexify minimizing bundles; it does not turn their infeasible midpoint into an attainable demand. Unique smooth demand and differentiability require extra regularity, not utility continuity alone.

Scoring: 2 for directional derivative and nondifferentiability; 2 for nonchoice supergradient construction; 1 for convexification versus attainability.

B1d

At new prices the y endpoint is strictly cheaper per utility unit. Every optimal choice is therefore the single bundle (0,3), still delivering six; expenditure for old utility is 6 min{3/2,1}=6. Thus 0. A household previously selecting (6,0) reallocates completely, while one already selecting (0,3) does not move. Zero compensation for the maintained target neither identifies the old tie selection nor implies unchanged goods demand. Repair the report by distinguishing continuity/monotonicity from convexity, using set-valued demand at ties, and stating price differentiability only where the active expenditure branch is unique or where a proved envelope theorem applies.

Scoring: 2 for new demand and exact compensation; 2 for selection-dependent reallocation; 1 for precise regularity repairs.

2. A fixed cost that a marginal price cannot pay: solutions

B2a

The firm’s best positive output is p/2 when p>0, with maximal active profit p²/4−1. Consequently supply is zero for p<2; at p=2 it is the set {0,1}; for p>2 it is p/2. At zero price shutdown is also optimal. Income is ten plus nonnegative optimal profit, always greater than three, so household demand is x=max{3/p−1,0} at p>0 with the residual held as y. At p=0 utility grows without bound as free x rises, so demand has no maximizer. For 0<p<2 demand is positive and supply zero. At p=2 demand is one-half, absent from {0,1}. For 2<p<3, supply exceeds one and demand is below one-half; at p≥3 demand is zero and supply positive. Every price is excluded. The familiar active FOC root would lie below the price needed to cover fixed cost.

Scoring: 2 for complete firm supply and household demand; 2 for exhaustive price exclusion; 1 for zero-price/nonconvex scope.

B2b

Shutdown gives utility ten. Active welfare is 9−X²+3log(1+X), with feasibility 1+X²≤10. Its derivative is −2X+3/(1+X), strictly decreasing, and vanishes at X*=(√7−1)/2. Its second derivative is −2−3/(1+X)²<0, proving a unique active maximizer within the resource set. The feasible active choice X=1 already yields 8+3log2>10: for example integrating 1/t on[1,2] or a strict lower bound gives log2>2/3. Thus the active maximum beats shutdown. (output,numeraire)=(0.822875655532,8.322875655532). The numeraire is 9−X*². A global comparison of activation and shutdown is indispensable because the feasible production set has a fixed-cost discontinuity.

Scoring: 2 for active optimum and real feasibility; 2 for strict global concavity and shutdown comparison; 1 for need for activation comparison.

B2c

Set p*=2X*=√7−1 and pay s=1−X*²=√7/2−1 only to an operating plant, using a tax s on its owner. The firm’s active profit is p*X−1−X²+s, uniquely maximized among positive outputs at X*. At that output it equals zero, tying shutdown; explicitly select operation when indifferent. Household income is 10−s plus zero net profit, and its interior demand 3/p*−1 equals X* by the planner FOC. It consumes y=10−s−p*X*=9−X*². The numerical subsidy is 0.322875655532. Consumer payments plus the subsidy cover 1+X*² exactly, and the household tax equals that subsidy. Both accounts consolidate to x production using its actual resources. This is an implementation with an activation selection rule, not robust strict activation or a recovered unsubsidized equilibrium.

Scoring: 2 for supporting subsidy/price and private choices; 2 for household tax and firm/real accounts; 1 for activation tie and scope.

B2d

The deterministic plant cost at X=1/2 is (actual,convexified)=(5/4,1). A half-weight average of shutdown (output0,cost0) and operation at one (output1,cost2) has mean output one-half and mean cost one. That average is not physically attainable by this single deterministic plant. A lottery would deliver random output rather than one-half surely; concave household utility does not generally value that lottery at utility of expected output. A continuum of independently activated plants with appropriately scaled ownership/resources, or a genuinely divisible activation technology, would be a different economy requiring its own pooling and clearing assumptions. Convexifying the set silently deletes the very nonconvexity responsible for nonexistence.

Scoring: 2 for actual and convexified costs; 2 for physical infeasibility and lottery distinction; 1 for required alternative institution.

3. The joint information law matters even when mean output fits: solutions

B3a

Since each quantity is at most four, total output never exceeds eight; within this domain profit conditional on type is qᵢ(8−cᵢ−E[qⱼ|cᵢ]−qᵢ). It is strictly concave, so the global best reply is the clipping of (8−cᵢ−E qⱼ)/2 to[0,4]. Under independence every type of a firm faces the same rival mean. On the four-dimensional type-strategy cube, conditional averaging has sup-norm Lipschitz constant one and the one-half coefficient plus clipping makes the reply map a contraction with constant one-half. Iterates are Cauchy by a geometric increment bound; continuity gives a fixed point, and contraction gives uniqueness. The interior solution has each rival mean 7/3 and (q low,q high,mean firm quantity)=(17/6,11/6,7/3). These coordinates satisfy every unclipped reply and the caps. The symmetry follows from uniqueness, not an unsupported initial restriction.

Scoring: 2 for conditional global best replies; 2 for full contraction existence/uniqueness and solution; 1 for symmetry derived rather than assumed.

B3b

The proposed type quantities are (8/3,2), whose mean is also7/3. Against that mean, the correct best replies remain (17/6,11/6): low-cost firms should raise quantity by1/6 and high-cost firms lower it by1/6. Strict concavity makes both deviations profitable; each profit gain is (1/6)²=1/36. At equilibrium the conditional expected margin equals own optimal quantity, so conditional profit is q(c)². Hence (mean total,expected firm profit,profitable deviation gain)=(14/3,205/36,1/36). Aggregating cancels the opposite type-level errors. A mean fitted exactly therefore fails to verify the Bayesian first-order conditions, much less the full conditional distribution.

Scoring: 2 for both type deviations and gain; 2 for mean output and expected profit; 1 for aggregate fit limitation.

B3c

Under perfect correlation, observing own cost before choosing reveals the rival’s cost with certainty. Each realized subgame is a complete-information Cournot game with equal costs. Its contraction best replies give q(c)=(8−c)/3, so type quantities are (8/3,2); this formerly incorrect formula is now correct. Mean total quantity remains14/3. In the independent economy total quantities are17/3,14/3,11/3 with probabilities1/4,1/2,1/4, yielding variance1/2. Under correlation they are16/3 and4 equally, giving variance4/9. (independent variance,correlated variance,correlated firm profit)=(1/2,4/9,50/9). Profits average[(8/3)²+2²]/2. The marginal type distribution alone has not changed; the conditional information relevant to strategic choice has.

Scoring: 2 for revealed rival type and correlated equilibrium; 2 for variance and profit comparison; 1 for dated joint information scope.

B3d

Collect synchronized pre-choice firm-level cost measures, market membership and dated output/price records, with a sampling design that preserves which firms competed. Joint cost frequencies or conditional rival cost given own cost distinguish independence from perfect comovement; quantity variance and type-conditioned choices provide separate overidentifying implications in this maintained demand/cost model. Demand shocks, measurement errors or capacity variation can also move output variance, so a credible design measures or separates those alternatives rather than assigning every covariance to information. Survey or institutional evidence about what firms observed and when is needed to support the assumed knowledge structure: the econometrician learning a law does not prove firms knew it at decision time. Freeze prediction targets and use uncertainty respecting market-level dependence; report unavailable information instead of treating a good mean fit as structural verification.

Scoring: 1 for joint cost and market observations; 1 for reserved output restrictions; 1 for uncertainty/dependence; 1 for demand or measurement confound; 1 for players knowledge versus statistical law.

4. An efficient assignment does not identify its payoff division: solutions

B4a

The diagonal matching produces8+1=9; the cross matching produces6+7=13. A single pair produces at most8 and an empty matching zero, so the cross assignment is uniquely efficient. Stability requires uᵢ+vⱼ≥Sᵢⱼ for every pair: if their current total is smaller, divide the positive gap to make both strictly better off. Conversely, if this inequality holds, no two members can both improve using only their pair surplus. For any larger coalition, add the pair inequalities over a proposed reassignment and the nonnegative outside utilities of unmatched members. Its feasible total production cannot exceed its members’ current total utility, so it cannot make every member weakly better with at least one strictly better. This additive certificate uses disjoint one-to-one production and transferable utility; there is no coalition synergy beyond pair sums.

Scoring: 2 for all assignment totals and efficiency; 2 for pair inequalities and coalition proof; 1 for transferable-additive domain.

B4b

Cross-pair budget equalities give vB=6−u₁ and vA=7−u₂. Nonnegativity requires0≤u₁≤6 and0≤u₂≤7. The two unmatched-pair inequalities are u₁+7−u₂≥8 and u₂+6−u₁≥1, equivalently (lower,upper)=(1,5) for u₁−u₂. These conditions are jointly necessary and sufficient, by the previous global coalition argument. Two examples are ((3,1,6,3),(4,1,6,2)) in (u₁,u₂,vA,vB) order; each sums to13 and satisfies the inequalities. Unique efficient matching identifies who produces together here, not how its surplus is divided. Choosing a point requires bargaining, competitive normalization or another selection institution.

Scoring: 2 for complete payoff polytope; 2 for two funded stable divisions; 1 for allocation versus payoff uniqueness.

B4c

With S₂A=5, cross surplus is11, still strictly above diagonal9 and every single pair. Equalities become vB=6−u₁,vA=5−u₂, with0≤u₁≤6,0≤u₂≤5. Stability requires3≤u₁−u₂≤5. The old vector is not even funded: its worker2–firmA sum is7>5. Keeping old worker utilities would also imply vA=4 and leave worker1–firmA total7<8, creating a block. A replacement is (4,1,4,2), whose cross totals are6and5, unmatched totals8and3 meet the8and1 constraints, and utilities sum to11. Lower total surplus and changed support inequalities restrict the division, but still leave a set rather than a unique wage prediction.

Scoring: 2 for new assignment and complete inequalities; 2 for old failure and funded replacement; 1 for remaining payoff multiplicity.

B4d

The proof allows arbitrary monetary transfers that move utility one-for-one, enforceable pair contracts and no liquidity limit; nontransferable preferences or wage restrictions can invalidate its additive inequalities. Verifiable S must include costs and outside options, and unmatched opportunities may change the relevant core. An empirical comparison should measure pair productivity opportunities, match assignments and worker/firm outside payoffs before and after a plausibly external technology shift, rather than using a single wage vector to infer all S entries. An institutional deviation such as a binding nonnegative firm-profit or contractual wage cap must be included before predicting a new match or transfer. With unobserved bargaining selection, even unchanged feasible surplus can generate changed wages. Thus an efficient observed match is evidence compatible with the model, not proof of stability, full information or a particular wage-setting mechanism.

Scoring: 1 for transferable utility assumption; 1 for outside options and contracts; 1 for measured opportunity set; 1 for institutional constraint; 1 for unobserved bargaining selection.

5. The same insurance choice can hide different risks and tastes: solutions

B5a

Under CARA, uninsured expected utility is −e^(−10a)[1−p+p e^(6a)]. Full insurance at premium H has utility −e^(−10a)e^(aH). Indifference at H=3 therefore requires e^(3a)=1−p+p e^(6a), so p=(e^(3a)−1)/(e^(6a)−1)=1/(1+e^(3a)). This gives the full continuum for a>0, with0<p<1/2. As a↓0 its limit is1/2, matching risk-neutral willingness to pay expected loss6p=3. At a=log2/3, p=1/3, verifying world R; N has p=1/2 and linear utility. One observed full-insurance willingness to pay confounds tastes and risks, even when measured without noise. No source theorem is needed: both models satisfy the same explicit expected-utility equality.

Scoring: 2 for complete positive-CARA family; 2 for risk-neutral limit and two-world equality; 1 for observational equivalence.

B5b

For benefit b, indifference gives h=(1/a)log{[1−p+p e^(6a)]/[1−p+p e^(a(6−b))]}. At b=3 on the restricted family, put r=e^(3a); the numerator is r and the denominator2r/(1+r), hence h₃=log[(1+e^(3a))/2]/a. World N pays at most3p=3/2, so rejects5/3. World R pays 1.754887502163=3log(3/2)/log2, so strictly accepts. Its expected insurer profit is 2/3=5/3−(1/3)3. There is no N contract sale at this price, so quoting its hypothetical margin as realized profit would be wrong. A single accept/reject response only imposes an inequality on h₃(a), leaving an interval of parameter pairs; it separates these two candidate worlds without point-identifying the continuum.

Scoring: 2 for partial reservation derivation and choices; 2 for actual accepting-world insurer account; 1 for set versus point identification.

B5c

The risk sample likelihood is proportional to p³⁰(1−p)⁶⁰. The log derivative is30/p−60/(1−p), positive below1/3 and negative above, and the endpoints give zero likelihood. Thus its unique global maximum is 1/3. Combining an exact full reservation3 with a population p<1/2 implies a=(1/3)log[(1−p)/p], yielding log2/3 at the fitted probability. At p=1/2 this tends to the risk-neutral boundary; p>1/2 is incompatible with positive CARA in this exact family, rather than an admissible negative estimate of risk aversion. Ninety Bernoulli observations are noisy evidence, so uncertainty in p and in a measured reservation premium must be propagated through a justified joint procedure. The sample estimate alone does not prove the population equals world R or establish the structural expected-utility model.

Scoring: 2 for global likelihood maximum; 2 for structural inverse and probability domain; 1 for joint finite-sample uncertainty.

B5d

Let f(a)=log[(1+e^(3a))/2], so f(0)=0 and f″(a)=9e^(3a)/(1+e^(3a))²>0. For a>0, derivative of f(a)/a has numerator g(a)=af′(a)−f(a). Since g(0)=0 and g′(a)=af″(a)>0, the partial reservation premium is strictly increasing. Its limits are3/2as a↓0 and3as a→∞; hence an exact interior value in that range uniquely determines positive a and then p. A prospective design would reserve independent untreated loss outcomes, elicit several funded contracts with randomized presentation and prespecified tie treatment, and validate out-of-sample choice restrictions with an uncertainty analysis. Insured selection, effort induced by coverage, changed eligibility or aggregate shocks could make the untreated p inapplicable. In particular an observed coverage-induced prevention change invalidates the maintained exogenous-risk comparison and stops that transport claim. Account for benefit reserves and selection of actual accepters; these are proposed tests, not completed empirical verification.

Scoring: 2 for strict monotonicity proof and range; 2 for prospective choice/risk validation; 1 for selection or effort transport failure.
