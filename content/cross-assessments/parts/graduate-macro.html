Macro-finance Form A — construct an economy and its evidence

This graduate field examination samples five essential domains through original models. Each form has five20-point problems, each containing four5-point subparts; total100. Allow300minutes per form with a basic calculator and no stored solutions. Exact expressions earn full credit; computational-looking subparts have finite algebraic solutions. Time and advancement thresholds are uncalibrated instructional choices. Require85overall,15in every domain, correction of every critical feasibility or inference error, and a changed-primitive oral defense. No institutional equivalence or professional qualification is implied.

Prerequisite routes are dynamic verification, distributional states, funded policy, risk prices, international accounts, and quantitative accuracy. The scored contract problem also requires graduate hidden-action contracts; the identification problems require graduate instrumental-variable identification and causal design. These integrations require actual derivations, not recognition of terminology. Every numerical input is synthetic.

A1. A settlement option and a disappearing cohort

A household may exercise one nontransferable settlement option at date0,1or2. Observed state z∈{L,H} has transition ((3/4,1/4),(1/2,1/2)); exercising pays (1,4) goods and ends the option. Waiting pays nothing. At date2 exercise is mandatory. Maximize E[β^τx(zτ)] with 4/5; initially z=L. There is no other trade or reinvestment opportunity. An outside issuer reserves4goods at date0 in storage returning one, pays the settlement and receives the unused reserve back; no output is produced by the transfer.

  1. Construct the full backward value vectors and exercise rule at dates0,1,2. Prove global optimality against randomized history-dependent stopping rules and state a tie convention.
  2. Find the joint distribution of exercise date and state, expected date and expected discounted settlement. Reconcile the reserve for every outcome. Explain why renormalizing surviving low-state mass as if no one had exercised changes the cohort.
  3. Remove maturity while leaving all primitives fixed. Construct and globally verify the infinite-horizon value and rule, including never-exercise plans. State why the live-state transition alone has no stationary probability law carrying all original mass.
  4. For the infinite option derive the entire exercise-time distribution from L, its mean and its discounted payoff. Give the limiting full distribution after adding absorbing settled states; distinguish eventual absorption from convergence of an ergodic income process.

A2. Pay-as-you-go pensions in a capital economy

One unit-mass cohort is born each date, lives young and old, supplies one unit of labor only when young, and maximizes log cy+log co. Competitive production is F(K,L)=3K^(1/3)L^(2/3); capital fully depreciates. In capital-per-young units k>0, wages and gross rentals are w(k)=2k^(1/3),R(k)=k^(−2/3). A wage tax t∈[0,1) on the young finances the same date's pension tw(k) to the old. Young saving s≥0 becomes next capital. Households take future factor prices and pensions as fixed. Compare (0,1/3), beginning with k₀=1. No foreign asset, government debt or hidden transfer exists.

  1. Derive each young household's global saving choice at given positive future rental/pension. Combine household choice, capital clearing and the government budget to construct the interior equilibrium law kₜ₊₁(kₜ,t). Do not let an atomistic saver differentiate aggregate future wages.
  2. Prove existence and uniqueness of a positive stationary capital level for each t<1 and convergence from every k₀>0. Compute stationary capital, rental, wage and young/old consumption at both tax rates, and reconcile goods.
  3. Suppose the tax rises permanently from0to1/3 before date0 choices while inherited capital is1. Compute date0 old consumption, date0 saving and the first young cohort's two consumptions. Verify both dates' pension funding; explain why stationary comparisons cannot stand in for this transition.
  4. Compare baseline and reform lifetime utility for the first young cohort and for a stationary reform cohort. Determine whether the calculation establishes a Pareto improvement. Identify precisely the role of fixed labor and of the government's balanced budget.

A3. Implement effort before pricing a loan

An entrepreneur invests I=10, contributing internal cash n,0≤n≤10, and borrowing10−n. The project pays20on success and0on failure. Hidden effort H succeeds with probability4/5; L succeeds with probability2/5and gives the entrepreneur a nonpecuniary private benefit3. This benefit is utility, not lender cash. All parties are risk neutral in money; lenders' gross opportunity return is11/10. Internal cash can earn that return outside the project. A contract pays lenders d on success and0on failure,0≤d≤20. The entrepreneur keeps the remainder and chooses effort after funding. Break effort ties toward H only when explicitly agreed. The primitive tuple is (I,Y,pH,pL,B,R)=(10,20,4/5,2/5,3,11/10).

  1. Derive the necessary and sufficient global effort IC condition and lenders' break-even equation for an H contract. Construct the exact minimum internal cash that permits both, including limited liability and entrepreneur participation relative to storing n.
  2. At n=1 construct the competitive H contract and verify all incentives and cash flows. At n=0 show why pricing the same intended high-effort debt without checking IC is inconsistent. Do not conclude that failure to implement H rules out every L contract.
  3. Now a government guarantees5to lenders only on project failure. Reprice and verify a fully externally financed H contract at n=0. State the effort-tie issue and the effect of lowering the guarantee slightly; do not treat an expected guarantee receipt as a sure private project payoff.
  4. Fund the guarantee on every history by a date0 tax reserve invested safely, rebating its unused terminal balance to taxpayers on success. Compute required reserve, expected net fiscal cost and state-by-state receipts. Explain what is needed before calling the guarantee a welfare improvement.

A4. A currency portfolio with a real position limit

A household has current endowment2, future income(1,3), equal state probabilities, and utility log c₀+Elog c₁. A domestic bond pays(1,1)and costs1. A foreign-currency face pays X=(2,1/2) domestic goods and costs17/16; current spot is one domestic unit per foreign unit.   2. Solve the exact unconstrained b,h holdings. Prove global optimality, including existence, positive consumption and the absence of a hidden initial transfer. Identify the dated cash receipts of each leg.

A5. A propagation coefficient needs an observation law

Centered variables obey Y=θX+u and observed Z=X+η. X,η,u are mutually independent; Var(X)=2,Var(η)=3. An instrument W satisfies Cov(W,X)=2 and is orthogonal to η,u. The observed moments are (VarZ,CovZY,CovWZ,CovWY)=(5,3,2,3). Interpret θ=1/(1−κ) as the response of a stipulated scalar linear mechanism y=d+κy,0≤κ<1, not a complete national resource model.

  1. Derive the naive regression slope and the IV coefficient, prove the IV moment implication under the stated observation law, and recover κ. Separate measurement-error attenuation from a general claim that IV is valid.
  2. Add a second proposed instrument whose covariance with Z is1and covariance with Y is9/5. Test whether one θ satisfies both exact moments. Minimize the unweighted squared-moment criterion, calculate both remaining moments and explain why its positive value alone is not a statistical overidentification test.
  3. For d=1/5 and the κ identified by the first valid instrument, solve the true linear mechanism. An analyst instead uses κ̃=2/5 and reports its exact fixed point and zero residual. Compute the true-operator residual there, prove a valid error bound and compare it with the actual error.
  4. Construct an explicit violation of the first instrument's exclusion that changes the structural interpretation while preserving its ratio. State a credible new measurement/exclusion check and a reserved outcome test. Explain which welfare and funding objects remain absent from the linear mechanism.
