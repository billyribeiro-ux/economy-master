Econometrics Form A — Construct and certify

This original closed-book field form has five 20-point problems, each with four 5-point subparts: 100 points. Allow 300 minutes, including reading and checking. Timing, form equivalence and thresholds are uncalibrated design choices. Use blank paper and a nonprogrammable calculator; exact fractions, logarithms and symbolic roots earn full credit, with no probability tables, code, internet or notes required. The three fields are separate sittings. All numerical laws here are synthetic; exact population facts are not sample estimates.

Advance with at least 85/100 overall and 15/20 in each of the five essential domains, after repairing every critical identification, independence, support, financing or integrity error and defending one changed-primitive oral problem. Equivalent valid proofs earn full credit. Carry forward a minor arithmetic error where subsequent reasoning is correct, but do not convert a wrong estimand or missing assumption into full credit. These criteria confer no external academic qualification.

1. Demand information and robust pricing —20 points

A unit mass of risk-neutral buyers each wants at most one unit. A buyer with reservation value v buys exactly when p≤v; ties buy. Values lie in (0,1,2,3). Independent random posted prices have support {1,3}, each with positive assignment probability, and are independent of values. The exact population purchase probabilities at these prices are (4/5,1/5). There is no other information about values. A monopolist has zero marginal and fixed cost, cannot discriminate, and may choose only p∈{1,2,3}. Its action cannot change the value distribution. Prices, values and profits are in the same currency per potential buyer. These are population identification facts, not estimated rates.

  1. (a) Characterize every value distribution consistent with the entire observed price/purchase law. Prove both necessity and sufficiency; do not select an arbitrary interpolation.
  2. (b) Derive the sharp set for mean reservation value and for demand at price2. Give complete endpoint witnesses and explain why more observations under the same price support cannot identify the missing demand.
  3. (c) Characterize the profit-maximizing price for every admissible distribution, including ties. Derive the sharp set of maximized profits and the profit of the fixed policy p=2.
  4. (d) Before learning the missing distribution, choose a randomized price to minimize worst-case regret relative to the distribution-specific best price. Derive the global optimum over all probability mixtures on the three prices and its worst-case regret.

2. A product of two estimated transmission links —20 points

For i=1,…,N, observe independent vectors (Xᵢ,Zᵢ)∼N₂((a,b),Σ), with known Σ=((1,1/2),(1/2,1)) and (a,b)∈ℝ². In an explicitly maintained two-link macro response, a unit intervention changes an intermediate quantity by a, and each unit of that quantity changes the final outcome by b. There are no other channels, so the structural response is r=ab. This interpretation is an invariance assumption, not a result of the Gaussian fit. Let â=X̄ and b̂=Z̄. For the regular numerical case use (a,b)=(1,2). A separate case has a=b=0. You may use the standard normal facts E[U²]=1, E[U⁴]=3 and zero odd moments, and the iid Gaussian sample-mean law; derive the required product results.

  1. (a) Prove that (â,b̂) is the unique global likelihood maximizer and give its exact sampling covariance. Identify the independent unit and the conditional economic interpretation required for r.
  2. (b) Derive the root-N limit for r̂=âb̂ at general fixed (a,b) with nonzero gradient, then evaluate its variance at the regular numerical case. Explicitly bound the product remainder in probability.
  3. (c) Construct an exactly unbiased estimator of ab. Derive the exact finite-N variance of the uncorrected and corrected estimators, rather than retaining only the leading asymptotic term.
  4. (d) At a=b=0, derive the correct scaling and limiting law, its mean and variance. Diagnose a first-order normal interval that treats its zero oracle delta variance as exact certainty.

3. Unequal cluster sizes and the random denominator —20 points

Four fixed clusters contain n=(1,2,3,4) persons. Within each cluster every person's outcome equals the cluster mean. The complete teaching potential means are Y(0)=(0,1,2,3) and Y(1)=(2,2,2,2). Exactly two clusters are assigned treatment, uniformly among six pairs, with no interference between clusters and no missing persons. Assignment cannot change cluster size. The actual treated pair is {1,4}. A hypothetical analyst observes only the assigned outcomes; the full potential table is granted to you solely to study the procedures. The target is the average individual treatment effect over these ten fixed persons.

  1. (a) Compute the target and the equally weighted cluster effect. Construct a Horvitz–Thompson estimator of the individual target using known inclusion probability1/2, and prove its exact unbiasedness.
  2. (b) Enumerate that estimator over treated pairs12,13,14,23,24,34. Compute the actual value, exact randomization variance and standard error. State whether that variance is generally computable from this analyst’s single observed assignment.
  3. (c) An analyst instead differences the observed individual treatment and control means, dividing by the realized numbers of persons in each arm. Compute this ratio contrast at every assignment and its exact bias. Explain why equal cluster treatment probabilities do not make the random denominators harmless.
  4. (d) Suppose K independent replications of this entire fixed four-cluster experiment are available, with the same potential table and independent assignments. Give the variance and limiting law of the average HT estimator. Separate that asymptotic experiment from claiming a person-level CLT from the ten rows of one replication.

4. Forecast selection with a genuinely fresh test —20 points

Future outcomes are iid Bernoulli(q), q∈(0,1). A probabilistic forecast p incurs Brier loss (Y−p)². Two fixed candidate forecasts, chosen without seeing validation outcomes, are pL,pH=(1/4,3/4). A validation sample has three independent outcomes; choose the candidate with smaller mean validation loss. The candidates are never refitted. Let J be the number of successes. A second test sample, when used, is independent of all candidate construction and validation. For exact numerical selection and optimism calculations set q=1/2. Keep probability forecasts distinct from binary classification decisions.

  1. (a) Prove that truthful probability p=q uniquely minimizes population Brier risk on[0,1]. Calculate both candidate risks and their difference at general q; characterize all ties.
  2. (b) Derive the selected candidate as a function of J. Find its selection probability at general q, and prove the expected future risk formula for the full selection algorithm.
  3. (c) At q=1/2, enumerate the selected validation mean loss at J=0,1,2,3. Calculate expected reported validation loss, actual future risk and their difference. Explain the information source of optimism.
  4. (d) Construct an unbiased fresh-test estimator of the selected candidate’s conditional future risk. At q=1/2 derive its conditional variance for a test set of size m, and explain why looking at that test set to choose another forecast changes the claim.

5. Selection bounds and a new target population —20 points

Treatment D is independently randomized with probability1/2. Every person has potential response indicators S(0),S(1) and potential outcomes Y(0),Y(1)∈[0,4]; outcomes are defined even when unrecorded, and there is no interference. Grant individual monotonicity S(1)≥S(0), consistency and independence of D from all potentials. Exact observed population response rates are (1/2,3/4) in control/treatment order. Among responding controls Y=1 with probability1. Among responding treated persons, Y equals (0,2,4) with equal probability1/3. Define A={S(0)=S(1)=1}, the always-observed stratum. A separate target population is known, by an external oracle, to have share 2/5 in its analogous stratum A. Only the mean treatment effect within A transports from trial to target. Target non-A outcomes remain in[0,4] with no other restriction; target potential-outcome levels need not match trial levels. No sampling confidence statement is requested.

  1. (a) Identify the three allowed response-stratum masses and the share of A among treated responders. Compute the naive responder-mean contrast and state exactly why randomization does not give it the always-observed interpretation.
  2. (b) Derive the sharp interval for E[Y(1)−Y(0)|A]. Prove the trimming bound for these atoms and construct complete trial endpoint worlds that reproduce the entire observed law.
  3. (c) Derive the sharp target-population ATE interval under exactly the stated transport and support restrictions. Prove attainability, allowing target levels to differ; do not replace an identification set with a sampling interval.
  4. (d) Write a concrete audit and additional-evidence plan: distinguish testable response rates from individual monotonicity, identify the missing target information, and specify how data versions, nonresponse indicators and new measurements would be preserved. State what remains a proposal rather than an executed empirical result.

1. A relevant shifter that is not excluded —20 points

The proposed simultaneous structural equations are Y=βD+δZ+U and D=γY+Z, with β∈(0,1) (closed endpoints), δ∈[0,2], γ∈ℝ, E[U]=0 and U independent of Z. Its variance is not known or fixed across candidate structures. The second equation is exact, with no additional disturbance. Each candidate must have a unique simultaneous solution. 2. An endpoint where regular asymptotics fail —20 points

3. Sequential paired randomization —20 points
