Form B — Divestment, promises and executable finance

This original reassessment contains five 20-point cases, for 100 points. Suggested time is 300 minutes, an uncalibrated design target. Use a nonprogrammable calculator and blank paper; no notes, internet or code. All required numerical inputs appear below. Keep exact fractions where practical, state units, and show the reason for each journal, value, constraint and decision. The authored advancement standard is at least 85 overall and 15 in every domain, followed by an unfamiliar transfer and teach-back. A critical accounting, identification, funding, units, double-counting or integrity error requires remediation regardless of the total. This is not a credential or an empirically equated examination.

The cases follow fictional Briar Engineering, a related investment mandate, its commodity hedge and a lending bank. They are separate, fully specified decision snapshots, not one continuous set of financial statements. Each restates the resources and rights it needs. Do not import cash, debt or a permission from another question. CU is the single fictional currency unit. Physical probabilities are labeled P; pricing weights and probabilities are labeled separately. No current market quote, national reporting rule, statutory priority or actual funding commitment is asserted.

Question 1. Two estimates cannot manufacture a receipt — 20 points

Briar begins a year with cash 12, gross trade receivables 30 less allowance 2, inventory 24 and net equipment 80. Liabilities are trade payables 20 and debt 70; equity is 54. During the year it earns cash revenue 16 and credit revenue 90, collects 84 from receivables, buys inventory for 36 entirely on trade credit, pays suppliers 32, consumes inventory costing 40 and pays other operating expenses 20. There is no other transaction, tax, interest, distribution or financing flow.

At the start of the year, genuinely new engineering evidence changes the equipment's estimated remaining life from six years to four, retaining residual value 8. Past entries were correct when made. Apply straight-line depreciation prospectively to the opening carrying amount. After the full year's depreciation, independent evidence establishes recoverable amount 50 for this independently cash-generating equipment. Apply the stipulated cost-model rule: impairment equals the positive excess of carrying amount over recoverable amount; there is no upward revaluation. At year-end the required total receivable allowance is 5. There are no writeoffs or recoveries. These are supplied measurement conventions and evidence, not a conclusion about an actual filing's compliance.

  1. (a) Give the depreciation, impairment and allowance-adjustment entries. Compute profit and the complete closing balance sheet, including cash and equity. Reconcile both sides. Explain why neither a contra-asset balance nor an impairment charge is a cash reserve.
  2. (b) Derive operating cash flow directly and indirectly. Show one indirect bridge using gross receivables and a second using net receivables. A spreadsheet uses the net-receivable change and also adds the allowance expense: quantify its error and explain it.
  3. (c) A controller proposes retaining the old six-year estimate to “raise this year's profit by six.” Compute the counterfactual depreciation and impairment while retaining the independently established recoverable amount 50. Assess the claim, the prospective-treatment issue and the evidence needed before changing an estimate. Does the numerical reconciliation validate that evidence?

Question 2. A valuable restructuring can still need creditor consent — 20 points

In a new decision snapshot, a division carried at 50 can be sold immediately for 64 to a buyer with that much available cash. Transfer of control and cash is simultaneous; the seller pays a disposal-service provider 3 immediately. Alternatively the division delivers certain unlevered distributable cash 20 at date 1 and 44 at date 2. The latter includes all final disposal proceeds; there is no remaining asset. These cash flows are already net of every relevant operating and investment outlay. The compatible date-0 discount factors for certain cash are 0.95 and 0.90. There is no tax. Debt service is a financing allocation, not an additional deduction inside these operating cash flows.

For parts (b) and (c), the sale has been selected and completed. Only the following incremental restructuring scope remains. Net sale cash is 61. An old creditor has principal face 70, with no further interest; its contract requires all available sale proceeds to repay principal immediately, reducing the remaining face one-for-one. The rest is due at date 2. The retained core produces final distributable cash 50 in state U and 10 in state D. Supplied strictly positive date-0 state prices for those date-2 receipts are 0.45 and 0.45; they sum to the stated date-2 discount factor. They are not physical default probabilities.

A proposed rehabilitation consumes 25 cash immediately and changes the retained core's final cash to 90 in U and 30 in D. All required work is included in that expenditure; there is no later funding need or other asset. Releasing 25 from the cash sweep requires the creditor's explicit consent. In that proposal only 36 is repaid as principal now. At date 2 the creditor receives the smaller of its remaining face and core cash; existing equity receives the residual. An unpaid nominal debt claim remains recorded, but there are no further assets, guarantors or recoveries in this closed scope. Its economic value after the final distribution is zero; no legal discharge is presumed.

The existing owners also have five CU of personal cash, separate from all corporate resources. They may pay a consent fee f directly to the creditor now. The fee is a separately agreed transfer and does not reduce remaining principal. It conveys no new equity rights. Unpaid personal cash remains theirs. Both parties use the supplied pricing rule to compare present values; the creditor accepts weakly equal value. Their only alternatives here are the contractual cash sweep without rehabilitation or the stated rehabilitation with an affordable fee. No compulsory restructuring power is available.

  1. (a) Find net sale cash, accounting disposal gain, the value of holding the division and the incremental value of divestment. Distinguish the two gains and explain why neither the book amount nor a second subtraction of debt service belongs in the hold-value calculation.
  2. (b) Before any consent fee, construct both complete creditor/equity waterfalls at date 2 and price each old claimant under each policy, including immediate principal payments. Reconcile total stakeholder value and the rehabilitation surplus. Identify the retained nominal deficiency. Does positive total surplus alone establish the creditor's agreement?
  3. (c) Derive the full feasible fee interval that makes both parties weakly better off and respects the owners' funded cash limit. Find the smallest fee, each party's change in value at that fee, and the owners' remaining personal cash. State the necessary execution evidence. Do not finance the fee again from the 25 already committed to rehabilitation.

Question 3. A feasible investment mandate need not fund every liability — 20 points

A separate employee reserve has 100 CU now and must pay a date-1 liability of 90, 100 or 110 in states L, M and H. A default-free cash investment costs one and pays one. One risky unit costs one and pays 0.5, 1 or 1.5 in those states. Physical probabilities P are (0.2, 0.4, 0.4). One supplied positive state-price vector is (0.25, 0.50, 0.25). Only cash and the risky asset are traded. Do not infer complete markets from the existence of three states or from the supplied vector.

The reserve begins entirely in cash. Buying n risky units incurs an additional execution fee 0.02n, paid now to an outside execution provider. Fractional units are allowed. The remaining b CU stays in the cash investment. No shorting or borrowing is permitted, and 0≤n≤30. The first mandate is deliberately limited: maximize P-expected terminal surplus over the liability, subject only to covering the liability in L. It does not require full coverage in M or H. No sponsor contribution is currently authorized.

For part (c) only, a separate sponsor can instead transfer a nonrepayable g≥0 before investment from an independently funded one-CU cash budget. The sponsor receives no claim; unused sponsor cash remains outside the reserve. The reserve then invests 100+g under the same cost and position restrictions. No other mechanism changes the liabilities.

  1. (a) Calculate the risky unit's P-expected return and verify both traded prices using the supplied state prices. Price the liability and exhibit a frictionless portfolio that replicates it. Explain why its price does not require a unique price for every untraded payoff.
  2. (b) Under the first mandate, derive the full feasible holding interval and prove the maximizing allocation. Give b, all three terminal wealth and surplus amounts, and expected surplus. Account for the execution provider's receipt; do not spend that cash twice.
  3. (c) Find the smallest funded sponsor contribution that allows payment of every liability in all states. Prove global necessity and give an attaining portfolio, fee and sponsor cash remainder. Checking one candidate is insufficient.
  4. (d) A sales presentation reports a 14% gross annual return from the best of twenty inspected historical windows and calls the part (b) allocation “fully immunized.” Give a concise evidence-based response addressing selection, the mandate and the cash required for a valid full-coverage decision. Do not treat the stipulated P law as an estimate authenticated by this presentation.

Question 5. A signed subscription after the cutoff is still late — 20 points

At a separately supplied bank stress snapshot, Elm Bank has cash 12, forty securities carried at one each, and loans 98. Deposits are 112, notes 28 and equity ten. Before the payment cutoff, eight of the existing loan principal is collected in cash. Independent credit evidence also requires a five-CU reduction in the value of other existing loans, with no cash receipt. The entire securities pool is marked once from one to 0.9 per unit before any sale. This is a stipulated market-value risk ledger, not a statement that a national accounting framework requires this recognition.

Ten securities are already pledged and cannot be released. The other thirty can be sold to an independent buyer at 0.9 each; that buyer has exactly 25 cash and no borrowing access. Fractional units are allowed and sales settle before the cutoff. Any unsold free units could secure a repo at 0.6 per unit, subject to a separate lender's twenty-CU cash capacity, but operational and contractual conditions make its earliest cash delivery after the cutoff. A sold security cannot also be pledged. There is no other receipt, sale or borrowing route before the cutoff.

Thirty of deposits must be paid now. Briar has also made a valid contractual request for a twenty-CU cash loan draw at that cutoff; this is the fixed twenty-CU draw in this case, not permission to import or reselect the strategy from Question 4. The supplied contingency contract gives the due deposits payment priority, then requires delivery of as much of Briar's draw as the remaining cash allows. An undelivered amount stays recorded as an unperformed contractual draw request, not a funded loan asset or an automatic cancellation. No penalty or damages amount is specified; do not invent one. The loan actually delivered has the same initial value as its principal and earns nothing within this snapshot.

A new equity investor has five CU in a separate available cash account. It has signed a subscription for five, but the actual settlement currently scheduled is after the cutoff. Only an effective, verified acceleration could make it usable earlier. New subscription cash creates equity, not a repayment liability. Its share allocation is outside this ledger; total equity does not identify who owns each part. All other claims retain their stated amounts and dates.

  1. (a) Find the maximum early sale, available cutoff cash and minimum early equity cash needed for full performance. Prove the resource bound. More generally let the buyer's funded cash capacity be B≥0, retaining every other rule. Derive required early contribution g and distinguish that requirement from what is feasible with the investor's five. Explain both errors in a spreadsheet that adds eighteen of early repo cash to the maximal sale proceeds.
  2. (b) Reconcile the marked balance sheet after collections, both marks, maximal sale and the priority payments under the currently late subscription. Show actual new lending, remaining securities, liabilities, equity and the unperformed request. Then repeat with the full five equity cash actually settled before the cutoff. Record no second sale loss after marking and no loan asset for undelivered cash. Do not count the funding gap itself as another asset impairment.
  3. (c) Give a conditional governance decision on a report claiming that positive capital and the signed subscription establish full timely performance. State the funded amount, timing and authority evidence needed for release, the relevant independent reconciliation, an accountable owner and an escalation trigger. Keep feasible prospective repair, observed settlement and residual legal uncertainty separate.
