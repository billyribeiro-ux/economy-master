Problem design and meaningful assessment

Capability. Write original assessments that elicit economic reasoning, distinguish a calculation from its interpretation, and test a changed assumption with explicit scoring. Defend what a score can establish and identify when an item measures an unintended burden.

Prerequisites. The objective and prerequisite work of Chapter 1, representation discipline in Chapter 2, and the course/seminar design sequence through Chapter 4. The economic methods are one-period present value and finite binary-outcome accounting; both are restated below. These assessment specimens, responses and scores are invented. They are not results from an administered exam or evidence of learner competence.

1. An assessment is an argument about capability

A student obtains the right number after recognizing yesterday's worksheet. Can you conclude that the student can choose the relevant method in a new setting? Only if the evidence supports that further inference. Begin an assessment specification with the capability, the population and conditions of use, the response evidence, and the decision the score will inform. “Rank mutually exclusive investments using present value under stated assumptions” is narrower and more inspectable than “is good at finance.”

A construct is the characteristic or capability the assessment intends to measure. Validity concerns the warrant for a particular interpretation and use of scores; it is not a permanent seal attached to an attractive question. Construct underrepresentation occurs when an important part of the intended capability is omitted. Construct-irrelevant variation concerns influences on scores outside that target. Reliability concerns consistency or precision under a measurement procedure; consistently scoring the wrong capability does not fix a validity problem. These bounded meanings follow the NCME assessment glossary; this chapter does not claim psychometric validation of its examples.

For the investment objective, a test consisting only of discount-factor recall underrepresents choosing and defending a project. A timed puzzle that hides the cash-flow date in obscure prose may instead reward reading speed or familiarity with a trick. If rapid execution itself is an intended capability, timing can be relevant; otherwise explain why it belongs. A calculator can remove arithmetic burden while leaving the choice of discounting rule and interpretation to the learner.

A useful validity argument connects several questions. Does the task sample the intended reasoning? Does the learner's response show that reasoning rather than copying or guessing? Do graders apply the criteria consistently? Does success persist on a suitably changed task? Is the proposed decision proportionate to that evidence? Collect actual responses and examine rival explanations. Content review is useful evidence, but it cannot alone demonstrate reliable scores, fair use across all intended groups or predictive success in a profession.

2. Design the problem and its answer together

Write a short blueprint before the story. Specify the economic decision or claim, essential assumptions, allowed resources, expected reasoning, and one plausible failure you want the task to reveal. Then construct the numbers and solve the problem independently. Make each quantity's unit, date, denominator and availability clear. A missing assumption can make an item genuinely indeterminate; do not reward learners for guessing the examiner's preferred convention.

The Eberly Center's assessment guidance recommends considering objectives, purpose, instructions, time and scoring during design. We use that professional guidance to build the following original artifacts, not to claim that these exact tasks have been tested. Decide whether the exercise is formative, used to guide improvement, or summative, used as evidence for a consequential judgment. The same mathematical problem can serve either purpose under different administration and feedback arrangements.

Distinguish intended reasoning demand from observed item difficulty. Before administration, you can describe the operations required: selecting a model, reconciling two quantities, deriving a boundary, defending an assumption. You cannot honestly announce the proportion of a real cohort that will succeed without data. A low pass rate could reflect demanding reasoning, poor instruction, ambiguity, an inaccessible format or insufficient time. Diagnose the process rather than celebrating difficulty for its own sake.

Build transfer deliberately. A new number can check execution while preserving every familiar cue. Removing a cue can test recognition of a known structure; changing a financing constraint can test whether the learner revises the feasible set; revealing a missing outcome can test whether the learner updates a bound. Teach the relevant reasoning beforehand, but keep the exact assessment application unseen. Give a concise definition of unfamiliar industry terms when their recall is not the target.

Originality means constructing an intellectually coherent new task, not disguising a copied problem with renamed people. Keep a private author solution, assumptions, alternative valid responses and common errors. Have another reader attempt the task before seeing your answer. If a defensible interpretation produces a different result, either accept that interpretation explicitly or repair the prompt before use. After administration, preserve the original wording, document the defect and apply a consistent remedy to all affected responses through the actual course process.

3. Make scoring inspectable

A rubric connects criteria to descriptions of performance and a scoring rule. The Eberly Center's rubric guidance distinguishes criteria, descriptors and levels. The tables below are analytic rubrics: points are attached to distinct pieces of reasoning. Their totals are local design choices, not calibrated proficiency scales.

Separate calculation, model choice, argument and scope. State which intermediate work earns credit. Do not award the same argument twice under “method” and “explanation,” or repeatedly deduct the same arithmetic slip while ignoring otherwise correct dependent reasoning. A carried error can preserve evidence of method; a change from the stated target to another target is more consequential than a transcription slip. Mark what is demonstrated, and record the misconception separately from the total.

Before grading a class, try the rubric on a correct response, a plausible misconception and an incomplete response. Compare independent ratings and revise vague descriptors. This is a calibration activity, not a claim of achieved agreement until actual ratings exist. Retain explanations of adjudicated cases. The next chapter develops this feedback and grading process more fully.

4. Two complete original assessment sets

Set A — choose a project, then change the decision conditions

Blueprint and administration. Assess discounted-value calculation, objective-based ranking and revision under changed constraints. Learners have studied present value; the formula is supplied so memorization is not the target. Use individual work with a calculator and the stated formula, requiring written reasons. A proposed 25-minute allocation must be piloted for the actual audience and access arrangements; it is not an observed completion time.

Method supplied before the task. A project costs C>0 at date 0 and pays a certain amount X≥0 at date 1, one period later. At a common opportunity rate r>−1, its date-0 net present value is V=X/(1+r)−C. Its simple one-period return is R=(X−C)/C, a proportional measure, not total value created. With mutually exclusive projects and an outside option of zero NPV, maximize V over feasible choices. Remaining funds earn the opportunity rate and therefore have zero incremental NPV. All values below are currency units; no taxes, fees or additional cash flows are present.

Complete prompt. Project A costs40 and pays55; project B costs100 and pays132. The opportunity rate is10%. At most one project may be undertaken, both are initially financeable, and the objective is maximum date-0 incremental value. (a) Calculate both NPVs. (b) Choose among A, B and the outside option and justify the criterion. (c) Calculate both simple returns and explain why their ranking does not decide this objective. (d) Change only available date-0 project funding to80, with no borrowing: decide again. (e) Restore initial financeability but change the rate to40%: decide again, retaining the outside option. The primitive tuple is (40,55,100,132,1/10,80,2/5).

Full answer. Discounting gives55/(11/10)=50 and132/(11/10)=120. Subtracting the respective costs gives (10,20). B is the unique maximizer because20>10>0. Returns are (3/8,8/25), or37.5% and32%. A earns more per unit invested but B creates more incremental value in this mutually exclusive, initially financeable choice. No reinvestment or leverage assumption silently converts the percentage ranking into the stated value objective.

With only80 available and no borrowing, B is infeasible because100>80. A remains feasible and its NPV10 exceeds the outside option0, so choose A. At r=40%, both are financeable again but NPVs are (-5/7,-40/7):55/(7/5)−40 and132/(7/5)−100. Choose the outside option. A is the less negative project; that does not make it preferable to doing neither.

Why the changed assumption matters. The first change modifies feasibility; the second modifies value. A response that simply retains “B” after either change has not transferred the decision rule. More generally, VB−VA=77/(1+r)−60. Because1+r>0, equality occurs at r=17/60. B ranks higher below that rate, A above it. At the crossing, both have NPV 20/7, above zero. This algebra proves the ranking boundary, not that either project is always acceptable: beyond each project's zero-NPV rate, the outside option can dominate.

Set B — missing outcomes change what a numerical answer means

Blueprint and method. Assess denominator choice, complete-case versus full-roster quantities, exact missing-data reasoning and interpretation. A binary outcome is0or1; an absent outcome is neither value until observed or assumed. For a group of N fixed units, observed successes s and m missing outcomes imply total successes s+k for integer k∈{0,…,m}. Its mean is(s+k)/N. To compare two fixed groups, enumerate feasible missing values in both groups. An interval joining the smallest and largest values can summarize bounds, but in a finite binary roster not every interior real number is necessarily attainable.

Complete prompt. A synthetic eight-enterprise roster has four offered a training invitation and four not offered. At a fixed follow-up date, outcome1 means a complete, consistently defined filing was submitted; outcome0 means it was not. Offered records contain two1s, one0and one missing outcome. Non-offered records contain one1, two0s and one missing outcome. Group membership is fixed; no missing record may be dropped from the full-roster denominator. The assignment mechanism is unspecified. (a) Compute the observed complete-case difference in success proportions. (b) Find the exact set of possible offered-minus-non-offered proportions for the full roster. (c) Exhibit completions attaining both endpoints and explain why the set is sharp. (d) State what these quantities establish and whether they prove a causal effect or a confidence interval. (e) An authorized follow-up now verifies that the offered group's missing outcome was1; the other remains missing. Update the exact set. Counts are (4,2,1,4,1,1), ordered(NT,sT,mT,NC,sC,mC).

Full answer. Each group has three observed outcomes, so the complete-case difference is2/3−1/3=1/3. It compares the observed subsets. Let u be the offered missing value and v the comparison missing value. Full-roster D=(2+u)/4−(1+v)/4=(1+u−v)/4. The four completions are:

The exact compatible set is (0,1/4,1/2). The lower endpoint uses(u,v)=(0,1); the upper uses(1,0). Every permissible pair is in the table, so there are no further values, and each listed value has a witness. This proves sharpness for this descriptive contrast. The interval[0,1/2]is its smallest containing interval, not the exact finite set:1/3lies inside the interval but is not attainable for this roster. That is why replacing missing outcomes with “the observed average” silently changes the stated binary completion problem.

Neither the complete-case number nor the completion set identifies a causal invitation effect. No assignment or identification argument is supplied, and even a credible randomized design would require an appropriate inference argument in addition to this missing-outcome accounting. The set has no confidence level: it follows from binary support and observed records, not a probability distribution for missingness. If follow-up verifies u=1, retain only the last two rows; the new set is (1/4,1/2). This change uses new outcome information, not an assumption that missing means successful.

Ask learners to distinguish this assumption from the genuinely verified u=1case. The difficulty is recognizing the evidential status of a value, not performing additional subtraction. A good reassessment can remove the word “missing” and describe an unreturned follow-up form while keeping the outcome definition and available information explicit.

5. Preserve meaningful evidence without creating irrelevant barriers

Exam security serves the interpretation that the work demonstrates the assessed learner's capability under the declared conditions. It cannot be achieved simply by making a question obscure. The Eberly Center's remote-exam guidance discusses clear resource expectations, explaining work, format practice, version design and careful oral follow-up. We adopt those bounded design considerations; no current local institutional rule, surveillance requirement or guaranteed prevention claim follows.

For these specimens, a concrete proposed instruction is: “Work individually. You may use the supplied formulas and a calculator; do not exchange answers or use external answer services or generative tools during this sitting. Show the setup and reasoning. Submit a readable text or approved accessible equivalent. Report an ambiguous instruction or technical failure through the announced instructor channel; retain your work while the instructor applies the documented procedure.” This is a specimen rule to align with an actual course's policies before use, not a claim about what a university currently requires.

Protect unreleased items and keys with limited authorized access and a version record. Before reuse, inspect whether an item has become public and replace exposed applications where independent performance is needed. Distinct forms should follow the same capability blueprint and scoring expectations; random numbers alone neither establish transfer nor prove equal difficulty. Check that generated values do not change a positive-NPV task into an unintended tie or an impossible denominator. Review actual response patterns before claiming comparability.

Make administration accessible: define terms, use readable tables and non-color cues, offer a practice submission format, arrange appropriate access supports and allow time for logistics when those are not the target. A short follow-up can ask the learner to explain a selected step or changed assumption, using announced criteria and equivalent opportunities. A hesitant oral response or a detector score alone is not proof of unauthorized help. Preserve relevant evidence, hear the learner's explanation and use the institution's actual review process; this chapter supplies no sanctions or automatic accusation rule.

6. Review the item as well as the learner

Before release, independently solve each branch, test ties and outside options, check that every point corresponds to requested evidence, and read for unintended clues. Ask a reviewer to identify assumptions the intended audience may reasonably supply differently. Keep an answer that explains valid alternatives, not merely an answer letter. This is especially important when an open problem evaluates a policy argument rather than a single number.

After actual administration, inspect response processes, timing and patterns of error. A high score could reflect sound reasoning, prior exposure or excessive cues; a low one could reflect a real misconception or a defective item. Use that evidence to revise instruction or the assessment and preserve the version change. Report domain-level reasoning as well as totals when decisions depend on essential distinctions. Do not infer teaching causality, broad financial competence or integrity from these two sets alone.

2. Repair the ambiguity

Complete prompt: “A costs40now and pays55in one period; B costs100now and pays132then. Payments are certain, the common opportunity rate is10%, both are financeable and at most one can be chosen. Outside has zero incremental value. Which choice maximizes date-0 NPV? Show both NPVs and justify the choice.” Answer:10and20; B because20>10>0. Rubric: one point for each correct NPV, one for B and one for the NPV/outside comparison. “Show sophistication” is removed because it names no assessable criterion.

A different prompt is: “Only future payoffs55and132are known. Identify two additional pieces of information required for a date-0 NPV choice and explain why the larger payoff alone is insufficient.” Full answer: costs and timing/appropriate discount rate are needed; feasibility and exclusivity also affect the decision. The larger future payoff may require a much larger cost or occur later. Accept any two consequential missing inputs with a correct explanation. Do not grade this second question as if the missing numbers were secretly supplied.

3. A solved new application

Prompt: “Choose the maximum-NPV option under the stated financeability conditions, then impose funding90with no borrowing and explain the revision.” Present values are750/11and1500/11, so NPVs are (90/11,180/11). Both returns are (1/4,1/4). Initially B creates more incremental value; under funding90, only A and outside are feasible, so choose A because90/11>0. This variant also makes percentage returns tie, exposing whether the learner can use value when return ranking gives no answer. Merely swapping values without asking for the changed interpretation could still test familiar substitution. Credit complete assumptions, correct calculations and both justified decisions; do not claim the variant is psychometrically equivalent without evidence.

6. Assess interpretation directly

Prompt: “You are given that Set B's full-roster descriptive contrast lies in the exact set{0,1/4,1/2}. The invitation assignment mechanism is unspecified. Does this prove that invitations caused filing to increase, and is the containing interval a95%confidence interval? Explain both answers.” Full answer: no causal effect is identified without an assignment or identification argument; group differences may reflect other differences. No95%level follows because the completion set uses possible binary values, not a sampling or missingness probability model. Rubric:2points for the causal limitation with reason;2for the confidence-level limitation with reason. Partial response “It is descriptive, so it need not be causal; the bounds are a95%interval” earns2/4. This item tests interpretation, not independent derivation or sharpness of the completion set. Credit all requested artifacts and a clear limit.

7. A usable administration notice

“This is individual work. Use the supplied formulas and a calculator; no answer exchange or external answer-generating service is permitted during the sitting. Show setup and reasons. Submit readable text or the approved accessible equivalent; practice the submission format beforehand. Report an ambiguity or technical problem to the announced instructor contact, retain your work, and follow the documented remedy. A brief follow-up may ask you to explain one step or change one assumption, using the same announced criteria; access needs will be addressed through the course process.” This is proposed wording requiring actual local policy alignment.

Concrete forms: give one group Set A's original payoffs55/132and another A66/B132, at the same costs/rate/initial financeability. The first has NPVs10/20and uniquely chooses B; the second has20/20and must identify both maxima. The second form adds a tie-recognition demand, so equal point totals or randomized allocation do not establish identical difficulty or interchangeable scores. Revise the blueprint or deliberately include comparable tie reasoning in both forms, then inspect actual evidence. Credit resources, access, problem handling, proportionate follow-up and the solved equivalence warning.

8. Separate agreement, validity and integrity

Corrected statement: “The two graders agreed on the observed responses under this rubric. That agreement alone neither establishes the intended broad research-capability interpretation nor proves why a student's performance changed.” Investigate whether tasks sample original question formation, evidence, argument and independent defense before making a research-capability claim; these two narrow sets do not. Inspect response processes and use suitably scoped independent tasks. Report actual observations and assistance rather than a certification inferred from agreement.

For the improvement, compare task versions, prior exposure, feedback, access conditions and reasoning; invite the learner to explain the work under a fair announced process. Preserve evidence and use actual institutional procedures if a concrete concern remains. An ambiguous prompt that omits financeability can cause knowledgeable learners to choose different projects and then score poorly against a narrow key. Repairing that defect can improve later scores without any misconduct. Credit both corrected inferences, a feasible evidence plan and a specific item-defect alternative; do not invent either guilt or exonerating observations.

Teach-back and delayed retention

Teach-back. Explain why an assessment can be difficult, consistently graded and still poorly suited to its claimed use. Use one concrete defect from these sets and propose a repair. Answer guide: a formula-recall item may be hard and reliably scored yet omit choosing between NPV and return percentage; a project-choice prompt without financing assumptions may reward guessing the key. State the intended capability, supply the missing conditions or change the requested inference, and retain a full solution and rubric. Evaluate the reasoning rather than rhetorical confidence.

Delayed task. Without the worked set, consider groups of4each with2observed successes in T and1in C, one missing value in each. A learner says that assigning both missing values0has established a positive causal effect. Give the calculation, correct the claim and propose a changed check. Full answer: that completion gives2/4−1/4=1/4, but absent records were assumed failures; other completions yield0or1/2, and assignment information still does not establish causality. A changed check verifies the comparison missing value1and asks for the updated set{0,1/4}and witnesses. Credit denominator, assumption status, causal limit and the complete check. A copied answer is practice; later independent reasoning is the evidence sought.

Source scope and next step

The NCME glossary supplies narrow measurement terminology; the three linked CMU pages supply bounded guidance on assessment planning, rubrics and administration. Their original empirical citations and linked policy pages were not inspected or imported. All cases, numbers, prompts, flawed responses and scoring judgments are original teaching material. None establishes measured reliability, fairness across groups, actual misconduct or classroom effectiveness. High-stakes measurement needs further validation expertise and evidence.
