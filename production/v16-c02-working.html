Map arguments, evidence and unresolved differences

Capability. Produce a bounded literature map that changes your research design. The prerequisite is choosing a field and a research agenda. Bring its question contract: Mira's proposed descriptive comparison of bank loan-stock changes across groups defined by pre-window short-term funding share. Her macro-finance ambition concerns credit transmission, but her first project lacks borrower linkage and a defended causal assignment. The literature search must expose the consequences of those limitations.

A useful review answers four questions: what is already known, how is it known, where do conclusions differ, and which difference your project can address. A bibliography answers only who wrote something. Do not turn abstracts into an essay ordered by publication date. Organize around the chain from question to measurement, identifying variation, assumptions, result and interpretation. A paper can be a strong contribution while answering a different question from yours.

A search record describes where, when and how you looked. An evidence table links each relevant claim to a specific version and location. A citation chain connects a paper to references it uses and later work that uses it. A research frontier is the evolving set of unresolved questions and approaches; it is not equivalent to the newest search result. A seminal paper is influential in establishing a question or approach. Influence motivates reading, but neither citation counts nor publication prestige establish that an argument is correct or applicable to your setting.

1. Search from a question, then broaden deliberately

Begin with the economic objects, not only an estimator. For Mira, combine terms for funding or liquidity, bank lending or credit supply, and identification or borrower demand. Search synonyms and alternative mechanisms: deposits, interbank funding, loan reallocation, bank specialization and credit demand. Use title searches to verify a known paper's identity; use topic searches to discover competing approaches. Search an official journal page, author page or institutional repository for the actual document. Search-result snippets are leads, not substitutes for the methods section.

Write inclusion criteria before judging whether a result supports your preferred story. Here a core item must analyze the link between bank funding and lending and offer an explicit treatment of the supply–demand distinction. An adjacent item may challenge an assumption, document a measurement problem or explain an institutional mechanism. Papers on asset prices alone are not automatically irrelevant, but their route to the lending question must be stated. Record exclusion reasons such as wrong outcome, duplicate version or no accessible substantive text; do not write “irrelevant” when the actual problem is that you could not obtain it.

Use three reading passes. First identify question, setting, contribution and document status. Next read the methods, variable definitions, sample construction and the exact evidence behind the main claim. Finally test the interpretation: what alternative explanation survives, what observations are excluded, and what changes when the population or assumptions change? A selective reading can answer a bounded question, provided you name what was read. “Methods inspected” is different from “all appendices checked” or “results reproduced.”

A practical stopping rule is question-specific saturation: after following the leading competing approaches and checking recent updates, another round adds no new material assumption, dataset or argument for the current question. This is a reason to write a provisional synthesis, not proof of exhaustive coverage. Put unresolved branches on a queue. A narrow two-paper exercise, including the worked map below, is explicitly below that saturation threshold for a real research proposal.

2. Preserve versions and the meaning of each row

Give each intellectual work a stable identity, then attach its versions. A working paper and journal article can share a question while changing sample, estimator, results or wording. Record title, authors, date or version label, source URL, retrieval date and printed page or section. If the downloaded file says “forthcoming,” cite that inspected version when giving page locators. A later publication record verifies bibliographic status; it does not prove that every sentence in the earlier file survived revision.

Separate an author's claim from your judgment. An evidence row can say “authors interpret this comparison as a supply effect” and then identify the assumption you must evaluate. Record the outcome and its units, the relevant population, the comparison, the assumption, the result at the level actually read, and the limit. Use “not inspected” for uncertainty about an appendix; “not reported in the inspected passage” is narrower than “the paper never reports it.” A blank cell is especially dangerous because a later reader may mistake it for no limitation.

Evidence independence also needs a column. Two papers may analyze the same episode, reuse the same administrative source or share authors and methods. That does not invalidate either paper, but counting them as independent replications can overstate corroboration. Likewise, two versions of one work are not two confirming studies. A review should distinguish agreement in logic, agreement in signs, similarity in numerical estimates and actual replication; these are different claims.

3. Compare assumptions before declaring disagreement

When papers seem to disagree, first align their targets. Are they studying lender-level loans or total borrower credit, stocks or flows, a price or a quantity, an intensive margin or entry and exit? Do they use the same treatment scale, horizon, baseline population and weighting? A positive loan response in one environment and a negative response elsewhere may be consistent with different shocks or substitution opportunities. Conversely, matching signs need not validate a common mechanism.

Then separate four possible sources of disagreement: measurement, identification, model restrictions and transport across settings. A useful critical paragraph states a claim, the evidence used for it, the assumption that bridges them, and a concrete observation that could change the assessment. “More robustness checks are needed” is not enough. Name the threat and the check's diagnostic power. A placebo can reveal one inconsistency; passing it does not prove every identifying assumption.

Worked case 2. A critical comparison that changes the design

Completed comparison. Both inspected approaches make borrower-level comparisons central, but the funding disturbances and exposure definitions differ. Khwaja–Mian's draft expressly discusses bank-specific demand as a remaining threat; Schnabl's draft states its demand restriction alongside the sample restriction. Thus “firm effects solve demand” is an incomplete literature takeaway. The appropriate question is which demand component is removed, under what maintained condition, for which borrowers. This is our reading judgment, not a claim that we have disproved either paper.

Original diagnostic illustration. To understand that question, use a self-contained bookkeeping example, not either paper's data or estimator. Let yfb denote a loan-change index for firm f borrowing from bank b. Let xb be a bank funding-change index. Assume the stipulated additive relation yfb=af+βxb+ufb; the indices are dimensionless and β is an index response, not a percentage elasticity. Two firms each borrow from banks A and B. Set (xA,xB,beta,a1,a2)=(-1,0,2,3,5) and initially u=0. Ordered (1A,1B,2A,2B), the outcomes are (1,3,3,5). Subtract bank B from bank A within each firm. The common af cancels: outcome differences are (-2,-2), and dividing by xA−xB=−1 yields (2,2).

Now add a lender-specific demand component (u1A,u1B,u2A,u2B)=(-1,0,-1,0). Outcomes become (0,3,2,5). Within-firm differences and the resulting ratios are (-3,-3,3,3). The component did not cancel. Exactly the same outcomes arise from β=3 and all u=0 while retaining the same x and a. Therefore these four observations alone do not distinguish a response of two with differential demand from a response of three without it. An added assumption or evidence is necessary. This finite construction illustrates the logic; it supplies no empirical claim about the papers' samples.

Completed referee-style paragraph for the proposed design. “The proposed study must distinguish a common borrower shock from demand tied to a particular lending relationship. If borrowers use different banks for different activities, a shift in activity can change the within-borrower allocation even without the posited funding mechanism. I would first inspect loan-purpose and activity definitions and assess whether the exposure comparison is plausibly unrelated to those shifts. A comparison within a more homogeneous activity, where observed and substantively justified, could diagnose this threat, but it changes the population and cannot remove unmeasured demand by declaration. Until that case is made, describe the association and report the uncertainty.” This paragraph requests evidence targeted at an assumption; it neither demands every possible control nor invents an instrument.

Decision for Mira. She cannot implement a within-borrower check with bank statements alone. Her immediate action is to finish the coverage and measurement audit, label the proposed association accurately, and record the additional information a causal branch would require. She must not borrow the symbol β, attach firm effects she cannot estimate, and call the original question identified. Literature mapping earns its space by changing that design decision.

4. Maintain the frontier without erasing the historical record

Keep a dated update queue with four states: discovered, screened, substantively read, and incorporated into the synthesis. Record why an item moved between states. Check author pages, journal records and institutional working-paper repositories; follow citation chains in both discovery directions. Search beyond your seeds to avoid inheriting their omissions. A new version requires a comparison of the relevant claims, not merely replacing the date in your bibliography. An inaccessible item stays unresolved; no abstract should silently become a verified result.

Actual bounded update. A later title search found Paravisini, Rappoport and Schnabl, Specialization in Bank Lending: Evidence from Exporting Firms, published in 2023. The publisher page's abstract concerns activity-specific borrowing and bank specialization. On 16 October 2026 we inspected its metadata and abstract only. It is a relevant candidate for the differential-demand branch; its methods, appendix and replication package have not been reviewed here. The queue state is screened, substantive reading pending. This is neither a claim that it overturns the older designs nor a claim that a 2023 item is the latest available research.

Completed update instruction. Next inspect the candidate's measurement and identification sections, record the exact version, and ask whether it changes a specific assumption in the current map. If it does, revise that row and the conclusion; if it addresses a different question, record the connection without treating it as a direct contradiction. Also search for more recent work and corrections before making a live research proposal. The present chapter records a bounded demonstration, not the completion of those future searches.

Write the review in paragraphs organized by issues. One paragraph can explain why observed lending mixes demand and supply, another how a design addresses that problem, and a third where the remaining uncertainty leaves your project. Cite at the claim being supported. Separate your original assessment from source claims, and avoid copied language or patchwork paraphrases. The purpose is an argument a reader can check, not a display of how many PDFs you possess.

Complete answers and evaluation criteria

1. A search with a defined purpose

One query family combines “bank funding maturity,” “loan stock” and “reporting population”; another combines “bank credit supply,” “borrower demand” and “measurement.” Include studies that define the funding and lending quantities relevant to the contract, and studies that evaluate how sample construction or an identifying comparison affects the claim. Exclude duplicate versions from study counts while retaining them in the version register. Prioritize the closest measurement study and the strongest challenge to the intended interpretation. Stop the first drafting pass after the leading alternatives have been read and a further documented search adds no new material argument; retain a dated unresolved queue and update before claiming novelty. The current two-paper sample omits much relevant work, especially nearby descriptive studies. Full credit requires distinct query purposes, explicit gates and an honest coverage limit, not a fixed number of citations.

2. Identify what was actually inspected

Entry: “Work W; authors and title as on the document; inspected version labeled forthcoming, retrieved on the recorded date from the saved source URL; method passage at printed p. 18; publisher metadata confirms a later journal publication, whose full text has not been compared.” Cite that draft and locator for the method claim, with the publication record separately if useful. If a later revision changes the sample, create a new version entry, compare the affected claim and population, and preserve the old note. Do not transfer the page number or conclusion automatically. Full credit requires document identity, version, source, date, location and the unresolved final-version comparison; no invented version date earns credit.

3. Common components cancel; absent variation does not appear

The new a values are (7,-2). Outcomes in the stated order are (5,7,-4,-2). Differences are again −2 and −2, yielding ratios (2,2). Negative indices are allowed because these are changes, not loan stocks. When xA=xB=0, both within-firm exposure differences are zero; the numerator is zero under u=0, but 0/0 is undefined. Replicating this pattern adds no exposure variation. The comparison needs different exposure or another justified design, not a convention that calls an undefined ratio zero.

4. A larger violation changes the apparent response

The outcomes are (-1,3,1,5), the within-firm differences are −4 and −4, and the ratios are (4,4). Setting β=4 and u=0 gives the same observations with the same a and x. Thus the data plus an unrestricted relationship-specific component do not identify β. The calculation does not establish that β is four, nor that a real paper has this violation. It demonstrates which extra restriction does the work.

5. Separate corroboration from overreach

Errors include treating a shared method as proof; ignoring its maintained demand restriction; claiming statistical independence without checking shared sources, methods and relationships; transporting a selected borrower comparison to all banks; replacing a loan outcome with national investment; and assuming a universal response across funding disturbances. A defensible replacement is: “The inspected designs motivate examining borrower demand and funding exposure explicitly; applying that reasoning to our setting requires matching the relevant data, assumptions and outcome, and our current bank-only description does not establish a causal investment effect.” Credit at least four distinct errors and a corrected claim with its scope; a blanket dismissal of fixed effects is not an adequate answer.

6. Different outcomes can move differently

A borrower may replace a lost loan with credit from another lender, so the affected relationship contracts while total credit is unchanged. The two estimates can therefore be compatible. Check whether the studies follow the same borrowers, include new lender relationships and nonbank credit, measure the same dates and currencies, and identify comparable shocks. A matched reconciliation of relationship losses and gains would support the substitution account. If the targets and populations align but results still differ, inspect assignment, measurement and uncertainty before calling a contradiction. Precision alone does not make distinct estimands interchangeable. This is a possible explanation, not an inferred fact about the named papers.

7. An audit trail preserves uncertainty

Register the working paper and journal version under one work identity, with two version entries and the exact passages actually read. Use the blog only as a discovery lead unless its own claim is the object of analysis; do not substitute it for the original method. Record the appendix as inaccessible and its arguments unverified. Register the 2023 item as a separate candidate with metadata/abstract screened and substantive review pending; do not present its methods or results as independently validated. A revised title or journal issue does not by itself create independent evidence. Full credit distinguishes all five supplied document situations, their reading status and limits, and preserves unresolved access rather than filling gaps from memory.

8. Make the review change the proposed paper

“The evidence table makes the supply–demand distinction a central design requirement, while our own diagnostic example shows why canceling a common borrower component does not remove arbitrary relationship-specific variation. Mira's current records can support a carefully defined bank-level description and a coverage audit, but they do not support the missing borrower comparison or an investment effect. 6. Teach-back and delayed retention

Teach-back. Explain an evidence table to a colleague who has only read abstracts. Use the worked map to distinguish work identity from inspected version, an observed citation edge from a discovery route, and an author claim from your own assessment.

Answer guide

A complete explanation names the precise draft used for a method claim, retains separate publication metadata, and describes the citation arrow in its actual direction. It explains that a design's assumptions must be inspected rather than inferred from an abstract. Your assessment should identify a concrete implication for the proposed project; it must not turn a reading exercise into a claim that results were reproduced.

Delayed retention. A week later, an adviser gives you a new working paper with a familiar title and a revised sample. Write a three-part update memo: what changed, what conclusion is affected, and what remains unresolved. Then name one reason to keep the older version.

Criteria and illustrative memo

“The new file is a version of the existing work, not another independent study. Its sample now excludes a previously included borrower group, so I must compare the relevant estimand and result before applying the older conclusion to that group. I have verified the sample-description change but have not rerun code or checked all robustness results. I retain the older version to make the change and the provenance of my prior claim auditable.” Full credit distinguishes an observed change from an anticipated consequence and states an actionable unresolved comparison. If only the abstract was read, the memo must say so.
