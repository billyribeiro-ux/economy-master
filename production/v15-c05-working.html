Capability and prerequisites

Analyze how trade changes firms' market participation, production links and workers' opportunities, then identify the evidence needed for a policy comparison. Prerequisite: development, poverty and structural change. We also use trade prices and ownership, global producer optimization and instrumental-variable identification. Every economy below is synthetic. Tariffs are hypothetical specific charges in a common numeraire, not descriptions of current law.

A shipment is not a welfare statistic

A factory imports components, exports an assembly and employs workers who could work elsewhere. An import tariff can protect its domestic sales while raising its component bill. A shipping improvement can admit new exporters whose productivity is lower than that of existing exporters. A displaced worker may face moving costs even if another employer expands. These are compatible outcomes. They become contradictory only when an analyst treats every firm, input and worker as the same representative unit.

We will keep five objects separate: physical productivity, private profit, domestic value added, household welfare and a causal effect identified from data. Comparative advantage concerns opportunity costs; it does not rank people's worth. An export receipt pays for resources and income claims. Gross exports can contain earlier imports and domestic content that crossed a border before returning. A policy's effect depends on which prices, technologies, entry opportunities, resource constraints and private responses are held fixed. State that closure before calculating a gain.

1. Export entry, scale and the firms being averaged

Take a finite set of existing firms with productivities φ>0, measured as units produced per unit of variable labor. They sell different varieties only abroad. The wage is w>0 and delivery requires τ≥1 units produced for each delivered unit: the extra τ−1 units are physically used in shipping. An avoidable export setup requires F>0 numeraire units of labor expenditure. Firms have no capacity constraint. Foreign demand for each offered variety is q=Bp−2, B>0. This is a constant-elasticity residual demand with elasticity two, not a complete heterogeneous-firm free-entry model.

One foundation for this demand is a foreign buyer with utility N+Σᵢ2√(Bqᵢ), a sufficiently large numeraire endowment and nonnegative consumption. Price-taking buyer choice gives √B/√q=p. A firm choosing its own delivered quantity has revenue √(Bq) and delivered marginal cost c=wτ/φ. For q>0, set z=√q. The operating contribution satisfies

√Bz−cz² = B/(4c)−c[z−√B/(2c)]².

This proves a unique global positive-branch optimum q*=B/(4c²), p*=2c and contribution B/(4c), including comparison with arbitrarily large quantities. Compare that contribution minus F with the zero profit from not entering. Entry is strictly preferred if φ>4wτF/B, absence if the inequality reverses, and both q=0 and q=q* are optimal at equality. No intermediate positive quantity ties. Setup paid irreversibly before the current decision instead belongs to both alternatives; then any finite c admits profitable positive operating contribution, although total accounting profit may be negative.

Lower τ increases each incumbent's delivered sales, holding B,w and F fixed, and weakly expands the set able to cover setup. That statement is conditional. An equilibrium change in wages or foreign demand can reverse a particular firm's response. Exporter productivity premia may reflect selection into paying F rather than learning caused by exporting. The prior MIT heterogeneous-cost trade lecture supplies the method context; the square-root demand economy and accounts here are original.

2. Global production networks: trace quantities and payments

A production network records which sector uses another sector's output. Let aij be units of input i needed per unit of output j; columns describe a producer's input recipe. With gross outputs x and final uses y, material balance is x=Ax+y. Intermediate shipments are inputs, not extra final consumption. If A is nonnegative and its largest column sum is a<1, then ||Az||₁≤a||z||₁. Consequently Σk≥0Aky converges absolutely, is nonnegative for y≥0, solves the balance and is unique: any difference d=Ad has ||d||₁≤a||d||₁, forcing d=0. Each power traces another production round. The column-sum condition is sufficient, not necessary.

Let v be contracted primary-factor cost per unit of gross output. At zero operating profit and without taxes, prices satisfy p=v+A′p. The same argument in the sup norm gives a unique price vector under the stated column bound. Multiplying material balance by p gives p′y=p′(I−A)x=v′x. Final expenditure equals primary value added, while Σpᵢxᵢ includes intermediate bills. A tariff is a transfer to its receiving government and changes the private unit-cost equation; an iceberg loss changes physical requirements. They must not be substituted for one another in a resource ledger. OECD's TiVA overview distinguishes the country origins of value added from gross trade; our two-sector inverse and pricing proof are independently derived.

3. Protection of an output can tax its own production

A nominal tariff concerns a traded product. Effective protection compares the amount available to pay domestic factors after imported inputs. With one imported input per final unit and fixed world prices p_Y,p_M, the free-trade margin is p_Y−p_M>0. Specific tariffs t_Y,t_M change it to p_Y+t_Y−p_M−t_M, so the proportional change in that margin is (t_Y−t_M)/(p_Y−p_M). This is an accounting measure, not an employment or welfare theorem. If input coefficients change, prices react abroad or output ceases to be imported, the calculation needs a new model.

We now supply one. The country is small, world final and component prices are10and6, and imports of both are available at those prices. Home processing uses one component and real numeraire cost C(q)=2q+q²/2. A representative domestic household owns processing profit, starts with100numeraire goods and has utility N+20D−D²/2 over numeraire N≥0 and final consumption D≥0. Government rebates tariff receipts lump sum. Use 0≤t_Y≤4 and0≤t_M≤4; these bounds preserve the import-parity candidate, including a possible zero-final-import boundary. No other wedge, export outlet or financing distortion is introduced.

Strict concavity gives demand D=10−t_Y and processing q=max{0,2+t_Y−t_M}; the nonnegative output corner is part of the solution. In this tariff rectangle D≥q, so final imports M=D−q are nonnegative. Profit is q²/2 when q>0 and zero otherwise. Imports finance input q and final M; government revenue is G=t_YM+t_Mq. Consolidating household, firm and government gives national surplus above the100endowment:

W=20D−D²/2−C(q)−10(D−q)−6q =52−[(D−10)²+(q−2)²]/2.

Thus the untaxed allocation uniquely maximizes this national objective over feasible D,q≥0, by a global completed-square proof. Counting government revenue again would break the identity. This result does not cover a foreign terms-of-trade response, a corrective externality or a distribution-weighted objective. The earlier MIT tariff method route supplies the small-country surplus context, not the original processing example.

4. Adjustment changes private choices and real resources

Workers are not instantly interchangeable across places or skills. Some costs are real moving expenses or lost time; others are transfers such as fees; some are temporary while others persist. An earnings change among workers who remain in a region differs from the change for the original residents including movers. A present-value comparison needs dates, the alternative job and feasible finance. Observed reallocation does not prove that all losers were compensated.

In the following one-period adjustment economy, one unit mass of workers starts in a sector whose current export receipts support a wage6per worker. Foreign customers buy its output at that fixed opportunity price, paying6for each unit supplied. A new domestic numeraire sector has output F(m)=12m−2m²,0≤m≤1, hiring competitively at its marginal product12−4m. Its owners receive profit2m². Workers can take either job; an individual with moving cost k pays k real numeraire goods if switching. Costs are uniformly distributed on[0,12]. Every worker has an initial12numeraire, enough to finance any relevant move and the policies below; no borrowing constraint or labor disutility is omitted. Workers treat wages and lump-sum taxes as fixed. All accounts are in the same period.

For a moving mass m, efficiency requires assigning the lowest-cost workers first: replacing a mover of higher cost with an otherwise identical lower-cost stayer raises resources. The marginal selected cost is12m and total moving resources are∫₀¹²ᵐk/12 dk=6m². Private switching requires k≤(12−4m)−6, giving the unique market-clearing mass m=3/8. To prove completeness, at m=0a positive mass strictly wants to move; at m=1many workers prefer staying; the desired moving mass declines continuously in m and the equation12m=6−4m has one solution. A marginal type is indifferent, a measure-zero choice that cannot change aggregate employment.

5. Trade exposure needs an exclusion argument

A regional exposure index often combines predetermined industry shares sᵢⱼ with industry trade changes gⱼ: Xᵢ=Σⱼsᵢⱼgⱼ. Fixing the shares prevents current employment from appearing mechanically on both sides of a regression; it does not randomize historical specialization. A foreign shock instrument Zᵢ=Σⱼsᵢⱼg*ⱼalso requires a reason that its variation affects the target outcome only through the specified treatment. Common demand, technology, domestic trends, imported inputs and supplier links can violate that claim. Lagging shares does not remove every such channel.

Autor, Dorn and Hanson (2013), section I.B construct local exposure using industry employment and discuss an instrument based on imports into other high-income markets, including threats from correlated demand. That is a methodological reference, not a claim that the design automatically validates a new application. We neither reuse the paper's estimates nor assert that our counterexample describes its data.

Worked case 5 — predetermined shares do not identify the effect

Three equal-weight regions have (shares,exposure multiplier,structural effect,other trend) = ((0,1/2,1),2,-1,1). Their observed exposure is X=2s. Potential employment under exposure x is Yᵢ(x)=100−x+sᵢ: the s term is a separate regional trend, not a consequence of x. Observed X=(0,1,2)and Y=(100,199/2,99). A regression with intercept, or an instrument proportional to s, reports a slope−1/2, whereas the structural derivative holding s fixed is−1. The complete observed equation is Y=100−s, so the exposure and trend cannot be separated from these observations.

More generally Y=100+θX+γs and X=2s imply the same observed data whenever2θ+γ=−1. If γ∈[0,1]is the maintained sensitivity restriction, the complete identified interval is (observed slope,theta lower,theta upper)=(-1/2,-1,-1/2). Every point fits all three regions with γ=−1−2θ; no sampling uncertainty is involved. Controlling perfectly for s does not solve this example: the residualized X and instrument are both zero. Additional independent trade variation or a justified restriction on γis necessary.

Networks also change the estimand. If Yᵢ=θXᵢ+λΣⱼwᵢⱼXⱼ+uᵢ, a shock moving own and neighbor exposure can identify a combined response rather than θ. A valid design should prespecify whether the target is a direct response, a cluster total response or an aggregate equilibrium effect. Record residents before the shock and follow movers; otherwise an apparent regional recovery may reflect replacement of people. Preserve pre-period shares, shock construction, firm and worker identifiers, supplier links, prices, income components and a held-out test of a rival mechanism. Dependence from common industry shocks requires an appropriate sampling design and uncertainty method, not an automatic count of regions as independent observations. These requirements define a research plan; they are not evidence that the plan has already succeeded.

Teach-back

A cabinet asks whether more exports, more movers and more tariff revenue prove that trade policy worked. Answer with one firm entry inequality, one network resource identity and one worker budget. Name one empirical restriction that cannot be inferred from the resulting accounting totals.

Answer guide

Entry requires Bφ/(4wτ)≥Fwith a tie convention; observed exporter averages can change through selection. A causal policy effect additionally needs a credible counterfactual, such as an exclusion restriction that rules out correlated sector trends; accounting cannot prove that restriction.

Scope and next step

The chapter establishes finite selection, conditional input-output accounts, a small-country tariff resource comparison and a complete worker-adjustment equilibrium. It does not prove a general free-entry trade theorem, universal tariff incidence, an empirical causal effect, or automatic compensation of losers.
