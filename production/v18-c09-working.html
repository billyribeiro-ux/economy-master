Operations, innovation and change

Capability. Improve a process by locating its binding resource, testing a defined change and deciding what evidence warrants implementation. Evaluate an AI-assisted financial workflow with explicit losses, coverage, uncertainty, staffing and rollback. Continue financial institution leadership, and retain the probability and experiment-design tools of preanalysis planning and reproducible analysis and exact randomization.

All organizations, prices, cases, assignments, measurements and records below are original synthetic teaching specimens. No actual experiment, model training, human evaluation or customer deployment has occurred. A complete plan or an internally consistent simulation is not observed leadership performance.

1. Improve the whole process

A process transforms an input into an output through tasks, queues and handoffs. A capacity is the maximum rate a resource can sustain under stated conditions; throughput is the rate actually completed. Lead time runs from entry to completion, including waiting. A bottleneck is a resource whose constraint limits the whole flow. Making a nonbinding task faster may change neither completed output nor waiting at the bottleneck.

Define the unit before dividing. A review visit and a completed student submission are different units when work returns for revision. If each specialist review independently returns the work for another review with probability r, where0≤r<1, the number V of visits has Pr(V≥k)=r^(k−1). Since a nonnegative integer V equals the sum of indicators1{V≥k}, its expected value is the sum of those probabilities: E[V]=1+r+r²+…=1/(1−r). The finite geometric sums converge because r<1. Thus a reviewer pool with v visits/day supports v(1−r) completed submissions/day in a long-run fluid workload approximation. At r0 there is one visit; at r1 no work exits this loop and the finite-expectation formula is unavailable.

For a serial intake–review–release process with intake capacity a, review visits v and release capacity f, our fluid model has completed-work capacity μ=min{a,v(1−r),f}. Each completion consumes one intake,1/(1−r) review visits on average and one release, giving these three necessary bounds. In the stipulated divisible steady-flow model, any flow below all three can be scheduled at these workload rates, so their minimum is attainable as the model's capacity. This is not a finite-day stochastic service guarantee: variable arrivals, correlated rework, staff absence, travel between stages, finite buffers or scheduling restrictions can require slack and a richer queue model.

For a separate aggregate fluid planning ledger, let Q be unfinished work, λ arrivals/day and μ available completion capacity/day. With same-day fluid availability, Q(t)=max{0,Q(0)+(λ−μ)t}; cumulative completions equal Q(0)+λt−Q(t). If μ>λ, a positive backlog drains in Q(0)/(μ−λ) days. If μ=λ, it does not drain; if μ<λ, it grows. These claims follow directly from conservation of work while a queue is positive and reflection at zero. Capacity left idle after the backlog clears is not fabricated output. The formula suppresses individual transit times and gives a planning benchmark, not a promised completion date for every person.

2. A change needs a comparison and an owner

Write the mechanism before buying the tool: which task changes, which resource it frees, what quality standard must remain and what new work it creates. Fewer returned submissions can mean better preparation or weaker review. Faster complaint routing can mean useful prioritization or overlooked urgent cases. Preserve the output criterion and measure both speed and defects.

A before/after comparison can mix the change with seasonality, case composition and staffing. Random assignment can support a causal comparison when the assigned units, delivery and outcomes are well defined and interference is controlled. Assigning individual cases within a shared congested queue can affect untreated cases too. The assignment unit may need to be an entire team or queue; the analysis must respect that unit. Held-out classifier evaluation answers a different question: how a fixed prediction rule performed against labels on an unseen test sample. It does not identify the causal effect of using the rule on waiting times, customer outcomes or staff behavior.

The official NIST AI RMF Playbook page, inspected17 October2026 and marked updated10 June2026, describes voluntary guidance organized around Govern, Map, Measure and Manage. It says the Playbook will be updated after the framework revision. We use it as bounded measurement/governance context, not a legal mandate, certification or claim about a vendor's current capabilities.

The official Measure guidance emphasizes documenting test sets and metrics and evaluating conditions relevant to deployment. Its discussion distinguishes computational tests from real-world impacts. Our cases implement that distinction with original numerical rules. Their loss weights and gates are institutional design assumptions; applicable privacy, customer, employment or research requirements would need separate current review before a real pilot.

3. Two complete operating decisions

Case A — shorten academic feedback queues without weakening the standard

Process and mandate. The fictional Elm Methods Center returns substantive feedback on student research designs. Its process is: intake checks completeness → specialist reviews identification and inference → a returned design loops to specialist review after revision → an accepted design receives an independently checked feedback release. The model treats revision availability as immediate and includes all specialist visits; actual student revision time is an omitted lead-time component that the pilot will separately record. Intake and release have no additional rework loop. The assessment standard does not change.

Baseline capacities are intake60 submissions/day, specialist40 visits/day and release50 completed designs/day, with r1/5. Expected visits are5/4 and bottleneck capacity32 completions/day. Independent analysis. Mean treatment time is13/2 and mean control time17/2, giving difference−2. Under the sharp null, every team's outcome stays fixed when assignment changes. Each pair's difference flips sign independently, giving16 equally likely statistics of the form(±2±3±1±2)/4. Only all-positive and all-negative signs attain absolute mean at least2; the inclusive two-sided p-value is2/16=1/8. The tuple(treatment mean,control mean,effect,tail,total,p) is (13/2,17/2,-2,2,16,1/8). We do not reject the sharp null at5%; this is not proof of zero effects or a confidence interval for an average effect.

Why the estimate targets the team effect. Within a pair of teams i,j, averaging over the fair assignment gives expected treated-minus-control difference equal to[(Y_i(1)−Y_i(0))+(Y_j(1)−Y_j(0))]/2. Averaging the four equally weighted pair differences therefore targets the mean of all eight team effects, assuming their outcomes depend only on their own assignments. A shared staff queue would undermine that interpretation. This unbiasedness statement does not make the realized estimate equal the true effect.

Why that test is exact. Under the known assignment law and sharp null, the16 fixed absolute statistics are equally likely. Rank them from largest to smallest, keeping ties in their full upper-tail groups. The fraction of assignments whose inclusive tail probability is at mostα cannot exceedα. Therefore rejection at p≤α has probability at mostα under that null. A weak null of average effect zero does not fix each team's counterfactual outcome; this proof does not test it without additional assumptions. Neither independent student observations nor a normal approximation is needed for this four-pair enumeration.

This is a synthetic operational definition, not a statement of a statutory deadline or a determination of liability. The assistant cannot close complaints, decide compensation, contact customers or change accounts. A false negative routes an urgent case as routine; a false positive routes a routine case as urgent.

An uncertainty gate changes the decision. The fictional risk committee requires a one-sided95% upper confidence bound at most1/20 for the urgent-miss probability among truly urgent cases in the automatically routed population of the fixed primary rule. This is an internal chosen gate, not a regulatory rule. S has zero observed urgent misses, but only4 truly urgent automatic cases. If their miss indicators are independent Bernoulli(p) with a common p for that fixed target population, Pr(zero misses)=(1−p)^n. Inverting this lower-count tail atα1/20 gives pU=1−α^(1/n) when zero misses occur. Values above pU would produce zero misses with probability belowα. For a general observed count x<n, the corresponding upper endpoint solves Σ(k=0,…,x) C(n,k) pU^k(1−pU)^(n−k)=α; set the endpoint to1 at x=n. This inverts lower-tail tests of candidate miss probabilities. For any true p, the observations that exclude p above their upper endpoint form a lower-count tail with probability at mostα; hence the procedure has at least1−α coverage. The zero-count expression is its special case, not a95% posterior probability statement or proof that the actual p is below it.

For n4 the upper endpoint is approximately0.527129, vastly above0.05. S fails the uncertainty gate even though its observed automatic urgent-miss rate is0. A's corresponding observed rate is2/12=1/6 and its whole-test urgent-miss rate is2/30=1/15 under perfect fallback; these denominators answer different questions. Overall accuracy83% for T hides6 misses among30 urgent cases. Neither a cheap average cost nor high overall accuracy resolves the specified critical-risk concern.

For a new prospectively fixed validation sample of truly urgent cases eligible for S, zero misses would meet pU≤0.05 exactly when(19/20)^n≤1/20. Since this probability decreases in n, testing n58 and59 establishes the smallest integer59: upper endpoints are approximately0.050339 and0.049508 respectively. This is not a promise of zero misses, a power calculation or permission to keep sampling until the gate passes. Do not count the same four inspected cases as fresh validation after a redesign. Collecting representative urgent-H cases may require screening many more complaints, and the conditional sample cannot estimate overall coverage. Counts for the exact integer witness are (4,58,59); corresponding rounded upper endpoints are (0.527129,0.050339,0.049508).

4. Preserve a useful record when the change disappoints

An implementation log should distinguish prediction, authorization, action and observed result. For each version retain the process/model specification, eligible population, staffing/cost commitment, quality criterion, experiment or test design, evidence release, decision and owner. A change can fail because the mechanism was wrong, implementation differed, a resource was unavailable or the measurement changed. Diagnose the specific failure before adding technology.

Reversible pilots reduce exposure but still consume staff time and can affect people. Define rollback before launch, including restored capacity, unresolved cases and responsibility for repairing harm. The real-world continuation requires actual authorized implementation, observed records, participant or customer evidence and independent human assessment. Neither correct arithmetic nor a polished change memo substitutes for those achievements.
