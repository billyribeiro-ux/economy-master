Volume 18 · Chapter 1

Institutional purpose and strategy

Capability. Turn an institution's stated purpose into choices, exclusions, resource commitments and evidence that could justify changing direction. Defend a feasible strategy for an academic institution and a financial organization without confusing attractive forecasts with accomplished results.

Prerequisites. Retain the cash/profit distinctions of Volume 7, incremental value and feasible alternatives in Volume 8, the cash/equity ledger of Volume 11, the claim and scope discipline of Volume 16, and the teaching/evidence requirements of Volume 17. All institutions, mandates, records, forecasts and decisions below are fictional. The numerical floors are case-specific internal choices, not current regulatory requirements.

1. A mission must change a decision

“Advance knowledge and serve society” can express a worthwhile purpose, but it does not decide whether scarce faculty time should support an introductory bridge, a research studio or a professional course. “Serve customers and remain resilient” does not decide which customers, through which activities, with what limits on risk and resource use. Strategy becomes inspectable when the institution names an alternative it will decline and explains why.

A mission states the institution's intended contribution and whom it exists to serve. A stakeholder is a party whose legitimate interests, resources, obligations or exposure matter to a decision. A strategy is a coherent set of choices about whom to serve, what to provide, how to provide it and what resources and activities to forgo. A goal describes a desired result; a measure is the operational quantity used to examine progress. A constraint defines what is permissible or feasible. These roles differ: a cash floor is not a mission, a participation count is not a learning result, and a growth target is not a strategy by itself.

The HBS Institute for Strategy and Competitiveness's strategy guidance emphasizes distinctive value, activities, trade-offs, fit and continuity. We use those bounded professional concepts to ask what an institution will actually do. We do not adopt the page's company examples as evidence about our invented cases, or infer that a chosen strategy is guaranteed to succeed.

Distinguish a competitive advantage hypothesis from a demonstrated advantage. An academic institute might expect its advising relationships and local employer knowledge to support working learners. A bank might expect experienced relationship teams to understand a particular client need. Name the mechanism and what evidence would contradict it: insufficient demand at the proposed schedule, inability to provide appropriate support, higher service costs, or failure to meet the promised standard. A self-description such as “world class” identifies neither a comparison nor a test.

Institutions can have more than one purpose. Do not silently turn all purposes into a single financial objective or assign invented monetary values to scholarship, access and trust. An explicit priority order can be more honest: satisfy an agreed service floor and protected commitments, then maximize a specified capacity measure among feasible alternatives. That is still a normative choice. It must be visible, defensible to the relevant decision makers and open to revision; mathematics cannot select the institution's mission for it.

2. Write a decision contract and compare feasible alternatives

A usable decision contract contains the decision and horizon; affected population; mission priority; protected obligations; available resources and dates; feasible alternatives; expected outcomes and evidence quality; decision owner; and a trigger for review. Separate commitments already made from new discretionary choices. Use the same boundary and period for all alternatives. A prospective fee cannot finance an upfront payment unless an actual funding arrangement makes it available in time.

For a finite menu, let x denote a complete choice and let X be all choices under consideration. Let C(x) be resource use, H(x) required staff time and M(x) the mission measure. With available resources B, time T and a mandated access floor A, a simple feasible set is F={x∈X:C(x)≤B,H(x)≤T,access(x)≥A}. An illustrative rule chooses the x in F with greatest M(x). If F is empty, the result is an infeasible mandate, not permission to ignore a constraint. If several choices tie, report the tie and use an explicit secondary consideration or seek more information.

Enumeration is a proof of optimality for a small finite menu only when the menu is complete. Three independent yes/no modules create eight portfolios. Add each portfolio's resource uses, eliminate infeasible ones and compare the declared objective among the survivors. A ratio such as places per currency unit can be informative, but it does not generally solve a problem with indivisible modules, several resources and an access floor. Nor does a superior total score compensate for an unmet hard condition.

The opportunity cost of a choice concerns the best feasible alternative it prevents, under the relevant objective. It need not equal accounting expenditure. A project can leave cash unspent while using all available expert time. Do not call that cash “waste” without identifying a feasible useful purchase. A resource's marginal value can also depend on other resources: one extra teaching slot may accomplish nothing until funding for the accompanying module is available.

The HBS discussion of fit across activities distinguishes improving an individual activity from combining activities that support the chosen position. In our cases, compatible schedules, advising and assessment can support an educational promise; client service, funding and risk controls can support a financial promise. These are mechanisms to investigate, not numerical benefits to add without evidence. An alleged synergy must not erase a resource cost already incurred elsewhere.

3. Measure the promise without changing its meaning

Distinguish inputs, outputs and outcomes. Staff hours are an input; supported places are planned service capacity; enrollment is an observed output when it occurs; independent achievement of a stated capability is an outcome requiring actual work and an assessment. A bank's forecast capacity to onboard clients is not realized service, good customer outcomes or profitable lending. For each measure name the numerator, denominator, eligible population, date, data owner and support or measurement conditions.

Use a small set of measures that can reveal a failure of the strategy. Pair access counts with the standard of service or learning, and pair growth with cost, payment capacity and risk. Preserve the initial cohort when reporting completion unless a different population is explicitly requested. Report missing outcomes and subgroup denominators; neither dropping nonresponders nor counting them as known successes is a neutral choice. A convenient metric can be improved while the mission worsens—for example by admitting only learners already able to pass or serving only clients needing little support.

A revision trigger names a condition, an accountable person and the next action. “Monitor costs” is incomplete. “If the bridge quote exceeds 50 kCU, the finance lead must recompute feasible portfolios before any new commitment” is testable. A trigger can call for investigation rather than automatic cancellation: one poor outcome may reflect implementation, measurement, selection or a wrong strategic premise. Preserve the prior decision and evidence so a changed plan can be explained rather than rewritten as inevitable.

4. Two complete strategy decisions

Case A — access, research and a scarce faculty calendar

Purpose and mandate. The fictional Harbor Institute supports working adults' rigorous economic reasoning and maintains applied scholarship. Its existing teaching obligations and 600 hours of protected research time are already funded outside this incremental decision. For the coming semester it has 90 kCU of unrestricted incremental cash, must retain 10 kCU immediately after commitments, and has 600 additional faculty hours. There is no borrowing or receipt available before the payments. Its fictional governing committee sets this pilot-year rule: provide at least 12 supported places for a predefined priority-access group, then maximize total supported places from the finite menu. The protected research baseline must remain intact. This is an explicit local priority, not a universal academic mission.

A supported place includes the stated teaching, advising and independent assessment capacity, not a guaranteed completion. Cohorts are disjoint, so counts do not double-count a person enrolled in two modules. Priority eligibility is specified before selection. Each module keeps its own substantive capability standard; equal place counts do not imply equal learning gains or credential equivalence. All costs below are paid upfront, faculty hours cover all incremental delivery/support, and there are no unlisted revenues or cost savings.

Before commitments, a quote for B above 50 kCU requires a new allocation decision because B+E would breach the cash floor. Before the next semester, review actual hours, demand, completion evidence and the deferred studio opportunity. None of the projected places is yet an observed learning success.”

A falsifiable boundary. Let the bridge quote be c with all other inputs fixed. B+E respects cash precisely when c+30≤80, hence c≤50; it already exhausts hours. At c 50 it remains eligible with cash 10. At c 51, B+E fails; B alone remains eligible and offers 24 places. The change is a discrete portfolio switch, not a claim that every extra currency unit has the same value. If the institution adds a requirement of 12 studio places while retaining the priority minimum, no current portfolio satisfies all conditions. It must revise scope or obtain both additional money and time, not write a more ambitious mission statement.

Measurement specimen. Suppose a later, separately fictional release reports outcomes for the selected 42-person roster:30 independently meet their module's full criterion,7 do not, and 5 outcomes are missing. The respondent rate is 30/37; the full-roster rate is sharply bounded by 30/42 and 35/42, or (30/37,5/7,5/6). The lower endpoint assigns no missing success; the upper assigns five. The counts do not identify what the program caused, compare equivalent difficulty across modules or prove the advising hypothesis. They do show why reporting “42 successful places” would turn a capacity promise into fabricated achievement.

Case B — a service strategy has two different financial limits

Mission and menu. The fictional Mesa Bank seeks durable service for small organizations needing relationship support. Its committee considers one of three mutually exclusive channel investments, or no expansion. For this decision it requires capacity for at least 40 additional priority clients over the next year, then chooses the greatest forecast priority-client capacity among plans satisfying the stated financial screens. The forecast counts refer to distinct service onboarding capacity, not approved loans, funded credit or improved client outcomes. New lending would require a separate funding and credit decision. A positive recurring-income forecast is a preliminary viability screen, not proof of lifetime economic value.

Timing and accounting. Initially the bank has cash 60 mCU, net loans 140 mCU, deposits 170 mCU and book equity 30 mCU: assets 200 equal liabilities plus equity 200. At date 0 it pays the selected setup cost and expenses it immediately, reducing both cash and equity. At the next specified stress date, deposit withdrawals reduce cash and deposits equally; a newly recognized loss reduces net loans and equity, with no additional cash payment at that instant. There are no other flows, asset sales, emergency borrowing, taxes or dividends. The stress withdrawal and loss are separate specified events, not two deductions for the same expense. Annual recurring-income forecasts occur later and are not credited to the stress-date balances.

The committee's internal screens require stress-date cash at least 10 mCU and equity at least 12 mCU. They are case assumptions, not regulatory minima, solvency certification or a complete risk assessment. Cash and equity cannot be added into one usable payment buffer. The primitive tuple(cash,net loans,deposits,equity,cash floor,equity floor,priority minimum) is (60,140,170,30,10,12,40).

Here each cash/equity movement is nonpositive, so the final balances are the minima across the specified stages; checking them checks those stages. “Our advantage hypothesis is that trained relationship staff and a consistent review process can serve these clients more effectively than a generic acquisition channel. Before expanding, verify the actual approval route and a complete operating/funding plan. Track priority clients actually served, service quality, complaints, staff workload, cash deadlines and loss evidence. Do not reward raw signups while omitting suitability or unresolved service failures.

“Review R before commitment if its loss scenario exceeds 12 or withdrawals exceed 44, since either would breach a floor with setup 6. If broker withdrawal evidence defensibly falls to 46 or less, reopen the strategy comparison: the broker plan may then pass the cash screen and offer greater priority capacity. Do not lower a stress assumption merely to select a preferred channel. Preserve the original scenario, the new evidence and the committee's rationale.”

Thresholds and limits. R passes when 6+w≤50 and 6+ℓ≤18, giving w≤44 and ℓ≤12. B's cash screen is 4+w≤50, or w≤46. At w 46 it has cash 10 and equity 14; at w 45 it has cash 11. If all other assumptions and capacity forecasts remain valid, B then wins the stated capacity rule with 60 versus 48. Boundary tuple is (44,12,46,10,11). This conditional switch is not a recommendation to accept an unsupported optimistic scenario. Floors are inclusive here; crossing below them is materially different from meeting them exactly.

5. Keep strategy and its evidence revisable

Use a short strategy record: purpose and population; chosen and rejected alternatives; resource/date reconciliation; key assumption with evidence status; named outcome measures; decision owner and review date or trigger. Keep disagreement visible. Faculty may reasonably value the deferred studio; a bank's owners may prefer higher income. A coherent strategy explains which priority resolves that disagreement for this decision and who has authority to reconsider it. A mathematical ranking under one rule is not a moral ranking of all stakeholders.

Review implementation and the strategic premise separately. If costs exceed a quote, repair the budget or scope. If the intended group does not want the service, a perfectly executed plan may still fail its purpose. If measured outcomes disappoint, examine selection, missingness and standards before claiming a causal failure or success. The next chapter develops budgets in greater detail; later chapters examine incentives, governance and actual leadership evaluation. This chapter establishes a discipline of coherent, feasible and contestable choices.

Complete solutions and evaluation criteria

1. A decision contract that makes an exclusion

“Harbor will support working adults' economic reasoning while retaining its already funded research baseline. For this semester the committee requires at least 12 priority-access places and then maximizes supported places from the three-module menu. It has 90 kCU cash before upfront payments, a 10 kCU floor and 600 incremental faculty hours, with no new borrowing or receipt. Choose B+E, using 70 kCU and 600 hours for 42 distinct supported places,18 priority places, and defer the research studio. Faculty seeking additional research training may prefer the studio, but B+R exceeds both current resource limits and R+E fails the agreed access floor. The finance and academic leads will reopen the choice if the bridge quote exceeds 50 or the demand/staff assumptions fail, while reporting actual learning evidence separately from planned capacity.” Full credit requires all six requested elements and correct scope; equivalent wording or an explicitly defended different mission proposal is acceptable, but it must not pretend to be the original rule.

2. A quote crosses a discrete boundary

With B 51, the portfolios meeting the priority minimum are B, B+R, B+E and all three. Their costs/hours are(51,400),(101,700),(81,600),(131,900). Only B respects expenditure≤80 and hours≤600. It offers 24 places and 16 priority places, leaving cash 39, cash above floor 29 and time 200. The selected resource vector is (51,400,24,16,39,29,200). Notice: “The new bridge quote makes the former B+E commitment exceed available spending by 1 kCU. We will not consume the required cash floor to preserve the headline 42 places. Under the unchanged mandate choose B alone, revise the advertised capacity to 24 and retain the evening module as deferred. Verify the quote and notify affected parties through the actual institutional process before commitment.” Assess correct feasibility comparisons, slacks and a concrete notice preserving the mandate.

3. Money and time are complements here

Only portfolios containing R can supply 12 studio places. R and R+E provide just 4 and 6 priority places, so meeting both mission floors also requires B. B+R needs 90 kCU expenditure and 700 hours; all three need 120 and 900. Current limits 80 and 600 admit neither. With opening cash 100/reserve 10 and 700 hours, B+R fits exactly, leaves cash 10 and provides 36 places,20 priority and 12 studio: (90,700,36,20,12,10). It is the only feasible portfolio satisfying both floors. Raising cash alone still leaves 700>600 hours; raising time alone still leaves 90>80 spending. The new mandate creates a different problem, so its 36-place choice does not contradict the original 42-place optimum under a different objective/constraint set. Full credit requires an exhaustive argument, not just naming B+R.

4. Report outcomes on the promised population

There are 37 observed outcomes, so the respondent rate is 30/37. If k of the five missing people meet the criterion, the full rate is(30+k)/42 for integer k=0,…,5. The exact set is (5/7,31/42,16/21,11/14,17/21,5/6); endpoint bounds are 5/7 and 5/6. Paragraph: “Thirty of 42 roster members have documented independent completion of their module's full criterion; seven observed outcomes do not meet it and five remain unknown. Respondents average 30/37 completion, while full-roster completion lies between 5/7 and 5/6. These are synthetic performance counts, not a causal effect or evidence that different modules have equal difficulty.” One additional metric is a versioned audit of entry preparation and priority eligibility for the full offered/enrolled population, linked to unchanged assessment criteria and assistance status. Inspect whether an apparent improvement came from excluding harder-to-serve learners or relaxing the standard. Do not assert that either manipulation occurred without evidence. Credit the exact grid, full paragraph and a usable diagnostic measure.

5. A changed stress assumption can change the choice

Broker cash 60−4−45=11; loans 140−12=128; deposits 170−45=125; equity 30−4−12=14. Assets 139 equal deposits 125 plus equity 14. Cash and equity exceed 10 and 12, priority capacity 60 exceeds 40 and recurring income is positive. The vector is (11,128,125,14,139,60). Among now eligible R and B, select B under the stated greatest-priority-capacity rule because 60>48. This is conditional on the revised withdrawal evidence and all other forecasts. Treasury and the risk reviewer must retain the old 50 scenario and explain why the revised one is decision-relevant. Full credit requires the ledger, all screens, rule-based choice and evidence condition.

6. Cash can pass while equity fails

R now leaves cash 60−12−35=13 and equity 30−12−7=11. Cash passes by 3, equity fails by 1: (13,11,3,-1). Broker still fails cash; Digital still fails the 40 priority-capacity floor. Thus no expansion satisfies all conditions. O preserves the financial screens but does not meet the expansion mandate. Decision: “Suspend the expansion commitment and return an infeasibility report to the committee. Seek a documented lower-cost design, actual additional resources or an explicit mandate revision; compare the revised alternatives before approval. Maintaining the existing service is a temporary fallback, not a claim that the 40-client expansion target has been achieved.” With original stress amounts, R's setup must satisfy c≤15 for cash and c≤11 for equity; equity is the tighter bound. Full credit requires both different screens and an honest fallback.

7. A favorable metric is not dominance

“Digital's later recurring-income forecast 5 exceeds Relationship's 4, but its priority capacity 24 is below Relationship's 48 and below the mandate 40. It therefore does not dominate Relationship across the relevant criteria and is ineligible under this rule. Digital's closing cash is 20 and equity 14, not a combined 39; even their arithmetic sum 34 is not a cash account.” Cash is already an asset in the identity 20+134=140+14. Equity is the residual after deposits, not an extra asset that can be spent on top of cash. Counting it again as payment capacity double-counts a funding claim. The two screens must be tested separately and later income cannot be moved back to the stress date. If the institution instead chose income as its priority after revising the mandate, it would need an explicit new decision and a complete value/funding analysis. Credit correct numbers, nondominance, ledger logic and the changed-rule distinction.

8. A review note that can reject preferred evidence

“Harbor's revised B+E cost is 49+30=79, leaving 11 cash and still using all 600 faculty hours. It remains eligible for 42 supported places and 18 priority places under the original mandate, with only 1 kCU cash headroom. The finance lead must verify the quote and commitments before spending; the academic lead must verify the timetable and demand. Five missing outcomes remain missing, so any performance report keeps the full-roster bounds 5/7 to 5/6 and the distinct respondent rate. Our advantage hypothesis is that prerequisite support plus the evening schedule enables the intended working learners to participate under the unchanged standard; compare actual eligible demand, attendance barriers and independent work before attributing gains to the design.

“The broker plan would pass the stated numerical screens at withdrawals 45, but the sponsor's preference supplies no evidentiary basis for replacing 50. Preserve the original scenario and do not approve a plan by editing its risk premise. Treasury must supply a dated, reviewable withdrawal analysis and the risk lead must challenge its scope; until then use the original comparison, where Relationship alone meets all conditions. Our bank advantage hypothesis is that the relationship process can reliably serve 48 priority clients without the broker funding exposure; test actual service demand, staff load and risk evidence rather than counting applications as successful service. Each committee must preserve its decision log, identify unresolved assumptions and schedule the next evidence review. Neither institution has demonstrated causal impact, comprehensive resilience or actual leadership success merely by completing this memo.”

Cash result at Harbor is (79,11,1). Evaluate factual reconciliation, truthful missingness, rejection of unsupported scenario editing, named owners/actions and two falsifiable hypotheses. A different defensible review can earn full credit if it does not change the given facts or conceal the mission trade-offs.

Teach-back and delayed retention

Teach-back. Explain in three minutes why “maximize income” and “maximize supported places” are incomplete without constraints and a declared institutional purpose. Use one rejected alternative from each case and identify its exact failure. Guide: Harbor's R+E fits resources but misses priority access; B+R exceeds money and time. Mesa's Broker promises more income/capacity but fails cash, while Digital passes financial screens but misses the priority mandate. These choices express the supplied priorities, not universal institutional rankings.

Delayed transfer. After a gap, set Harbor's bridge cost to 50 and keep all other original inputs. For Mesa keep R's setup 6 and withdrawal 35 but raise its loss to 12. Decide whether each selected plan still meets its floors, then state what happens if each changed input rises by one more unit. Full answer: Harbor B+E spends 80 and leaves 10 cash, with 600 hours and 18 priority places; at 51 it fails cash and B alone becomes the original-rule choice. Mesa R at loss 12 has cash 19,equity 12 and remains eligible; at 13 equity 11 fails, and with other original plans no expansion meets all conditions. The boundary vector is (80,10,19,12,11). Meeting a modeled floor does not prove robustness to additional unmodeled costs or shocks. Record actual unaided reasoning before claiming retention.

No full Porter article, company-performance study or universal causal claim is inferred from them.
