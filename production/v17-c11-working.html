Observed teaching and reflective practice

Capability. Evaluate teaching through a declared observation purpose, specific evidence of instruction and learner reasoning, a justified revision and a fresh check. Separate what an observer saw, what a submitted response demonstrates and what can be inferred about learning or teaching effectiveness.

Prerequisites. Use misconceptions and learning evidence, criterion-based feedback and communication of bounded findings. The disciplinary examples revisit demand elasticity and portfolio variance; all needed calculations are supplied. Every observation note, teacher and learner response below is fictional calibration material. No real lesson has been observed or learner certified by writing this chapter.

1. Decide what the observation is for

An observation is a record of events within a stated window and vantage point. An interpretation explains what those events may mean. An evaluation judges performance against declared criteria. Reflection connects that evidence to a reasoned decision about what to retain, change and examine next. “I felt the class went well” may be an honest impression, but it is not an analysis of what learners understood.

Agree before the visit whether the purpose is developmental feedback, a course review or a consequential personnel decision. These purposes have different disclosure, authority and evidence requirements. A colleague's confidential coaching conversation must not silently become a formal employment evaluation. The CMU classroom-observation guidance recommends discussing goals, materials and a feedback focus before observing and discussing the evidence afterward. The Cornell program description provides an example of a developmental conversation explicitly distinct from personnel evaluation. Those scoped professional examples do not grant this library access to a classroom or establish another institution's confidentiality rules.

A usable pre-observation agreement names the teacher, qualified observer, audience, lesson/version, intended capabilities, relevant prerequisites, focus questions, observation window, evidence to be collected, permitted recipients and actual retention/access arrangements. In a real setting, fill these fields from the participants' agreement and applicable procedures. Leave unavailable information pending; an attractive form does not establish permission. If recording or identifiable learner work is proposed, resolve the actual consent, access and use conditions before collecting it. A generic promise of anonymity is not enough when examples could identify a person.

Choose a narrow enough focus to observe well. “Does the lesson help learners distinguish a derivative's units from a proportional sensitivity?” directs attention to examples, questions and responses. “Is this instructor excellent?” requires much wider evidence than a short visit. Ask the observer to record both a possible strength and an obstacle, with their locations. A visitor may miss a quiet conversation or have limited disciplinary expertise; record those limits rather than treating every absence from their notes as proof that an event did not happen.

2. An evidence-based observation rubric

The following rubric is a proposed developmental instrument. It has no compensating numerical total. A missing opportunity to observe is different from evidence of failure. Use the states supported in this window, partly supported, material concern and not observed, then state the evidence. The same wording is not an empirically validated rating scale and should not be repurposed for high-stakes comparison without further work.

Write notes in two columns: event and provisional interpretation. An event might say, “At minute 11 the instructor asked for a denominator, paused two seconds and supplied it.” A defensible interpretation is that this instance gave limited opportunity to observe independent selection. “Students were lazy” is neither an event nor a justified diagnosis. Record exact task versions and sample responses so a second reviewer can examine the inference.

Triangulation means bringing different relevant evidence to the same question. A clear explanation, a learner's independent derivation and a delayed transfer response together support more than any one alone. Three repetitions of the same satisfaction judgment are not three independent demonstrations of mathematical understanding. Observation, learner work and course materials answer different questions; do not force them into one score without a justified interpretation.

In a post-observation conversation, let the teacher describe what they intended and what they noticed. Compare that account with the notes and actual work. Correct factual disagreements in the record. Identify one consequential improvement, its mechanism and a new evidence task. Preserve justified disagreement about interpretation. A reflection should say why the proposed change addresses the observed obstacle and what result would lead to another revision.

3. Two complete calibration dossiers

Case 1 — A correct slope with the wrong interpretation

Pre-observation agreement, fictional completed specimen. A teacher plans a 20-minute introductory demand lesson for four learners who have practiced derivatives and ratios. Objective: calculate and interpret point price elasticity while distinguishing it from slope. The observer will inspect the displayed example, question/response sequence and four anonymous exit slips; the developmental report goes to the teacher in this fictional scenario. No video is collected. The observer will not infer durable learning or causal effectiveness from this one session. A real agreement needs real names, permissions and dates; these are not supplied by the specimen.

Original lesson and complete key. Quantity q is measured in units per week and price p in currency per unit. The stipulated demand curve is q(p)=100−10p on 0<p<10. The coefficients and assessed price are (100,10,2), ordered intercept, positive slope magnitude and price. Point price elasticity is ε=(dq/dp)(p/q) where q>0. At p=2, q=80, dq/dp=−10 and ε=−1/4, recorded as (80,-10,-1/4). Slope has units of quantity per price; elasticity is dimensionless because the units in p/q cancel those in the derivative. Near that point a 1% price increase produces approximately a 0.25% quantity decrease under this curve. It is a local approximation, not an exact finite-change rule or an estimated real demand relation.

At p=4, q=60 and ε=−2/3 despite the same slope−10. In general ε(p)=−10p/(100−10p), so it varies with p even on a straight line. The formula is undefined at q=0, outside the stated domain. Revenue R(p)=pq(p) has derivative q+p(dq/dp)=q(1+ε); at p2 this is60, so a sufficiently small price rise increases revenue in this maintained model. The derivative identity connects the interpretation to an economic consequence without claiming that a real firm can hold the demand curve fixed.

Full observer feedback memo. “The derivative and elasticity substitutions are mathematically correct and the text labels are available. Completed rubric judgment. Accuracy: supported for displayed computation, partly supported for communication of units. Sequence: partly supported because the interpretation objective lacked an explicit comparison. Elicitation/response: material concern for the unresolved percent question and reliance on agreement. Access/participation: text route observed; broader access not assessed. Follow-up: not observed yet. These domain judgments have locators; they do not average to a grade for the teacher or attribute a stable trait to a learner.

Teacher reflection and revised ten-minute segment. “I treated agreement with the arithmetic as evidence of the target interpretation. I will retain the correct worked derivative, add a units comparison, and replace the agreement question with an individual changed problem.” Minutes0–3: write slope units and cancel them in ε; ask which quantity measures absolute change and which proportional change. Minutes3–6: compare p2 and p4 on the original curve; learners explain why−1/4 and−2/3 coexist with a constant−10 slope. Minutes6–9: individually solve q=120−20p at p2 without the original completed table. Minute9–10: collect the interpretation and name the remaining uncertainty. These are proposed actions; they become an implemented revision only when actually carried out.

Fresh task and full key. On q=120−20p at p2, q=80, slope−20 and ε=−1/2: (80,-20,-1/2). A 1% local price increase approximately reduces quantity0.5%, conditional on this curve. Revenue derivative is40. The new coefficient matters: copying−1/4 from the original curve fails even though q remains80. A second check at p3 gives q60, ε=−1 and revenue derivative0; this is a first-derivative boundary, not by itself a claim about every finite price change.

Fictional follow-up release and disposition. In a later session, the case stipulates independent submissions: A and C give−1/2 with the full dimensionless local interpretation; B gives−1/2 but again says “currency units”; D does not submit. Among the three returners, two satisfy the full criterion. Across all four original learners, the proportion satisfying it lies in (1/2,3/4) depending on D's unobserved performance. The initial complete-response proportion was1/4. That descriptive increase does not identify the revision's causal effect: the tasks, practice and time differ, and missingness remains. The decision is to retain the revised contrast, target B's unit explanation with a new task, and seek D's evidence through an agreed route. It is not “all learners now understand elasticity.”

Case 2 — An observer must check the mathematics too

Pre-observation agreement, fictional specimen. An advanced finance instructor asks a disciplinary peer to observe a 25-minute risk lesson. The objective is to derive the risk of a weighted return and explain when averaging individual standard deviations fails. The observer will check the formula and inspect four written responses. A presentation-only visitor without the relevant mathematics would need another qualified reader before issuing an accuracy judgment. The purpose remains developmental; no real personnel decision is implied.

Full teaching population. Four states have equal probability. X and Y are returns measured in percentage points, with arrays ((2,2,-2,-2),(-4,4,-4,4)). This is the complete synthetic probability law, not a four-observation sample requiring estimation. Both means are0. Variance is E[(X−E[X])²], covariance is E[(X−E[X])(Y−E[Y])], and standard deviation is the nonnegative square root of variance. Here (Var X, Var Y, Cov(X,Y))=(4,16,0), in squared percentage points. Standard deviations are2 and4 percentage points.

For weight w on X and1−w on Y, linearity gives the mean, and expansion of the squared centered sum gives Var(wX+(1−w)Y)=w²Var X+(1−w)²Var Y+2w(1−w)Cov(X,Y). No independence is needed for that identity. At w=1/2, portfolio returns are (-1,3,-3,1), variance is 5 and standard deviation is √5 percentage points. Averaging the two standard deviations gives3, which is wrong for these primitives;3²=9 does not equal the directly enumerated variance5.

Correction, reflection and revised segment. Teacher response: “I accept the mathematical error and will preserve the original key with a correction note. I will regrade every affected response under the corrected criterion, explain how the misleading key will be handled through the actual course procedure, and distinguish a student's independent reasoning from following my faulty instruction. I will derive the squared-sum identity, enumerate the state returns, and ask for a changed covariance case. My mistake was treating a special comovement case as a universal rule.” The revised 15-minute plan uses4 minutes to enumerate returns,4 to derive the cross term,4 for the independent changed case and3 for explanation/collection. These times are a planning allocation, not evidence that the revision occurred.

Boundary demonstration. If Y=2X in every state, the equal-weight return is(3/2)X, so its SD is3; averaging individual SDs happens to work. If Y=−2X instead, it becomes−X/2 and SD is1. Both Y versions have SD4, but covariance is respectively8 and−8. The ordered equal-weight variances are (9,1). This proves the original average-SD claim fails globally while identifying its perfect-positive-comovement special case. It does not say correlation will be stable in real markets.

Fresh independent task and complete key. Use the same X array with Y=−2X, permit w∈[0,1], and ask for all weights eliminating return variation. Portfolio return=(3w−2)X, variance=4(3w−2)². Because Var X=4>0, zero variance holds iff w=2/3. The remaining1/3 is on Y; the portfolio is identically0 in every state, so its mean also equals0. The result (X weight,Y weight,variance,mean) is (2/3,1/3,0,0). A zero-risk zero-return portfolio in this constructed model is not an arbitrage profit or a recommendation about actual assets.

Follow-up evidence status. The fictional case contains the corrected plan and key but no later learner submissions or second observation. Therefore “revision prepared” is supported, while “revision implemented,” “students improved” and “transfer demonstrated” are not. Request an actual follow-up visit and individual changed-case responses before upgrading those states. A strong reflection can correctly admit that the new plan is untested. A polished account that invents successful responses would undermine the very evidence it purports to provide.

4. From a visit to defensible reflective practice

Preserve a chain of versions: original goal and materials; observation notes; work samples and conditions; observer feedback; teacher response; revised materials; actual follow-up evidence; final scoped judgment. Each link answers a different question. An observer's recommendation does not establish implementation; implementation does not automatically establish learning; learning on one task does not demonstrate all-purpose competence. The chain helps identify exactly which next action is needed.

Reflection should include a rival explanation. In Case1, a later correct answer may reflect additional practice, a simpler task or help outside the session. That does not make the evidence worthless; it limits the claim to performance under the recorded conditions. To estimate a causal instructional effect, a credible comparison and appropriate measurement/design would be needed. Do not retrofit a causal experiment onto an informal observation without its required design and authorization.

Accessibility review is concrete: can learners read the quantitative labels, access the representation, use the intended response mode and demonstrate the target without an irrelevant barrier? A text copy in Case1 is one observed feature, not proof that all needs were met. Ask about barriers without requiring public disclosure. If a graph's shape is the learning target, an alternative must preserve the information needed to reason about that shape. Record what changed and what remains to be tested.

A final report should include context, actual evidence window, criterion findings with locators, strengths to retain, material concerns, teacher response, agreed revision, follow-up evidence and limits. Avoid personality labels, unsupported comparisons with other teachers and promises of confidentiality beyond actual arrangements. If the observer and teacher disagree, retain both arguments and request a relevant independent check. The goal is a warranted account of teaching and learning, not a flawless narrative.

Complete solutions and evaluation criteria

1. Record events before diagnosing them

Note: “Between minutes8–12, the teacher asked for agreement and two audible voices answered yes; no individual interpretation was collected then. Between12–16, a learner asked whether slope−10 meant a10% fall, and the teacher repeated the formula and supplied another substitution. This gives limited evidence of independent interpretation and leaves the voiced units question unresolved in the observed exchange. We do not know what every learner understood or felt. Ask the teacher what evidence they intended the agreement check to elicit and inspect individual explanations of slope versus elasticity.” Credit precise events, a bounded inference, an explicit unknown and an actionable question; replacing one personality label with another is not a repair.

2. A fixed absolute slope need not mean fixed proportional sensitivity

At p2, q80, slope−10, ε−1/4; at p4, q60, slope−10, ε−2/3. The vectors are ((80,-10,-1/4),(60,-10,-2/3)). Explanation: “One extra currency unit of price changes predicted weekly quantity by−10 units along this curve. Elasticity rescales that change relative to the current price and quantity, so it has no units and changes as the base values change.” At p10, q0 and p/q is undefined; that point is outside the positive-quantity domain. Credit both computations, units, local versus finite interpretation and the boundary. A negative sign alone does not answer what is measured.

3. Keep the roster denominator

Two of three observed follow-up responses meet the criterion; one of four original learners is missing. Full-class complete performance is between2/4 and3/4, so its difference from the initial1/4 lies in (1/4,1/2), or25–50 percentage points. This is a descriptive comparison of the specified tasks under recorded conditions, not a causal effect or unrestricted mastery. The missing learner's response, comparability of task demand and assistance, and a defensible causal comparison remain unresolved. Credit denominators, attainable bounds and both interpretation repairs. Do not treat a missing response as automatic failure when stating a bound on underlying performance.

4. An actual revised task

Prompt: “Use q=90−15p for0<p<6. Define p and q as in the original lesson. At p2, find quantity, slope and point elasticity, and explain a small1% price rise in a written or approved equivalent response. Repeat at p3 and explain the difference.” The key is ((60,-15,-1/2),(45,-15,-1)). At p2 the approximate quantity fall is0.5%; at p3 it is1%, under the fixed curve. Supply the formula and unit labels in accessible text, but no completed substitution. Criterion: correct values plus dimensionless local interpretation at each base and explanation that the slope stays−15 while the p/q factor changes. An oral format can be an agreed alternative when writing fluency is not the construct; record assistance. Credit the full prompt, key, usable access route and inspectable standard, not a generic instruction to differentiate teaching.

6. Covariance changes the optimal weights

At weight3/4 on X the returns are (1/2,5/2,-5/2,-1/2), mean0 and variance13/4. With zero covariance, V(w)=4w²+16(1−w)²=20(w−4/5)²+16/5. Thus the unique constrained minimizer is w4/5, inside[0,1], and its variance is16/5; the checked tuple is (13/4,4/5,16/5). Completing the square proves global optimality, including endpoints; it is not only a stationary-point check. In the negative-comovement task covariance is−8 and V(w)=4(3w−2)², whose zero is2/3. Different joint laws produce different objectives despite the same individual standard deviations. Credit all four returns, exact variance, the global proof and the covariance distinction.

Teach-back and delayed retention

Teach-back. Explain to an observer why a correct teacher calculation, a correct learner response and a successful lesson revision are different claims. Answer guide: Case1's substitution was right while the targeted units distinction remained incomplete in some work; Case2's prepared correction has no follow-up implementation evidence. Name the actual artifact and its limit for each claim, then request the next needed observation rather than guessing its result.

Delayed retention check. On the original zero-covariance risk law, a learner uses w1 on X. Give the resulting mean, variance and standard deviation, and compare its variance with the optimal16/5. Full key: mean0, variance4, SD2; excess variance is4/5. All-X is feasible but not minimum variance under that law. Then rewrite “the observer liked my lesson, therefore students retained it” as an evidence statement: positive observer feedback is recorded, while delayed independent learner performance remains to be observed. A real examiner must collect the response before treating this key as evidence of retention.
