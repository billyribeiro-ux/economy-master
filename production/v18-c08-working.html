Volume 18 · Chapter 8

Financial institution leadership

Capability. Connect a financial institution's commercial promise to dated cash, loss absorption, operating controls and accountable decisions. Defend both a growth allocation and a funding response, including the conditions that would invalidate them.

Prerequisites. Continue the institution-specific governance discipline of academic institution leadership; retain bank balance sheets, capital and liquidity, dated repayment analysis and constrained optimization. All institutions, contracts, forecasts and committee records here are original fictional cases. Amounts are millions of arbitrary currency units, mCU, for one legal entity and one currency. No case threshold is a legal or regulatory minimum, and no actual leadership event is claimed.

1. A business model must survive its funding dates

A bank can earn a spread by funding loans with deposits and other liabilities, but the spread does not itself provide cash when depositors withdraw. Credit losses can reduce the assets available to support claims; payments can drain cash without reducing equity. A leader must ask which customers and services justify the exposures, what resources each business consumes, when receipts and payments occur, and who can stop an unsupported commitment. A forecast that answers only “how much income?” is incomplete.

Let C be usable cash, S securities at stipulated carrying amount, N net loans, D deposits, B borrowing and E book equity. Our simplified identity is C+S+N=D+B+E. There are no other accounts, taxes, dividends or valuation movements unless stated. Book equity is a residual funding claim, not cash to add to C. A loss allowance is already reflected in N; a newly recognized loss reduces N and E once. A loan paid to another bank converts cash into a loan asset. A withdrawal reduces C and D equally. A new cash equity issue raises C and E; borrowing raises C and B and cannot replace equity loss absorption.

Capital allocation here means deciding how much of the institution's capacity to absorb modeled losses a business may consume. It is not a second transfer of cash into a department. Risk appetite is the chosen exposure to risk, expressed through explicit limits and exclusions; capacity is what resources and constraints can support. An appetite choice must fit that capacity. A cash floor and an equity floor answer different questions. Neither a risk-weighted ratio nor our book-equity test is a complete safety certificate.

The official BIS FSI governance summary, published 27 April 2023, connects board oversight, management accountability, independent control functions and remuneration aligned with risk. It explicitly recognizes jurisdictional differences. We use that narrow governance framework, not a universal legal allocation of powers. The cases below supply their own decision rights and numerical rules. A real institution must identify its charter, supervisor, applicable jurisdiction, consolidation boundary and effective requirements before acting.

2. Make a limit executable

A usable limit has a defined quantity, valuation, population, horizon, owner and breach action. “Keep risk low” is not one. “Before settlement, treasury must confirm usable cash after all authorized payments remains at least the internal floor; an unconfirmed receipt cannot be counted” is executable. An internal warning can be stricter than a hard floor. A changed floor requires its authorized owner, a rationale and a new feasible analysis; changing a spreadsheet label is no approval.

In our cases, the board owns internal appetite and major exceptions. Management proposes a business plan; treasury owns cash and funding execution; credit risk challenges loss and collateral assumptions; finance reconciles the ledger and forecasts; compliance identifies applicable duties and notification routes; internal audit later tests the control process independently. These are the fictional charters for the exercises, not statements of every bank's legal organization. Independence means a revenue owner cannot silently overrule a control challenge; it does not mean the control function becomes responsible for selling or operating the product.

Business performance needs a denominator and a horizon. Volume ignores underwriting quality, funding needs and operational capacity. One-period profit can omit later losses. Risk-adjusted profit is meaningful only when the charge and losses are defined; an internal charge is not an observable law of nature. Pair it with conduct and control evidence. Deferred pay is a contractual design requiring actual authority, and neither a delay nor a score proves that incentives work. Preserve good-faith risk reporting; a bad state realization alone does not establish misconduct.

The official BCBS liquidity-guidance publication page, dated 25 September 2008, emphasizes dated obligations, stress scenarios, collateral and operational contingency funding. Its HTML overview was freshly inspected on 17 October 2026; the linked full paper is outside this inspection. The original guidance page was labeled current when retrieved, but that is not a statement of local legal applicability. We adopt no liquidity-coverage ratio or national threshold. Our exact cash models teach the timing problem directly.

3. Two complete leadership decisions

Case A — allocate lending capacity without spending the capital buffer twice

Mandate and timing. Fictional Juniper Bank serves established local firms. It has verified eligible demand for up to 12 mCU of each of two divisible loan books, X and Y. Eligibility and borrower repayment review are prerequisites, not outcomes produced by this allocation. At date 0 cash is30, net loans170, deposits180 and equity20. There are no securities or borrowings. Newly originated loans x and y are credited to borrower deposits and then entirely transferred to other banks before the specified stress date. Thus origination first raises loans and deposits by x+y; external transfer then reduces cash and deposits by x+y, leaving aggregate deposits unchanged from their opening180. A separate withdrawal of5 follows. A new loss of4 on existing loans and stress losses x/10 and3y/10 are then recognized. There are no intervening receipts.

The board requires cash at least10 and equity at least13 at every stated stage. It authorizes management to maximize forecast annual contribution P=2x/25+3y/25 within those limits and demand caps. Contribution is after forecast funding, servicing and expected credit costs, but before any separate internal capital charge. It is a planning objective, not date-0 cash, a realized profit or a full franchise NPV. Stress losses are a separate adverse scenario; do not deduct them again from that ordinary forecast or add the forecast to stress equity. We hold the book margins constant within the stated capacity range.

Input tuple(C,N,D,E,withdrawal,existing loss,cash floor,equity floor,demand cap) is (30,170,180,20,5,4,10,13,12). Book tuples(stress loss per unit,forecast contribution per unit) are ((1/10,2/25),(3/10,3/25)). All rates are stipulated, not estimated default probabilities.

Feasible allocation. After the stated events, C=25−x−y, N=166+9x/10+7y/10, D=175 and E=16−x/10−3y/10. Both sides of the balance sheet equal191−x/10−3y/10. Cash and equity decline over these specified stages, so their final floors also protect earlier stages. The constraints are x+y≤15, x/10+3y/10≤3 and 0≤x,y≤12. The amount3 is the remaining stress-loss capacity after the existing loss and equity floor; it is not another cash asset.

Global solution and proof. For every feasible pair, P=(3/50)(x+y)+(1/5)(x/10+3y/10)≤(3/50)15+(1/5)3=3/2. Equality requires both resource constraints to bind because both multipliers are positive. Solving x+y=15 and x+3y=30 gives x=y=15/2, inside both demand caps, so it attains the bound and is the unique optimum. This is a complete upper-bound certificate, not a sampled graph. The allocation/forecast tuple is (15/2,15/2,3/2). Its closing ledger(C,N,D,E,total assets) is (10,178,175,13,188). Equity/assets is13/188 under these book conventions, not a regulatory capital ratio.

Y has the higher margin per unit, but lending12 entirely to Y consumes stress-loss capacity18/5, above3. Giving all feasible risk capacity to Y permits y10 and earns6/5, below3/2. X12/Y3 earns33/25 while leaving some risk capacity unused. Liquidity and loss absorption jointly determine the optimum; neither a raw margin ranking nor an exclusive focus on one resource solves it. The shadow values3/50 and1/5 apply locally while the same constraints bind, not to arbitrary large changes.

Complete committee decision. “Approve a conditional allocation ceiling of7.5 to each book under the supplied model. Forecast contribution is1.5; stress cash10 and equity13 meet our internal floors exactly. We approve no dividend and no additional book beyond these ceilings. This boundary solution has no modeled buffer for an omitted cash payment or loss. The commercial head must supply eligible borrower files and staffing capacity before commitments; treasury must validate every settlement date and the separate5 withdrawal; credit risk must challenge both new-book loss rates and the existing4 loss. Finance must reconcile the opening200 balance and closing188 balance without recording forecast income as cash.

“Treasury and credit risk jointly release each tranche only after the cumulative committed amounts satisfy both inequalities. A pending loan counts against its authorized limit before it is funded; cancellation requires a recorded release. If a verified assumption changes, freeze uncommitted growth and return a revised allocation before execution. The board alone owns any floor change. Management will report actual contribution by origination cohort, losses, service failures and cash timing, with expected and stress figures in separate columns. A favorable volume total does not excuse an unauthorized limit breach.”

Case B — meet runoff without treating collateral as free cash

Opening position and menu. Fictional Ridge Bank has cash12, securities80 at book value, net loans108, deposits180 and equity20; total assets200. It must meet a specified deposit runoff40 by date1 and retain cash8. Before payment it recognizes an additional loan loss6, leaving net loans102 and equity14. It may sell s of the securities at90% of book value, recognizing loss s/10, and borrow principal b up to20. Borrowing requires an upfront cash fee b/50, expensed immediately; net new cash is49b/50. There is no later interest in this stipulated contract, and principal b is due at date2. This is an explicit simplified accounting convention, not a statement about real effective-interest accounting.

The secured lender advances at most75% of the book amount of the unsold eligible securities: b≤3(80−s)/4. The25% haircut is stipulated against this collateral basis, not inferred from the10% sale discount. The same security cannot be sold and pledged. All securities are initially unencumbered; other assets cannot be sold or pledged in this menu. Quotes and facility are contractually executable before runoff, with no settlement lag, margin call or other flow within date1. Actual execution requires those assumptions to be verified. After the fee and sale loss, equity must remain at least10. Cash floor8 applies after runoff; sale/borrowing proceeds arrive before payment. No uncertain rescue is counted.

Tuple(C,S,N,D,E,runoff,new loan loss,cash floor,equity floor,borrowing cap,sale discount,fee,haircut) is (12,80,108,180,20,40,6,8,10,20,1/10,1/50,1/4). The committee minimizes known immediate equity erosion L=s/10+b/50, conditional on meeting cash, equity and collateral constraints. It does not maximize long-run bank value or assert that selling and borrowing are equally reversible.

Cash and capital constraints. Final cash is12+9s/10+49b/50−40, so9s/10+49b/50≥36. Equity is14−s/10−b/50, requiring L≤4. Also0≤s≤80,0≤b≤20 and the collateral inequality holds. Borrowing principal increases liabilities; its fee reduces cash and equity. The principal itself is not income. Selling securities changes their composition into cash and realizes the discount; it does not create the full sale proceeds as profit.

Global optimum. From the cash condition, s≥(36−49b/50)/(9/10). Since b≤20, this lower bound is positive. Therefore L≥4−4b/45≥20/9. Equality requires b20 and s164/9. These choices satisfy the sale bound and collateral capacity3(80−164/9)/4=139/3, which exceeds20. They also satisfy L20/9≤4. Hence the unique least-cost feasible plan is (164/9,20,20/9,139/3) for(s,b,L,collateral capacity). The strict decrease of the bound in b makes uniqueness explicit. The preference depends on the assumed fee and sale discount; it is not a universal instruction to maximize secured borrowing.

Full ledger. After the loan loss, sale, borrowing principal and fee, then runoff, the closing(C,S,N,D,B,E,total assets) is (8,556/9,102,140,20,106/9,1546/9). Assets equal140+20+106/9. Equity headroom is16/9 and cash headroom zero. Remaining securities556/9 support the borrowing; the sale amount164/9 is excluded from that pool. Counting the entire original80 as still pledgeable would conceal the lost collateral.

Repayment continuation. At date2 the case stipulates collection of25 of the remaining net loan principal from outside the bank, with no additional income or loss. Cash rises from8 to33, loans fall from102 to77; repaying principal20 leaves cash13 and borrowing0. Equity remains106/9, securities556/9 and deposits140. The tuple(C,N,B,E,total assets) is (13,77,0,106/9,1366/9), and it balances. The receipt exchanges assets; it is not profit. If only15 arrives, cash after full repayment is3, below the8 floor by5; a funding plan based on25 must be revised before relying on it. The model does not guarantee collection because the arithmetic balances.

Complete funding-committee minute. “Authorize, conditional on verified execution, principal borrowing20 and sale164/9 of the designated securities. Net funding36 covers runoff while preserving cash8; the combined sale loss and fee20/9 leave equity106/9. Treasury must identify separate sold and pledged lots and confirm net fee-adjusted cash before releasing deposit payments. The controller must book the new loan loss6, sale loss82/45 and fee2/5 once each, and reconcile borrowing20. The risk function must independently challenge the quoted discount, facility availability, haircut and collection25. The plan adds a date2 principal obligation and encumbers assets; it is not a permanent cure for an unviable business model.

“At this date1 optimum, any adverse unmodeled cash flow requires an immediate revised funding decision. A missed quote or unavailable facility stops this plan; the treasury head informs the incident lead and the board risk chair under our fictional charter. Do not describe a settlement shortfall as a successful payment. Before date2, obtain a verified collection schedule and a replacement plan if collectible principal falls below20. Releasing the pledge follows the actual repayment documentation. We make no automatic assumption of central-bank assistance, deposit stability or permission to delay a payment.”

Controls, incentives and reporting attachment. “Operations maintains lot-level sale/pledge status and two-person payment authorization. Finance independently reconciles gross borrowing, the upfront fee, remaining securities and losses; the controller signs the date1 and date2 balances. Compliance maps actual reporting and notification duties; the authorized officer supplies a dated packet containing the original position, the40 runoff scenario, actual payments versus projections, counterparties and collateral availability, the two funding costs, unresolved collection evidence, actions and next update. It must explicitly state which figures are forecasts; this exercise sends no notification and invents no statutory deadline.

“Treasury's evaluation records verified timely execution, truthful escalation, cost relative to the feasible menu and preserved customer/operating duties. It does not reward suppressing runoff reports or treating a future refinance as already received. Independent review distinguishes market deterioration from an unauthorized exposure or concealed fact. The remuneration committee applies the authorized deferred-pay process to verified conduct and performance; a numerical funding success cannot erase a control breach, and a loss alone cannot prove one. Audit tests whether the lot register, quote and approval actually existed before execution.”

4. Stress the decision rule, not just the selected number

The funding case uses sale discount d and fee f. With d,f below1, cost per net cash unit is d/(1−d) for a sale and f/(1−f) for borrowing, before collateral, caps and repayment limits. Borrowing is cheaper on this immediate measure exactly when f<d. If f=d, these cost ratios tie and multiple allocations can minimize cost when all other constraints permit them. If f>d, sale-first may be preferable. A binding collateral constraint or a future repayment shortfall can overturn any unconstrained ranking.

For the original f1/50 and cap20, consider adverse discounts d≥1/10. The cash lower bound at b20 is s≥(82/5)/(1−d). Since d>f, increasing b reduces the minimum immediate cost, so no smaller b can rescue an equity failure at b20. Minimum cost is (82d)/(5(1−d))+2/5. Requiring it≤4 yields100d≤18, or d≤9/50. At d9/50, s20 and b20 meet cash and equity exactly, and remaining collateral supports45. Above9/50 the capital and cash conditions cannot both hold in this menu; this is a feasibility failure despite positive initial equity and substantial securities. The derivation assumes the same fee, cap, haircut, cash requirement and executable sale terms. It makes no probability claim about the discount.

A real risk-appetite record also needs concentration, currency, legal-entity transfer restrictions, intraday cutoffs, operational outages, customer protection and scenarios outside these small models. Add a missing constraint when evidence makes it relevant; do not hide it in an arbitrary score. For each revision preserve the old inputs, new evidence, recalculated feasible set, named approver and executable next step.
