Volume 18 · Chapter 10

Crisis leadership and high-stakes judgment

Capability. Make and revise a time-critical institutional decision from the evidence actually available, preserve accountability, communicate useful uncertainty and learn from the response without rewriting its history. Prerequisite: operations, innovation and change; recall conditional decisions and the value of information. Every incident, time, message, likelihood and outcome below is fictional. The cases teach decision discipline, not technical incident remediation, current emergency law or proof of actual leadership.

1. Triage changes the order of work

A crisis combines consequential exposure with limited time or impaired capacity. Triage means ordering attention and action by urgency, potential harm and available response—not by who speaks most loudly. First identify any immediate safety or essential-service condition requiring the actual emergency process. Then identify affected people and services, irreversible decisions, deadlines, available resources, uncertain facts and the person authorized to act. The small monetary models below begin only after their stated safety, access and authorization conditions hold; they do not price permission to violate an essential obligation.

Assign an incident lead, an evidence owner, an execution owner and an authorized communications owner. One person can hold more than one role in a small institution, but a revenue target should not silently overrule an independent control check. Keep an alternative communication route available. Protect personal information and evidence; do not distribute credentials, learner records or client instructions merely to demonstrate transparency. A cause can remain unknown while a service disruption and a useful protective action are already known.

Maintain a short decision log: time, source and version; what is verified; what is uncertain; feasible actions and deadlines; decision criterion; selected action and owner; trigger and next update. A proposal, authorization, command, acknowledgment and completed action are different events. Record them separately. A rumor repeated by several people is not automatically several independent observations. A receipt notification is not a second receipt when its identifier matches the first.

2. Evidence can change beliefs, available actions, or both

For an action a and a state s, let L(a,s) be the stipulated loss. If a defensible probability p is supplied, expected loss is a probability-weighted average. Choose the feasible action with the smallest expectation under the stated objective. This criterion is a design choice; tail exposure, rights or distributional safeguards may require extra constraints. State probabilities and loss values are model inputs, not facts established by the act of calculating.

A diagnostic Z changes beliefs through its likelihood. With adverse state P and prior p, Pr(P|Z)=p Pr(Z|P)/[p Pr(Z|P)+(1−p)Pr(Z|not P)], provided the denominator is positive. A signal with identical likelihood in both states leaves the prior unchanged. Repeated copies of the same signal do not multiply its likelihood. Another piece of evidence may change no state probability but establish that cash, a room or an approval is now available.

A contingent policy specifies which action follows each possible evidence release. If evidence arrives before action and does not change the feasible menu, minimize expected loss separately in each signal branch and average those minimum losses. This is globally optimal because each branch contribution has a nonnegative probability and its decision does not affect another branch in the supplied model. Add the diagnostic's cost and any delay consequences. Information cannot worsen the best attainable decision if it is free and may be ignored while the same actions remain feasible; a costly or late test need not help. Do not use that theorem after its conditions have disappeared.

When only a probability interval is justified, two different criteria are possible. Minimum worst-case expected loss minimizes the largest expected loss across that interval. Minimax expected regret minimizes the largest gap from the best feasible action at each probability. They need not agree. Neither is a worst-realized-loss guarantee. Declare the criterion before seeing which action it favors, and recompute the comparator menu when an action becomes unavailable.

3. Communicate a useful decision, not invented certainty

The CDC's communication chapter advises stating what is known and unknown, acknowledging concerns, explaining action and giving the next update time. We transfer only those bounded communication practices from its public-health context; we make no medical claim or claim that a message guarantees trust. A useful institutional update names the affected service, current action, audience instruction, uncertainty and next contact. Correct an earlier error explicitly rather than quietly replacing its timestamp.

Do not disclose a sensitive diagnostic detail simply because it exists. Equally, “we are investigating” without a next action can leave affected people unable to plan. The following complete messages are original examples; actual notices require the real institution's authority, accessibility arrangements and applicable obligations.

4. Academic crisis: a useful diagnostic has a deadline

5. Financial crisis: a funding fact is not a probability update

Case B — a disrupted payment-service route

09:00 decision contract. Northbridge, a fictional financial service firm, must choose a response for a payment batch by 09:15. Settlement principal is separately protected and cannot fund incident expenses. The case assumes every listed action is within its actual authorization and customer-handling requirements; otherwise that action must be removed. State P means the original route fails to complete by its service target; S means it works. The evidence supports only p∈[1/10,2/5], not a point probability or a confidence interval. The numbers are stipulated scenario bounds. There is no instruction here to bypass payment controls or to infer a cyberattack.

Two criteria, two recommendations. Expected losses are 20 p,6 and 9. Over the supplied interval, worst expected losses are (8,6,9), so the declared minimum-worst-expected-loss criterion selects A. It protects against uncertainty about p, not every realized loss in every possible incident. By contrast, the best expected loss at a given p is min(20 p,6); H is dominated by A. R's maximum regret is 2 at p 2/5, A's is 4 at p 1/10, and H's is 7 at p 1/10: (2,4,7). A minimax-regret decision would select R.

Global proof. Each expected-loss function is affine in p, so its maximum on an interval is attained at an endpoint. Regret is La(p)−minjLj(p)=maxj[La(p)−Lj(p)], a maximum of affine functions and hence convex. For any p between the endpoints, convexity bounds regret by the corresponding weighted endpoint regrets, which cannot exceed their maximum. Endpoint comparison is therefore sufficient for the full interval, not just a numerical grid. Choosing between these criteria is a policy judgment. Northbridge declares the first before the subsequent evidence releases; it does not switch criteria to defend a preferred action after the fact.

6. Close the response without closing the evidence

The inspected NIST SP 800-61 Revision 3 places response within broader risk management and continuous improvement. We use that limited process connection; its cybersecurity scope does not establish that either fictional outage was malicious or that every institution has the same duties. The original after-action records here separate facts, judgments, outcomes and proposed repairs.

Evaluate a decision using the information and feasible actions available when it was made. Evaluate implementation using the actual command, timing and outcome. Evaluate the model using a suitable body of evidence, not whether one signal “came true.” These are related but different reviews. A successful result can coexist with a broken approval control, and a costly result can follow the best available decision. For each lesson, specify the failed or successful mechanism, evidence, owner, due date, test and who will accept the correction. Preserve the earlier record so improvements can be checked against it. Stop claiming the crisis is fully closed while material reconciliations or affected people remain unresolved.
