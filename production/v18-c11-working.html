Real responsibility and leadership evaluation

Capability. Evaluate attributable leadership from authorized decisions, actual execution records, resource use and informed external feedback. The prerequisite is crisis leadership and high-stakes judgment; retain its discipline of recording what was known when a decision was made. This chapter also uses evidence-based personnel evaluation, returns and payoffs and potential outcomes. Both dossiers below are wholly invented, including dates, signatures described in words, observations and feedback. They depict completed actions inside fiction; they are not actual learner work or independent human certification.

1. Responsibility needs a traceable object

A polished retrospective can describe work its author never owned. An accurate outcome number can conceal whose decision produced it. Begin with a bounded assignment: the decision rights, people, resources, constraints, deliverables and time interval. Then identify the records that connect the assigned person to actual acts. Approval to run a project is evidence of authority; it is not evidence that the project ran. A submitted artifact is evidence of production; it is not necessarily accepted delivery. A favorable result is an observation; it does not by itself identify a person's causal contribution.

Ownership means an accountable role with specified authority and obligations. Execution evidence records what was done, by whom, when and with what assistance. Acceptance evidence applies the agreed criteria to delivered work. Outcome evidence records the subsequent result and its denominator. Attribution can mean an accounting allocation of an observed result or a causal comparison with an unobserved alternative; always state which. External feedback is an identifiable review by someone other than the person being evaluated, with access, relationship, conflicts and scope disclosed. External to the owner does not necessarily mean independent of the institution or incentive to praise.

Use an evidence chain: original mandate; dated plan and baseline; approvals and changes; work and cost records; acceptance or rejection; feedback; response; later verification. Preserve failed attempts and prior versions. A later correction can improve the result without making the original error disappear. Team members deserve accurate contribution credit, while an owner remains accountable for the coordination and escalations within the role. Avoid both claiming every team achievement and attributing every external setback to an individual.

The Department of Energy's earned-value overview explains why spending must be compared with completed work valued at the planned budget, as well as with scheduled work. We use that measurement idea, not DOE contracting requirements or certification. The CFA Institute's public portfolio-evaluation summary distinguishes measurement, attribution and appraisal and emphasizes appropriate benchmarks and method limits. These sources orient the two original models; neither validates our fictional assessments or supplies a universal promotion rule.

2. Derive the measure before judging the person

For a project with tasks i, let bᵢ be the original budget assigned to each task, zᵢ(t) indicate whether it was scheduled complete by date t, and aᵢ(t) indicate whether it has actually passed the declared acceptance test. Here acceptance is binary, with no partial credit. Let cᵢ(t) include every cost incurred on the task, including rejected work and rework. Then total baseline budget BAC=Σbᵢ, planned value PV=Σbᵢzᵢ, earned value EV=Σbᵢaᵢ, and actual cost AC=Σcᵢ. All four are currency amounts; earned value is neither revenue nor market value.

Cost variance EV−AC compares earned budget with spending. Schedule variance EV−PV compares accepted with scheduled scope, expressed in budget units. When AC>0 and PV>0, cost performance index CPI=EV/AC and schedule performance index SPI=EV/PV. They are descriptive ratios. An SPI of.75 is not proof of a25% calendar delay; task dependencies and dates matter. At eventual completion EV=PV=BAC even if delivery was late. If a denominator is zero, report the corresponding ratio as undefined and retain the underlying amounts; zero divided by zero is not evidence of excellent performance.

Forecasts need additional assumptions. If the remaining work costs its original budget divided by the observed CPI, estimated total cost is AC+(BAC−EV)/CPI, equal to BAC/CPI when CPI=EV/AC>0. That extrapolation can fail after a specific defect is fixed or new work differs. A bottom-up forecast instead lists remaining tasks and their expected costs. Neither forecast becomes an actual cost until the work is done and recorded. An authorized funding ceiling can exceed the original baseline; disclose both rather than moving the baseline to erase an overrun.

For a one-period fully invested portfolio with no external cash flows or interim trades, let pᵢ and bᵢ be opening portfolio and benchmark weights, each nonnegative and summing to one. Let rᵢ and sᵢ be total returns of the selected assets and the corresponding benchmark sector. Define Rₚ=Σpᵢrᵢ and Rᵦ=Σbᵢsᵢ. In this section bᵢ is a weight, not the earlier task budget; the case tables make the units explicit. Add and subtract terms after writing pᵢ=bᵢ+Δwᵢ and rᵢ=sᵢ+Δrᵢ:

Rₚ−Rᵦ = ΣΔwᵢsᵢ + ΣbᵢΔrᵢ + ΣΔwᵢΔrᵢ = A+S+I.

A is the allocation term, S selection at benchmark weights, and I their interaction. Expanding (bᵢ+Δwᵢ)(sᵢ+Δrᵢ) proves the identity for every allowed vector, not only the forthcoming numbers. Since ΣΔwᵢ=0, replacing sᵢ with sᵢ−Rᵦ in A leaves its total unchanged. One may combine S+I=ΣpᵢΔrᵢ, but must not then add I again. This is an arithmetic decomposition of a stated comparison; it is not a separate experiment assigning decisions to individuals. With fixed end-period incremental fee F and opening wealth W>0, net active return is A+S+I−F/W. State the benchmark's costs on the same basis.

Distinguish comparator from personal causal contribution. Let Y(1)=10 be this account's observed net profit under the actual team/owner assignment. Let Y(0) be net profit under a specified replacement-owner process, with the same opening resources and dates; that alternative might choose the benchmark or something else permitted. The registered benchmark profit14 is observable as a stipulated implementable comparison, but the fact that it exists does not prove that the replacement process would choose it. The team has one realized assignment, not random allocation of owner responsibility.

As an explicitly additional sensitivity restriction, suppose Y(0)∈[8,14] mCU. Then (-4,2) are the sharp bounds on θ:10−14=−4 and10−8=2. Sharpness follows because each endpoint, and any intermediate Y(0), can be paired with the same observed Y(1)=10; the case imposes no further coupling restriction. These are conditional logical bounds, not confidence limits, estimated probabilities or evidence that the interval restriction is true. Positive contribution is guaranteed only if the defensible upper bound on Y(0) is strictly below10; a bound equal to10 permits zero. Even θ identified for this account would not isolate Theo from Vale, Uma and Oren without a different intervention and further assumptions.

3. Turn the specimen into actual responsibility

A learner must obtain a real, appropriately bounded assignment from someone authorized to give it; reading the dossier supplies none. Agree in advance on decision rights, budget, time, support, safety/ethical limits, deliverables, review criteria and who can observe. A small academic service or permitted analytical operations project can provide more usable evidence than an impressive but unverifiable title. Do not take control of institutional money, people or sensitive data merely to complete an exercise. The actual institution supplies authority and access rules.

Retain permitted evidence without exposing confidential records: an authorized work description, dated decision/cost register, actual output, acceptance or rejection, identified human observations and a response to feedback. Where raw records cannot be shared, arrange an authorized verifier and disclose the restricted scope; do not fabricate public substitutes and call them observed. Record assistance and team ownership. Ask the evaluator to separate direct observation from reported claims, disclose relationships, examine a difficult or failed decision, and specify what wider responsibility remains untested.

A defensible retrospective answers four connected questions in prose: what authority and information did I have; what did I actually decide and deliver; which results and constraints followed; what does the record justify about my contribution and next responsibility? Use the same original baseline and comparison even when unfavorable. A proposed improvement becomes evidence only after execution and review. External feedback can be mistaken; resolve a factual disagreement through the underlying record and retain both versions rather than treating praise or criticism as infallible.
